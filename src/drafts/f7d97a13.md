In 1895, the Lumière brothers projected workers leaving a factory. The camera worked, but cinema had barely begun.

In 2026, we ask a large language model to write a poem, get a polished copy of an old form and call the medium slop. We are judging the camera before anyone has found the cut.

A large language model, or LLM, learns patterns from training data and predicts the next likely piece. Familiar forms make imitation the easiest place to begin.

Musician and [Patreon co-founder Jack Conte](https://www.patreon.com/about) describes this phase in his SXSW talk, [*The future of creativity on the internet in a world with AI*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=17_HcR95YBc), published on 16 July 2026. Slop, in his framing, is the lazy period when a new medium reproduces work that humans already make better.

I think the insult contains a map. Slop shows an old assignment wrapped around a new machine.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>LLMs begin with imitation because familiar forms give them a clear target. Slop tells us more about the assignment than the eventual limits of the medium.</p></div>

## Film had to escape the stage

Early filmmakers aimed a fixed camera at actors arranged like a theater cast.

In 1896, Maxim Gorky watched a Lumière program. [Conte quotes him](https://youtu.be/17_HcR95YBc?t=284) describing a world without sound or color, with everything "dipped in monotonous gray." The machine could capture motion. Theater still supplied the standard.

Georges Méliès changed what the screen could do. His 1902 [*A Trip to the Moon*](https://www.acmi.net.au/works/603--a-trip-to-the-moon-le-voyage-dans-la-lune/) used cuts and camera tricks to make bodies vanish and places change instantly.

The cut became part of thought itself. In *2001: A Space Odyssey*, a bone thrown into the air becomes an object in orbit. Millions of years pass in a second. A stage cannot make that jump.

I think generative AI is close to that fixed-camera moment. We ask for an image that looks hand-drawn or a scene that looks filmed. Better prompts can improve the copy while the creative job stays borrowed.

## New media also rearrange livelihoods

The history becomes less comforting when the machine reaches someone's paycheck.

The American Federation of Musicians called recorded accompaniment ["canned music"](https://www.afm.org/125-years-afm-history/). Its history estimates that talkies cost about 22,000 theater musicians their jobs. The union began a recording strike in 1942.

The 1939 Hammond Novachord brought the same fear inside the instrument. Union [rules published that year](https://www.worldradiohistory.com/Archive-All-Music/International-Musician/30s/International-Musician-1939-05.pdf) stopped orchestras from reducing the number of players when they added the machine. Solo use in a venue that had employed musicians required local union consent.

The economic pressure was real. [Baumol and Bowen's cost disease](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/insights/no-easy-remedy-symphony-orchestra-cost-disease) explains why a quartet still needs four players even as productivity rises elsewhere. A Stanford summary of 2008 research reported deficits at 46 of 63 American orchestras studied.

Artists eventually used sound in ways silent work could not hold. Charlie Chaplin resisted synchronized dialogue for years, then made the 1940 [*The Great Dictator*](https://www.bfi.org.uk/film/009e372a-646d-5b6e-a6b0-59f96722a1fd/the-great-dictator). Its closing speech turned sound into a direct human appeal. The Beatles released [*Revolver*](https://www.thebeatles.com/revolver) in 1966 with tape loops and reversed sounds. The studio entered the composition.

I think hope needs this bill attached. A medium may create new work while old livelihoods take the impact. Calling slop temporary gives us no excuse for unclear licenses or missing attribution.

## Slop gives the model the wrong job

A request for a lost Beatles song begins with a human answer and asks the model to copy its surface.

Conte proposes a [thought experiment](https://youtu.be/17_HcR95YBc?t=2828). Train a music model only on recordings made before 1959, then ask it to discover the Beatles, Prince, Nirvana, Biggie, Beyoncé or Jacob Collier. This is a philosophical challenge rather than a published benchmark. It asks whether pattern learning can produce a cultural break before anyone has supplied examples of the break.

Artists carry a different kind of pressure. Conte quotes *Sing Street*: ["Rock and roll is a risk. You risk being ridiculed."](https://youtu.be/17_HcR95YBc?t=3006) He credits Karen X Cheng with a related line: "Art is scarcity. Art is not what's easy to make, it's what's hard."

A model can search unusual outputs. It carries no reputation when one fails. A musician releases the strange choice under their own name. Conte makes the same point through [*good kid, m.A.A.d city*](https://youtu.be/17_HcR95YBc?t=3287): the record reaches us with Kendrick Lamar's life attached.

I think this is why better imitation can deepen [the pull toward familiar output](/posts/the-elevator-music-already-started-playing/). Fidelity improves while the human stakes remain outside the model.

Now change the song assignment. A listener hears sixteen bars and selects a short motif. The system writes the next passage in response. A human composer owns one hard rule: every version must contain a full measure of silence.

Generation now shapes the experience, while the audience can still hear a human decision in every version.

## Build the song as a bounded system

My take is to start with the replacement test.

Swap the model for a static track. If the central experience survives, remove the model. Try a written branch library next. Generation earns its place when new listener motifs make a fixed set of branches run out.

The state can stay small:

```python
from typing import TypedDict

class SongState(TypedDict):
    consent: bool
    chosen_motif: str
    source_license: str
    silent_beats: int
    composer_approved: bool
```

A `TypedDict` is a Python type hint for a dictionary with expected keys. It can warn about a missing `chosen_motif` during development. Runtime validation still needs real code.

[LangGraph](https://docs.langchain.com/oss/python/langgraph/graph-api) is a Python library for workflows that branch or repeat. Its `StateGraph` connects named steps through shared state. For the song, those steps are consent, compose, validate and composer approval.

[ReAct](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629) means reasoning and acting. A model chooses an action, reads the result and uses that observation for its next choice. The song only needs ReAct when the model chooses among tools. A failed silence check can follow a fixed edge in the graph.

Keep the prompt and model version for each run, along with cost and latency. The composer approves release, while ordinary code enforces the silent measure. That boundary belongs in [the spec before delegation](/posts/the-spec-is-the-cognitive-work/).

I think junior engineers are well placed to run this kind of experiment. The graph is small enough to inspect. One rejected passage can teach more than another polished demo because the failure points at the medium's unfinished edge.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>`TypedDict` describes the song state, while `StateGraph` controls the route. Use ReAct only when the model must choose an action from an observation. Code enforces the composer's rule, and the composer controls release.</p></div>

## Keep the answer open

Conte describes Pomplamoose's career as an ["infinite sine wave"](https://youtu.be/17_HcR95YBc?t=2390). YouTube changed discovery. Streaming weakened the band's iTunes model. COVID changed how the musicians recorded together. Vertical video forced another production setup.

Each stable answer moved. The band changed with it.

I don't know whether generative AI will escape imitation. Some media never do. By 2030, we should be able to point to a work whose central experience collapses when a template or fixed branch replaces the model. The human author should remain easy to name. If every celebrated example survives the replacement, the 1895 comparison has failed.

The next build does not need to imitate Kendrick or rescue poetry. Give one model a bounded job inside the adaptive song. Remove it. See what breaks.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The 1895 comparison is a testable bet. By 2030, a serious AI work should fail the replacement test while keeping its human author visible. Until then, remove the model from each prototype and see what breaks.</p></div>
