In 1895, the Lumière brothers filmed a train arriving at a station and called it cinema. Critics found early film lifeless. In 2026, we prompt a large language model to write a Beatles song and call it AI-generated content. We are making the same mistake.

A large language model, or LLM, learns patterns from existing work and predicts what could come next. A familiar request keeps our attention on the old form.

I tried to treat AI slop as a quality problem. Jack Conte, a Pomplamoose artist and Patreon's co-founder, gave me a wider frame in his [2026 SXSW talk](https://youtu.be/17_HcR95YBc). I think we are watching a young medium imitate before it finds a grammar of its own.

<div class="article-tldr">
<span class="article-tldr__label">The takeaway</span>
<p>AI slop is the imitation phase of a young medium, and that phase can still hurt working artists. Junior engineers can move past it by building experiences that remember one person while preserving a choice owned by a human.</p>
</div>

## The Gorky phase

Maxim Gorky watched a Lumière screening in 1896 and saw "a world without sound, without colour" where everything was "dipped in monotonous grey."

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Young media often begin by copying an older form. Film found the cut, and recorded music found the studio, after painful years of imitation.</p></div>

I think Gorky's review was fair. Early filmmakers put theater in front of a fixed camera. The machine worked, but the experience felt thinner than the stage it copied.

Georges Méliès changed the assignment. In 1902, *A Trip to the Moon* used cuts and camera tricks to move a story between worlds. Kubrick pushed the same grammar further in *2001: A Space Odyssey*. One cut joins a falling bone to a spacecraft and crosses millions of years. A stage cannot do that.

Recorded music brought heavier consequences. The American Federation of Musicians attacked "canned music" in 1930s ads. By 1930, the union estimated that sound film had cost 22,000 moviehouse musicians their jobs. A separate union recording ban ran from 1942 to 1944. The people who feared the machine had evidence.

Artists still found a new form inside it. The Beatles made *Revolver* in 1966 with tape loops and reversed guitar. Conte quotes producer George Martin describing the studio as "a new canvas on which they had to paint." Charlie Chaplin resisted talkies for years before using his voice in *The Great Dictator* in 1940.

The Hammond Novachord brought the same conflict to one 500-pound instrument. Unveiled in 1939, the first commercial polyphonic synthesizer used 163 vacuum tubes. Conte describes a union prohibition, although independent evidence is thin.

The labor problem remained. Baumol and Bowen named the cost disease of the performing arts in 1966: a Haydn symphony still takes about 25 minutes while its costs rise. Conte cites a [2008 Stanford GSB analysis](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/insights/no-easy-remedy-symphony-orchestra-cost-disease) that reported deficits at 46 of the 63 largest US orchestras.

History offers evidence rather than a guarantee. I think these years deserve a name: the Gorky phase, when we force a working medium to do the previous medium's job.

## Slop copies the artifact and loses the risk

Our fake Beatles song makes the missing human risk easy to hear.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Slop copies the surface of work whose meaning came from a person taking a risk. Better fidelity can polish the copy while leaving the human stake outside it.</p></div>

Conte defines slop as "that lazy phase after the introduction of a new medium where we're all just trying to reproduce existing things that are honestly just better if the people do them." Ask the model for a Beatles song and the request points straight at familiar patterns in its training data.

Pretraining gives common patterns a wide path. Preference tuning pulls answers toward responses that human raters accept. A surprising verse can still sit inside a culturally safe song.

AlphaGo's Move 37 against Lee Sedol in 2016 gives us a counterexample. A trained system found a move that professional players would have avoided. Games have also used procedural generation for decades. They give the machine rules and a score it can test.

Culture has no clean win function. In Conte's thought experiment, a model trained only on pre-1959 music could not find its way to the artists who followed. I think current models can search a style far better than they can decide which rule deserves to break.

*Sing Street* gives the gap one sentence: "Rock and roll is a risk. You risk being ridiculed." The model carries no reputation when our fake Beatles song fails. A musician who releases a strange track under their own name carries the response.

In the talk, Conte credits creator Karen X Cheng with the phrase "Art is scarcity" and the claim that difficult work carries weight. I could not trace the phrasing elsewhere. Kendrick Lamar's *good kid, m.A.A.d city* carries his life along with the sound.

## Give the song a memory and one human rule

Our fake Beatles song becomes a different engineering problem once every listen can change its next version.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Let the song remember one listener, then keep one composer-owned rule outside the model's control. The rejected edit becomes the point where authorship shows up in the system.</p></div>

Imagine an evolving song that remembers one listener's favorite motif. The composer requires a full bar of silence in every version, even when fewer listeners finish it.

The state can stay small:

```python
from typing import TypedDict

class SongState(TypedDict):
    listener_id: str
    favorite_motif: str
    last_skip_second: int
    required_silence_bars: int
    version: int
```

A `TypedDict` tells Python tools which fields belong in this dictionary. In our example, it helps your editor catch a misspelled `listener_id`. A guard, a function that checks writes while the program runs, enforces that only the composer may change `required_silence_bars`.

LangGraph is a Python library for connected LLM steps. Its `StateGraph` class passes `SongState` between the composer and the guard. The app saves a version after the guard accepts it.

ReAct is short for reasoning and acting. In this loop, the model chooses an action, sees the result and chooses again. Version 12 notices that the listener skipped the silent bar. It proposes changing `required_silence_bars` from `1` to `0`.

The guard rejects that transition and sends the unchanged state back. The model may move the silence or change the tempo, while the composer's field survives. [The spec becomes the cognitive work](/posts/the-spec-is-the-cognitive-work/) because one line decides which optimization the system must refuse.

I think this is a better junior AI project than another chat wrapper. You can build the first version with a model API and a small database. The test is whether the song develops a history while keeping the composer's decision.

## Run the imitation test on Monday

A team can apply the 1895 frame without turning it into an innovation workshop.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Classify AI pilots by whether they copy old work or require the medium's new abilities. A five-day build can test the second kind before the team funds a bigger pilot.</p></div>

Mark each AI pilot as a copy or a medium experiment. An email summary may still save real time. Our evolving song loses its purpose when a fresh run erases the relationship.

Give one junior engineer five days and a €100 cloud budget. Store listener events in SQLite, with the composer rule in application code where the model cannot rewrite it. At the end of the week, compare the first song with the latest one and inspect the rejected transitions. The version diff and guard log tell you whether the experiment worked.

Conte calls Pomplamoose's career an infinite sine wave. A YouTube algorithm change flattened its growth. The band rebuilt around iTunes and later Spotify. COVID stopped touring, then TikTok changed discovery again. Change kept forcing new work.

The Gorky phase will also repeat inside AI. Once someone finds a native pattern, model makers and product teams will copy it into a preset. That cycle is already visible in [the elevator music generated by AI loops](/posts/the-elevator-music-already-started-playing/).

My bet is dated so it can fail. By 2030, the AI work people care about most will carry a history with one person, and a human-owned constraint will shape every version. If the celebrated result is still a cleaner fake Beatles song, my prediction failed.

I don't know who will find the first native verb for this medium. I think a junior engineer can be the person who builds it.

We already know the prompt works. I want to see what you make after you stop filming the stage.
