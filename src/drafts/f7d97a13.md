In 1895, the Lumière brothers filmed workers leaving their factory with a fixed camera. People crossed the frame as if the lens were a theatre seat.

In 2026, we ask a large language model to write a poem and call it AI-generated content. An LLM predicts likely next words from training patterns, producing something shaped like a poem.

I think we are repeating the early filmmakers' mistake. We judge a young medium by how well it copies work built for an older one.

Pomplamoose musician and Patreon co-founder Jack Conte has skin in this argument. In his July 2026 talk, [*The future of creativity on the internet in a world with AI*](https://youtu.be/17_HcR95YBc?t=844), he calls slop the lazy phase when a new medium reproduces things humans already make better.

That frame lowers the temperature without dismissing the damage. Slop may pass while a working artist loses income.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>AI slop resembles early cinema because both began by copying an older creative form. The phase may pass, while the cost to working creators arrives immediately.</p></div>

## Film found the cut

Early cinema inherited theatre's rules because those were the rules its makers knew.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Film grew once filmmakers used the break between images as part of the art. AI remains early while imitation is its main proof of quality.</p></div>

Maxim Gorky saw a Lumière programme in 1896 and described ["a world without sound, without colour"](https://publish.iupress.indiana.edu/read/91ddc4e0-5576-4f98-a3b6-ba8a97703eda/section/2fe1a387-c815-45d7-9609-2a47615dc9e8), with everything "dipped in monotonous grey." Moving pictures had flattened the life he knew.

Stage magician Georges Méliès filmed an empty box, stopped recording, put a person inside, then restarted. Joining the strips made the person appear. That join was the cut. By 1902, [*A Trip to the Moon*](https://www.acmi.net.au/works/603--a-trip-to-the-moon-le-voyage-dans-la-lune/) used film grammar to build a voyage beyond the theatre.

Decades later, *2001: A Space Odyssey* opens with an early human discovering that a bone can be a tool. He throws it into the air. Kubrick cuts to a similarly shaped spacecraft, compressing millennia between two frames. The [BFI calls it cinema's most famous match cut](https://www.bfi.org.uk/film/cefccdb2-b558-5623-9c17-72b4be7bd4c1/2001-a-space-odyssey).

I think AI creativity still has its camera in the tenth row. Better prompts often sharpen the same stage. Méliès offers a harder standard: put the machinery inside the work.

## Creators pay before a medium matures

Technical discovery moves slowly compared with a rent payment.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Recorded sound removed jobs before artists found its strongest creative uses. Exploration earns trust when creators keep control and share in the value during the transition.</p></div>

The American Federation of Musicians called recorded accompaniment ["canned music"](https://www.afm.org/125-years-afm-history/). Its history says about 22,000 theatre musicians lost their jobs when talking pictures arrived in 1927. The union formed the Music Defense League in 1930.

The Hammond Novachord triggered a similar fight in 1939. This early synthesizer could play several notes at once. AFM [rules published that May](https://www.worldradiohistory.com/Archive-All-Music/International-Musician/30s/International-Musician-1939-05.pdf) protected orchestra headcount. Solo use where musicians had worked required local consent.

William Baumol and William Bowen later called this pressure the [cost disease of the performing arts](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/insights/no-easy-remedy-symphony-orchestra-cost-disease). A string quartet still needs four players and roughly the same time while other industries get faster. Speed changes the music.

Recorded sound gave artists choices beyond a live room. Charlie Chaplin resisted synchronized speech for years, then used it for the closing address in 1940's [*The Great Dictator*](https://www.charliechaplin.com/en/films/1-The-Great-Dictator). The Beatles used backwards sound and tape experiments on 1966's [*Revolver*](https://www.thebeatles.com/revolver).

I think AI builders inherit two obligations. Set the licence and payment rule before a prototype runs. Keep release control with a named creative owner. A future masterpiece cannot repay a creator whose work was taken today.

## A model has no reputation to lose

Creative risk belongs to the person who may be ridiculed when the work arrives.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Models can recombine learned patterns, while artists put a name and a life behind a break with convention. That human stake changes how creative work is made and heard.</p></div>

Conte offers a thought experiment [47 minutes into the talk](https://youtu.be/17_HcR95YBc?t=2828). Train a music model only on recordings made through 1959, then ask it to reach the Beatles, Prince, Nirvana or Beyoncé.

The model searches learned patterns. Cultural jumps often come from a person choosing the low-probability move before an audience rewards it. The musician risks trust. A model output gets a lower evaluation score.

I don't know whether future systems will make those breaks without a person choosing the risk. A generated song has no person who must defend the choice on stage tomorrow.

Conte explains this through Kendrick Lamar's *good kid, m.A.A.d city* [near the end of the talk](https://youtu.be/17_HcR95YBc?t=3317). Kendrick's background and pain shape how listeners hear the album. Conte [hates the word "content"](https://youtu.be/17_HcR95YBc?t=3272) because it flattens that bond into one more file.

I think this is why endless generated culture can feel like [elevator music](/posts/the-elevator-music-already-started-playing/) even when each file is technically polished. Human contact changes the experience before the first note plays.

## Give generation a test it can fail

A junior AI engineer can turn the 1895 argument into one bounded product test.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Compare generation with a serious authored baseline before it enters the product. Keep the model only when it improves the listener's experience inside limits approved by the creator.</p></div>

Suppose a composer builds a call-and-response song. A listener plays four notes, and the system replies inside the composer's rules. The motif was absent during production. Generation might earn its place here.

Build the baseline first. The composer records replies for known motif families, and code routes inputs to authored branches. Compare those branches with generated replies on held-back motifs. Hide the source of each reply, then apply a pre-agreed listening measure and the composer's approval.

Give the pilot two weeks and a fixed spend. The composer owns release approval. Product owns the listener measure. A licence owner signs the data scope. One engineer owns the kill switch.

| Result | Decision |
|---|---|
| Rights or creator approval fails | Reject release |
| Generated replies fail to beat authored branches on the agreed listener measure | Ship the branches |
| Generated replies beat the baseline within the cost and latency limits | Continue a limited pilot |

The state can stay small and visible:

```python
from typing import Literal, TypedDict

class SongState(TypedDict):
    motif: list[int]
    rights_verified: bool
    composer_approved: bool
    status: Literal["draft", "rejected", "released"]

def validate(state: SongState) -> None:
    if state["status"] == "released" and not (
        state["rights_verified"] and state["composer_approved"]
    ):
        raise ValueError("illegal release transition")
```

`TypedDict` lets a type checker catch a missing field before the program runs. The guard rejects an illegal release, and a test should trigger it with false rights.

If the flow later branches or loops, LangGraph's `StateGraph` can route this shared state between steps. ReAct alternates model reasoning with tool calls. This pilot has one fixed model call, so ReAct would add machinery before a tool-choice problem exists.

I think this is a better starting point than adding an agent because the architecture diagram looks modern. [The spec is the cognitive work](/posts/the-spec-is-the-cognitive-work/), especially when the easiest decision is removing the model.

## Change comes in waves

Conte has lived this cycle as an artist, which gives his optimism a personal cost.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Pomplamoose survived repeated platform shocks by rebuilding how it worked. AI can develop a valuable creative form, but history offers a direction rather than a guarantee.</p></div>

Pomplamoose built an audience through YouTube and digital music sales. Conte calls the band's disruptions an ["infinite sine wave"](https://youtu.be/17_HcR95YBc?t=2390). Growth flattened around YouTube's Cosmic Panda redesign, although he says they did not know the cause at the time. Streaming weakened the iTunes model. COVID changed how the band recorded, and vertical video later demanded new production systems.

Each wave broke a setup that had worked. The band rebuilt and kept making music.

I think cheap imitation could dominate feeds while a more valuable AI form grows elsewhere. History permits exploration and leaves us responsible for its conditions.

Slop is what we get while the camera is still in the tenth row. The medium begins when somebody makes the cut that only this machine allows, then accepts responsibility for releasing it.
