In 1895, the Lumière brothers filmed workers leaving their factory. The camera stayed still, like a seat facing a theatre stage.

In 2026, we prompt a large language model to write a poem. An LLM learns patterns from training data and predicts likely pieces of text. The result sounds vaguely like poems we already know.

I think we are repeating an early mistake. We judge a new medium by how well it copies an object made for an older one.

Jack Conte sees the tension as a Pomplamoose musician and Patreon co-founder. In his July 2026 talk, [*The future of creativity on the internet in a world with AI*](https://youtu.be/17_HcR95YBc?t=844), he calls slop the lazy phase when a new medium reproduces things people already make better.

That frame leaves the harm in view and places today's generated flood in media history. Slop may be a medium's awkward first use.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>AI slop resembles early cinema because both began by copying an older creative form. The phase can pass while the harm to working creators remains immediate.</p></div>

## Film had to invent the cut

Early film often looked theatrical while its screen language was still developing.

Maxim Gorky watched a Lumière programme in 1896 and described ["a world without sound, without colour"](https://publish.iupress.indiana.edu/read/91ddc4e0-5576-4f98-a3b6-ba8a97703eda/section/2fe1a387-c815-45d7-9609-2a47615dc9e8), with everything "dipped in monotonous grey." The machine moved images, but the copy felt drained of life.

Georges Méliès found a possibility that belonged to film. He filmed an empty box, stopped the camera, placed a person inside, and restarted it. The joined strips made the person appear. That splice was a cut. By 1902, [*A Trip to the Moon*](https://www.acmi.net.au/works/603--a-trip-to-the-moon-le-voyage-dans-la-lune/) used such effects to create screen transformations.

In *2001: A Space Odyssey*, a prehistoric ape discovers that a bone can be a tool and throws it into the air. Kubrick cuts to a similarly shaped spacecraft. The [BFI describes the edit as "justly celebrated"](https://www.bfi.org.uk/film/cefccdb2-b558-5623-9c17-72b4be7bd4c1/2001-a-space-odyssey). Millennia pass at the join.

I think much of AI creativity still has its camera in the tenth row. Models copy the look of drawings or the voice of writers. Imitation works, yet reveals little about generation's own grammar.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Film grew when the join between images became part of the art. AI remains early while resemblance is its main proof of creative value.</p></div>

## The bill arrives before the language

New media can find beautiful uses after they have damaged somebody's livelihood.

When talking pictures arrived in 1927, theatres needed fewer live musicians. The American Federation of Musicians called recorded accompaniment ["canned music"](https://www.afm.org/125-years-afm-history/). About 22,000 theatre musicians lost their jobs. The union formed the Music Defense League in 1930.

The Hammond Novachord caused another fight in 1939. This early commercial synthesizer could produce sounds beyond acoustic instruments. AFM [rules published that May](https://www.worldradiohistory.com/Archive-All-Music/International-Musician/30s/International-Musician-1939-05.pdf) required orchestras to keep their existing headcount. Solo use where musicians had worked needed local union approval.

William Baumol and William Bowen later named the [cost disease of the performing arts](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/insights/no-easy-remedy-symphony-orchestra-cost-disease). A string quartet still needs four players and roughly the same time, even while other industries get faster.

Artists eventually used recorded sound as material. Chaplin built the Little Tramp through silent film, where the character crossed language borders, and resisted synchronized dialogue for years. In 1940, he used speech for the closing address of [*The Great Dictator*](https://www.charliechaplin.com/en/films/7-The-Great-Dictator). The Beatles used tape loops and backwards sound on 1966's [*Revolver*](https://www.thebeatles.com/revolver), making the studio part of the composition.

I think this history makes easy reassurance dishonest. New art appeared, while those theatre musicians still lost their work. Any serious AI experiment needs licensed material and a creator who can stop release.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Recorded sound removed jobs before artists found some of its strongest uses. AI experimentation earns trust when creators agree to the source terms and keep release authority.</p></div>

## Prediction carries no reputation

[Forty-seven minutes into the talk](https://youtu.be/17_HcR95YBc?t=2828), Conte asks whether a model trained only on music through 1959 could produce the break that led to the Beatles or Beyoncé.

This is an argument about invention rather than a proof about every future model. A model searches what training made probable. An artist can choose something that sounds wrong, attach a name to it, and face ridicule if the bet fails.

I don't know how far future systems will move beyond learned patterns. Models already produce surprising combinations. A model faces none of the social consequences of a failed show. The person who releases the work does.

That person changes how we hear the result. Conte says Kendrick Lamar's *good kid, m.A.A.d city* means more because Kendrick's background and pain enter the experience [near the end of the talk](https://youtu.be/17_HcR95YBc?t=3317). He [hates the word "content"](https://youtu.be/17_HcR95YBc?t=3272) because it flattens that bond into one more file.

This helps explain why polished generation can feel like [elevator music](/posts/the-elevator-music-already-started-playing/). The output may be competent. The listener still has nobody to believe, forgive, or follow.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Models can recombine learned patterns, while an artist puts a name and a livelihood behind a break with convention. The artist's stake becomes part of the work an audience receives.</p></div>

## Give generation a job

Return to the poem. A poet asks each reader for one line about a place they miss. The system writes a new stanza inside rules the poet approved. Only generation can answer that unseen line directly.

The fair baseline is a set of responses the poet wrote for common themes. Hide which path produced each reply. Ask readers which feels connected to their line, then compare that result with the added cost and wait time. A generated reply that wins earns another test. If the authored replies work better, ship those.

Keep the first build plain:

```python
from typing import TypedDict

class PoemState(TypedDict):
    reader_line: str
    rights_verified: bool
    poet_approved: bool
    stanza: str

def may_release(state: PoemState) -> bool:
    return state["rights_verified"] and state["poet_approved"]
```

`TypedDict` lists the keys a Python dictionary should contain. It catches missing fields during type checking, while runtime policy lives in `may_release`.

If the flow later needs retries or review branches, LangGraph can help. Its `StateGraph` connects steps around shared state such as `PoemState`. ReAct lets a model alternate reasoning with tool actions. This poem has no tool-choice problem, so ReAct adds machinery without improving the experience.

Before the prototype leaves a developer's machine, four decisions need owners:

| Check | Owner | Stop condition |
|---|---|---|
| Source rights | Data or licensing owner | The licence scope is undocumented |
| Creative release | Poet | Approval is missing |
| Reader value | Product owner | Generation loses to the authored baseline |
| Submission retention | Privacy or security owner | Deletion verification fails |

I think this is the builder's test: use generation for an experience built around unseen input, then make it earn its cost and risk. Start with clear behavior, the discipline behind [treating the spec as cognitive work](/posts/the-spec-is-the-cognitive-work/). Frameworks come later.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Generation deserves a place when the experience depends on live input and beats a serious authored baseline. A small typed flow can block release when rights or creative approval fail.</p></div>

## Slop is a phase we can test

Pomplamoose has lived through several internet resets. Conte calls the pattern an ["infinite sine wave"](https://youtu.be/17_HcR95YBc?t=2390). The band grew through YouTube and iTunes. Growth flattened around YouTube's Cosmic Panda redesign, with the cause unclear at the time. Streaming weakened sales. COVID changed recording, and vertical video required new production systems.

Each wave broke a setup that had worked. Pomplamoose adapted by changing the work around the medium.

My take is that cheap imitation may dominate public feeds while a more valuable form develops somewhere smaller. That claim can fail. If the strongest generated culture in 2030 is still judged mainly by its resemblance to human-made poems, songs, or images, the 1895 frame has failed.

Criticism can defend consent and expose deception while engineers run smaller, creator-approved experiments. Exploration gives the medium a chance to find its own grammar.

The cut belonged to film. Our job is to find what belongs to AI.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Platform change repeatedly forced Pomplamoose to rebuild, and the band continued. AI's creative promise becomes credible when creator-approved generation produces value beyond resemblance.</p></div>
