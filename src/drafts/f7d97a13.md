# AI slop is the first draft of a medium

Maxim Gorky saw the future of cinema in 1896 and hated what he saw. After a Lumière programme, he described ["a world without sound, without colour"](https://publish.iupress.indiana.edu/read/91ddc4e0-5576-4f98-a3b6-ba8a97703eda/section/2fe1a387-c815-45d7-9609-2a47615dc9e8), where everything was "dipped in monotonous grey." He saw moving people and still felt that life had gone missing.

That sounds like an AI slop review written 130 years early.

Prompt an LLM for a poem today and the complaint is similar. It can reproduce the shape while leaving the reader cold.

In [his talk on creativity and AI](https://youtu.be/17_HcR95YBc?t=844), musician and Patreon co-founder Jack Conte calls slop "that lazy phase after the introduction of a new medium," when people reproduce things humans already make better. I think his framing earns a hearing because he stands on both sides of this change. Slop may be a phase, and history says phases send bills to real people.

## Count the musicians before celebrating the medium

The first bill for synchronized film went to the people playing beside the screen.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>New creative forms can emerge from technological shocks while displaced workers pay an immediate price. Historical patience has to include creator consent and compensation.</p></div>

When talkies reached cinemas in 1927, about [22,000 theatre musicians lost their jobs](https://www.afm.org/125-years-afm-history/), according to the American Federation of Musicians. The union called recorded accompaniment "canned music" and founded the Music Defense League in 1930.

The Novachord brought the fear into orchestras. AFM rules from 1939 said the electronic instrument [could not reduce the number of musicians](https://www.worldradiohistory.com/Archive-All-Music/International-Musician/30s/International-Musician-1939-05.pdf). A string quartet still needs four players and roughly the same time to perform, even as other industries produce more each hour. Baumol and Bowen later named that pressure the performing arts' [cost disease](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/insights/no-easy-remedy-symphony-orchestra-cost-disease).

Charlie Chaplin resisted synchronized dialogue for years, then placed the human voice at the centre of [*The Great Dictator*](https://www.charliechaplin.com/en/films/7-The-Great-Dictator) in 1940. The Beatles used tape loops and backwards sound on [*Revolver*](https://www.thebeatles.com/revolver) in 1966.

I think the parallel is useful only when both outcomes stay in frame. Tools may open new forms, yet a future masterpiece does nothing for a creator whose work was used without permission today.

## Cinema began as a bad seat at the theatre

Before filmmakers controlled time, they kept the camera still and asked actors to perform in front of it.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Early film copied the stage until editing gave it control over time and space. AI slop repeats the earlier mistake whenever a model imitates a mature human form.</p></div>

The Lumière brothers' [1895 train film](https://www.institut-lumiere.org/le-cinematographe-lumiere) used a static frame, while [many early filmmakers positioned the camera like an audience member](https://youtu.be/17_HcR95YBc?t=261). Actors entered, performed and left.

Georges Méliès approached the camera as a magician. He filmed an empty box, stopped the machine, placed a person inside and started it again. Joining the film made the person appear. By 1902, [*A Trip to the Moon*](https://www.acmi.net.au/works/603--a-trip-to-the-moon-le-voyage-dans-la-lune/) used these effects to travel through scenes no stage could hold.

The cut gave cinema control over sequence. It let filmmakers compress an hour, reverse an action or move between places. The camera finally left its seat.

Now return to an LLM writing a poem. The prompt tests AI against a form humans have refined for centuries. The same is true when an image model fakes a hand drawing or a camera shot. I think slop is useful as a diagnosis: it shows us where the machine is still impersonating an older medium.

## The strange choice still needs an owner

A new grammar still needs someone willing to look foolish in public.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Models generate from an existing cultural record, while artists put identity and reputation behind a choice. Human risk gives experimental work part of its meaning.</p></div>

Conte imagines a music model trained only on work available through 1959. I don't know whether it would eventually reach something like the Beatles or Prince. Cultural invention often begins outside the pattern with evidence behind it.

Conte points to [the hot-dog-finger universe](https://youtu.be/17_HcR95YBc?t=3029) in *Everything Everywhere All at Once*. The Daniels made an absurd idea central to the film and carried the risk of releasing it. A model can surface the strange option, while the filmmakers have to stand behind it.

He also credits [Karen X Cheng](https://youtu.be/17_HcR95YBc?t=3090) with the idea that difficulty and scarcity give art weight. *good kid, m.A.A.d city* matters to Conte because it carries Kendrick Lamar's life and pain. That connects with [what happens when production gets cheap](/posts/2026-04-11-when-ai-makes-building-cheap-human-reality-starts-to-matter-more/): identity and accountability become easier to see.

I think the artist's role may shift from producing every mark to choosing which risk deserves their name.

## Give the 1895 analogy an expiry date

A historical analogy becomes comforting mythology unless it can fail.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Remove AI and see whether the work keeps its central experience. If acclaimed AI work remains interchangeable with conventional poems or films by 2030, the 1895 analogy has failed.</p></div>

Take the generated poem from the opening. If a human-written poem can replace it without changing the experience, AI added speed or volume. Generation belongs to the medium only when removing it breaks something essential in the work, and the result can still be terrible.

By 2030, creators should be able to show work whose core experience collapses when the model is replaced with prewritten material. If the strongest examples remain conventional poems or films made faster, I will revise my view.

Conte describes Pomplamoose rebuilding as YouTube changed, streaming displaced iTunes, and vertical video altered production. He calls it an "infinite sine wave." Each broken method sends the artist back to learning.

I think criticism earns its place when it protects creators and rejects lazy imitation. Unchecked generation already settles into [visual elevator music](/posts/the-elevator-music-already-started-playing/), so exploration should search for work that loses its meaning when the machine leaves.

AI may never discover its equivalent of the cut. That possibility is a reason to experiment with more care, then judge what the medium actually becomes.
