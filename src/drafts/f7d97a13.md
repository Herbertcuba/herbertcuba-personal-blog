# AI is looking for its first cut

The train is still arriving.

In a Lumière film, one fixed camera watched it enter a station. In 2026, a model can generate the scene in seconds. Generation shrinks the production time, yet the creative instruction remains almost identical.

Jack Conte has skin on both sides of this argument. He makes music with Pomplamoose and co-founded Patreon, a company built around people paying creators. In [his talk about creativity and AI](https://youtu.be/17_HcR95YBc?t=844), he calls slop the lazy phase after a medium appears, when we use it to reproduce things humans already make better.

I think this gives us a better question than whether AI output looks convincing. Where is AI's first cut?

## What made film become cinema?

The machine arrived before artists understood its expressive language.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Early film copied the stage until the cut gave filmmakers control over sequence and time. Recorded music made a similar leap when the studio became part of composition.</p></div>

After a Lumière programme in 1896, Maxim Gorky described ["a world without sound, without colour"](https://publish.iupress.indiana.edu/read/91ddc4e0-5576-4f98-a3b6-ba8a97703eda/section/2fe1a387-c815-45d7-9609-2a47615dc9e8), where everything was "dipped in monotonous grey." The machine captured movement while leaving him cold.

Georges Méliès approached it as a magician. He filmed an empty box, stopped the camera, placed a person inside and started again. Joining the pieces made the person appear. That physical join was a cut.

By 1902, [*A Trip to the Moon*](https://www.acmi.net.au/works/603--a-trip-to-the-moon-le-voyage-dans-la-lune/) used cuts to build events that depended on film. The train could enter the station, jump to a passenger's face and return years later. A theatre audience could only watch each moment unfold in sequence.

Recorded music found its own move too. The Beatles used tape loops and backwards sound on 1966's [*Revolver*](https://www.thebeatles.com/revolver). The recording studio had become an instrument.

I think slop begins when a new tool has speed but still borrows its imagination.

## Who pays while the language develops?

The people losing work cannot wait for the new medium to produce a masterpiece.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Creative breakthroughs never cancel the immediate cost of displacement. Exploration needs consent and compensation from the beginning.</p></div>

According to the [American Federation of Musicians](https://www.afm.org/125-years-afm-history/), about 22,000 theatre musicians lost their jobs when talkies arrived in 1927. The union called recorded accompaniment "canned music" and formed the Music Defense League in 1930.

The anxiety moved into the orchestra. AFM's 1939 rules covered the electronic Novachord and Hammond organ. Adding either could not reduce the ensemble. Baumol and Bowen later named the underlying pressure [cost disease](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/insights/no-easy-remedy-symphony-orchestra-cost-disease): a string quartet still needs four people and the same amount of time, even when other industries become faster.

Charlie Chaplin resisted synchronised dialogue for years, then made the human voice central to 1940's [*The Great Dictator*](https://www.charliechaplin.com/en/films/7-The-Great-Dictator). His artistic breakthrough arrived beside a labour loss that remained unpaid.

I think history becomes dishonest when we use it as a sedative. The possibility of great AI art leaves today's questions about consent and payment fully open.

## What does the model put at risk?

A model can make the weird thing, while a person has to release it and risk being laughed at.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Models can produce surprise, but ridicule and responsibility still land on people. The creator's connection to the work remains part of what an audience values.</p></div>

Conte asks us to imagine a music model trained only on recordings available through 1959. Would it reach the Beatles, Prince or Nirvana without encountering them first? I don't know.

I think a model can surprise us. It still has no career to embarrass and no audience it can disappoint. Those consequences reach the person who publishes the work.

Conte attributes to Karen X Cheng the idea that difficulty gives art scarcity. He values *good kid, m.A.A.d city* through Kendrick Lamar's experience and pain, rather than the waveform alone. The identity behind the work becomes easier to see [when production gets cheap](/posts/2026-04-11-when-ai-makes-building-cheap-human-reality-starts-to-matter-more/).

A model can invent a purple train with a choir in every carriage. The artist still decides why anyone should care, then signs their name to that decision.

## Stop asking for a better train

A sharper synthetic train proves rendering speed and leaves the creative question untouched.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>AI needs experiments whose central experience depends on generation. Faster imitation will keep producing slop, regardless of how polished it becomes.</p></div>

The science-fiction film *Arrival* offers a useful image. Linguist Louise Banks faces a language written in circular symbols. As she learns its form, her experience of time changes. Treating those symbols as unusual handwriting would miss the capability carried by their structure.

An AI-native train story might rebuild later scenes from choices an audience made earlier. Branching stories already exist, so generation would need to go further. The world would have to respond consistently in ways the maker never pre-authored, while still carrying the maker's judgment. It might be terrible. At least the experiment would test the medium rather than its ability to counterfeit a camera.

Conte calls the repeated disruption in Pomplamoose's career an "infinite sine wave." Platforms changed and old production methods broke. The band kept learning.

AI may never find a creative language of its own. Some media never do. I think creators should search anyway, while refusing the consent failures and [elevator music](/posts/the-elevator-music-already-started-playing/) already filling the feed.

More polish keeps the synthetic train in 1895. The medium moves forward when generation takes it somewhere a camera cannot.
