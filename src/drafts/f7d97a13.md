In 1895, the Lumière brothers [projected workers leaving a factory](https://www.institut-lumiere.org/le-cinematographe-lumiere). In 2026, we ask a large language model for a lost Beatles song. Both moves expose the same problem: a new medium is being graded on how well it copies an old one.

A large language model, or LLM, learns patterns from training data and predicts the next likely piece. Familiar forms make imitation easy, so the result can sound finished while the idea stays borrowed.

Musician and [Patreon co-founder Jack Conte](https://www.patreon.com/about) describes this phase in his SXSW talk, [*The future of creativity on the internet in a world with AI*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=17_HcR95YBc), published on 16 July 2026. He calls slop the lazy period after a medium arrives, when people use it to reproduce work that humans already make better.

I think slop is useful evidence. It tells us the assignment is older than the machine.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>LLMs begin with imitation because familiar forms give them a clear target. Slop reveals an old creative assignment wrapped around a new machine.</p></div>

## A new medium begins by copying

Early film often looked like theater with a camera placed in the audience.

In 1896, Maxim Gorky watched a Lumière program. [Conte quotes him](https://youtu.be/17_HcR95YBc?t=284) describing a world without sound or color, with everything "dipped in monotonous gray." Theater still supplied the shape.

Georges Méliès pushed past it. His 1902 [*A Trip to the Moon*](https://www.acmi.net.au/works/603--a-trip-to-the-moon-le-voyage-dans-la-lune/) used cuts and camera tricks to make bodies vanish and locations change instantly.

The cut gave film its own grammar. In *2001: A Space Odyssey*, Stanley Kubrick moves from a bone in the air to an object in orbit. Cinema crosses millions of years by joining two images a stage could never hold together.

I think generative AI is close to that fixed-camera moment. Asking for a lost Beatles song is like filming a play from row ten. Better fidelity improves the copy while the creative form stays where it was.

## The invention arrives with a bill

Creative progress can share a calendar with job loss.

The American Federation of Musicians called recorded accompaniment ["canned music"](https://www.afm.org/125-years-afm-history/). Its history estimates that talkies cost about 22,000 theater musicians their jobs. The union began a recording strike in 1942.

The Hammond Novachord raised a similar fear in 1939. Union [rules published that May](https://www.worldradiohistory.com/Archive-All-Music/International-Musician/30s/International-Musician-1939-05.pdf) protected orchestra headcount when the instrument was added and required local consent for solo use in venues that had employed musicians.

[Baumol and Bowen's cost disease](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/insights/no-easy-remedy-symphony-orchestra-cost-disease) explains the pressure. A quartet still needs four players even as productivity rises elsewhere.

Artists found new uses too. Charlie Chaplin resisted synchronized dialogue for years, then used it for the final humanist speech in the 1940 [*The Great Dictator*](https://www.bfi.org.uk/film/009e372a-646d-5b6e-a6b0-59f96722a1fd/the-great-dictator). The Beatles released [*Revolver*](https://www.thebeatles.com/revolver) in 1966 with tape loops and reversed sounds. The studio became part of composition.

I think any anthem about invention needs this bill attached. A medium can open creative space while taking income from working artists. Exploration needs source rights, clear attribution and an accountable human author.

## Slop is a poor assignment

Our lost Beatles song gives the model a bad brief.

Conte proposes a [thought experiment](https://youtu.be/17_HcR95YBc?t=2828). Train a music model only on recordings made before 1959, then ask it to discover the Beatles, Prince, Nirvana, Biggie, Beyoncé or Jacob Collier. He presents this as a philosophical test rather than a published benchmark. Can a system trained on existing patterns make a cultural break before anyone has supplied an example of that break?

Artists carry a pressure that model outputs avoid. Conte quotes *Sing Street*: ["Rock and roll is a risk. You risk being ridiculed."](https://youtu.be/17_HcR95YBc?t=3006) He also repeats a line whose original speaker's name is unclear in the available captions: "Art is scarcity. Art is not what's easy to make, it's what's hard."

A model can produce unusual notes without risking a reputation. A musician signs the work with a name and a life. Conte makes that point through [*good kid, m.A.A.d city*](https://youtu.be/17_HcR95YBc?t=3287): the record reaches us with Kendrick Lamar's story attached.

I think this is why polished imitation deepens [the pull toward familiar output](/posts/the-elevator-music-already-started-playing/). The sound improves while risk and authorship remain outside the model.

Keep the song project, then change its job. The listener hears sixteen bars and selects a short motif. The system writes the next passage in response. A human composer owns one hard rule: every version must contain a full measure of silence.

## Give the song a job only generation can do

The adaptive song can stay small enough for a junior engineer to inspect.

Start with the replacement test in figure 03. Compare a static track with a written branch library. Generation earns its place when new motifs exhaust those branches and the reply remains central to the experience.

The state can fit on one screen:

```python
from typing import TypedDict

class SongState(TypedDict):
    consent: bool
    chosen_motif: str
    source_license: str
    required_silent_beats: int
    silent_beats: int
    composer_approved: bool
```

`SongState` is the Python dictionary carrying one run. [`TypedDict`](https://docs.python.org/3/library/typing.html#typing.TypedDict) tells a type checker which keys to expect, while ordinary code handles runtime checks.

[LangGraph](https://docs.langchain.com/oss/python/langgraph/graph-api) is a Python library for workflows that branch or loop. Its `StateGraph` moves shared state through named steps. Here those steps collect consent, compose, count silent beats and request approval. A failed check follows a fixed route back to composition.

[ReAct](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629) lets a model choose an action, read the result and choose again. The song needs it only if the model selects tools such as a chord analyzer or tempo matcher. Code keeps the deterministic silence rule.

Run the pilot for two weeks, ten listener sessions and a €500 cap. The composer owns release. The engineer records the prompt, model version, cost and latency. Sources must be licensed, vendor retention disabled, and listener motifs deleted after each session. Stop if fixed branches create the same experience or the provider fails the rights boundary.

I think junior engineers can learn from this build because every state change is visible. The model has one bounded job, code holds the measurable rule, and the composer decides what reaches a listener.

That boundary begins in [the spec before delegation](/posts/the-spec-is-the-cognitive-work/).

ReAct belongs only where the model chooses a tool from an observation. Code enforces the measurable rule, and the composer controls release.</p></div>

## Keep the bet falsifiable

Conte describes Pomplamoose's career as an ["infinite sine wave"](https://youtu.be/17_HcR95YBc?t=2390). YouTube changed discovery, streaming weakened the band's iTunes model, COVID changed recording, and vertical video required another setup. Pomplamoose rebuilt each time.

I don't know whether generative AI will find a native creative grammar. Some media remain copies and disappear. By 2030, we should be able to name at least one serious work whose central experience collapses when a template or fixed branch replaces the model. Its human author should remain easy to name. If we cannot, the 1895 comparison has failed.

Criticism can identify harm and bad incentives. I think it becomes more useful when it gives builders a test. For the adaptive song, replace the model and listen for what the work loses.

Film found its grammar when filmmakers stopped asking the camera to behave like a seat in the theater. Generative AI needs the same kind of disobedience.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The 1895 comparison is a testable bet. By 2030, a serious AI work should fail the replacement test while keeping its human author visible. Until then, each prototype should prove what generation makes possible that a fixed system cannot.</p></div>
