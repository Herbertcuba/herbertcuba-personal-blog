A $200 Claude subscription just generated $7,470 worth of API calls. Do the math on a million subscribers, and Anthropic's land grab starts to look like a controlled fire.

The numbers come from Om Patel, a Max subscriber who ran claude-cost on his own July 2026 usage. One month, one user, 6.16 billion tokens across 36,881 responses. At API list prices that month costs $7,470.35. He paid $200. The subsidy is 37x at list price.

I tried to poke holes in it. The arithmetic holds.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>A power user on a $200 Max plan consumed $7,470 of list-price API value in one month. The subscription is subsidized 37 times over, and the gap is the story.</p></div>

## What $7,470 of usage looks like

The bill breaks down in a way that surprised me.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Almost 90 percent of the cost is reading, not writing. Cache reads and cache writes dominate the bill, and three frontier models split it nearly evenly.</p></div>

Cache reads came to $3,596, nearly half the total. Cache writes added $2,842. The actual output tokens, the words the models generated, cost $1,016. Only 13.6 percent.

A quick explainer, because the terms matter here. When a CLI coding tool talks to Claude, it resends a huge amount of context with every request: your files, your history, its instructions. Anthropic lets the tool store that prefix in a cache so repeat reads cost less than fresh processing. Patel's estimate is that CLI tools burn somewhere between 500 and 1,000 input tokens for every output token, while a custom toolchain runs closer to 1:1.5. Treat those ratios as his field observation, not a published benchmark. The shape of his bill backs the direction either way.

Per model: Claude Fable 5 ate $2,794, Opus 4.8 took $2,330, Opus 5 another $2,141. Three frontier models, split almost evenly, all serving one subscriber.

I think the ratio is the part that deserves a second look. List price is a retail number. Anthropic's actual compute cost sits below it, and I don't know by how much. Nobody outside the company does. Public estimates from firms like Epoch AI and reporting from The Information put gross margins for frontier labs in the 30 to 40 percent range, which means the true subsidy is smaller than 37x. Smaller, and still enormous.

## Three months of one power user

Extend the same example and the discount gets personal.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Three months of Patel-level usage costs $600 on the plan and would cost about $22,410 at API list price. A $21,810 discount per power user, per quarter.</p></div>

Three months at Max pricing: $600 paid. Three months at that consumption rate: roughly $22,410 in list-price API value. Call it $21,810 in discount, per power user, per quarter.

Patel's framing of why this works is a mobile plan analogy. Most subscribers never hit their limits. They pay for the ones who do. A gym membership with tokens. The light users fund the heavy ones, and the heavy ones generate the usage stories that sell the plan to the next light users. Cheap consumer AI is customer acquisition.

My take: the whole industry is in this phase. Investor money goes in, locked-in users come out, and profitability is a promise dated later. Anthropic's revenue run rate is reported in the tens of billions of dollars this year, raised on roughly $95 billion of funding. A few billion in consumer subsidy is a marketing line item against that.

## A million subscribers, six months

Here is the ceiling case, and then the honest one.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>If a million subscribers all consumed like Patel, Anthropic would book $1.2 billion against $44.8 billion of list-price value over six months. They will not all consume like Patel, and that is precisely the bet.</p></div>

One million Max subscribers over six months: $1.2 billion in revenue against $44.8 billion in list-price value. A $43.6 billion gap.

That number is fiction, because most users are light. Run a version closer to reality: if 5 percent of a million subscribers burn at Patel's rate, the subsidy lands around $2.2 billion over six months. Real money, and clearly affordable inside a $95 billion war chest.

This is where the sci-fi aside earns its place. In *Alien*, the Nostromo's crew finds out what they are to the company in two words: crew expendable. Weyland-Yutani did the math and decided the specimen was the asset and the crew was the acquisition cost. I think the consumer AI market runs on the same ledger. The subscribers burning at a loss are the acquisition cost. The lock-in is the specimen.

I think the honest read is this: Anthropic is not losing control of the burn. A fire you can size, budget, and point investors at is a strategy.

Cheap AI was never cheap. Somebody else is paying, and the invoice is due later.
