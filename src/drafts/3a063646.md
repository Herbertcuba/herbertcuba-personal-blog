On August 1, 2026, engineer Om Patel [shared](https://x.com/om_patel5/status/2083401185172541449) a striking July total. His $200 Claude Max subscription had carried 6.16 billion tokens through [Claude Code](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/costs), Anthropic’s coding agent. Its dashboard priced the same traffic at $7,470.35 under [standard API rates](https://docs.anthropic.com/en/docs/about-claude/pricing): 37.4 times the fee.

This was neither Patel’s bill nor Anthropic’s compute cost. It was a retail counterfactual: what identical traffic would cost if every token were metered. The gap matters because subscriptions hide both price and whether users are learning to work efficiently.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Flat fees make heavy use affordable while hiding the habits behind future metered bills.</p></div>

## The discount hides the traffic

Patel’s dashboard assigned 86.2% of its API-equivalent value to cache reads and writes. Prompt caching discounts a stable repeated prefix; it does not erase the input. Each turn can resend instructions, history, tools, files, and old results.

[Nate B. Jones](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Y8vAQ1FgNbM), who sells a context-management framework, measured one day in his workspace: 3.59 billion of 3.77 billion tokens across 143 coding threads were reused input, almost 96%. This is one engineer’s result, not an industry rate. In 2025, the team behind [Manus](https://manus.im/blog/Context-Engineering-for-AI-Agents-Lessons-from-Building-Manus), a production AI agent, reported a roughly 100:1 input-to-output ratio. Different systems expose the same pattern: generation is often the small part.

Cost is only one consequence. The peer-reviewed 2024 [Lost in the Middle](https://aclanthology.org/2024.tacl-1.9/) study found weaker retrieval when relevant information appeared midway through a long input. Longer inputs can therefore be discounted and still make the model less reliable.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Cached context costs less, not nothing, and too much of it can weaken retrieval.</p></div>

## Context hygiene is a production skill

Context hygiene means choosing what the next turn needs. Start a new thread when the task changes. Carry forward a compact artifact with the goal, constraints, decisions, evidence, and current state. Load tools only when needed. Keep stable instructions stable for caching; remove obsolete results and conversational debris.

[Auto-compaction](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/costs) helps near a limit, but the harness chooses when to summarize and what to discard. Engineers should decide while the evidence remains visible.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Good context is selected working material, not a transcript of the work.</p></div>

## Turn hidden traffic into an engineering test

Choose a recurring task with an objective acceptance check. Keep the model, task, tests, reviewer, and settings fixed. Run repeated matched trials with accumulated context, then with curated context, alternating the order. Record fresh input, cached input, output, latency, total cost, and whether the result passes.

Then calculate cost per accepted result: total trial cost divided by accepted outputs. A cheaper run that needs another attempt is not cheaper; it shifted inference savings into delay and review. The winning workflow is the one with the lowest cost per accepted result, not the fewest tokens.

Curated does not mean minimal. Synthesis and frontier reasoning may require evidence; the acceptance test decides whether those tokens earn a place.

Flat pricing buys time to learn, and practiced context discipline becomes leverage if metering tightens.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Learn now; optimize for accepted outcomes.</p></div>
