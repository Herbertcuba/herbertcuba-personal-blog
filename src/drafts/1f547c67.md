The architecture of an AI-first company begins with two linked claims. First, every business role acquires a technical dimension. Second, the company can become flatter when AI agents absorb meaningful execution and coordination. An AI agent is software that uses a generative model to interpret a goal and work through approved tools with some autonomy; as that work shifts, more ownership can move to the people closest to it.

Those claims do not carry the same evidentiary weight. In a recorded interview, Arvind KC, then Chief People Officer at OpenAI, the AI research and product company behind ChatGPT, said that “every role as we go into the future is fundamentally a technical role.” He immediately placed that technical dimension inside a person’s business domain.

Technical judgment does not mean that everyone must code. It means that people in sales, operations, finance, marketing, and support can judge what AI can handle reliably, what evidence it needs, where it is likely to fail, and when a human decision remains necessary. KC’s observation supports that first claim. The flatter-company proposition is my operating hypothesis, and it has to be proven inside each organization.

I think this distinction matters because an org chart can be changed in an afternoon while coordination work is much harder to remove. A responsible CIO starts with the work, makes authority explicit, and deals with the people consequence before deciding that a management layer has become unnecessary.

<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Technical judgment is becoming part of every business role. A flatter company is a possible consequence of agents removing coordination, not an automatic consequence of adopting them.</p>
</div>

## Redesign the workflow around domain judgment

An AI-first operating model redesigns a complete business workflow around what people and machines can each do well. Installing an agent beside an unchanged process is a much smaller move.

Consider a customer-refund workflow. An agent might read the request, retrieve the order, classify the reason, and draft a response. Yet employees may still copy the result into another system, ask a manager for approval, trigger the payment, update the customer record, and repair mistakes. One task has accelerated, while the surrounding coordination remains intact.

Benjamin Simkin, author of the 2026 book [*The AI First Company*](https://www.amazon.co.uk/dp/B0H45WZRX5), calls adding AI without redesigning the process the “Retrofit Trap.” Escaping it requires domain judgment: the customer-service owner defines a valid outcome and the exception policy; finance defines payment authority; technology and risk define the controls. The people who understand the work can then own more improvement at the edge without receiving unlimited authority.

That is the practical meaning of bottom-up ownership. Ideas and routine decisions move closer to the domain, while shared boundaries remain visible across the company.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>An AI-first company changes the whole path from request to verified outcome. Faster isolated tasks leave the old approvals, routing, and repair work in place.</p>
</div>

## Prove that coordination has actually disappeared

A management layer is a bundle of work. Managers gather information, resolve exceptions, allocate resources, approve commitments, translate policy, and coach people. Agents may absorb status collection, standard routing, follow-up, and some policy-based recommendations. They do not erase accountability for ambiguous trade-offs, people decisions, or changes to policy.

This makes the unit of evidence important. A case is one complete piece of business work, from its trigger to an auditable outcome. A correctly closed case has reached the intended result, complied with the relevant controls, and avoided hidden human repair. In the refund example, a polished reply is not closure if the payment is duplicated, the ledger is wrong, or an employee quietly fixes the record later.

Measure representative case types across the full path. The record should include elapsed time, human routing touches, repair effort, outcome quality, control failures, total cost, and the judgment that still requires a person. It should also preserve failed and escalated cases, because a pilot built only from easy work cannot support an organizational decision.

The evidence available for this article contains no Swedish or Norwegian production benchmark for safely removing management layers. Acceptance conditions must therefore be declared locally before the trial, based on the workflow’s risk and case mix. A flatter structure becomes defensible only when coordination demand falls in sustained operation and every remaining responsibility has somewhere credible to go.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Evaluate correctly closed cases across the complete workflow, including hidden repair and residual judgment. Local evidence must justify removing a layer because no transferable Nordic threshold does it for you.</p>
</div>

## Put authority outside model reasoning

The replacement for hierarchy is visible authority, not free-form model judgment. Domain-mapped ownership gives each workflow a named business owner who is accountable for its outcome. Decision rights state who may approve which action, for which case, within what limit, and who owns an exception.

Within that structure, the agent may interpret evidence and propose or initiate an allowed step. Ordinary software should enforce identity, permissions, current case state, policy limits, and the permanent record. The authoritative state is the company’s official current record of the case. Text inside an email or attachment can inform the agent, but it cannot expand the agent’s authority.

For a refund, the control path should identify the agent, the person or function on whose behalf it acts, the permitted account and action, the amount limit, the expiry of that permission, and what revocation does to work already in progress. A stale case version, missing evidence, or over-limit amount should leave the official record unchanged and route the case to a named human.

Retries need equal care. [RFC 9110](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9110.html#name-idempotent-methods), a 2022 web standard from the Internet Engineering Task Force, the standards body for core internet protocols, defines an idempotent request as one where repeated identical requests have the same intended effect as one request. A unique action key can help prevent a refund from being submitted twice. It cannot remove uncertainty if an external payment succeeds and the internal state update then fails, so the workflow also needs reconciliation, a check between the payment provider and the internal record, before retrying. A named human must own any corrective action.

Stop authority is the named power to pause the workflow when its evidence, behavior, or controls are no longer acceptable. The design should say who can stop it, how in-flight actions are handled, which actions can be reversed, and how irreversible effects are corrected. Every proposal, approval, execution, override, stop, and restart should leave an audit trail that a reviewer can follow.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Agents may interpret and propose within boundaries. Named owners and ordinary software must enforce permissions, state, exceptions, retries, audit, and the power to stop.</p>
</div>

## Rebuild the career system at the same time

The management pyramid is also a career system. It distributes pay, status, coaching, access to decisions, and a familiar route for progression. Removing coordination-heavy roles without replacing those functions narrows ambition for specialists and pushes development work onto whoever remains.

Leaders should first separate the tasks inside each affected role. Routine routing may disappear; domain coaching, difficult judgment, control ownership, and capability building may become more valuable. The new design then needs credible specialist progression, pay bands, learning time, coaching responsibilities, and routes into leadership. Where work genuinely declines, funded reskilling, redeployment choices, and honest exit support belong in the operating case.

Participation must begin while the design can still change. In Sweden, Section 11 of the [Co-Determination Act, MBL (1976:580)](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-1976580-om-medbestammande-i-arbetslivet_sfs-1976-580/) requires an employer bound by a collective agreement to negotiate with the relevant employee organization before deciding an important change to its operations or to employees’ working conditions. The executive sponsor and HR should begin that process before roles are fixed, with workflow scope, acceptance conditions, role effects, training, redeployment, and timing open to influence. Local legal and collective-agreement duties still need local review.

This transition will create friction because authority, identity, and opportunity are moving together. Treating it as a funded design stream gives the organization a better chance of retaining judgment while routine coordination falls away.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Changing the pyramid also changes careers, pay, coaching, and employee voice. Participation and a funded transition belong inside the operating decision, while meaningful choices remain open.</p>
</div>

## Make the organizational decision auditable

A CIO should take a decision contract to the executive team or board, rather than asking for faith in an “AI-first” destination. The contract below is a fillable trial record, not an established benchmark. Evidence provenance means recording where each measure came from and who verified it.

| Decision field | Complete before the trial |
| --- | --- |
| Workflow and boundary | [Trigger, end state, included systems, excluded work] |
| Correctly closed case | [Outcome, required evidence, controls, allowed exceptions] |
| Baseline and case mix | [Volume, quality, time, repair effort, cost, source and date] |
| Trial limits | [Duration, volume, spend, data, autonomy, capacity] |
| Named authority | [Domain owner, system owner, risk owner, exception owner, stop owner] |
| Evidence provenance | [Logs, review sample, cost records, human-time capture, sign-off owner] |
| Acceptance conditions | [Conditions for stop, extension, redesign, or structural change] |
| Dependencies and exit | [Supplier access, log export, data location, continuity, termination support] |
| Workforce commitments | [Participation forum, paid learning, redeployment, pay and progression decisions] |
| Review and reversal | [Decision date, forum, rollback path, reconciliation and corrective-action owner] |

At the review, record which coordination work disappeared, which human judgment remains, and where every responsibility moved. A proposal to remove a layer should also show that the remaining managers and domain owners are not inheriting invisible repair work. If the evidence fails, redesign or stop the workflow. If permissions or workforce commitments remain unresolved, structural change is premature even when the agent performs well.

An AI-first company can become materially flatter, with more ownership at its edges. The defensible sequence is to distribute technical judgment, prove complete-workflow performance, expose authority, fund the transition, and then decide whether a layer still has necessary work to do.

<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Change the org chart only through a signed record that joins workflow evidence, decision rights, control limits, supplier dependencies, workforce commitments, and a reversal path.</p>
</div>
