An AI-first company rests on two linked propositions: every business role gains a technical dimension, and organizations can become flatter and more bottom-up as agents absorb routine execution and coordination.

In a [June 2026 interview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8atlQX8HTwQ&t=342s), [Arvind KC](https://openai.com/index/arvind-kc-chief-people-officer/), OpenAI’s Chief People Officer, said future work would be “fundamentally a technical role” applied to a particular domain—a bounded area of business responsibility. OpenAI is an AI research and product company operating at the frontier of AI development. Its structure is not evidence that the same model will work in a Nordic bank, manufacturer, or public agency. KC’s useful point is narrower: people closest to a business decision must understand what AI can do, where it fails, and when human judgment must take over.

The organizational proposition follows. An AI agent is software built around a model that can pursue a defined goal through approved tools and steps. If agents can carry routine work across a complete process, ownership can move closer to the domain and some coordination layers may become unnecessary. That possibility has to be earned. Faster output says little about hidden repair, genuine authority, or what happens to people whose roles were built around coordination.

A responsible CIO changes the sequence: distribute technical judgment first, prove complete workflows second, make authority visible, and redesign careers before touching the org chart.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Distributed technical judgment can support a flatter company, but structural change must follow workflow evidence, visible authority, and a credible workforce transition.</p></div>

## Technical judgment is a business discipline

Technical judgment is the ability to decide what AI may do inside a business area, what evidence it needs, which failure patterns matter, and when a person must intervene. It is business literacy with consequences. It does not require every employee to write software.

Consider a business-customer renewal. Sales knows which customer facts and commercial terms an agent may extract or propose. Finance owns margin limits and financial exposure. Legal retains authority over unfamiliar commitments. Operations defines when the renewal is complete and which official records must change. The same pattern applies elsewhere: marketing owns source and brand evidence, support owns cases involving vulnerability or relationship consequences, and operations owns failure and recovery paths.

This judgment matters because AI capability is uneven. A [2026 experiment published in *Organization Science*](https://doi.org/10.1287/orsc.2025.21838), a peer-reviewed management journal, involved 758 consultants and GPT-4, an OpenAI language model. On the selected task outside the model’s tested capability frontier—the boundary between work it handled well and work it did not—AI users were 19 percent less likely to reach the correct answer.

[Article 4 of the EU AI Act](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:02024R1689-20260727), the European Union’s 2024 risk-based AI regulation, also requires providers and deployers to support AI literacy with people’s knowledge, training, and use context in mind. AI literacy means enough context-specific understanding to use and oversee a system responsibly. For a CIO, that makes literacy part of role design, not a generic training badge.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Technical judgment belongs with domain experts who can recognize useful work, weak evidence, capability boundaries, and the moment a person must take over.</p></div>

## The workflow is where the organization hides

In his 2026 business book [*The AI First Company: Engineering Your Business to Thrive in the AI Economy*](https://www.amazon.co.uk/dp/B0H45WZRX5), author and business coach Benjamin Simkin calls one common design failure the Retrofit Trap: AI accelerates an isolated activity while the surrounding manual process survives. The name is useful. The evidence still has to come from the workflow.

An agent may draft a renewal offer quickly while people still collect usage data, route a margin exception, wait for legal approval, correct an unsuitable clause, release the offer, update customer and finance records, and repair a disputed outcome. The generated document is one activity. The operating model is the entire dependency chain.

Coordination researchers [Thomas W. Malone and Kevin Crowston](https://doi.org/10.1145/174666.174668) defined coordination in 1994 as “managing dependencies between activities.” Organizational-design scholar [Jay R. Galbraith](https://doi.org/10.1287/inte.4.3.28) explained in 1974 that established rules can handle routine cases while hierarchy receives exceptions the rules cannot resolve. Agents may absorb information assembly, status collection, routing, and known-exception handling. Managers still resolve competing goals, allocate resources, coach people, interpret weak signals, and remain accountable for difficult decisions.

A complete workflow runs from its initial trigger through the business outcome, record updates, exceptions, and cleanup. For structural decisions, define a correctly closed case as one that meets declared quality and policy conditions, updates the official record, counts every human intervention and repair, and leaves no hidden work behind. This is a proposed management test, not an established industry benchmark.

Measure four categories: workflow effort, including elapsed time, routing, approvals, and repair; outcome quality, including correct closure and reopened cases; control performance, including unauthorized, stale, duplicate, privacy, and audit failures; and total cost, including models, integration, monitoring, review, exceptions, and supplier fees. Record the judgment still left to people. Compare the agent-supported process with both today’s process and the strongest conventional alternative, such as workflow software that follows predefined rules and states.

No validated Swedish or Norwegian production case in the available evidence supplies a numerical threshold for safely removing a management layer. The company must set its own proof conditions before the trial begins. If coordination returns as frontline cleanup, informal supervision, risk work, or supplier tickets, it has moved rather than disappeared.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Judge agents by correctly closed workflows and net coordination removed, not by the speed or fluency of one output.</p></div>

## Authority must be designed, not assumed

Flattening reallocates decisions. In 1997, economists [Philippe Aghion and Jean Tirole](https://doi.org/10.1086/262063) distinguished formal authority, the assigned right to decide, from real authority, the practical ability to determine what happens. A renewal team that owns the outcome on paper but cannot inspect the rules, change a supplier, or stop a release lacks real authority.

A decision right is explicit authority to propose, approve, reject, override, stop, or restart an action. Domain-mapped ownership assigns those decisions and outcomes to people with relevant business expertise instead of scattering them across committees. From the center, this can look untidy. The boundary must therefore become easier to inspect as ownership moves outward.

A governed workflow separates five responsibilities:

1. Domain owners define the outcome, the case types, the evidence required, and the limits.
2. The agent interprets unstructured material and proposes the next action.
3. Company-controlled software, using predefined logic, checks identity, permission, policy limits, and the case’s authoritative state—the latest approved status and version—before anything consequential happens.
4. A named human owns exceptions, overrides, stop, restart, and accountability.
5. The official business record captures the action, actor, time, outcome, and recovery path.

Suppose a renewal-release request is sent and times out before confirmation. The agent should not guess and try again. Give the intended release a stable action identifier, meaning one unique label for that business action, place the case in a pending state, and reconcile whether the external commitment occurred. [RFC 9110](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9110.html#section-9.2.2), a 2022 Internet standard for web requests, defines idempotency as repeated identical requests having the same intended effect as one request. At business level, the same action identity must not produce a second commitment. A rollback restores internal software, configuration, or workflow state to an earlier safe version. An external commitment that cannot simply be erased needs a compensating action: a new step that corrects its real-world effect.

An audit trail is a reviewable record of the actor, action, time, relevant state, and outcome. Every consequential action needs an audit trail, and a named human needs stop authority: both the right and the practical mechanism to halt new actions.

Real authority also requires supplier exit—the tested ability to replace a provider or operate manually while retaining company control of state, policy, records, and recovery. Legal boundaries depend on the use. The EU AI Act’s override and stop provisions apply where a system meets the legal definition of high-risk AI, not automatically to every business agent. The [General Data Protection Regulation](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32016R0679), applicable since 2018 and commonly called the GDPR, governs personal-data processing and certain solely automated decisions with legal or similarly significant effects. An internal risk tier, a company category for potential harm, helps organize controls but cannot replace legal classification, privacy review, labor duties, or collective agreements.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Let agents interpret and propose, while company-controlled software enforces permission and state and named people retain practical authority to override, stop, recover, and replace suppliers.</p></div>

## Redesign careers before reporting lines

The old hierarchy carried more than approvals. It also carried pay progression, status, coaching, access to senior decisions, and opportunities to practice leadership. Removing coordination work without replacing that career machinery can leave the edge operationally empowered and the people inside it with nowhere to grow.

The [International Labour Organization](https://doi.org/10.54394/HETP0387), the United Nations agency for labor standards and research, concluded in its 2025 generative-AI exposure analysis that task exposure does not imply an occupation’s disappearance. Transformation is more likely because most occupations retain work requiring human input. That directs leaders toward activity-level redesign: identify which routing work shrinks, which judgment remains, and which new responsibilities appear in assurance, coaching, customer trust, and exception ownership.

Build the replacement before changing titles. Define transparent redeployment and selection rules. Fund learning time during working hours. Create expert levels with credible pay and status for people who do not manage headcount. Assign coaching responsibility explicitly. Preserve opportunities to practice larger decisions rather than reserving them for a shrinking executive group.

Social dialogue means structured discussion among employers, employees, and their representatives. It should begin before a trial changes how work is performed and return before evidence is used to change roles, pay, or reporting lines. In Sweden, the [Co-Determination in the Workplace Act of 1976](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-1976580-om-medbestammande-i-arbetslivet_sfs-1976-580/) can require negotiation before qualifying significant changes; current law, collective agreements, and local circumstances determine the precise duty. A Norwegian operation requires its own legal and collective-agreement review. Participation becomes meaningful when representatives can inspect the evidence and pause the organizational decision if agreed conditions have not been met.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>A flatter company needs new paths for pay, expertise, coaching, learning, ambition, participation, and redeployment before the old ladder is removed.</p></div>

## Give the board a decision it can reject

Do not bundle an agent trial with approval of a future org chart. Put a one-page workflow decision record before the executive team or board instead:

- **Scope and ownership.** Name the domain owner, the permanent service owner accountable after the trial, the control owner who can block release, an evidence owner independent enough to reject a favorable interpretation, and the executive sponsor.
- **Comparison and evidence.** Test the current process, the strongest conventional rules-based design, and the agent-supported workflow against the same predeclared case types and dates. Include ordinary, exception, stale-data, duplicate, unauthorized, disputed, and supplier-failure cases.
- **Authority and recovery.** State what the agent may propose, what software must enforce, who may approve, stop, and restart, how duplicates and uncertain outcomes are reconciled, and who owns rollback, compensating action, and supplier exit.
- **People commitments.** Record the activities expected to change, funded learning, redeployment and selection rules, career alternatives, coaching ownership, and employee-participation checkpoints.
- **Decision options.** Allow the workflow to expand, narrow, enter normal operations, or stop. Treat organizational redesign as a separate later decision.

The trial should stop on conditions declared in advance: an unauthorized or stale release, unresolved duplicate behavior, a missing audit record, uncontrolled personal data, supplier failure without fallback, or incomplete employee participation. The limits and evidence period are company-set choices, not universal benchmarks. Unfavorable cases stay in the record.

Only then should leadership ask whether a coordination layer remains justified. Which responsibilities disappeared? Where does residual judgment live? Who can halt the system? Can the organization recover without the supplier? What is the full operating cost? What happens to the people whose work changes? If those answers are weak, keep the chart and repair the operating model.

An AI-first company is not defined by how many agents it deploys. It is defined by where judgment sits, how authority is enforced, and whether coordination actually leaves the system. Let the workflow earn the flatter architecture. Make leadership earn the right to change people’s work.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Authorize a reversible workflow trial with named owners, falsifiers, controls, and workforce commitments; change the org chart only when correctly closed cases prove that coordination has genuinely left the system.</p></div>
