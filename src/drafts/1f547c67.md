Two linked claims define the architecture of an AI-first company. Every business role acquires a technical dimension. And when AI agents absorb a meaningful share of execution and coordination, ownership can move closer to the work and the organization can become flatter.

[Arvind KC](https://openai.com/index/arvind-kc-chief-people-officer/), chief people officer at OpenAI, the AI research and deployment company, gave the first claim useful precision in a May 2026 interview. He [said](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8atlQX8HTwQ&t=343s) that “every role as we go into the future is fundamentally a technical role,” then explained that the technical role is applied to a particular domain. He also described innovation moving from the bottom up, with ideas emerging near the work rather than waiting for a central program.

Technical judgment does not mean that everyone must code. It means that a salesperson can judge when an AI-produced account analysis is reliable, that an operations owner can recognize an exception the system should escalate, and that a finance team can set the evidence required before an automated action changes a financial record. A domain, in this context, is a business area with its own outcomes, policies, data, and expertise.

Here, an AI agent means software that uses a generative AI model to interpret a case and propose or take actions through business tools within defined limits. That capability can strengthen the edges of a company, but it does not by itself remove the need for hierarchy. A management layer often carries information, approvals, exceptions, coaching, and accountability. The structure should change only after the operating model has absorbed enough of that coordination without hiding the work somewhere else.

<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>AI-first architecture links distributed technical judgment with more bottom-up ownership, but a flatter company is a conditional design choice. Leaders must first prove that agents remove real coordination while human authority and accountability remain visible.</p>
</div>

## Make the complete case the unit of proof

Benjamin Simkin, author of the 2026 business book [*The AI First Company: Engineering Your Business to Thrive in the AI Economy*](https://www.amazon.co.uk/dp/B0H45WZRX5), calls the act of bolting AI onto an old structure the “Retrofit Trap.” The phrase matters because task acceleration can disguise an unchanged operating model.

Consider a disputed customer invoice. A model may read the email and draft a response in seconds, while sales still checks the contract, finance approves a billing correction, an operator enters the change, and a coordinator chases missing evidence. The visible task became faster. The case still moves through the same queues, with the same handoffs and opportunities for repair.

The relevant unit of proof is therefore the complete workflow, from the customer’s request to a correctly closed case. “Correctly closed” means that the business outcome is valid, required controls were met, connected records agree, the customer was informed, and no uncounted repair remains. Measure representative routine cases, ambiguous cases, and known difficult cases. Then record human routing touches, rework, quality, control failures, exception load, adoption, elapsed time, total cost per correctly closed case, and the judgment that still belongs to people.

An agent should also compete against a simpler design. If a fixed form, clearer policy, queue, or rules-based workflow can remove the delay more reliably, use it. An agent earns a trial where evidence arrives in varied forms, interpretation is necessary, and the next action cannot be specified fully in advance.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>A fast AI task is weak evidence for organization change. Test the complete business case, include hidden routing and repair, and compare the agent-supported design with a simpler fixed-rule process before funding more complexity.</p>
</div>

## Remove routing before roles

A conventional hierarchy is partly a coordination system. Managers translate priorities, assemble information from different functions, sequence approvals, and decide where exceptions should go. They also coach people and accept responsibility for difficult trade-offs, which are different kinds of work and should not be casually bundled together.

In the invoice case, an agent-supported workflow could collect the order, contract, correspondence, and payment history; identify what is missing; propose the next route; and keep the case moving. A person would still decide whether to make a commercial concession, change policy, or accept material risk. The potential layer reduction comes from removing repeated routing, status chasing, and routine repair, rather than relabelling those activities as “oversight.”

This is where bottom-up ownership becomes concrete. The finance and sales domain owners closest to the outcome can shape the policy, review exceptions, and improve the workflow from operating evidence. Central technology leaders provide shared controls and platforms, but they do not need to dictate every domain decision.

The evidence used for this article contains no Swedish or Norwegian production case that establishes a universal numerical threshold for safely removing a management layer. That boundary matters. A local trial is not a ceremonial pilot; it is how the leadership team learns whether coordination disappeared, shifted to informal work, or returned as a new burden of supervision.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Flattening is justified only when measurable routing and repair work has actually gone away. Human judgment, coaching, and risk acceptance remain, while domain teams gain more ownership over policies, exceptions, and improvement.</p>
</div>

## Put authority where everyone can see it

A distributed organization needs tighter clarity about authority, not looser control. Decision rights specify who may propose, approve, execute, override, and stop an action. Domain-mapped ownership then connects each business outcome and exception class to a named person rather than to an anonymous committee.

The agent should interpret evidence and propose a move. Ordinary software should hold permissions and case state, meaning the authoritative record of where the case currently sits. That software can be workflow software or a business system; its job is to reject an action that is not allowed, even when the agent gives a persuasive explanation.

A local state-and-permission contract for the invoice case might look like this:

| Actor | Current case state | Requested action | Enforced result |
|---|---|---|---|
| AI agent | Required evidence is missing | Issue a billing credit | Rejected and logged; the agent has no execution right |
| Finance approver | Evidence is complete and the dispute is approved | Authorize the credit | Allowed if the organization’s policy checks pass |
| AI agent | Credit has already been issued | Retry the same credit | Rejected as a duplicate and sent for reconciliation |

Retries need their own protection. [RFC 9110](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9110.html#section-9.2.2), the 2022 standard for how web requests behave, uses *idempotent* for a request whose repeated execution has the same intended effect as one execution. A stable case or request identifier helps prevent duplicates, but connected systems still need reconciliation to confirm that a credit, payment, or record was created only once across the workflow.

Auditability means retaining the evidence, proposed action, policy result, approval, system effect, and final outcome. A named control owner must also have stop authority, a tested recovery route, and a rollback plan for returning the workflow to a safe configuration. Some external effects cannot simply be undone, so recovery may require a compensating action, meaning a new transaction that counteracts the first, followed by human review.

Legal and privacy owners should record which obligations apply to this workflow, including the [European Union Artificial Intelligence Act](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj), the EU’s 2024 risk-based AI law, and the [General Data Protection Regulation](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj), its data-protection law applicable since 2018, where relevant. Procurement must cover supplier access, data location, retention, and exit. The organization should retain portable policy definitions, evaluation cases, case history, and a workable fallback path rather than allowing one supplier to become the only place where the process can run.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Agents may interpret and propose, while ordinary software enforces permissions, state, and duplicate protection. Named owners must hold approval, exception, stop, recovery, legal, privacy, and supplier responsibilities, with an auditable record of what actually happened.</p>
</div>

## Rebuild progression with the structure

The old pyramid also carried the employment system. It provided promotion steps, pay progression, coaching capacity, status, and a way for junior staff to learn by taking on gradually harder coordination. Removing a layer without replacing those functions creates a thinner organization and a weaker institution.

Start with the work rather than the job title. Separate routine routing, chasing, and formatting from domain judgment, relationship work, coaching, and accountable risk decisions. That inventory shows which responsibilities can move into the workflow, which can form a richer domain role, and where a role may genuinely disappear.

The people plan belongs inside the trial budget. It needs a named owner, time for paid learning and supervised practice, routes into domain ownership or exception handling, and new expert career paths with credible pay and status. A flatter structure also needs enough human coaching; distributing authority without developing judgment merely distributes failure.

Some transitions will still involve role closures. Leaders should state that possibility early, fund redeployment where it is credible, and handle exits honestly where it is not. Employee representatives should enter the process according to the applicable local law and collective agreements before the organization decision hardens. Participation may slow a rushed announcement, but it improves the chance that the operating consequences are visible before they become irreversible.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>A management layer carries careers and learning as well as coordination. Any redesign must fund new paths for expertise, pay, coaching, and progression, involve employees early, and be honest about redeployment and roles that may end.</p>
</div>

## Take a local decision record to the board

A CIO should ask for a bounded operating trial before asking for a flatter company. Choose a workflow with a measurable closed outcome, meaningful coordination load, bounded consequences, and a credible recovery path. Agree in advance that the agent-supported design will be judged against both the current baseline and a simpler process redesign.

The executive or board record can be one page, but its blanks must be filled locally:

- **Scope and accountability:** [workflow], [business outcome], [executive sponsor], [accountable outcome owner], and [fixed review date].
- **Trial envelope:** [budget], [domain-expert capacity], [platform owner], [risk, legal, and privacy owners], [people and employee-participation owner], [procurement owner], and [review cadence].
- **Baseline and case mix:** [current quality, time, routing, repair, and total cost], plus [routine, ambiguous, exception, and known difficult cases].
- **Evidence contract:** [correctly closed cases], [human touches], [rework], [control failures], [exception burden], [adoption], [elapsed time], [total cost per successful outcome, including supplier, integration, oversight, control, and repair costs], and [residual human judgment].
- **Control proof:** [replayable activity records], [rejected illegal actions], [duplicate-effect reconciliation], [stop test], [recovery exercise], and [fallback route].
- **Applicable constraints and supplier exit:** [AI Act and privacy assessment where relevant], [data-location and retention requirements], [portable assets], and [fallback owner].
- **Workforce consequence:** [responsibilities changed], [learning and redeployment capacity], [career, pay, and coaching design], [participation route], and [role-closure assumptions and cost].
- **Decision conditions:** [local acceptance conditions], [reversal triggers], [owner for each action], and [date of the next authorization].

These fields are a decision contract, not an established benchmark. The leadership team sets its own acceptance and reversal conditions before seeing the result, so enthusiasm after a good demonstration cannot quietly lower the standard. At review, it can retain the current process, redesign the workflow, authorize limited production, or consider an organization change as a separate decision.

The org chart should move only when the record shows that coordination has disappeared rather than migrated, authority can be inspected, recovery works, total cost is understood, and the workforce plan is funded. If that evidence remains mixed, keeping the layer is responsible leadership. An AI-first company distributes technical judgment now and earns structural change one complete workflow at a time.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Bring the board a funded local trial, named owners, a fixed review date, complete-workflow evidence, tested controls, and a workforce plan with predeclared acceptance and reversal conditions. Change the organization only after that record makes the decision defensible.</p>
</div>
