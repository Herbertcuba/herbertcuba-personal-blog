Two linked claims define an AI-first company for me: every role gains a technical dimension, and the organization can become flatter and more bottom-up when AI agents absorb real coordination. An AI agent is a software system that uses a generative model to interpret a situation and take or propose actions through business tools within set boundaries.

[Arvind KC](https://openai.com/index/arvind-kc-chief-people-officer/), chief people officer at OpenAI, the AI research and deployment company, gave the first claim useful precision in a [May 2026 interview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8atlQX8HTwQ). He said that “every role as we go into the future is fundamentally a technical role,” then explained that the technical role is applied to a particular domain. The point is broader technical judgment, not universal coding.

The second claim needs more care. When agents take over execution as well as the work of moving information, checking status, and routing routine exceptions, ownership can move closer to the business domains where decisions arise. That makes a flatter structure possible. I do not use KC’s interview as evidence that OpenAI itself operates this way, and the evidence here does not establish that companies can safely remove management layers at scale. Flattening remains an operating hypothesis that must be proven in the work.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
  <span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
  <p>Technical judgment is spreading across roles, but a flatter company is a conditional design choice. The workflow must prove that agents removed coordination before the org chart reflects it.</p>
</div>

## Technical judgment belongs in every function

Technical judgment means knowing what AI can handle reliably, what evidence it needs, how failure becomes visible, and where a person must decide. A salesperson needs it when an agent prepares a customer commitment. Finance needs it when a model classifies a transaction. Marketing needs it when AI-generated creative content carries factual or rights risks, while support needs it when confidence is too low for an automated resolution.

These employees do not need to build the underlying models. They need enough understanding of capability, data, and consequence to shape the work and challenge a bad output. A central AI team can provide shared platforms and controls, but it cannot hold every pricing rule, customer promise, local process, or risk trade-off in its head.

This is where bottom-up ownership becomes practical rather than rhetorical. Domain teams should be able to propose and test better ways of working inside clear company boundaries. They gain room to act, but they do not inherit unlimited authority.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
  <span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
  <p>Every function needs people who can connect domain knowledge to AI capability and risk. Central technology teams set common boundaries; domain teams own the quality of the work inside them.</p>
</div>

## The Retrofit Trap hides in the handoffs

Benjamin Simkin, author of the 2026 business book *The AI First Company: Engineering Your Business to Thrive in the AI Economy*, calls the act of [bolting AI onto an old structure the “Retrofit Trap”](https://www.amazon.co.uk/dp/B0H45WZRX5). The term is useful because a fast model call can hide an unchanged operating model.

Consider new-supplier onboarding. An agent might extract registration and bank details in seconds. Yet a coordinator may still chase missing documents, decide which review applies, collect approvals, create the supplier record, confirm the outcome, and repair duplicates. One task became faster, while the surrounding routing survived.

An AI-first redesign uses the complete workflow as its unit: the path from the initial supplier request to an approved or rejected outcome recorded in the company’s system. The agent may check completeness, compare evidence with policy, propose a risk class, and advance routine cases. People still decide genuine exceptions and own the policy. The important change is that routine coordination leaves the workflow instead of moving to a less visible queue.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
  <span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
  <p>A faster isolated task does not create an AI-first operating model. The test is whether the whole business request reaches a controlled, recorded outcome with less manual routing and repair.</p>
</div>

## A flatter company has to earn its shape

Hierarchy is partly an information and exception-routing system. Managers combine updates, send decisions to the right level, resolve ambiguity, allocate resources, coach people, and carry accountability. Agents may absorb routine parts of the first three jobs. They do not make policy legitimate, handle human conflict, develop talent, or assume executive accountability.

That distinction changes the evidence leaders should request. A “correctly closed case” is one that reached the intended business outcome, followed policy, was recorded in the authoritative system, and did not depend on hidden repair. Measure that across the full case mix, then examine human routing touches, repair effort, quality failures, control breaches, end-to-end cost, and the judgment still supplied by people.

Set the baseline, review horizon, acceptance conditions, and reversal signals before results arrive. The task-local evidence offers no Swedish or Norwegian production case that supplies a universal numerical threshold for removing a management layer, so each company must choose its own conditions according to risk and operating context. Those conditions should test whether coordination disappeared or merely returned as exception queues, cross-domain meetings, rework, or unmet coaching needs.

The post-change business owner should bring those loads back to a named executive forum on a fixed review date. If they cross the locally agreed limits, the organization restores capacity or narrows the agent’s scope. Reversal then becomes an operating commitment rather than a caveat at the end of a presentation.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
  <span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
  <p>Management layers should change only after complete-workflow evidence shows sustained reductions in routing and repair without transferring the burden to exceptions, control work, or neglected people leadership.</p>
</div>

## The replacement architecture makes authority visible

Removing a layer without replacing its controls makes the company harder to govern. Start with a domain map: a clear view of the business areas, outcomes, and rules that the workflow crosses. Name an outcome owner and a policy owner. Separately name who decides exceptions, who owns the technology, and who has authority to stop it. One person may hold several roles, but every responsibility must remain visible.

The control boundary should also be plain. Agents interpret documents and propose classifications or actions. Ordinary software enforces permissions and authoritative state, meaning the company’s recognized status of a case at that moment. In supplier onboarding, an agent may recommend approval, but the workflow system checks whether the actor has permission and whether “under review” is allowed to become “approved.” Free-form model reasoning should not be able to invent a new state or bypass an approval rule.

Every consequential action needs an audit record showing the actor, permission, applicable policy, model and tool versions, state change, external action, outcome, and any human intervention. An override is an authorized departure from the agent’s recommendation or normal route, with the reason recorded. Stop authority suspends new actions. Rollback returns the internal workflow to a safe state, while recovery also checks and reconciles effects already sent to external systems.

Retries deserve special attention because they can create duplicate records or payments. [RFC 9110](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9110.html#section-9.2.2), the 2022 specification for HTTP semantics, defines an idempotent request method as one where identical requests have the same intended effect as a single request. That principle helps, but it does not guarantee exactly-once execution across several business systems. Duplicate protection still needs business identifiers, provider safeguards, and reconciliation against the recorded outcome.

Supplier dependence belongs in the same control contract. Name the contract owner, preserve portable case records, workflow configuration, versioned prompts, and operating instructions, and maintain a tested fallback path. The trigger for changing provider should be agreed before service quality, contract limits, or cost forces the decision.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
  <span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
  <p>A flatter company needs stronger visible boundaries: named domain owners, software-enforced permissions and state, auditable overrides, real stop and recovery paths, and a practical route away from a failing supplier.</p>
</div>

## People are part of the architecture

Coordination-heavy roles should be broken into the work they actually contain. Status collection and routine routing may shrink, while coaching, conflict resolution, domain learning, policy stewardship, and accountable judgment remain. Leaders then have to rebuild careers around those contributions instead of assuming that progression, pay, and ambition will continue to depend on managing a larger span.

The transition plan should state which responsibilities are likely to change, which roles can grow, what learning is funded, how much time people receive, how redeployment decisions are made, and how pay is handled. Where a role genuinely disappears, the process and possible exits should be explained without pretending that reskilling guarantees a place for everyone.

Employee participation starts while the design can still change. In Sweden, the [Co-Determination in the Workplace Act (1976:580)](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-1976580-om-medbestammande-i-arbetslivet_sfs-1976-580/) governs employer-union participation. Section 11 requires negotiation before significant decisions in defined collective-agreement circumstances, although the exact duties depend on the situation. Swedish leaders therefore have a concrete reason to involve labor relations, counsel, and employee representatives before decisions are fixed; leaders elsewhere should apply their local law and agreements.

This is not a soft appendix to the operating model. If coaching, learning, and fair progression lose their owners, the missing coordination returns as weak decisions, stalled careers, and avoidable resistance.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
  <span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
  <p>Role redesign, career paths, pay, learning, participation, redeployment, and honest exit choices belong in the operating architecture because the old hierarchy carried human development as well as routing work.</p>
</div>

## Put the board decision last

The org chart should be the third decision, not the first. I would carry one decision record through three gates, with every threshold chosen locally and written down before the gate opens.

| Gate | Owner and forum | Evidence required | Decision and reversal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Workflow trial | Business outcome owner in an operations, technology, and risk review | Workflow boundary, baseline, case mix, review horizon, acceptance conditions, and tested stop path | Authorize only the defined cases; stop on a permission breach, untraceable action, or policy failure |
| Limited production | Business owner and CIO delegate in the production-control forum | Correctly closed cases, routing and repair load, quality and control failures, total cost, recovery exercise, and supplier fallback | Widen or hold scope; roll back the workflow or change provider when pre-agreed conditions fail |
| Organization change | Executive team or board, with a named people owner | Sustained operating evidence, residual human responsibilities, employee participation, career and pay design, and a dated post-change review | Change roles, spans, or layers; restore capacity or narrow scope if routing, repair, exceptions, or coaching load returns |

Only the final gate authorizes a change to the organization. A flatter company may look less tidy from the center because more decisions happen at the edges, but that is acceptable only when authority and outcomes become more visible rather than less.

The leadership commitment has to work in both directions: remove coordination that the workflow no longer needs, and restore capacity when evidence shows that the burden came back somewhere else. With complete-workflow proof, explicit authority, controlled complexity, and a credible human transition, the architecture is defensible. Without them, the pyramid has merely been redrawn.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
  <span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
  <p>Ask the board to approve a staged operating thesis with named owners, evidence, review dates, supplier and people commitments, and reversal conditions. Change the hierarchy only at the final gate.</p>
</div>
