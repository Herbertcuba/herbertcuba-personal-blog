Two linked claims define the architecture of an AI-first company. Every business role acquires a technical dimension, and the organization can become flatter as more execution and coordination move to the edges. Together, those changes create room for stronger bottom-up ownership.

Here, an [AI agent](https://openai.github.io/openai-agents-python/) means software that uses a generative AI model, a system that can interpret and produce language, to understand a goal and call approved tools. Technical judgment means knowing what that system can handle reliably, what evidence it needs, where it tends to fail, and when a person must decide. It does not mean that everyone must learn to code.

A flatter company is a hypothesis worth testing, not a reason to redraw the chart in advance. A responsible CIO changes the organization only after complete-workflow evidence shows that coordination work has genuinely fallen, authority remains enforceable, and the company has made credible commitments to the people whose roles will change.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>An AI-first operating model distributes technical judgment and may support more bottom-up ownership, but flatter structures must be earned through workflow evidence, explicit authority, and a funded workforce transition.</p></div>

## Technical judgment has to reach the edge

[Arvind KC](https://openai.com/index/arvind-kc-chief-people-officer/), chief people officer at OpenAI, the artificial-intelligence company behind the ChatGPT conversational service, made the first claim unusually clear. In a [June 2026 interview with the business-interview channel DECODE](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8atlQX8HTwQ&t=343s), he said that every role is becoming “fundamentally a technical role” and that the technical role is applied to a particular domain. His point matters because domain knowledge and technical judgment now have to work together.

A salesperson needs to know which account signals an agent can interpret without inventing certainty. Finance needs to know which supporting records are required before a proposed action is safe. Operations, marketing, and support need the same ability to set boundaries, recognize weak output, and reserve consequential decisions for people. The common capability is disciplined judgment about the system, not software development.

KC also described innovation at OpenAI as bottom-up. That is an observation about one unusual company, not proof that every enterprise should remove a management layer. I think the broader implication is still important: when people closest to a domain can shape and improve agent-supported work, useful change no longer has to travel through a central technology queue.

This shift fails when AI is inserted into one task while the surrounding organization stays untouched. Benjamin Simkin, author of the 2026 business book [*The AI First Company: Engineering Your Business to Thrive in the AI Economy*](https://www.amazon.co.uk/dp/B0H45WZRX5), calls that the **Retrofit Trap**. The term describes adding AI to an existing structure without redesigning the workflow around what the technology changes.

Consider a hypothetical customer-credit request. An agent may summarize the complaint and draft a recommendation in seconds. If a supervisor still forwards it to finance, finance repeats the policy check, another person posts the credit, and support repairs the customer record, the company has accelerated a task while preserving the routing and rework. An AI-first design starts with the complete case and asks which handoffs remain necessary.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Technical judgment belongs inside sales, finance, operations, marketing, and support because those functions know the domain boundaries. AI remains a retrofit when one fast task is surrounded by the same approvals, handoffs, and repair work.</p></div>

## Flattening is an evidence decision

The reason hierarchy can change is easier to see when hierarchy is treated as a coordination system. In his 1974 paper [“Organization Design: An Information Processing View”](https://doi.org/10.1287/inte.4.3.28), organization-design scholar Jay R. Galbraith explained that rules coordinate predictable work while hierarchy handles exceptions for which the rules are insufficient. Managers also set direction and coach people, but much of the daily structure routes information, approvals, and unresolved cases.

Agents may absorb parts of that routing by gathering context, checking routine conditions, updating status, and escalating a case with the relevant evidence attached. Human authority still governs contested trade-offs, employee decisions, **risk appetite**, the level of exposure leadership accepts, and **stop authority**, the named power to pause the workflow. A layer becomes removable only when its coordination load has disappeared rather than resurfaced as cleanup, risk work, or informal help.

I would judge that change through a **correctly closed case**: a complete case that reaches the intended business outcome, satisfies its controls, and leaves no hidden repair behind. The evidence should include human routing touches, repair effort, outcome quality, control failures, total cost including integration and oversight, and the judgment that still belongs to people. A fluent agent response is not a closed case.

No Swedish or Norwegian production case in the available task evidence provides a numerical benchmark for safely removing a management layer. That gap should remain visible. Each company therefore needs to set its own acceptance conditions before a trial, rather than turning an attractive thesis into a universal threshold.

A one-page trial decision record should be approved before the first case runs:

- **Scope and accountability:** why the workflow qualifies for the trial portfolio, its business outcome, domain owner, independent evidence owner, funder, and company-defined risk tier, meaning its potential harm and approval level.
- **Evidence base:** the current baseline and a representative mix of routine, disputed, and high-consequence cases.
- **Boundaries:** the cost limit; the data boundary, meaning the information the trial may access; the evidence window; and the fixed review date.
- **Decision conditions:** the outcomes, control performance, residual judgment, and sustained reduction in coordination required to continue or scale.
- **Stop conditions:** the failures, cost overrun, or concentration of exceptions that trigger a pause.
- **Decision rights:** the executive signatories, required employee-participation checkpoint, and available decisions: scale, narrow, transfer, stop, or consider one bounded structural change.

These fields are an evidence contract, not an established benchmark. Structural change becomes defensible only when the agreed case mix closes correctly and the lower coordination load holds for the declared period.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>A flatter structure is justified by sustained reductions in routing and exception work across complete cases, not by prompt quality or task speed. The baseline, review window, cost boundary, acceptance conditions, stop conditions, and decision owners must be agreed in advance.</p></div>

## Design the workforce transition during the trial

The people architecture cannot wait until the technology trial has produced a favorable result. Coordination-heavy roles contain routing and reporting work, but they also contain coaching, conflict resolution, institutional memory, and accountability. The trial must separate those activities before anyone concludes that a role has disappeared.

Some jobs will change substantially, and some may end. Others may move toward domain ownership, exception design, coaching, or control stewardship. The company should state in advance how it will assess skills, fund learning, select people for redesigned roles, support redeployment, and handle exits. If a flatter structure creates fewer management posts, progression and pay can no longer depend mainly on span of control; credible paths for domain mastery and people leadership need to exist before the old ladder is removed.

Employee participation belongs at two points. Before workflow design, employees and their representatives should map hidden repair work, difficult exceptions, and practical data risks. Before responsibilities, progression, or pay change, the employer should set out the proposed role consequences and complete the required consultation or negotiation.

For Swedish operations, Section 11 of the [Co-Determination in the Workplace Act (1976:580)](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-1976580-om-medbestammande-i-arbetslivet_sfs-1976-580/) requires an employer bound by a collective agreement to negotiate with the relevant employee organization before deciding on a significant change to the business or to employees’ working or employment conditions. The law does not provide an AI operating benchmark. It makes the timing of participation part of the decision architecture.

From the center, more ownership at the edges can look untidy. The real test is whether domain teams operate within clear boundaries, resolve more work without upward routing, and still receive coaching and challenge. Removing visible hierarchy while leaving people to carry invisible coordination would be a worse design.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Role redesign must distinguish routine coordination from coaching, judgment, and accountability. Employee participation, career paths, pay principles, learning, redeployment, and exit support are design inputs to the trial, not cleanup after an org-chart decision.</p></div>

## Keep enforceable authority outside the agent

Once the trial scope and people process are visible, the replacement architecture can be precise. **Domain-mapped ownership** means that each end-to-end workflow has a named business owner, defined policy, known systems, and explicit exception paths. The agent may interpret evidence and propose an action within that map. Ordinary software should enforce identity, permissions, limits, and case state.

A [state machine](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/step-functions/latest/dg/concepts-statemachines.html) is ordinary software that allows a case to move only through defined statuses. A **system of record**, the controlled business system that stores the official record, holds the **authoritative state**, the version of the case the company treats as true. In the customer-credit example, the agent can classify the request and propose an amount, but the controlled system verifies the customer, policy, approver, limit, and latest recorded case version before a credit is posted.

That final check should be indivisible. At the moment of posting, the system should confirm that authorization is still valid, compare the official case version, record a stable identifier for the request, and change the case state as one controlled operation. If the case changed or the same request was already processed, it should reject the action. **Idempotency**, a standard software control described in a [2022 internet standard for web requests](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9110.html#name-idempotent-methods), means that repeating the same request has the same intended effect as making it once. It works only when the receiving system records and reliably rejects duplicates.

Permissions, current case state, audit trails, and stop authority must remain under company control and be enforced outside the agent. Each action should record the evidence used, the rule applied, who or what acted, and the state before and after. Overrides need a named human owner and a reason. The stop owner must be able to pause new actions without asking the model to cooperate.

Recovery also needs exact language. **Rollback** means returning workflow configuration or case processing to a known safe version. A completed financial credit cannot simply be rolled back; finance must **reconcile** it by comparing the official records with the external result and, where appropriate, authorize a **compensating action**, a new transaction that offsets the first one. Both events belong in the audit history.

The company should own its policy, authoritative business state, audit history, request identities, and data boundaries. The system owner should maintain a tested supplier exit path so the model or vendor can be replaced without losing the governed workflow. The domain owner remains accountable for the outcome, the control owner sets exception and stop rules, and an independent evidence owner reports whether the trial met its agreed conditions.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Agents may interpret and propose, while company-controlled software enforces permissions, state changes, duplicate protection, audit, and stop authority. Named owners, tested recovery, clear compensation for irreversible effects, and a supplier exit path keep distributed execution governable.</p></div>

## What the CIO should decide

The board paper should request a bounded operating decision and state what remains unproven. Before changing the organization, I would require four documented commitments with responsible executives attached:

1. **Evidence:** complete cases have met the pre-agreed outcome, control, cost, and coordination conditions across the representative case mix and evidence window.
2. **Authority:** permissions, authoritative state, audit, stop, recovery, and supplier exit have named owners and have been tested.
3. **People:** participation obligations are complete, and the role, career, pay, learning, redeployment, and exit consequences are understood and funded.
4. **Structure:** the company can name the coordination work that has ceased, the human judgment that remains, the review date, and who restores capacity if exceptions rise again.

The decision should follow the evidence. If the workflow improves but coordination does not fall, scale the capability and leave the hierarchy alone. If risk or repair work breaches the agreed conditions, narrow, transfer, or stop the trial. If one domain shows a sustained reduction without concealed cleanup, make one bounded structural change and review it on the date already agreed.

Materially flatter, more bottom-up companies are a credible possibility. They become defensible when the old coordination load has stayed down, the new authority is visible, and leadership has accepted responsibility for the human transition.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The CIO should change the org chart only after signed evidence proves that coordination work has fallen, controls and recovery work, and the workforce transition is funded. Scale, narrow, transfer, stop, or restructure according to that evidence.</p></div>
