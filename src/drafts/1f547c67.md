An AI-first company rests on two linked propositions. Every business role acquires a technical dimension. As AI agents absorb meaningful execution and coordination, more ownership can move to the people closest to the work, making a flatter organization possible. An agent here means an AI system that can interpret a goal and call software tools across several steps, rather than only answer a prompt.

In a [video conversation published on June 1, 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8atlQX8HTwQ), [Arvind KC, Chief People Officer at OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/arvind-kc-chief-people-officer/), the AI research and product company behind ChatGPT, said that “every role as we go into the future is fundamentally a technical role.” He immediately grounded the point in domain expertise: the technology is applied to a particular field.

That supports the first proposition. The second is an organizational argument, and it needs a higher burden of proof. Technical judgment can spread now, but removing a coordination layer is justified only when its work has genuinely disappeared from complete workflows. I think the flatter-company thesis is credible. The org chart should still be the last artifact to change.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Technical judgment can move to the edges immediately; structural flattening must wait until agents have removed coordination work from complete workflows under visible human authority.</p></div>

## Technical judgment belongs in every function

A technical role does not mean that everyone becomes a programmer. It means that people in sales, operations, finance, marketing, and support can judge what an AI system may do reliably, what evidence it needs, where it can fail, and when a person must decide. That judgment becomes part of doing the business job well.

A finance owner, for example, should understand whether an agent is matching evidence or making an inference. A support leader should know which refund cases follow policy and which contain ambiguity the model cannot settle. Marketing must distinguish useful variation from claims that require human review. These are domain decisions with a technical dimension.

This creates room for bottom-up ownership because the people nearest a domain can improve how work is done. From the center, many local owners may look untidy. The model remains governable when each domain has a named owner, common controls, and explicit boundaries for decisions that stay local or move upward.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>AI-first work distributes the ability to judge systems, evidence, and failure across business functions; it does not turn every employee into a coder or remove the need for common boundaries.</p></div>

## The Retrofit Trap preserves the hierarchy

In his business book [*The AI First Company*](https://www.amazon.co.uk/dp/B0H45WZRX5), Benjamin Simkin calls the habit of bolting AI onto an old process the “Retrofit Trap.” The term matters because a fast task can hide an unchanged operating model.

Consider a customer refund. An agent may read the request, retrieve the order, and draft a recommendation in seconds. If a supervisor still routes the case, finance checks the same evidence, another team executes the payment, and support repairs the record after a failed handoff, the company has accelerated one step while preserving the coordination bill around it.

That bill is part of what hierarchy does. Organizational theorist Jay R. Galbraith described this mechanism in his 1974 article [“Organization Design: An Information Processing View”](https://doi.org/10.1287/inte.4.3.28): rules can handle routine situations, while unusual cases travel through the hierarchy to someone with authority. An agent-supported workflow may gather information, apply policy, route a real exception, and update the case record. Humans still own policy, consequential exceptions, accountability, conflict, and coaching.

A flatter structure becomes plausible only when the routine routing and repair work actually declines. Moving it into hidden queues, informal chat, or overextended expert roles does not count.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The Retrofit Trap speeds up an isolated task while leaving approvals, handoffs, and repair intact; fewer layers are justified only when that coordination work vanishes from the full process.</p></div>

## The workflow has to prove the structure

A complete workflow runs from the event that opens a case to a correctly closed outcome. “Correctly closed” should be defined before a trial begins: the business result is right, required controls were followed, the system of record was updated, and any customer or supplier consequence was completed.

Test the agent-supported design against both the current process and the strongest credible conventional automation, such as fixed rules and workflow software without a language model. Otherwise, a team may credit AI for an improvement that simpler automation could deliver with less cost and risk.

The evidence pack should cover four connected questions:

- **Outcome:** How many cases closed correctly, at the required quality, and how many control failures occurred?
- **Coordination:** How many human routing touches, repair minutes, overrides, and unresolved exceptions remained?
- **Economics:** What was the total cost, including labour, integration, software, model use, oversight, and supplier dependence? Report cost per correctly closed case.
- **Judgment:** Which decisions still required a person, who made them, and did that person have the necessary authority?

Set the case cohort or time window, budget cap, business and technology capacity, verifier, stop trigger, and decision date before work starts. I am not presenting a Swedish or Norwegian numerical benchmark for removing management layers here. The thresholds must be set for the workflow and approved in advance, rather than inferred after a promising result.

A board-ready sequence can fit on one page:

| Stage | Decision | Minimum record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Change now** | Distribute technical judgment and map the domain | Workflow owner, decision rights, current baseline, funding owner, case cohort, control and HR capacity |
| **Prove next** | Run the three matched designs | Cost per correctly closed case, routing and repair effort, stop trigger, independent verifier, supplier fallback, rollback route, decision date |
| **Authorize later** | Change roles or layers only within the proven scope | Repeated workflow evidence, residual human judgment, named exception owners, role and pay effects, funded people plan, executive approval |

A successful bounded trial may justify the next bounded scope. By itself, it does not authorize removal of an enterprise-wide layer.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Measure correctly closed cases across the current, conventional, and agent-supported designs, including coordination, control, total cost, and residual judgment; then limit any structural decision to the scope the evidence covers.</p></div>

## The replacement architecture makes authority visible

Conditional flattening requires a replacement for the useful work hierarchy performed. Start with domain-mapped ownership: every workflow, policy boundary, and material exception has a named human owner. Decision rights then state who may set policy, approve an exception, change a limit, override an outcome, stop the workflow, and restart it.

Inside those boundaries, let the agent interpret unstructured material and propose an action. Ordinary transactional software should enforce permissions and the current case state, meaning the trusted record of where the case is now. Every consequential command should carry a case identifier, expected state, permission, proposed action, idempotency key, external-effect status, committed state, and exception owner. The external-effect status records whether a payment is pending, confirmed, failed, or unknown; the committed state changes only when the result is established. A second refund proposed for a case already marked as paid should be rejected before execution and routed to the exception owner.

Retries need special care. [RFC 9110](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9110.html#name-idempotent-methods), the 2022 standard that defines core semantics for the web’s HTTP protocol, describes idempotency as making repeated identical requests have the same intended effect as one request. In the refund example, a unique idempotency key can prevent a timeout from producing two payments. If the payment result is uncertain, the case stays marked “unknown” until reconciliation confirms success or failure; a named finance operations owner resolves anything the software cannot establish.

The audit path should record the evidence, agent proposal, rule applied, action taken, override, and final result. Stop authority names the person who can halt new actions. Rollback names the safe process to restore if the trial fails. Supplier ownership must also be concrete: one owner covers approved data locations, access withdrawal, service interruption, and contractual exit, while the workflow owner remains accountable for the safe fallback.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Agents may interpret and propose, while ordinary software enforces permissions and state; named humans retain policy, exception, stop, reconciliation, supplier-exit, and rollback authority.</p></div>

## Flattening changes the career system

If agents remove routine routing, status consolidation, and standard approvals, some coordination-heavy roles will shrink and some may disappear. That consequence cannot be filed under communications after the architecture decision. It belongs in the decision itself.

Decompose roles before changing titles or headcount. Separate work the new workflow has removed from judgment, coaching, conflict resolution, policy design, and improvement work that still needs an owner. A wider span of control does not automatically create coaching capacity, and an agent does not become accountable for another person’s growth.

The old pyramid also carried ambition through management promotions. A flatter company therefore needs credible expert and domain-owner paths, with published scope, progression criteria, learning support, and pay consequences. Before authorizing structural change, fund a workforce workstream with an HR owner, an employee-participation gate under applicable local arrangements, a learning allocation, agreed redeployment or exit principles, and a date by which affected role and pay paths will be decided.

The board should see the operating benefit and the transition cost in the same case. Hiding one makes the other less defensible.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>A flatter structure changes jobs, coaching, progression, and pay; the approval case therefore needs role-level analysis and a funded transition owned jointly by the business and HR.</p></div>

## The CIO’s decision is staged

The first decision is to distribute technical judgment and map ownership without pretending the organization has already earned a new shape. The next is to fund a bounded workflow test with a named owner, budget, capacity, matched alternatives, independent verification, supplier exit plan, stop trigger, and workforce workstream.

Only then does the org-chart decision become legitimate. Ask whether complete cases close correctly at an acceptable cost, whether routine coordination has measurably declined, whether remaining judgment and exceptions have owners, whether the process can be stopped and restored, and whether affected people have credible paths. If the evidence fails, improve the workflow or keep the layer. If it holds across the intended scope, change the structure only within that scope.

An AI-first company is ultimately defined by where technical judgment lives and which coordination work no longer needs a hierarchy. The org chart should record that operating reality, not serve as an aspiration for it.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Change capability now, prove the complete workflow next, and authorize structural change only after economics, control, reversibility, and the workforce transition are defensible together.</p></div>
