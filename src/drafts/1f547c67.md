Two claims define the architecture of an AI-first company. Every business role acquires a technical dimension. And when agents absorb enough routine execution and coordination, ownership can move closer to the work and the organization can become flatter. The first claim is a capability agenda. The second is a design hypothesis that must be proven.

[Arvind KC](https://openai.com/index/arvind-kc-chief-people-officer/), appointed Chief People Officer at OpenAI in February 2026, expressed the first claim directly. In a [June 2026 DECODE business interview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8atlQX8HTwQ&t=342s), he said that every future role would be “fundamentally a technical role” applied to a particular domain. OpenAI, the AI research and product company behind ChatGPT, is an unusual organization rather than a template for a bank, manufacturer, or public agency. The formulation still identifies a change every leadership team must confront.

Here, an AI agent means software built around a model that pursues a defined goal through approved tools and steps. It may interpret information, propose an action, and advance a case. An AI-first company can distribute technical judgment and remove routine coordination, but responsible leaders alter the hierarchy only when complete-workflow evidence, explicit decision rights, controlled complexity, and a credible workforce transition make that choice defensible. From headquarters, distributed ownership may look untidy. Its discipline comes from boundaries that everyone can inspect.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Distributed technical judgment is a current capability need; a flatter hierarchy remains an operating hypothesis that must earn its way into the organization.</p></div>

## Put technical judgment where the work lives

Technical judgment is the ability to decide what an AI system may do in a business domain, what evidence it needs, where its capability ends, and when a person must take over. It is not a synonym for coding.

Take a hypothetical request for an urgent replacement part on a production line. Maintenance understands the failure and the consequence of delay. Procurement knows which suppliers and contract terms are acceptable. Finance owns spending authority. Operations judges whether the proposed repair window is workable. An agent may assemble the evidence and prepare a recommendation, but those functions must define what counts as reliable evidence, an acceptable action, and an exception.

That boundary matters because AI capability is uneven. In a 2026 *Organization Science* experiment, [Fabrizio Dell’Acqua and his coauthors](https://doi.org/10.1287/orsc.2025.21838) studied 758 consultants using OpenAI’s GPT-4 model. On one selected task designed to sit outside the tested capability frontier, participants using AI were 19 percent less likely to reach the correct answer. Fluent output did not remove the need to recognize an unsuitable task.

The [EU AI Act](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:02024R1689-20260727), the European Union’s 2024 risk-based AI regulation, reinforces a separate point. Article 4 requires providers and deployers to support AI literacy in light of people’s knowledge, training, and use context. That obligation does not turn every employee into an engineer. It makes role-appropriate understanding part of responsible deployment.

A central AI team can provide platforms, shared controls, and specialist support. The people closest to the domain still need authority over evidence, limits, and escalation. That is where bottom-up ownership begins.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Technical judgment belongs inside each business domain because useful AI decisions depend on context, capability boundaries, and explicit escalation—not coding alone.</p></div>

## Follow the case beyond the AI task

Benjamin Simkin, author of the 2026 business field manual [*The AI First Company*](https://www.amazon.co.uk/dp/B0H45WZRX5), calls it the Retrofit Trap: AI accelerates an isolated activity while the surrounding operating structure remains intact.

The replacement-part agent may read a maintenance report, identify an approved supplier, and draft a purchase request in seconds. The business outcome is still unfinished. Someone must confirm the current asset condition, validate the budget, authorize the commitment, issue the order, track delivery, update the maintenance and financial records, and handle a mismatch or supplier failure. If people continue chasing approvals and repairing records, the organization has a faster task rather than a new operating model.

Organization researchers Thomas W. Malone and Kevin Crowston defined [coordination in 1994](https://doi.org/10.1145/174666.174668) as “managing dependencies between activities.” Organization-design scholar Jay R. Galbraith’s [1974 information-processing account](https://doi.org/10.1287/inte.4.3.28) explained how rules can settle familiar cases while hierarchy receives exceptions the rules cannot resolve. Managers do far more than route work, but information assembly, approvals, status collection, and exception routing are real parts of what hierarchy does.

A complete workflow runs from the initial trigger through the business outcome, authoritative record updates, exceptions, and recovery. For this decision, define a correctly closed case as one that meets its declared quality and policy conditions, updates the official business record, accounts for every human intervention and repair, and leaves no hidden cleanup behind. This is a proposed management unit, not an industry benchmark.

The trial should compare the agent-supported workflow with both the current process and the strongest reasonable conventional alternative, such as workflow software or predefined business rules. An agent deserves no credit for coordination that simpler software can remove more reliably and at lower total cost.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The evidence unit is the correctly closed workflow, including approvals, records, exceptions, and cleanup—not the speed of an impressive AI task.</p></div>

## Prove that coordination has left

Flattening becomes defensible when operating evidence shows that specific responsibilities have disappeared or moved to clear owners closer to the work. The trial conditions must be declared before results arrive. Ordinary, exceptional, stale-data, unauthorized, duplicate, disputed, and supplier-failure cases should remain visible even when they weaken the conclusion.

Count correctly closed cases by case class, elapsed time from trigger to closure, human routing and approval touches, repair time, reopenings, outcome quality, and control failures. Add the full operating cost of models, integration, monitoring, review, exceptions, and supplier fees. Then inspect the residual human judgment and any work that has shifted into informal supervision.

If an agent reduces preparation time but creates a new verification queue, coordination has migrated. The same is true when risk teams inherit more exceptions, frontline employees repair more records, or a vendor becomes the only party able to explain and alter the workflow. A conventional rules-based design may also outperform the agent once reliability and total cost are included.

In the evidence reviewed for this argument, no validated Swedish or Norwegian production case establishes a numerical threshold for removing a management layer. A Nordic CIO therefore needs local evidence conditions rather than a borrowed benchmark. Routine information assembly, status collection, routing, and known-exception handling may shrink. Novel trade-offs, changes to risk appetite, conflict resolution, coaching, employment decisions, and accountability still require named human owners.

The structural decision should state which responsibilities vanished, which moved into the domain, which remain with leaders, and which new control work appeared. Without that accounting, a flatter chart can conceal a larger coordination burden.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>A layer can shrink only when complete-workflow evidence shows that its coordination work has genuinely left rather than resurfaced as review, repair, risk work, or supplier dependence.</p></div>

## Make authority executable

Economists Philippe Aghion and Jean Tirole distinguished [formal authority from real authority in 1997](https://doi.org/10.1086/262063): the assigned right to decide can differ from effective control over what happens. A domain team may have an empowerment mandate while vendor settings, inaccessible data, or centralized approval logic still determine every consequential action.

A decision right is explicit authority to propose, approve, reject, override, stop, or restart. Authoritative state is the official current status and version of a case. Domain-mapped ownership assigns each outcome and difficult exception to a business owner, while deterministic software—ordinary code applying predefined rules—enforces permissions and state.

1. The domain owner defines acceptable outcomes, case classes, limits, and escalation rules.
2. The agent interprets unstructured information and proposes the next step.
3. Company-controlled software checks identity, permission, current case state, policy limits, and whether the action has already been completed.
4. A named human owns exceptions, overrides, stop decisions, and restart.
5. The system records the action, actor, time, outcome, and recovery path.

Duplicate protection illustrates why the boundary matters. [RFC 9110](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9110.html#section-9.2.2), a 2022 Internet standard from the Internet Engineering Task Force, defines idempotency as repeated identical requests having the same intended server effect as one request. In a business workflow, a repeated command must not create a second purchase order. That requires a stable action identity, a check against current state, and a stored prior outcome—not a model instruction asking the agent to be careful.

If the supplier received the order but the internal confirmation failed, the case should enter a pending reconciliation state instead of being retried blindly. A compensating action is a new action that corrects an external effect that cannot simply be erased. Rollback, by contrast, returns internal software, configuration, or workflow rules to a known safe version.

Every consequential action needs an audit trail, and a named human needs stop authority: both the right and the practical mechanism to halt new actions. The audit trail records who or what acted, when, against which case state, and with what result. A tested supplier exit must also let the company change providers or operate manually without losing its state, policies, or records.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Flatter edges require visible decision rights and a company-controlled core that enforces permissions, state, duplicate protection, audit, stop, and recovery.</p></div>

## Build a career system for the new shape

A coordination-heavy job is a bundle of activities, responsibilities, relationships, and status. Some activities may shrink: chasing updates, assembling routine information, forwarding cases, or applying known rules. Coaching, conflict resolution, difficult judgment, accountability, and the development of future leaders still need owners.

The [International Labour Organization](https://doi.org/10.54394/HETP0387), the United Nations agency for labor standards and research, made the distinction clear in its 2025 analysis of generative-AI exposure. Exposure to AI-capable tasks does not imply immediate automation of an entire occupation; transformation is more likely because most occupations still contain work requiring human input.

The career architecture must therefore change with the workflow. Employees need expert progression that does not depend on managing a larger team, with credible pay ranges and organizational status. Coaching must become an assigned responsibility. Funded learning time, transparent redeployment and selection rules, and a clear route into new domain or service-owner roles must exist before the old ladder is removed.

Social dialogue means structured discussion and negotiation among employers, workers, and their representatives. It belongs at two points: before a trial changes how work is performed, and before trial evidence changes roles, pay, or reporting lines. In Sweden, Section 11 of the [Co-Determination in the Workplace Act of 1976](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-1976580-om-medbestammande-i-arbetslivet_sfs-1976-580/) requires negotiation before qualifying significant changes where its statutory and collective-agreement conditions apply. Norwegian operations require their own legal and collective-agreement review.

Employee representatives should receive the same operating evidence as executives and a practical way to pause the organizational decision when agreed conditions are unmet. A flatter company that removes credible paths for learning, pay, and ambition has redesigned only half of its architecture.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Workforce transition is part of the operating model: redesign progression, pay, coaching, learning, participation, and pause authority before removing the old career ladder.</p></div>

## Write the refusal conditions first

The executive paper should authorize a reversible operating trial with company-set conditions. It should fit on one page and make an unfavorable result actionable:

- **Scope and ownership:** Name one complete workflow, its domain owner, permanent service owner, control owner, independent evidence owner, and executive sponsor.
- **Evidence:** Declare case classes, comparators, dates, measurements, and the rule that adverse cases cannot be relabeled out of scope after observation.
- **Authority:** Record who may propose, approve, reject, override, stop, restart, and release each consequential action.
- **Failure and recovery:** Define stop conditions, pending-state reconciliation, rollback, compensating-action ownership, manual service, and supplier exit.
- **People:** Set checkpoints before work design changes and before evidence changes roles, pay, or reporting lines.
- **Decision:** Allow the result to expand, narrow, enter normal operations, or stop. Organizational redesign remains a separate authorization.

The evidence owner must be independent enough to reject the workflow owner’s interpretation. The permanent service owner must remain accountable after the pilot team leaves. Stop conditions should include unauthorized or stale releases, unresolved duplicate behavior, missing audit records, uncontrolled data use, and supplier failure without a workable fallback.

The board is making two decisions at different times. The first authorizes a reversible trial and the ownership needed to run it safely. The second asks whether proven changes in execution, coordination, authority, and careers justify changing roles or removing a layer. Structure should record an operating reality the company has already demonstrated—not gamble on one it merely expects.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Authorize the workflow trial with owners, comparators, refusal conditions, recovery, and workforce commitments; treat structural redesign as a separate decision.</p></div>
