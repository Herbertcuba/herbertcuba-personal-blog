An AI-first company begins with two linked claims. Every business role acquires a technical dimension, and the organization can become flatter when more execution and coordination happen at the edge rather than travelling up and down a hierarchy.

At a [recorded founders dinner in San Francisco](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8atlQX8HTwQ), Arvind KC, chief people officer at OpenAI, a company that develops generative AI systems, said that “every role as we go into the future is fundamentally a technical role” and described that role as technical capability applied to a particular domain. In the same discussion, he described innovation at OpenAI as bottom-up rather than top-down. That is a useful account of how one AI company operates, but it is not evidence that an established enterprise can safely remove management layers.

My position is conditional. An AI agent, meaning software that uses a generative model to interpret a goal and choose among permitted actions, can absorb routine work and some of the routing around it. A company should redesign roles and reporting lines only when evidence from complete workflows shows that this coordination has disappeared rather than moved into hidden repair work.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Distributed technical judgment and bottom-up ownership make a flatter company possible. They do not make it automatic: workflow evidence, visible authority, reliable controls, and a workforce transition must come first.</p></div>

## Technical judgment moves into every domain

Technical judgment is the ability to decide what an AI system can handle reliably, what evidence its output needs, how failure will appear, and when a person must take over. This does not mean everyone must code. It means that people in sales, finance, operations, marketing, and support need enough understanding to shape the system around the reality of their work.

A sales lead might decide which account signals can support a recommended next action and which customer commitments still require approval. A finance owner might permit automated matching of routine records while keeping ambiguous tax treatment with a qualified person. A support leader might let an agent gather context and propose a resolution while reserving refunds or safety cases for named owners. The technical capability is inseparable from domain knowledge because reliability depends on the meaning and consequence of each decision.

This pushes design authority closer to the work. Central technology, security, risk, and legal teams still provide shared controls, but they cannot specify every exception from the centre. Bottom-up ownership becomes credible when domain teams can improve a workflow inside clear boundaries and remain accountable for its results.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Every role becomes more technical when people must judge AI capability and failure within their own domain. Shared controls remain central, while workflow design and result ownership move closer to the people who understand the work.</p></div>

## Flattening begins with whole-workflow proof

Benjamin Simkin, an entrepreneur and author, calls the alternative the “Retrofit Trap” in his 2026 book [*The AI First Company: Engineering Your Business to Thrive in the AI Economy*](https://www.amazon.co.uk/dp/B0H45WZRX5). He uses the term for bolting AI onto an old structure instead of redesigning the work around it. The phrase names a useful design problem; it is not independent proof that any particular redesign will succeed.

Imagine that an agent reads supplier-onboarding documents in minutes, but employees still copy its answer between systems, chase approvals, resolve duplicate records, and repair incorrect classifications. One task is faster, yet the workflow still carries the same coordination burden. If leadership removes a role on the strength of the fast step, the remaining employees inherit the repair work.

A complete workflow runs from the original business trigger to a valid outcome. A correctly closed case reaches that outcome with the required evidence, permissions, records, and customer or supplier communication intact. For supplier onboarding, that means more than extracting data: the right legal entity is created once, risk checks are complete, exceptions reach the right owner, and every decision can be reconstructed.

This is where hierarchy enters the argument. A traditional management layer often collects information, routes approvals, resolves routine exceptions, and confirms that work is complete. An agent-supported workflow may absorb some of those functions, but leaders need to measure correctly closed cases, human routing touches, repair effort, elapsed time, quality, control failures, total cost, and the judgment that remains with people. The material reviewed for this edition offers no Swedish or Norwegian production case that establishes a transferable numerical threshold for removing a management layer. This is a bounded statement about the supplied evidence, not a claim that no such case exists anywhere, so acceptance conditions must be set locally before a trial begins.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The unit of proof is a correctly closed workflow, not a fast AI-assisted task. Delayering becomes defensible only when representative cases show that routing and repair work have genuinely fallen without shifting cost, risk, or unnamed judgment elsewhere.</p></div>

## The replacement architecture makes authority visible

Flattening removes a routing mechanism, so the replacement must make ownership clearer than the hierarchy it displaces. Map each workflow to a business domain, name its accountable owner, and write down the decision rights: which choices each person or system may make, for whom, over which resource, and within what limits. Stop authority is the named power to pause further automated action when evidence, controls, or outcomes become unsafe.

The model should interpret documents and propose the next permitted step. Ordinary control software should authorize any consequential action by checking the acting identity, the person or function on whose behalf it acts, the allowed resource and action, the current case version, the approval limit, and the authority’s expiry or revocation status. The design must also say whether revocation cancels an action already in progress.

A separate audit record should capture the attempted action, the evidence used, the policy and system versions, the result, and any human override. The authoritative state, meaning the company’s official record of where the case stands, must live outside the model’s free-form reasoning. That distinction matters when untrusted content enters the workflow. [NIST AI 600-1](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.600-1.pdf), the US National Institute of Standards and Technology’s July 2024 risk profile for generative AI, calls instructions hidden in retrieved material “indirect prompt injection.” Text inside an email or attachment may influence interpretation, but it must never grant permission.

The supplier case also exposes a harder failure. The purchasing system may create a supplier, then lose connection before the workflow records success. A blind retry can create a duplicate. [Request for Comments 9110 (RFC 9110)](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9110.html#name-idempotent-methods), a 2022 Internet Engineering Task Force standard for how web requests and responses behave, defines an idempotent method as one whose repeated identical requests have the same intended effect as one request. Business workflows need the same care through a unique action key, a check against the external system, and a named corrective owner when the outside action cannot simply be reversed.

The CIO also owns the complexity introduced by this control layer. Before production, require evidence that logs can be exported, material changes by the AI-service provider are disclosed, critical workflows can continue during an outage, and the organization can exit the service without losing its operating record. A flatter structure is a poor trade if it replaces visible coordination with a control system nobody can inspect or stop.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Agents may interpret and propose, while ordinary software enforces identity, scope, permissions, current state, and limits. Named people retain exception, stop, and corrective authority, and every consequential action needs an auditable path through execution, failure, and recovery.</p></div>

## A flatter company needs a new career system

The hierarchy was never only an information network. It also distributed coaching, status, pay, progression, conflict resolution, and a sense of ambition. If agents reduce the need for people who collect updates, chase routine approvals, and repair handoffs, those roles will change. The work does not vanish cleanly, and the people doing it cannot be treated as a late communication issue.

Start by separating tasks from titles. Some coordination-heavy tasks may disappear, while coaching, resource allocation, difficult judgment, and personal accountability remain. Then build progression that does not depend on accumulating direct reports: deeper domain authority, stronger craft roles, broader workflow ownership, and recognised coaching responsibility all need credible pay and status. Paid learning, redeployment options, and fair exit support belong in the investment case.

For a Swedish organization, participation also has a legal setting. [The Co-Determination in the Workplace Act (1976:580)](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-1976580-om-medbestammande-i-arbetslivet_sfs-1976-580/), a central Swedish labour-relations law, requires an employer bound by a collective agreement to initiate negotiations with the relevant employee organisation before deciding on a more important change to operations or to members’ working or employment conditions. My operating recommendation goes further: the labour-relations lead should convene employee representatives with the workflow owner before the target structure is fixed, while role scope, training, redeployment, controls, and review conditions are still open to influence.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>A flatter structure changes the system that carried coaching, progression, pay, and ambition. Leaders must redesign those functions, fund learning and redeployment, and involve employee representatives while consequential choices can still change.</p></div>

## Change the org chart last

The executive decision should begin as a bounded, reversible workflow trial, not a target number of managers. Before launch, the CIO can require one decision record that makes the local hypothesis, evidence, authority, and people commitments visible. Blank fields are deliberate because no universal threshold is being proposed.

| Decision field | Record before the trial |
| --- | --- |
| Proposed structural change | ________ |
| Workflow, business domain, and representative case mix | ________ |
| Frozen baseline and evidence provenance | ________ |
| Volume, spend, staffing, time, and risk limits | ________ |
| Accountable owner, decision rights, exception owner, and stop authority | ________ |
| Review cadence and decision date | ________ |
| Acceptance conditions for quality, control, cost, routing, repair, and residual judgment | ________ |
| Reversal path, including in-flight cases and external actions | ________ |
| Participation, learning, redeployment, pay, and exit commitments | ________ |
| Signed outcome: stop, redesign, extend, or change the structure | ________ |

At review, the evidence should show whether complete cases close correctly across the declared case mix, whether human coordination has fallen rather than become invisible, and whether failures leave the company in a known state. It should also show that the remaining judgment has a named owner, the control system can be operated without one supplier’s goodwill, and the workforce commitments are funded.

If those conditions hold, removing or reshaping a coordination layer may be the responsible design. If they do not, keep the structure, repair the workflow, or narrow the agent’s authority. The leadership decision is to change the org chart only after the operating model has earned it.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Require a bounded trial, local acceptance conditions, named authority, auditable evidence, a reversal path, and funded people commitments. Change reporting lines only when the complete workflow proves that the old coordination layer is no longer doing necessary work.</p></div>
