An AI-first company begins with two linked propositions. Every business role acquires a technical dimension, meaning the judgment to decide what an AI system can do reliably in that domain, where it fails, and when a person must decide. If AI agents absorb enough routine execution and coordination, ownership can also move closer to the people who understand the work, allowing parts of the company to become flatter.

By *AI agent*, I mean software in which a generative AI model can choose parts of a process and use approved tools toward a goal. [Anthropic, an AI company, distinguishes this](https://www.anthropic.com/research/building-effective-agents) from a fixed workflow whose path is set entirely in advance.

The first proposition has direct support. In a [2026 DECODE interview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8atlQX8HTwQ), [Arvind KC](https://openai.com/index/arvind-kc-chief-people-officer/), chief people officer at OpenAI, an AI research and deployment company, said that “every role as we go into the future is fundamentally a technical role,” then qualified it as technical work “applied to a particular domain.” The available interview does not establish a separate claim that innovation at OpenAI is bottom-up, so I will not use it as evidence for the second proposition. Flatter, more bottom-up ownership remains an operating hypothesis that each company has to prove.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>AI-first architecture links distributed technical judgment with the possibility of flatter ownership. The first is already visible as a business capability; the second still requires workflow evidence.</p></div>

## Technical judgment moves into every role

Technical judgment belongs inside the business because domain knowledge determines whether an AI-produced answer is useful. A salesperson needs to know which customer facts can support a proposal and when pricing or legal approval is still required. Marketing must judge whether generated material matches the evidence and brand constraints, while support must recognize when an apparently routine case involves unusual entitlement, emotion, or risk. Finance and operations need to know which source is authoritative and whether the current data is complete enough to act.

None of this requires everyone to write code. It requires people to describe the outcome, test the result, understand common failure modes, and place human judgment where the consequences demand it. Training only a central AI team leaves the people closest to the work dependent on an internal queue.

That is the practical case for bottom-up ownership. A domain, meaning a business area with responsibility for a set of outcomes and policies, should be able to shape its own bounded workflows. Shared controls still define what data, tools, and actions are allowed, but the domain team decides where the system helps and where it must yield.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Technical judgment means understanding AI in the context of a business domain, not turning every employee into a developer. Domain teams need room to shape workflows inside common limits.</p></div>

## The retrofit trap preserves coordination

Benjamin Simkin, author of the 2026 business book [*The AI First Company: Engineering Your Business to Thrive in the AI Economy*](https://www.amazon.co.uk/dp/B0H45WZRX5), calls the practice of bolting AI onto an old structure the “Retrofit Trap.” The term is useful because a faster task can leave the surrounding company untouched.

Consider supplier onboarding. A model may read registration documents and draft a risk summary in seconds, while employees still chase missing information by email, route approvals through several managers, enter the same facts into an enterprise system, and repair exceptions by hand. The extraction task improved. The complete operating path did not.

A hierarchy is partly a system for moving information and authority. Managers collect updates, interpret policy, sequence dependencies, approve actions, and route exceptions. An agent-supported workflow may absorb some of that coordination by collecting evidence, tracking case state (the authoritative record of where a case stands), requesting missing material, and proposing the next permitted action. People should retain policy ownership, consequential exceptions, accountability, coaching, and the authority to stop the process.

The organizational opportunity appears only when those coordination duties actually decline. Automating visible production while preserving invisible routing gives the company more software without giving it a new architecture.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The Retrofit Trap speeds up an isolated task while manual routing, approvals, and repair survive around it. An AI-first model changes the complete path through which information and authority move.</p></div>

## Flattening needs workflow evidence

The workflow, rather than the management layer, is the right unit of proof. A *complete workflow* runs from a defined request to a verified business outcome and includes escalation, repair, and record keeping. A *correctly closed case* reaches that outcome at the required quality and control level, with enough evidence to explain what happened.

Before a bounded trial, the accountable business owner should freeze the workflow boundary, representative case mix, baseline source and date, and organization-specific acceptance conditions. During the trial, the team should record correctly closed cases, human routing touches, repair effort, elapsed time, quality, control failures, total cost, and the decisions that still require human judgment. Cases abandoned, bypassed, or quietly fixed outside the system belong in the record too.

The task-local material available for this article contains no Swedish or Norwegian production case that establishes a transferable numerical threshold for safely removing a management layer because of agents. That is a limit of this article’s source set, not a claim about every Nordic company. It also means the board should not borrow a confident percentage from a vendor presentation or from this article.

A flatter design becomes defensible when representative complete workflows show that coordination demand has fallen without reappearing as hidden repair, control failure, supplier dependency, or extra work elsewhere. If the trial proves only that one step is faster, redesign the workflow and keep the reporting structure.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Delayering requires evidence from complete workflows and correctly closed cases. Measure hidden human work, control outcomes, cost, and residual judgment against conditions set before the trial.</p></div>

## Authority must become easier to see

Removing a routing layer without replacing its decision logic makes the company harder to govern. Each complete workflow therefore needs a named domain owner and explicit decision rights: who may propose, approve, execute, override, stop, and change the rules.

The agent should interpret unstructured material and propose an action within those rights. Conventional software, with explicit and testable rules, should enforce identity, permissions, limits, and current case state before anything happens. This separation matters because [NIST AI 600-1](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.600-1.pdf), the US National Institute of Standards and Technology’s 2024 risk profile for generative AI, describes *indirect prompt injection*: instructions hidden in material retrieved by an AI application can redirect the model. An email or attachment may supply evidence, but it cannot grant itself authority.

A delegation record should state which person or service is acting, on whose behalf, for which resource and action, within what limit, until when, and what revocation does to work already under way. An *audit trail* is the durable history of what was proposed, approved, executed, overridden, or stopped. *Stop authority* belongs to the named person or role allowed to pause the workflow when its limits fail, preserving enough state for investigation and recovery.

Execution needs one more safeguard. [RFC 9110](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9110.html#name-idempotent-methods), the 2022 standard describing HTTP web-request semantics, formalizes *idempotency*: repeating the same request should have the same intended effect as making it once. In the supplier example, every attempt to create a vendor record should carry one stable action identifier. Even then, a network failure can leave the company unsure whether an external system completed the action, so software must check that system before retrying and send uncertainty to a person rather than guessing.

Finally, rollback means restoring the earlier state where that is possible. When an external effect cannot be undone, the design needs a reconciliation and corrective-action path instead. Vendor dependency belongs inside the same control boundary: the company must know what evidence it can export, how it detects material service changes, and how the workflow continues or closes safely if the provider is unavailable.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Bottom-up ownership needs visible authority. Agents may interpret and propose, while conventional software enforces permissions and state, and named people own exceptions, stops, recovery, and rule changes.</p></div>

The mapping is organizational. Distributing tasks does not create ownership when objectives, limits, and rule changes remain concealed. An AI-first company empowers its edges only when domain teams can see the outcome they own, the evidence behind a decision, the permitted actions, the escalation path, and who can change the system.

## A flatter company needs a new career architecture

Coordination-heavy roles rarely contain coordination alone. They also carry coaching, conflict resolution, judgment, relationships, and accountability. Before removing a role, leaders should separate the work that the workflow has genuinely absorbed from the human work that still needs an owner. Otherwise the organization deletes a title and discovers the missing job later.

The old pyramid also turns management into the main route to higher pay, status, and influence. A flatter company needs credible expert and domain-owner paths with clear progression and comparable recognition. Coaching must become an assigned responsibility with capacity, while paid learning gives affected employees time to build technical judgment. If agents remove routine entry-level work, apprenticeship cannot depend on juniors learning through tasks that no longer exist; supervised cases and progression criteria have to be designed deliberately.

Sweden’s [Co-Determination in the Workplace Act (1976:580)](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-1976580-om-medbestammande-i-arbetslivet_sfs-1976-580/), commonly called MBL, provides a concrete participation anchor. For an employer bound by a collective agreement, Section 11 requires the employer to initiate negotiation with the relevant employee organization before deciding a significant change to operations or to employees’ working or employment conditions. The operating implication is to begin while workflow scope, role design, training, redeployment, and timing remain open to influence, then apply the relevant local law and agreement in each country.

Some roles may still end, and a serious transition cannot promise otherwise. It can commit to early participation, funded learning, transparent choices on redeployment and pay, and humane support where no continuing role exists. Those commitments need owners and budgets before the organizational decision, not after it.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Delayering changes careers as well as reporting lines. Preserve ownership of coaching and judgment, create respected non-manager paths, involve employees while choices remain open, and fund the transition honestly.</p></div>

## Take an evidence contract to the board

A board does not need an abstract promise that agents will make the company flatter. It needs a bounded decision record that connects operating proof, authority, and the human transition. The fields below are a proposed governance tool, not an established benchmark.

| Record | Fill in before the trial | Supply at the decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Workflow proof | Boundary: ___; case mix: ___; baseline date and source: ___; business owner: ___; acceptance conditions: ___ | Complete-case results; human touches and repair; quality and control failures; total cost; residual judgment |
| Authority | Permitted actions and limits: ___; rule owner: ___; exception owner: ___; stop owner: ___; provider dependencies: ___ | Audit evidence; overrides and stops; unresolved external actions; rollback or correction results; changes to permissions |
| People | Work and roles affected: ___; participation owner and start date: ___; learning and redeployment budget: ___; pay and progression choices: ___ | Work actually removed; roles redesigned or ended; pathways offered; consultation outcomes; unresolved commitments |
| Decision | Review date: ___; accountable executive: ___; signatories: ___; reversal trigger and path: ___ | Signed choice to stop, redesign, extend, or change the structure, with evidence, conditions, and a next review date |

The numbers in this record should come from the company’s own baseline, risk exposure, and economics. The decision should also remain reversible: a failed control, changed provider, deteriorating outcome, or unfulfilled people commitment must be able to stop the redesign rather than become an inconvenience explained away later.

I would support a flatter structure when the record shows that coordination work has durably disappeared, authority remains visible, exceptions are owned, and the workforce transition is funded. Until those conditions hold, the defensible leadership decision is to improve the workflow without changing the hierarchy around it.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Change the organization only through a signed evidence contract covering workflow outcomes, authority, recovery, and people. If the proof or commitments fail, keep the structure and continue redesigning the work.</p></div>
