An AI-first company begins with two linked claims. Every business role gains a technical dimension, and the organization can become flatter as AI agents absorb routine execution and coordination, allowing more ownership to sit with the people closest to the work.

An [AI agent](https://aws.amazon.com/what-is/ai-agents/) is software that uses a generative model to interpret a goal and take steps through connected tools within defined limits. That capability can change how work moves, but it does not by itself justify removing a management layer. A responsible CIO needs proof from complete workflows, a visible authority model, and a credible plan for people whose work will change.

This distinction matters because the org chart should follow the operating model. Change it too early and the company removes human coordination before the replacement can carry its weight. Change it too late and faster teams remain trapped inside old routing paths.

  <span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
  <p>AI-first architecture combines distributed technical judgment with the possibility of flatter ownership, but organizational change must follow evidence, control, and a funded people transition.</p>
</div>

## Technical judgment belongs close to the work

[Arvind KC](https://openai.com/index/arvind-kc-chief-people-officer/), chief people officer at OpenAI, the company behind ChatGPT, said in a [May 2026 video interview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8atlQX8HTwQ) that “every role as we go into the future is fundamentally a technical role.” He added that the technical role is applied to a particular business domain, meaning an area with its own goals, rules, and expertise. That is the useful reading for an enterprise leader. It does not mean everyone must become a programmer.

Technical judgment is the ability to decide what an AI system can handle reliably, what evidence it needs, which failure would matter, and when a person must take over. A salesperson needs that judgment when an agent proposes contract terms. Finance needs it before accepting a model’s explanation of an unusual payment. Operations, marketing, and support need it whenever AI-generated output can affect a customer, a record, or a commitment.

KC’s observation supports the first originating claim. I treat the second claim, that more ownership can move from the hierarchy to the edges, as an organizational hypothesis to be tested. Teams closest to a domain can improve its workflow without sending every choice upward, provided they share clear limits and a common record of what happened. From the center, that freedom may look like disorder. The difference between disorder and an empowered edge is whether authority, evidence, and accountability remain visible.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
  <span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
  <p>A technical role means applying informed judgment about AI inside a business domain. Bottom-up ownership becomes credible when local freedom operates within visible limits and accountability.</p>
</div>

## The hierarchy has to lose a job first

One useful way to read a hierarchy is as a system for moving information, approvals, and exceptions. Managers collect context, reconcile priorities, route unusual cases, and repair work that crosses team boundaries. An agent-supported workflow may absorb some of that load by gathering records, checking routine conditions, proposing a next step, updating systems, and escalating only the cases that need judgment.

The unit of proof is therefore the complete workflow. Benjamin Simkin, author of the 2026 business book [*The AI First Company: Engineering Your Business to Thrive in the AI Economy*](https://www.amazon.co.uk/dp/B0H45WZRX5), calls the alternative the “Retrofit Trap”: attaching AI to an old structure instead of redesigning how the work runs. A customer-service agent that drafts a refund response in seconds has achieved little if employees still search for the order, check policy, seek approval, initiate payment, update the case, and explain the outcome by hand.

Measure a **correctly closed case**: a case completed from its agreed starting point to its business outcome, at the required quality and control level, with the record finished. Compare it with a local baseline across representative cases. Count human routing touches, repair time, exceptions, quality failures, control failures, cycle time, total cost, and the judgment that still belongs to people. Total cost includes the agent service, integration, oversight, manual fallback, supplier costs, and workforce transition, not only the minutes apparently saved.

Before a trial begins, require a one-page local operating warrant with these fields filled:

- **Scope and baseline:** the named workflow, start and end states, representative case group, exclusions, current performance, and accountable domain owner.
- **Trial boundary:** duration, spend limit, workload and mix of normal and unusual cases, required human capacity, model version and supplier, review date, and production handover date.
- **Decision rules:** locally approved acceptance limits, an abandonment rule, immediate stop conditions, and the executive forum that will record the decision.
- **Continuity and rights:** permitted data use, where data may be stored or processed, audit access, notice of model changes, retained case history, supplier substitution, service commitments, and the owner who restores the manual route.
- **Signed ownership:** the executive sponsor, domain owner, workflow and service owner, control owner, supplier owner, and people owner, each with a stated decision right.
- **People commitment:** roles affected, paid learning, consultation owner and timing, redeployment budget, and the date on which any role decision may be considered.

These are fields for local values, not a universal benchmark. No Swedish or Norwegian production case in the available material establishes a safe numerical threshold for removing a management layer. The accountable forum should choose its boundary with the relevant business, risk, finance, and technology owners, then fix it before results are known.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
  <span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
  <p>A flatter structure is defensible only when complete-workflow evidence shows that coordination work has disappeared. The local warrant fixes scope, cost, controls, owners, decision rules, and the people commitment before the trial can redefine success.</p>
</div>

## Put authority where everyone can see it

Removing routing work does not remove authority. It changes where authority must be expressed. Start with domain-mapped ownership: for each bounded area of business rules and outcomes, name the person who owns the result, the policy, and the unresolved exceptions. Then state who may approve a normal case, grant an exception, override an automated proposal, pause the service, and authorize its return.

The technical boundary should be equally plain. The generative model interprets messy input and proposes an action, while controlled software enforces permissions and valid changes. The **authoritative state**, held in a case record that preserves each change, is the official account of the case’s current position and completed actions. Durable workflow systems are designed to preserve progress and support [checkpoints, retries, and recovery](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/azure-functions/durable/durable-functions-overview). A [policy engine](https://www.openpolicyagent.org/docs/latest/) applies declared rules instead of asking a free-form model to improvise them.

Return to the refund case. The agent can read the request, gather the order and policy, and recommend an amount. The controlled workflow checks whether that amount is permitted, whether the required approval exists, and whether the case is in a state from which payment may be issued. **[Idempotency](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9110.html#name-idempotent-methods)** means that repeating the same intended operation has the same intended effect as doing it once. A unique request key lets the refund workflow apply that rule, preventing a retry from paying twice. The audit trail records the inputs, model and policy versions, proposal, approval, action, and any human override.

Leaders also need named recovery powers. **Stop authority** means a named person can suspend new actions without waiting for the supplier. An **override** replaces a proposed action through an approved path and leaves evidence behind. **Rollback** returns software or case state to an earlier safe point when that is possible. Money already sent cannot simply be rolled back, so recovery may require a correcting transaction and a check that the business records agree. The manual route must remain usable until the service has proved stable, and changing the model or supplier must not erase case history or weaken control.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
  <span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
  <p>Agents may interpret and propose, while controlled software owns permissions, case state, and valid actions. Named humans retain exceptions, override, stop, recovery, and accountability.</p>
</div>

## Redesign careers before removing layers

Once system authority is visible, the people consequence becomes the next authorization question. A company cannot responsibly flatten its structure by treating that consequence as a communication task. Coordination-heavy roles contain different kinds of work. Routine status collection, forwarding, scheduling, and repair may shrink. Coaching, priority setting, conflict resolution, risk judgment, and accountability do not disappear merely because an agent can route a case.

The old pyramid also carries ambition. It often links progression and pay to managing a larger team, so fewer layers can quietly remove the path employees were told to follow. The replacement needs expert careers with growing decision scope, domain ownership, coaching responsibilities, and pay progression that does not depend on headcount. Managers whose roles change need a transparent role map, paid time to learn, and a fair route to redeployment or exit when no suitable role exists.

For a Nordic enterprise, **employee participation** means the formal information and consultation that applicable law and collective agreements may require with employees or their representatives. The people owner should identify the applicable duty and begin that process before a role decision, not after the new structure has effectively been chosen. Consultation timing, transition funding, and decision ownership belong in the operating warrant because they determine whether the redesign is actually executable.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
  <span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
  <p>Flattening changes careers as well as workflows. The new model needs protected human judgment, expert progression, pay paths, paid learning, early employee participation, and funded support for people who cannot move into the new roles.</p>
</div>

## Make the org chart the final approval

A CIO should separate production approval from organization approval. First decide whether the complete workflow is safe, useful, affordable, and supportable over the agreed review period. Only then ask whether the evidence shows a durable reduction in coordination work large enough to change roles, spans of control, or layers.

The executive or board record should contain the completed local warrant, results against its baseline and pre-agreed rules, signed ownership for the domain and controls, continuity of the supplier service and manual route, and the funded workforce plan. It should also show the residual judgment people still perform. A fast task, an impressive demonstration, or a lower model bill cannot answer those questions.

This preserves the strongest part of the legacy architecture thesis without turning it into a prediction. AI-first companies may become materially flatter, with more technical judgment and ownership at their edges. Only layers whose coordination function has been demonstrably removed are candidates for redesign, and only when the replacement authority and human transition can survive scrutiny.

The org chart should record a proven operating model, not serve as the experiment that discovers whether one exists.

  <span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
  <p>Approve the workflow on complete local evidence, then consider the organization as a separate decision. Structural change is warranted only where coordination has truly gone and its controls, continuity, ownership, and people commitments are ready.</p>
</div>
