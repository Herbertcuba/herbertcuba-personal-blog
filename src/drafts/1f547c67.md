An org chart cannot tell you whether a company is AI-first. It shows reporting lines, but not who can judge an AI system's limits, which approvals carry real judgment, which handoffs merely move information, or who can stop an automated action before it becomes a commitment.

Two linked propositions sit behind the architecture question. The first is that every business role acquires a technical dimension. In a [June 2026 interview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8atlQX8HTwQ&t=342s) published by DECODE, a business-interview video channel, [Arvind KC](https://openai.com/index/arvind-kc-chief-people-officer/), OpenAI's Chief People Officer since February 2026, said future roles would be “fundamentally a technical role” applied to a domain. OpenAI is the AI research and product company behind ChatGPT, and its unusual structure is not proof for a bank, manufacturer, or public agency. The useful point is narrower: domain expertise now includes judging what AI can do, what evidence it needs, and when a person must take over.

The second proposition is that a company may become flatter and more bottom-up when agents absorb enough execution and coordination. An AI agent is software built around a model that pursues a bounded goal through approved tools and steps. Unlike a chatbot that only replies, it can act inside a workflow. When that works, ownership can move toward the people with domain context instead of rising through coordination layers.

An AI-first company distributes technical judgment across the business and can move decisions closer to the work. A responsible CIO redesigns the hierarchy only after end-to-end evidence shows that coordination has actually left, decision rights are visible, complexity is controlled, and people have a credible route through the transition.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>AI-first design begins by moving technical judgment into business roles and testing whether agents remove real coordination. The org chart comes later.</p></div>

## Put technical judgment where the context lives

Technical judgment is not coding. It is the ability to decide what an AI system may do in a business domain, what facts it must use, where reliability ends, and which cases require escalation. Central AI specialists can create shared methods, but they cannot own every commercial, operational, or human consequence.

Sales needs to know which contract terms an agent may extract and which concessions require authority. Finance must separate routine matching from judgments that alter risk or an external commitment. Support must distinguish a standard policy case from an ambiguous dispute or a vulnerable customer. Marketing has to inspect sources and quality conditions, not merely accept fluent copy. Operations has to define valid states, failure conditions, and recovery.

This is why model proficiency alone is weak evidence. In a [2026 experiment published in *Organization Science*](https://doi.org/10.1287/orsc.2025.21838), organization researcher Fabrizio Dell'Acqua and his coauthors studied 758 consultants using OpenAI's GPT-4 language model. On one task selected to sit outside the model's reliable capability boundary, AI users were 19 percent less likely to reach the correct answer. The result does not generalize to every workflow. It shows why a business role needs judgment about where assistance stops helping.

[Article 4 of the EU AI Act](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:02024R1689-20260727), the European Union's 2024 risk-based AI regulation, also requires providers and deployers to support AI literacy in light of people's knowledge, training, and use context. That obligation supports role-appropriate literacy, not a company-wide demand that everyone become an engineer.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Technical judgment means understanding capability, evidence, consequences, and escalation inside a business domain. It belongs with the people who hold that context.</p></div>

## Expose the dependencies before automating them

Benjamin Simkin, the business author of the 2026 book [*The AI First Company: Engineering Your Business to Thrive in the AI Economy*](https://www.amazon.co.uk/dp/B0H45WZRX5), calls the opposite approach the Retrofit Trap: adding AI to one activity while leaving the surrounding structure intact.

Consider a hypothetical supplier-onboarding process. An agent can read forms, extract registration and bank details, identify missing documents, and draft a risk summary. Yet the case may still wait for procurement to chase an owner, information security to interpret an exception, finance to create records, and a manager to resolve a commercial trade-off. If staff must verify every field, repair inconsistent records, or discover a duplicate supplier after approval, the work has moved around rather than gone away.

The point is not that agents should own all these decisions. Leaders first need to see what the hierarchy is doing. Researchers Thomas W. Malone and Kevin Crowston [defined coordination in 1994](https://doi.org/10.1145/174666.174668) as “managing dependencies between activities.” Organization scholar Jay Galbraith's [1974 information-processing account](https://doi.org/10.1287/inte.4.3.28) showed how rules can handle familiar cases while hierarchy receives exceptions the rules cannot settle.

That gives Magnus a practical decomposition. Routine information assembly, status chasing, assignment, and known-exception routing may be candidates for software. Risk appetite, contested trade-offs, employment decisions, coaching, and accountability remain human responsibilities. Some structured work will be handled better by ordinary workflow software, which routes cases through predefined states and rules, or robotic process automation, software that repeats predefined interface and data-entry actions. An agent earns a place where interpretation of changing or unstructured information adds value.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The Retrofit Trap accelerates an activity while preserving its dependencies. Map those dependencies before deciding that an agent has removed organizational work.</p></div>

## Change the evidence unit

A complete workflow is the full path from a trigger through approvals, exceptions, record updates, and the final outcome. For this decision, use a correctly closed case as the evidence unit. It is a management test proposed here, not an industry benchmark: the outcome meets declared quality and policy conditions, updates the official business record, counts every human intervention and repair, and leaves no cleanup hidden in another team.

For supplier onboarding, the trial should predeclare ordinary, missing-document, duplicate, unauthorized, disputed, and supplier-failure cases. It should measure correctly closed cases, elapsed time, routing and approval touches, repair and reopening, outcome quality, control failures, total cost, and the human judgment still required. Model fees, integration, monitoring, exception handling, and informal supervision belong in that cost.

Use two comparators: the current process and the strongest reasonable design based on conventional workflow software or business rules. The agent-supported version should not receive credit for a gain that simpler software delivers more reliably.

Set the evidence period, case classes, and failure conditions before the trial. Adverse cases cannot be labeled out of scope after they appear. The evidence available for this article contains no validated Swedish or Norwegian production case that supplies a numerical threshold for safely removing a management layer. Magnus therefore needs a local decision record, not a borrowed percentage.

A layer becomes a redesign candidate only when the evidence shows which responsibilities disappeared, moved to a domain team, or remain necessary. If coordination returns as frontline cleanup, extra risk review, supplier dependence, or quiet supervision by experienced employees, the organization has not become materially flatter.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Judge complete workflows through correctly closed cases, full cost, residual judgment, and adverse conditions. Structural change needs local evidence rather than a universal threshold.</p></div>

## Make authority executable

Economists Philippe Aghion and Jean Tirole gave leaders a useful distinction in [1997](https://doi.org/10.1086/262063): formal authority is the right to decide, while real authority is effective control over what happens. A decision right is explicit authority to propose, approve, reject, override, stop, or restart. A domain owner may hold the title and still lack real authority if a vendor controls the model, a central team owns the data, or an invisible rule determines release.

At the outer layer, domain teams define outcomes, case classes, limits, and escalation rules.

Inside that boundary, an agent may interpret unstructured information, assemble evidence, and propose a next action.

Company-controlled software then checks identity, permission, the current approved case state, policy limits, and duplicate protection before anything consequential is released.

A named person owns exceptions, overrides, stop decisions, restart, and accountability.

The current approved case state is the authoritative state: the version the company recognizes as valid. A model can interpret information, but it should not improvise whether a stale proposal is still authorized.

Uncertain outcomes need a mechanical rule. Suppose supplier activation is sent and the connection fails before confirmation. The workflow should assign one stable identity to that intended action, record a pending state, and reconcile the result before retrying. The IETF, the open standards organization for the internet, [defines idempotency for HTTP in its 2022 RFC 9110](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9110.html#section-9.2.2) as repeated identical requests having the same intended effect as one request. A business workflow needs more: the action identity must connect to the stored outcome and the current case state.

Rollback means returning internal software or workflow configuration to a known safe version. It cannot erase a bank instruction or contract already released. That may require a compensating action, a new act that corrects the external effect. The recovery owner should be named before release.

Every consequential action needs an audit trail, and a named human needs stop authority: both the right and the practical mechanism to halt new actions. An audit trail records who or what acted, when, on which state, and with what result. The US National Institute of Standards and Technology, a federal standards agency, [recommended monitoring and documenting human overrides in its 2024 voluntary generative-AI profile](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.AI.600-1). That makes review and accountability practical rather than ceremonial.

Real control also requires a supplier exit: access to policy, state, and logs, plus a tested manual or replacement route if the provider fails. That organizational control test is separate from legal compliance. The [EU AI Act's override and stop provisions](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:02024R1689-20260727) apply when a system meets the legal definition of high-risk AI, not to every agent. The [General Data Protection Regulation](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32016R0679), the EU privacy law applicable since 2018, governs personal-data processing and certain solely automated decisions with legal or similarly significant effects. Legal classification must be done for the actual workflow.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Domain teams can hold wider responsibility when company-controlled systems enforce state and permission, while named people retain exception, recovery, override, stop, and restart authority.</p></div>

## Rebuild the career bargain

Coordination-heavy roles are not bundles of avoidable handoffs. Many managers coach, resolve conflict, allocate scarce resources, spot weak signals, and prepare people for larger decisions. A workflow trial should distinguish those responsibilities from routine routing before any role changes.

The [International Labour Organization](https://doi.org/10.54394/HETP0387), the United Nations agency for labor standards and research, concluded in its 2025 generative-AI exposure analysis that exposure of tasks does not mean an occupation disappears. Transformation is more likely because most occupations retain work requiring human input. That is a sounder starting point for workforce design than assuming an exposed role can be deleted.

Before changing reporting lines, leadership and HR should specify which activities shrink, which judgments remain, how selection and redeployment work, and who receives funded learning time. A flatter company also needs expert levels that do not depend on headcount, credible pay progression, visible status, and named coaching responsibility. Otherwise the pyramid disappears while its career function remains unattended.

Employees and their representatives need participation before a trial changes work and again before evidence changes roles, pay, or reporting lines. Social dialogue means structured discussion among employers, workers, and their representatives, with evidence and a real chance to influence the choice. In Sweden, [Section 11 of the 1976 Co-Determination Act](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-1976580-om-medbestammande-i-arbetslivet_sfs-1976-580/) requires negotiation before qualifying significant changes in activities or working and employment conditions. Local applicability and collective agreements still require review.

Give one named leader authority to pause the organizational decision if the evidence, consultation, learning commitment, or future ownership is incomplete. A reversible technology trial should not become an irreversible people decision through momentum.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Delayering changes coaching, progression, pay, learning, and participation as well as reporting lines. Design their replacements before making the transition irreversible.</p></div>

## Bring the board a reversible choice

The first board request should authorize a bounded workflow trial, not a new hierarchy. Its decision record needs to answer seven things:

- **Scope and hypothesis:** Which workflow and responsibilities are being tested, and what evidence would show that coordination declined?
- **Owners:** Name the domain owner, permanent service owner, control owner with authority to block release, independent evidence owner who can reject the conclusion, and executive sponsor.
- **Comparators and cases:** Test the current process, the strongest conventional automation design, and the agent-supported design against predeclared case classes.
- **Measures and period:** Record closure, time, human touches, repair, quality, control failures, total cost, residual judgment, and the company-set observation period.
- **Authority and recovery:** State who may propose, approve, override, stop, restart, reconcile uncertain outcomes, and own compensating actions.
- **Workforce commitments:** Set participation checkpoints before work design changes and before any later decision about roles, pay, or reporting lines.
- **Decision rule:** Allow the trial to expand, narrow, enter normal operations, or stop. Treat organizational redesign as a separate decision.

The trial is allowed to disappoint. If deterministic workflow performs better, use it. If human exception load increases, narrow the scope. If audit or stop controls fail, halt. If supplier exit or workforce commitments are not credible, do not turn the pilot into an operating model.

A positive result still does not automatically remove a layer. Leaders must show which dependency is gone, where every remaining decision right sits, who carries coaching and accountability, and whether performance holds under adverse cases for the declared period. Only then does changing the org chart become defensible.

An AI-first company can be flatter because judgment travels outward and routine coordination shrinks. It remains governable because permissions, state, audit, recovery, and stop authority grow stronger. It remains worth building because people can see a future in it. Those are the commitments Magnus should take to the board.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Authorize a reversible workflow trial with named owners, predeclared failure conditions, controls, and workforce commitments. Change reporting lines only after complete-workflow evidence shows which coordination responsibility has genuinely gone.</p></div>
