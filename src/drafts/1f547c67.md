An AI-first company changes two things at once: the competence inside each role and the place where decisions can be made. Every role gains a technical dimension. If AI systems can also absorb routine execution and coordination, more ownership can sit with the people closest to the work, and the organization may need fewer management layers.

Here, an AI agent means software that uses a generative model to interpret a goal, choose steps, and call approved business systems. A flatter company means fewer layers devoted to routing information, approvals, and exceptions. It does not mean weaker accountability.

That organizational conclusion must remain conditional. A company should change reporting lines only after evidence from the whole workflow, from incoming request to final recorded outcome, shows that coordination work has genuinely disappeared. The same decision also needs clear authority boundaries, controlled technical complexity, and a funded plan for the people whose work changes.

<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>AI-first design can distribute judgment and support a flatter organization, but the org chart should follow verified changes in work, authority, and responsibility.</p>
</div>

## Technical judgment belongs in every domain

At a May 2026 founders dinner in San Francisco, [Arvind KC, chief people officer at the artificial-intelligence company OpenAI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8atlQX8HTwQ), said that “every role as we go into the future is fundamentally a technical role.” He then described that technical work as being “applied to a particular domain.” The useful point is not that every employee must become a programmer. It is that technical judgment becomes a business capability.

Technical judgment is the ability to decide what an AI system can do reliably, what evidence it needs, how failure will appear, and where a person must decide. Sales needs it when an account summary informs preparation but cannot commit a price. Finance needs it when extraction is routine but materiality is a judgment. Marketing, operations, and support need the same capability wherever generated content or proposed action can affect a customer, a payment, or a regulated record.

A domain is a coherent area of business work, such as customer refunds. Domain ownership means that a named leader owns its outcome, policy, and improvement across functional handoffs. When that owner and the team understand the technology, ideas and decisions can move from the edge of the business instead of waiting for a central AI group.

KC’s observation supports the first claim about distributed technical judgment. It does not prove the second claim that AI-first companies can safely become flatter and more bottom-up. I treat that as an operating-model hypothesis: local judgment makes distributed ownership possible, but only removed coordination work can make fewer layers sensible.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Every function needs enough technical judgment to own AI-supported work in its domain, while the case for fewer management layers still has to be proven separately.</p>
</div>

## The hierarchy shrinks only after the workflow does

Benjamin Simkin, an entrepreneur and author, calls one common failure the “Retrofit Trap” in his 2026 book [*The AI First Company: Engineering Your Business to Thrive in the AI Economy*](https://www.amazon.co.uk/dp/B0H45WZRX5). The term describes bolting AI onto an old structure instead of redesigning the work around it. It matters because faster tasks and a new operating model are different outcomes.

Consider a hypothetical customer refund. An agent may classify the request and draft a recommendation in seconds, while a service representative still copies the result into another system, a team lead approves it, finance checks it, and someone later repairs mismatched records. The visible task became faster, but the coordination system survived.

A traditional hierarchy carries information upward, sends approvals downward, and finds owners for exceptions. An agent-supported workflow can gather evidence, apply routine policy, update records, and send a genuine exception directly to the right person. Yet if it creates another review queue or moves repair work into finance, the apparent efficiency has merely changed address.

The right unit of evidence is therefore the complete workflow. For the refund, that begins with the request and ends when the decision, payment, ledger, customer message, and case record agree. A correctly closed case is one in which the intended outcome is complete, policy has been followed, the official record is current, and no unresolved exception remains.

Compare those cases with a pre-trial baseline and the same mix of easy, difficult, and unusual work. Count human routing touches, repair time, reopened cases, quality failures, control failures, total cost, and the judgment that still remains with people. These are proposed local measures, not established benchmarks. There is no Swedish or Norwegian production case in the evidence reviewed here that establishes a transferable numerical threshold for removing a management layer because of agents.

The CIO should set acceptance conditions before the trial starts. Flattening becomes defensible only when coordination falls without hidden repair, quality loss, uncontrolled cost, or orphaned judgment. Otherwise the honest decision is to redesign the workflow and leave the reporting structure alone.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Task acceleration does not justify delayering. Evidence must show that complete cases close correctly with less routing and repair, acceptable control and cost, and a named owner for the judgment that remains.</p>
</div>

## Ownership needs hard boundaries

If the workflow passes that test, the replacement for hierarchy cannot be free-form model reasoning. It needs explicit decision rights, meaning a written account of who may decide what, within which limits, and who takes over when the limits are reached.

Four parts should be visible. The domain owner sets the outcome, policy, risk limits, and evidence standard. The agent interprets requests, gathers evidence, and proposes or performs only permitted actions. Ordinary control software checks identity, delegated authority, the permitted resource and action, and the current case version before anything changes. A named human owns exceptions, overrides, and stop authority, which is the power to suspend the workflow before more cases are affected.

The control software should also maintain the authoritative state, the company’s official record of where a case currently stands. Each attempted action should record which system acted, on whose behalf, the permission and limit used, the evidence considered, the case version, and the result. That creates auditability: the ability to reconstruct what happened, why it was allowed, and who remained accountable. Revoked or expired authority should block new actions; activity already in flight needs an explicit reconciliation owner.

This separation matters because an agent reads untrusted material. [NIST AI 600-1](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.600-1.pdf), the US National Institute of Standards and Technology’s July 2024 risk profile for generative AI, describes indirect prompt injection as an attacker placing instructions in data likely to be retrieved by an application using a large language model. A sentence hidden in a refund email may influence model output, but it cannot be allowed to grant permission. Retrieved text is evidence. The control system decides whether the proposed action is authorized.

External actions add another boundary. Suppose the payment provider receives the refund request, but the connection fails before the company records the response. [Request for Comments 9110](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9110.html#name-idempotent-methods), a 2022 standard for the web’s HTTP protocol, defines an idempotent method as one where multiple identical requests have the same intended effect as a single request. A unique action key can reduce duplicate-payment risk, but it cannot prove what an external provider did. The workflow must reconcile the provider’s record before retrying, and a person must own corrective action when reversal is impossible.

This architecture adds components, so controlled complexity matters. Each component needs a purpose, owner, test, failure path, and removal path. The point is to let domain teams move quickly inside visible boundaries while central leadership retains responsibility for common policy, shared controls, and systemic risk.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Distributed ownership works when the agent interprets and proposes, ordinary software enforces authority and official state, and named people own exceptions, stops, reconciliation, and corrective action.</p>
</div>

## Change the career system before the org chart

Fewer coordination layers change more than headcount. Middle roles often carry coaching, conflict resolution, sponsorship, institutional memory, and the first response to an unusual case. If routine routing disappears, leadership must decide where those human functions will live before removing the roles that currently provide them.

The career system has to move as well. Progression can reward domain depth, sound exception judgment, system stewardship, coaching, and influence across teams. Pay and status cannot remain tied mainly to the number of direct reports if the company says it wants expertise and ownership at the edge. Some coordination-heavy roles may still shrink or disappear, and a responsible plan should say that plainly.

In Sweden, [Section 11 of the Co-Determination in the Workplace Act (1976:580)](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-1976580-om-medbestammande-i-arbetslivet_sfs-1976-580/) requires an employer bound by a collective agreement to negotiate with the relevant employee organization before deciding an important change to operations or employment conditions. That legal floor matters. My recommendation goes further: begin participation while the trial’s scope, role design, measures, learning offer, redeployment path, and timing are still open to influence.

Before changing reporting lines, the executive sponsor should require a one-page trial charter. The blanks are deliberate because acceptance conditions must come from the company’s baseline, risk, and workforce context rather than a universal AI benchmark.

| Decision field | Record before the trial |
| --- | --- |
| Workflow and ownership | Domain: ___; start and end of the workflow: ___; business owner: ___; technology owner: ___ |
| Baseline and case mix | Baseline period: ___; case categories: ___; current routing, repair, quality, control, cost, and capacity evidence: ___ |
| Trial boundaries | Permitted actions: ___; excluded actions: ___; volume, spend, time, and permission limits: ___ |
| Evidence provenance | Source of each measure: ___; log owner: ___; method for preserving failed and reopened cases: ___ |
| Human authority | Exception owner: ___; override conditions: ___; stop owner: ___; external-action reconciliation owner: ___ |
| People commitment | Affected roles: ___; participation start and representatives: ___; paid learning and redeployment budget: ___; pay, progression, and exit decisions: ___ |
| Review and reversal | Review date: ___; acceptance conditions: ___; decision signatories: ___; workflow and reporting-line reversal path: ___ |

At the review, leadership can stop, redesign, extend the evidence period, or change the structure. Delayering is justified only when the workflow evidence, authority model, technical controls, and funded workforce commitments support the same conclusion. The org chart is the last artifact to change, because by then the operating model has shown what work, judgment, and responsibility remain.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>A flatter company needs a new career system and an honest transition. The board decision should rest on a predeclared trial charter that joins operating evidence, human authority, employee participation, funding, and a reversible structural choice.</p>
</div>
