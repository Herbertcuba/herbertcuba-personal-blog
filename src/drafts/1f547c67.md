Two claims define the architecture of an AI-first company. Every role that directs, receives, or is affected by AI-supported work acquires a technical dimension. And when AI agents absorb enough routine execution and coordination, more ownership can move into the business while some layers of hierarchy become unnecessary.

By *AI agent*, I mean software that uses a language model to interpret a goal, choose steps, and act through connected tools. An AI-first operating model redesigns complete workflows and decision rights around that capability. The prospect of a flatter company follows from this redesign, but it remains an organizational hypothesis to prove locally, not an industry result that leaders can copy.

That distinction should shape the board discussion from the start. Before changing reporting lines, the CIO should present a bounded trial record covering one complete workflow: its baseline, case mix, owners, time and spend limits, acceptance conditions, stop authority, review date, and workforce commitments. The org chart comes after the evidence.

<p>AI-first design links distributed technical judgment to the possibility of fewer coordination layers, while making any structural change conditional on a bounded, board-visible operating trial.</p>

## Technical judgment belongs in the business

[Arvind KC](https://openai.com/index/arvind-kc-chief-people-officer/), chief people officer at OpenAI, an AI research and deployment company, put the capability shift plainly in a [2026 recorded interview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8atlQX8HTwQ): “every role as we go into the future is fundamentally a technical role.” He immediately qualified the statement by saying that the technical role is “applied to a particular domain.”

That qualification matters. Technical judgment does not mean that everyone must code. It means a sales leader can judge when an agent may prepare an offer and when commercial discretion is required. A finance team can separate a routine match from a disputed invoice. Operations can define the evidence needed before a case advances, while support can recognize when empathy, ambiguity, or customer risk calls for a person.

The common capability is the ability to reason about what an AI system can do reliably, where it fails, and which consequences require human judgment. A central AI team can supply platforms and controls, but it cannot hold all the domain knowledge needed to make those calls. My organizational hypothesis is that ownership can therefore move closer to the people who understand the work, provided their authority is bounded and visible.

This is also where the old operating model resists change. Benjamin Simkin, author of the 2026 business book *[The AI First Company: Engineering Your Business to Thrive in the AI Economy](https://www.amazon.co.uk/dp/B0H45WZRX5)*, calls the practice of bolting AI onto an old structure the **Retrofit Trap**. Imagine a finance team using a model to draft late-payment messages while people still select every account, check the ledger, approve each message, copy the outcome into another system, and manually route disputes. The draft became faster; the workflow did not.

AI-first design starts with the whole case, from an overdue invoice being identified to payment, an agreed plan, or a resolved dispute. It asks which steps require judgment, which are policy checks, which are information relays, and which exist only because systems and teams cannot coordinate directly. That is how technical judgment becomes a business capability rather than a collection of clever prompts.

<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Every domain needs enough technical judgment to direct and challenge AI-supported work. If the surrounding approvals, handoffs, and repair remain intact, faster task output is still a retrofit rather than a new operating model.</p>
</div>

## Prove that the hierarchy has less work to do

A hierarchy is partly a system for moving information and authority. Managers collect status, set priorities, approve departures from policy, route exceptions, reconcile conflicting views, and remain accountable when the process cannot decide. An agent-supported workflow may take over some collection, comparison, follow-up, and routing. It does not inherit the human authority to set risk appetite, resolve a material trade-off, make an employment decision, or accept accountability for an exception.

This mechanism gives the CIO a practical test for flattening. Measure whether recurring coordination work has disappeared across a complete workflow, rather than whether an individual task became faster. A **correctly closed case** is one that reaches the agreed business outcome, meets its quality and control conditions, and does not depend on hidden repair after the system declares success.

The trial record should fit on one page and contain six commitments:

- **Workflow and baseline:** Name one repeated end-to-end workflow, freeze the current case mix and performance baseline, define the sampled cases, and cap the volume exposed to the trial.
- **Owners:** Name the executive sponsor, domain owner, outcome evaluator, risk and security owners, people lead, supplier owner, and the person who can stop the workflow.
- **Envelope:** Set the start, review, and decision dates, along with fixed limits for staff time, supplier spend, and operational capacity.
- **Evidence:** Record correctly closed cases, human routing touches, exception and repair effort, elapsed time, quality failures, control failures, and total cost. Preserve failed and rejected attempts rather than counting only successful runs.
- **Acceptance and reversal:** Declare local conditions for continuing, changing, or stopping the trial, plus the process for returning cases to the previous workflow.
- **Decision:** Require the named business owner to sign the evidence and recommend whether to stop, extend, redesign the workflow, or examine a specific coordination layer.

No Swedish or Norwegian production case reviewed for this article establishes a transferable numerical threshold for safely removing a management layer because of agents. That gap should remain visible. Each company has to set its own acceptance and reversal conditions, expose them before the trial, and resist turning a local result into a general law.

<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>A flatter structure is defensible only when a bounded trial shows that complete cases close correctly with less human routing and repair, at an acceptable total cost and control level under predeclared local conditions.</p>
</div>

## Replace relay work with an authority map

When coordination shrinks, authority does not vanish. It needs a more explicit home. Each complete workflow should have a domain owner responsible for its outcome, a map of the decisions inside it, and a clear division between what an agent may interpret or propose and what the company’s ordinary rules-based software will permit.

Consider the late-payment case. An agent may gather account history, classify the customer’s response, and propose a payment plan. Before any offer is sent, ordinary software should check the user or system identity, the current case version, the permitted terms, required evidence, and the domain owner’s policy. If the proposal is based on stale data, repeats an earlier action, or exceeds the allowed terms, the system rejects the transition, leaves the case in its current **state**, meaning its authoritative recorded position, and routes the exception to a named person.

This separation keeps free-form model reasoning away from the final enforcement of permissions. It also limits a basic input risk: an email or attachment may contain text that tries to redirect the model. External content should be treated as untrusted data. It cannot grant the agent a new permission, alter policy, or decide which instruction takes priority.

The purpose of the execution record is to make every consequential attempt traceable and safe to retry. It should capture the case and version, actor, required preconditions, permitted effect, unique action key, execution result, next state, and accountable owner. An **audit trail** is this durable history of who or what proposed, approved, attempted, rejected, and completed each transition.

The retry question needs special care. [RFC 9110](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9110.html#section-9.2.2), the 2022 internet standard that defines the meaning of HTTP web requests, calls an operation **idempotent** when repeating it has the same intended effect as performing it once. A unique action key can help prevent accidental duplicates, but it cannot prove that an external payment, message, or supplier action happened exactly once. The workflow still needs reconciliation against the receiving system and, where an external effect cannot be undone, a named corrective action. **Rollback** can restore an earlier internal state; it cannot unsend a message or erase money already transferred.

Visible authority also extends to suppliers. Contracts should let the company test log export, data-location evidence, notice of model or service changes, failure notification, continuity arrangements, and termination assistance. A procurement or supplier owner must verify those capabilities during the trial, not discover their absence during an incident or exit.

<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>The replacement for management relay work is explicit domain ownership backed by software-enforced permissions and case state, named exception and stop owners, a complete audit trail, tested recovery, and supplier terms the company can exercise.</p>
</div>

## Redesign careers with the structure

The old pyramid carried more than approvals. It also supplied coaching, status, pay progression, access to senior decisions, and a familiar path for ambition. If coordination-heavy management work declines, removing a box from the chart without replacing those functions will weaken the organization even when the workflow performs well.

Leaders should separate the work inside affected roles. Routine status collection and routing may genuinely disappear. Judgment, coaching, conflict resolution, capability building, and accountability may become more valuable, but they will not automatically form a coherent job. Some roles will expand, some will be redesigned, and some may end. A responsible operating model says this before the trial begins.

The chief people officer or another named people lead should own this workstream and start employee participation while the trial scope is still open to change. The evidence record should include the roles affected, skills required, paid learning time, redeployment paths, revised pay and progression criteria, and support for people who leave. New career ladders can reward domain mastery, workflow ownership, control judgment, and coaching without forcing everyone through a widening management pyramid.

This is not separate from the business case. A workflow that reduces routing cost by pushing unpaid repair onto employees, leaving managers with impossible spans, or removing credible progression has not proved a better architecture. It has moved the cost somewhere less visible.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Flattening removes career and coaching infrastructure as well as coordination. The trial therefore needs an owned, funded people transition with early participation, credible progression, learning, redeployment, and honest support for roles that may end.</p>
</div>

## Make the organization decision last

The CIO should ask the board to authorize a controlled operating-model trial, not a flatter organization in advance. At the review date, the decision should turn on a short chain of evidence: domain teams demonstrated the required technical judgment; complete cases improved against a frozen baseline; recurring routing and repair genuinely declined; consequential actions remained within explicit permissions; exceptions, stops, and recovery worked under test; supplier controls were exercised; and the workforce commitments were funded and owned.

If that evidence holds, leadership can redesign the specific coordination layer whose work has changed and keep measuring the result. If it does not, the honest choices are to change the workflow, narrow the agent’s authority, strengthen the controls, or stop. Preserving the layer is not a failure when the work still requires it.

AI agents may allow a company to become materially flatter and more bottom-up because domain experts can act with less relay overhead. Yet the org chart should follow the operating evidence, not lead it. The leadership task is to show where the coordination went, where authority now lives, how failure is contained, and what the company will do for the people carrying the transition.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Authorize a bounded workflow redesign first. Change a specific layer only after the evidence, authority model, recovery controls, supplier obligations, and workforce transition can be defended together.</p>
</div>
