Two claims define the architecture of an AI-first company. First, every business role is acquiring a technical dimension. Second, organizations can push more ownership toward the people closest to the work—and may need fewer coordination layers—when agents take on meaningful execution and handoffs.

In a [June 2026 recorded interview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8atlQX8HTwQ&t=342s), [Arvind KC](https://openai.com/index/arvind-kc-chief-people-officer/), Chief People Officer at OpenAI, described every future role as “fundamentally a technical role” applied to a particular domain. OpenAI is a frontier AI research and product company, so its experience is an unusually technical starting point rather than a transferable enterprise blueprint.

Here, an AI agent means software built around a model that pursues a defined goal through approved tools and steps. Bottom-up ownership means that people near the work can initiate and decide within visible boundaries. The two ideas connect: when agents absorb information assembly, routine routing, and known exceptions, domain teams can own more of an outcome from beginning to end.

That possibility does not make flatter structures inevitable. An AI-first company distributes technical judgment and may become materially flatter, but structural change becomes defensible only after complete-workflow evidence, explicit decision rights, controlled complexity, and a credible workforce transition.

## Technical judgment moves into the business

Technical judgment is the ability to decide what an AI system may do in a business domain, what evidence it needs, where its capability boundary lies, and when a person must take over. Coding remains role-dependent. The shared requirement is sound judgment about capability, reliability, and escalation.

Sales needs that judgment when an agent extracts contract terms but a concession exceeds commercial authority. Finance needs it when a disputed reconciliation touches an accounting treatment or risk tolerance. Marketing needs it when fluent copy rests on weak source material. Support needs it when a routine request contains vulnerability or relationship consequences. Operations needs it when a proposed action conflicts with the current status of a case.

Consider a hypothetical service-recovery case after equipment reaches a business customer late. An agent can gather the order, correspondence, delivery record, and policy, then propose a response and account credit. The commercial owner still decides what evidence is sufficient, which exception is legitimate, and whether finance or legal approval is required. The technology becomes useful because domain judgment shapes its authority.

Capability is uneven. In a [2026 *Organization Science* experiment](https://doi.org/10.1287/orsc.2025.21838), Fabrizio Dell’Acqua and co-authors tested 758 consultants using OpenAI’s GPT-4 language model on tasks inside and outside a measured capability frontier—the boundary between work the system handled well and badly. On the selected outside-frontier task, AI users were 19% less likely to reach a correct solution. Its relevance here is narrow but important: confident output can cross the boundary before the user notices.

Article 4 of the [EU AI Act](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:02024R1689-20260727), the European Union’s AI regulation adopted in 2024, similarly treats AI literacy as contextual. Organizations covered by it must consider people’s knowledge, experience, training, and use context. A generic prompt course cannot substitute for competence inside the domain.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Technical judgment belongs with the people who understand the business consequence, while central specialists provide shared platforms, testing, security, and support.</p></div>

## The work between tasks is the architecture

Benjamin Simkin, a business author, calls the failure to redesign surrounding work the Retrofit Trap in his 2026 book [*The AI First Company: Engineering Your Business to Thrive in the AI Economy*](https://www.amazon.co.uk/dp/B0H45WZRX5). The term describes a company that gets faster tasks while keeping the same structural problems. It supplies useful vocabulary; prevalence has to be established elsewhere.

Return to the late delivery. Generating a response is one activity. The full outcome may still require someone to verify entitlement, route an exception, secure approval, issue the credit, update customer and finance records, monitor the response, and repair any disputed result. If those dependencies remain manual, a faster draft has improved a task without changing the operating model.

In a [1994 interdisciplinary review](https://doi.org/10.1145/174666.174668), researchers Thomas Malone and Kevin Crowston defined coordination as managing dependencies between activities. Organization-design scholar Jay Galbraith’s [1974 information-processing account](https://doi.org/10.1287/inte.4.3.28) explained why hierarchy becomes involved when existing rules cannot resolve an exception. Agents may absorb some information gathering, status checking, assignment, and known-exception handling because these are dependency-management activities.

Management also includes conflicting priorities, resource allocation, coaching, accountability, and novel judgment. The useful move is therefore to decompose the work rather than label an entire role as coordination.

For this decision, the stronger unit of evidence is a correctly closed case: a complete outcome that meets declared quality and policy conditions, updates the official business record, counts every human intervention and repair, and leaves no concealed cleanup. That definition is a proposed management test, not an industry benchmark.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>An AI-first operating model changes the dependency chain from trigger to verified outcome; accelerating one output leaves the Retrofit Trap intact.</p></div>

## Make the hierarchy claim rejectable

A serious claim about flattening must be capable of failing. Before changing roles, run a bounded trial across complete workflows and declare the evidence conditions in advance.

For the service-recovery example, the trial should include ordinary cases alongside disputed, stale-data, duplicate, unauthorized, and supplier-failure cases. Once observation begins, the team cannot revise the case definition merely to exclude an inconvenient result. Compare the agent-supported design with both the current process and the strongest conventional alternative, such as workflow software and predefined business rules.

Measure correctly closed cases by case class, elapsed time, human routing and approval touches, repair effort, reopening, outcome quality, control failures, and total operating cost. Include model fees, integration, monitoring, review, exception work, and supplier charges. Record the human judgment that remains and any work transferred into frontline chasing or informal supervision.

If routing becomes frontline chasing, repair becomes a quality team’s burden, or decisions disappear into a supplier dashboard, coordination has changed location without reducing. The trial should therefore have an evidence owner who is independent enough to challenge the workflow owner’s conclusion.

No validated Swedish or Norwegian production case establishes a numerical threshold for safely removing a management layer. The CIO must set local conditions before the trial and disclose that they are company choices. Afterward, map each responsibility: which moved into the domain, which became enforceable in software, which remains human, and which genuinely ceased. The organization chart should reflect that map rather than anticipate it.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Flattening becomes defensible only when a predeclared trial shows that net coordination declined across complete cases without hidden labor, weaker controls, or unacceptable cost.</p></div>

## Build hard boundaries around model interpretation

Wider ownership requires clearer authority. Economists Philippe Aghion and Jean Tirole distinguished [formal authority from real authority](https://doi.org/10.1086/262063) in 1997: the assigned right to decide can differ from effective control over what happens. A domain team may look empowered while a central platform, inaccessible model configuration, approval rule, or vendor still holds real authority.

A defensible design has four parts. Domain experts define the acceptable outcome and the cases that require judgment. The agent interprets messy input and proposes a next move. Ordinary software checks identity, permission, limits, and the current case state before anything consequential happens. A named human owns exceptions, override, stop, restart, and accountability.

Every consequential action needs an audit trail, and a named human needs stop authority: both the right and the practical mechanism to halt new actions. An audit trail records who or what acted, when, from which state, with what outcome. The authoritative state is the current approved case version held in company systems, rather than a version remembered by the model.

Retries need equal discipline. The [Internet Engineering Task Force’s 2022 HTTP standard](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9110.html#section-9.2.2) defines idempotency as repeated identical requests having the same intended server effect as one request. At business level, the consequence is simple: retrying a credit must never create a second commitment. Give each intended action a stable identity, check permission and state, and store or reconcile the outcome.

If an external credit succeeds while an internal update fails, the case should enter a pending reconciliation state instead of blindly repeating the action. A rollback returns an internal configuration or workflow to a safe version. A compensating action is a new corrective step for an external effect that cannot simply be erased.

Company controls do not settle legal classification. The EU AI Act’s high-risk provisions apply only where its legal definition is met. The [GDPR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32016R0679), the EU personal-data regulation applicable since 2018, may introduce further duties when personal data or solely automated significant decisions are involved. Applicability depends on the workflow and jurisdiction.

The company should also retain a supplier exit: a tested path to change provider or enter a manual mode without losing business state, policy, records, or stop control. Agents earn their place where interpretation of unstructured information adds value. Predictable permissions, limits, and state transitions belong in ordinary software.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Push interpretation and initiative toward the domain, while company-controlled systems enforce state and permission and named people retain genuine override, stop, and recovery authority.</p></div>

## Flattening rewrites the employment bargain

A management layer is also a career system. It carries coaching, visibility, pay progression, status, peer learning, and preparation for larger decisions. Remove the reporting relationship without rebuilding those functions and the organization may become flatter while its capacity to grow people becomes weaker.

Historical evidence makes the point without proving an AI outcome. Economists Raghuram Rajan and Julie Wulf studied [more than 300 large US firms between 1986 and 1998](https://www.nber.org/papers/w9633). Flatter structures coincided with wider executive spans and changes in the authority, pay, and incentives of divisional managers. The period predates generative AI. Its lesson is that delayering changes the employment bargain, not merely the diagram.

The [International Labour Organization](https://doi.org/10.54394/HETP0387), the United Nations agency for labor standards and research, reached another useful boundary in 2025: exposure to generative-AI tasks does not imply immediate automation of an entire occupation. Leaders should examine activities. Routine status collection, forwarding, and report assembly may shrink, while coaching, conflict resolution, accountability, and difficult trade-offs remain.

Some people in coordination-heavy roles may move into domain leadership, service ownership, assurance, or expert positions. That possibility is not a promise that every transition will be painless or that every role will survive unchanged. The company needs explicit selection and redeployment rules, funded learning time, expert levels with credible pay, named coaching responsibility, and a route to challenge decisions.

Employee participation belongs inside the operating design. Hold one checkpoint before a trial changes how work is performed and another before evidence is used to change roles, pay, or reporting lines. Social dialogue means structured discussion with employees and their representatives, supported by access to the evidence and practical authority to pause a decision. In Sweden, [Section 11 of the Co-Determination Act](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-1976580-om-medbestammande-i-arbetslivet_sfs-1976-580/) requires negotiation before qualifying significant changes in activities or working and employment conditions. National law, collective agreements, and local applicability need their own review elsewhere in the Nordics.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>A credible AI-first redesign funds new paths for learning, progression, pay, coaching, participation, and redeployment before the old hierarchy is taken away.</p></div>

## Bring the board a decision record, not a prophecy

Before any proposal to remove or recast a layer, the board should receive one concise decision record. It should answer six questions:

- **Workflow and hypothesis:** What complete outcome is being tested, and which coordination responsibilities might move or cease?
- **Ownership:** Who owns the domain outcome, permanent service, controls, independent evidence, and executive decision?
- **Evidence contract:** Which case classes, comparators, period, measures, and company-set conditions were declared before observation?
- **Authority and recovery:** What may the agent propose, what does ordinary software enforce, who can stop and restart, and how are uncertain or failed commitments reconciled?
- **People commitments:** Which activities change, how will participation work, and what are the funded provisions for learning, selection, redeployment, progression, coaching, and pay?
- **Decision boundary:** Will the workflow expand, narrow, enter normal operations, or stop—and will any structural change return as a separate decision?

The first leadership decision is to authorize a reversible trial whose adverse results remain visible. The later decision is whether the evidence justifies changing the organization. Keep them separate.

If the trial produces faster outputs but the same dependencies, retain the structure and redesign the workflow. If complete cases close reliably with fewer human handoffs, acceptable cost, enforceable authority, tested recovery, and a credible transition, then a flatter model may be defensible.

An AI-first company can distribute technical judgment and place more ownership at the edges. It earns that shape by proving where coordination went, preserving human authority over consequential judgment, and committing to the people who must build their careers inside the new architecture.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Authorize a reversible operating trial first; change the organization only when complete-workflow evidence, visible authority, recoverable controls, and funded people commitments survive scrutiny.</p></div>
