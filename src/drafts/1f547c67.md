Two claims define the architecture of an AI-first company. First, every business role is acquiring a technical dimension. Second, when agents absorb meaningful execution and coordination, more decisions and ideas can originate near the work—bottom-up ownership—and the organization can become flatter.

[Arvind KC](https://openai.com/index/arvind-kc-chief-people-officer/), appointed Chief People Officer of OpenAI in February 2026, put the first claim sharply in a [June 2026 interview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8atlQX8HTwQ&t=342s) with DECODE, a business-interview channel. He described every future role as “fundamentally a technical role” applied to a particular domain. OpenAI is an AI research and product company, so its experience is not a representative enterprise sample. The observation still matters because it places technical capability inside each business function, rather than in a translation queue between “the business” and an AI team.

An AI agent, here, means software built around a model that pursues a defined goal through approved tools and steps. Agents can gather context, propose actions, and perform bounded work. But the two claims carry different burdens of proof. Distributed technical judgment is a capability to build now. A flatter structure is an operating hypothesis. Reporting lines should change only after complete-workflow evidence shows that coordination has genuinely left the system, authority remains visible, failures can be stopped and recovered, and the people transition is credible.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Technical judgment should spread before the hierarchy shrinks; agents must earn any structural change through operating evidence.</p>
</div>

## Technical judgment moves to the edge

Technical judgment is not coding. It is the ability to decide what an AI system may do inside a business domain, what evidence it needs, where its capability ends, and when a person must take over.

In sales, that means distinguishing a routine proposal from a binding concession. Finance must separate reconciliation from a disputed accounting treatment or change in risk tolerance. Marketing must judge whether the sources and quality criteria behind AI-generated content are adequate. Support must recognize ambiguity, vulnerability, and relationship consequences. Operations must define valid case states, failure conditions, and recovery paths.

Consider a hypothetical supplier-onboarding workflow. An agent may extract company details, compare submitted documents with policy, and prepare a recommendation. Procurement still decides whether the commercial case is sound. Cybersecurity owns exceptions involving data access. Finance owns bank-detail controls. The relationship owner handles trade-offs that no policy table can settle. Technical judgment belongs in all four roles because each role defines a different boundary around the same system.

That boundary matters because AI capability is uneven. In a [2026 *Organization Science* experiment](https://doi.org/10.1287/orsc.2025.21838) involving 758 consultants and GPT-4, an OpenAI language model, participants using AI were 19% less likely to reach the correct answer on the selected task outside the model’s measured capability frontier—the irregular boundary between work it could and could not handle reliably. The European Union’s [AI Act](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:02024R1689-20260727), adopted in 2024, similarly makes AI literacy contextual to people’s knowledge, experience, training, and use setting. Neither point implies that everyone should become an engineer.

Bottom-up ownership therefore means that domain teams can identify valuable uses, define failure conditions, and challenge unsuitable output without waiting for a central AI group to interpret their work. Shared technology and control teams remain necessary, but they no longer own every idea or business decision.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Every function needs enough technical judgment to define what AI may do, recognize where it fails, and retain the decisions that require domain authority.</p>
</div>

## The org chart is a map of unresolved dependencies

Benjamin Simkin, a business author and coach, named a common design failure in his 2026 book [*The AI First Company: Engineering Your Business to Thrive in the AI Economy*](https://www.amazon.co.uk/dp/B0H45WZRX5). The Retrofit Trap is what happens when a company accelerates an isolated task while preserving the old routing, approvals, and repair work around it. The term identifies the failure; it does not establish how prevalent it is.

A supplier packet may be summarized in seconds while employees still chase missing tax documents, request security review, wait for financial approval, create the vendor record, resolve conflicting data, and repair an incorrect setup. The generated recommendation is faster. The operating model is unchanged.

Organization researchers Thomas W. Malone and Kevin Crowston defined [coordination](https://doi.org/10.1145/174666.174668) in 1994 as managing dependencies between activities. Organization-design scholar Jay Galbraith’s [1974 information-processing account](https://doi.org/10.1287/inte.4.3.28) explained why hierarchy is used when established rules cannot resolve an exception. Together, those ideas reveal part of what a hierarchy does: it gathers information, routes approvals, reconciles dependencies, and sends unusual cases toward sufficient authority.

Management is not reducible to routing. Managers also resolve conflicting goals, allocate resources, coach people, interpret weak signals, and accept accountability. The useful move is to decompose the role. Agents may absorb status collection, information assembly, routine routing, and known exceptions. Human authority remains necessary for novel trade-offs, changes in risk appetite, conflict, employment decisions, and responsibility for the outcome.

A flatter structure becomes plausible only when the first group of responsibilities shrinks without resurfacing as frontline cleanup, informal supervision, security review, or supplier dependence.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>An agent has not removed a layer when the same coordination reappears as approval chasing, repair work, hidden oversight, or vendor control.</p>
</div>

## Count closed outcomes, not generated work

The evidence unit cannot be the AI output. It must be the complete workflow: the path from a triggering request through decisions, exceptions, external effects, official records, and closure.

A correctly closed case is a proposed management unit for that evidence. It is a complete outcome that meets declared quality and policy conditions, updates the authoritative business record, counts every human intervention and repair, and leaves no hidden cleanup behind. A fast recommendation attached to an unresolved supplier record is not correctly closed.

The trial should measure elapsed time from trigger to closure; human routing touches, meaning people forwarding or chasing work; approval and repair effort; reopened cases; outcome quality; policy, security, and privacy failures; total operating cost; and the human judgment still required. New monitoring or exception work counts against the result. So does work transferred to local teams without resources or recognition.

The comparison must include both the current process and the best available non-agent workflow design selected before results are observed. Rule-based workflow software may handle structured routing and policy checks more cheaply and reliably than an agent. The agent earns a place only where interpreting unstructured or changing information adds value.

Case classes and acceptance conditions should also be declared in advance, including ordinary, missing-data, duplicate, unauthorized, disputed, and supplier-failure cases. There is no validated Swedish or Norwegian production benchmark in the available evidence that establishes a numerical threshold for safely removing a management layer. Each company must set and defend its own conditions without presenting them as an industry standard.

Finally, map every relevant responsibility as removed, transferred, or retained. Structural change is justified by that responsibility map, not by a demonstration that produces fluent output.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Judge agents by correctly closed workflows against preselected alternatives, counting hidden labor, failures, cost, and residual judgment.</p>
</div>

## Autonomy needs a visible constitution

When reporting layers shrink, authority must become easier to inspect. Economists Philippe Aghion and Jean Tirole distinguished [formal authority from real authority](https://doi.org/10.1086/262063) in 1997: the assigned right to decide can differ from effective control over what happens. A domain team may look empowered while a supplier configuration, inaccessible data owner, or concealed approval rule still decides for it.

Domain-mapped ownership prevents that ambiguity by assigning outcomes and decisions to the expertise that can judge them. Decision rights must state who may propose, approve, reject, override, stop, and restart. The operating boundary is then straightforward:

1. The domain owner defines the outcome, acceptable case classes, limits, and escalation rules.
2. The agent interprets unstructured information and proposes a next step.
3. Company-controlled software checks identity, permission, current case state, policy limits, duplicate protection, and release conditions.
4. A named person owns exceptions, overrides, stop decisions, restart, and accountability.

The current case state must be authoritative: the approved status and version held in the company’s official record. Rule-based software, which applies predefined checks rather than open-ended model reasoning, should enforce that state and the permissions around it. An override must be a practical ability to reject or reverse an action, not a ceremonial approval label.

Every consequential action needs an audit trail, and a named human needs stop authority: both the right and the practical mechanism to halt new actions.

An audit trail records who or what acted, the relevant state, the time, the outcome, and the recovery path. Those records matter when a supplier-creation request times out after the vendor record has already been made. A blind retry could create a duplicate. [RFC 9110](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9110.html#section-9.2.2), the Internet Engineering Task Force’s 2022 HTTP standard, defines idempotency as repeated identical requests having the same intended effect as one. A business workflow needs more: a stable action identity, a state check, a stored result, and a pending state when the outcome is uncertain. Reconcile that uncertainty before retrying.

A rollback returns an internal workflow or configuration to an earlier safe version. If an external commitment cannot simply be erased, a compensating action is a new action that corrects its effect. Both require named owners and testing before release.

Internal controls must also connect to applicable law. The AI Act’s high-risk oversight provisions apply only when a system meets the legal definition, while the [General Data Protection Regulation](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32016R0679), the EU privacy law applicable since 2018, may impose duties where personal data or solely automated significant decisions are involved. A company risk label does not replace legal classification. Nor should a supplier hold the only copy of business state, policy, or audit records. Supplier exit means the tested ability to change provider or operate manually without losing control of the service.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Agents may interpret and propose, but company-controlled systems and named humans must enforce permission, state, audit, recovery, and stop authority.</p>
</div>

## The career system cannot be collateral damage

The old pyramid is also a career system. Its layers carry pay progression, coaching, professional visibility, status, and preparation for larger decisions. Removing a reporting layer without replacing those functions creates a flatter chart and a narrower future.

Coordination-heavy roles should therefore be decomposed before they are redesigned. Routine forwarding, status gathering, and repair may shrink. Coaching, conflict resolution, resource judgment, institutional memory, and accountability still need owners. Some people can move into deeper domain roles, permanent service ownership, risk and assurance work, or expert leadership without headcount responsibility. Those paths need explicit levels, pay ranges, learning time, and promotion criteria.

The [International Labour Organization](https://doi.org/10.54394/HETP0387), the United Nations agency for labor standards and research, made an important distinction in its 2025 analysis: exposure to generative-AI tasks does not equal the disappearance of an occupation, and transformation is more likely because most occupations retain tasks requiring human input. A workforce plan should therefore identify which activities shrink, which judgment grows, and what each affected person can realistically move toward.

Participation must happen twice: before a trial changes how work is performed, and again before evidence is used to change roles, pay, or reporting lines. Social dialogue means structured discussion among the employer, workers, and their representatives. In Sweden, the [Co-Determination in the Workplace Act of 1976](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-1976580-om-medbestammande-i-arbetslivet_sfs-1976-580/) requires negotiation before qualifying significant changes where its conditions apply. Collective agreements and local law require case-specific review elsewhere.

A credible transition is concrete: funded learning time, named target roles, transparent selection rules, pay consequences, coaching responsibility, redeployment terms, and a point at which employee representatives can pause the organizational decision if agreed conditions have not been met. “Reskilling” without those commitments is not a plan.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Flattening changes careers as well as workflows, so progression, pay, coaching, learning, participation, and fair transition must be designed before reporting lines move.</p>
</div>

## Change the org chart last

A board-defensible proposal can fit on one page, but it cannot rest on one metric. The decision record should specify:

- the complete workflow and predeclared ordinary and adverse case classes;
- the domain, technology, permanent service, control, and executive owners;
- an evidence owner who is not accountable for delivering the trial result and can preserve adverse findings and reject the sponsor’s conclusion;
- the current process and the best non-agent workflow design chosen as comparators before observation;
- the company-set evidence period, acceptance conditions, and measures for closure, quality, coordination, cost, controls, and residual judgment;
- permissions, stop and restart authority, recovery paths, audit requirements, supplier dependencies, and supplier exit; and
- workforce checkpoints before work design changes and before any later decision about roles, pay, or reporting lines.

Treat the decision as three gates. Gate one authorizes a bounded, reversible trial whose adverse results remain visible. Gate two accepts the workflow into normal operations only when correctly closed cases, controls, recovery, permanent ownership, and workforce commitments hold. Gate three considers structural change only when the responsibility map shows that coordination has durably disappeared or moved to an explicitly owned place.

A failed trial may still teach the company where conventional automation is sufficient, where human judgment is essential, or where the process itself needs repair. That is a legitimate result. What is not legitimate is removing a layer because an agent completed an impressive fragment of work.

The architecture of an AI-first company is not the flat chart. It is technical judgment distributed across the business, authority made visible, complexity held inside enforceable boundaries, and people given a credible route through the change. Only when that architecture survives ordinary work and adverse cases does a flatter chart describe reality instead of ambition.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Authorize the trial first, accept the service second, and consider structural change last—each gate backed by named owners, adverse evidence, recoverable controls, and workforce commitments.</p>
</div>
