An AI-first company starts with two linked claims. Every business role gains a technical dimension: people need enough judgment to understand what AI can do reliably, where it fails, and when a person must decide. As that judgment spreads, ownership can move closer to the work and the organization can become flatter.

That second claim needs a condition. AI agents, software systems that interpret context and act through approved tools, may absorb routine execution and coordination. A new org chart becomes defensible only after they have removed that work across a complete workflow. Otherwise the company has simply hidden its hierarchy inside approvals, repair queues, and software that nobody clearly owns.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
  <span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
  <p>Distributed technical judgment can support a flatter company, but the hierarchy should change only after operating evidence proves that coordination work has gone and accountable authority remains visible.</p>
</div>

## Technical judgment moves to the edge

In February 2026, OpenAI, an AI research and deployment company, [appointed Arvind KC as its chief people officer](https://openai.com/index/arvind-kc-chief-people-officer/). In a [public DECODE interview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8atlQX8HTwQ), KC said that “every role as we go into the future is fundamentally a technical role,” then described that role as technical capability “applied to a particular domain.”

The useful business interpretation is not that everyone must code. Technical judgment means that a salesperson can question an AI-generated account recommendation, a finance specialist can recognize when an automated reconciliation is uncertain, and a support lead can decide which customer cases require human discretion. Domain knowledge remains central because reliability depends on the decision, the available data, and the cost of being wrong.

KC's statement supports the first claim. Bottom-up ownership remains an organizational hypothesis rather than a reported OpenAI outcome: when domain teams can shape AI-supported work directly, initiative can begin closer to customers and operations. That only counts as ownership when those teams also receive clear boundaries, resources, and decision rights.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
  <span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
  <p>Every function needs enough technical judgment to challenge AI output and place human judgment deliberately. Coding is optional for many roles; accountable domain reasoning is not.</p>
</div>

## Prove that coordination has disappeared

Author Benjamin Simkin's 2026 business book *The AI First Company: Engineering Your Business to Thrive in the AI Economy* gives AI added inside an unchanged organization a useful name in its [published product description](https://www.amazon.co.uk/dp/B0H45WZRX5): the Retrofit Trap. The label matters because making one task faster can leave the operating model untouched.

Consider supplier onboarding. An agent might read submitted documents, check required fields, and draft a risk summary in seconds. If employees must still copy data between systems, chase three approvals, resolve duplicate supplier records, and repair uncertain decisions, the surrounding coordination remains. The visible AI step is faster while the complete case may be no cheaper, safer, or easier to run.

Traditional hierarchy does more than supervise people. It collects information, carries approvals, translates priorities, and routes exceptions. An agent-supported workflow can take over status gathering, policy-based routing, reminders, and routine record updates. Human authority remains necessary for changing policy, resolving genuinely novel trade-offs, handling consequential exceptions, and taking responsibility for people.

I would evaluate this change through a **correctly closed case**: one that reaches the intended business outcome, follows policy, leaves official records consistent, and requires no concealed human repair. The evidence should cover the full mix of ordinary and exceptional cases, then show quality, control failures, total cost, human routing touches, repair effort, and the judgment still left with people. There is no transferable Swedish or Norwegian production benchmark in the available evidence that tells a CIO when removing a management layer is safe. The decision threshold therefore belongs in the company's own risk and evidence contract, not in a borrowed productivity claim.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
  <span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
  <p>Task acceleration is not operating-model change. Flattening becomes credible only when representative cases close correctly across the entire workflow with less routing, repair, and coordination.</p>
</div>

## Make authority explicit in the machinery

Removing a coordination layer creates a replacement problem: the company still needs ownership, limits, and a reliable record of what happened. Start by mapping each business domain, meaning a bounded area such as supplier onboarding, to an accountable process owner, a policy owner, a technology owner, and named owners for exceptions and evidence.

Within that domain, the agent may interpret documents and propose an action. Ordinary software should enforce identity, permissions, financial or risk limits, and the **authoritative state**, the official record of where the case currently stands. A useful path is request received, action proposed, approval captured where required, action committed, and outcome reconciled. **Reconciliation** means checking that the external action and the official record agree. A stale proposal, an over-limit action, or a duplicate command should be rejected or quarantined without changing that record.

Retries need the same discipline. **Idempotency** means that repeating the same request has the same intended effect as sending it once. The Internet Engineering Task Force, the standards organization for core Internet protocols, defines that property in its [2022 standard for HTTP](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9110.html#name-idempotent-methods), the request-and-response protocol used by web services. In business terms, a unique action marker can block an accidental duplicate, while reconciliation establishes whether an uncertain external action already happened before anyone retries it. These are established workflow and ledger controls applied to agents, rather than decisions left to free-form model output.

The human control path must be equally concrete. **Stop authority** names the person who can pause the workflow; **rollback** is the tested route back to the last safe state when reversal is possible. Overrides need an owner, a reason, and an audit trail. This architecture allows initiative at the edge without turning autonomy into concealed authority.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
  <span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
  <p>Agents can interpret and propose, while governed software holds permissions, limits, and official state. Named people retain exception, stop, override, and rollback authority through an auditable control path.</p>
</div>

## Redesign the people system at the same time

When routine routing shrinks, coordination-heavy roles will change. Some managers can spend more time on coaching, domain stewardship, difficult exceptions, and capability building. Other positions may narrow or disappear. A responsible plan has to state both possibilities before structural savings are booked.

The old pyramid is also a career system. It connects progression, pay, learning, status, and ambition to larger teams and higher layers. If the company removes those layers without creating expert paths and broader domain roles, it can give people more accountability while taking away visible routes to advancement.

For every affected role, leadership should identify the work being removed, the judgment that remains, the skills people need next, and the funded path into a future role. That path includes learning time, pay consequences, progression criteria, redeployment options, and honest treatment when no suitable role exists. In a Nordic organization, the people lead and employee representatives should join the design early enough to influence it, rather than receiving a finished chart after the operating decision.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
  <span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
  <p>A flatter structure changes careers as well as workflows. Coaching, expert progression, learning, pay, redeployment, and employee participation must be designed and funded before the old pyramid is removed.</p>
</div>

## Put the decision contract before the org chart

The CIO should begin with one complete workflow and a named forum, not a company-wide promise. The business process owner prepares the case boundary and baseline. A separate evidence owner verifies the case mix, human effort, failures, and total cost. The technology and risk owners document permissions, state, dependencies, stop authority, and reversal. The people lead and relevant employee representatives own the role and transition commitments.

Set the review rhythm before the trial begins, aligned to the workflow's real operating cycle. At each review, the forum should use one signed record with three decision areas:

- **Operating evidence:** representative cases, correctly closed outcomes, quality and control failures, routing touches, repair effort, residual judgment, and total cost.
- **Authority and resilience:** named decision rights, exception routes, system limits, audit records, dependencies, stop conditions, and tested rollback responsibilities.
- **People commitments:** affected roles, future work, learning capacity, progression and pay consequences, redeployment, participation, and transition funding.

The forum records one of three outcomes: continue the trial, stop and revert, or submit a structural change for executive approval. The CIO is accountable for that decision. If the workflow must be reversed, the process owner restores the operating fallback and the technology owner restores the last safe system state; those responsibilities should be assigned before launch.

This record cannot prove that every AI-first company should become flat. It can show where coordination has genuinely disappeared, where human judgment still carries value, and whether the organization is ready to honor the people consequence. Change the org chart only there. That is the point at which a provocative architecture becomes a defensible leadership decision.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
  <span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
  <p>Require a signed decision record with verified workflow evidence, visible control ownership, and funded people commitments. Continue, stop, or redesign the hierarchy only through that forum, with accountable owners for the decision and its reversal.</p>
</div>
