An AI-first company begins with two linked claims. Technical judgment spreads into every business role, and the organization can become flatter as AI agents absorb some of the execution and coordination that hierarchy used to carry. The first claim is becoming easier to defend. The second still has to be earned.

Here, an AI agent means a software system that uses context and approved tools to carry work across several steps, consistent with [OpenAI's practical guide to agents](https://cdn.openai.com/business-guides-and-resources/a-practical-guide-to-building-agents.pdf). OpenAI is the company behind ChatGPT, its AI chatbot. In a governed company, an agent works inside permissions it did not create and cannot expand on its own.

That boundary matters because a flatter company is not simply an existing company with fewer managers. It is an operating model in which people closer to a business problem can use technical judgment, routine cases move with less human routing, and consequential authority remains visible. The operating evidence must come before the reorganization.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>AI agents make a flatter operating model plausible only when distributed technical judgment, reduced coordination work, and visible human authority develop together.</p>
</div>

## Technical judgment becomes part of every function

Arvind KC, chief people officer at OpenAI, put the first claim plainly in an [interview published by the DECODE YouTube channel on 1 June 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8atlQX8HTwQ): “Every role as we go into the future is fundamentally a technical role.” He immediately placed that role inside a domain, meaning a particular field of business knowledge. The point is not that every employee becomes a software engineer.

Technical judgment is the ability to reason about where an AI system can be trusted, what information it needs, how failure becomes visible, and when a person must decide. A salesperson needs it when an agent prepares a proposal from customer records. Finance needs it when a model explains a variance. Operations, marketing, and support need it whenever an AI-produced recommendation could change a customer outcome or a company record.

Once this judgment exists inside the functions, bottom-up ownership becomes practical. A domain team can define the problem, inspect failed cases, and propose a better workflow without sending every detail through a central translation layer. Technology leaders still set common platforms and control boundaries, but the edge of the organization can own more of the change. From the center, that may look untidy; in practice, it can be disciplined local ownership.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Technical judgment means knowing how AI behaves inside a business domain, not knowing how to code. It lets domain teams own more improvement while central leaders retain shared controls.</p>
</div>

## The workflow has to change, not just the task

Author Benjamin Simkin uses the term “Retrofit Trap” in his 2026 book [*The AI First Company: Engineering Your Business to Thrive in the AI Economy*](https://www.amazon.co.uk/dp/B0H45WZRX5). The term describes adding AI to an old structure without rebuilding the structure around what the technology can do. It gives a name to a common operating mistake: making one activity faster while leaving the surrounding queues untouched.

Consider a disputed customer invoice. An agent might read the contract, invoice history, and correspondence, then identify the likely cause and draft a response. But the workflow has not changed if finance must re-enter the finding, an account manager still carries it between approval meetings, and operations later repairs inconsistent records. The analysis became faster while the organization kept paying for routing, waiting, and repair.

This is where hierarchy enters the mechanism. A management layer often does valuable work, but part of that work is an information system: it gathers status, resolves routine ambiguity, routes approvals, and pushes exceptions toward someone with authority. An agent-supported workflow may absorb the gathering, comparison, follow-up, and routine routing. It cannot decide who is allowed to waive policy, accept financial exposure, or stop the service.

The useful unit of evidence is therefore a correctly closed case. For the invoice dispute, closure means the customer received the right outcome, the approved adjustment reached the finance system once, related records agree, required approvals are recorded, and any exception has a named owner. A convincing answer from the agent is only one event inside that case.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>The Retrofit Trap accelerates an isolated activity while preserving the queues around it. Organizational redesign becomes relevant only when complete cases close correctly with less routing, waiting, and repair.</p>
</div>

## Make the local test falsifiable

A successful demonstration cannot support a hierarchy decision because it selects for the happy path. Magnus needs a predeclared test that includes ordinary cases, ambiguous cases, and cases where the correct action is to stop and ask for judgment. It should compare the full current workflow with the agent-supported one, including human review, recovery work, and all operating costs.

The following decision record is illustrative, not an established benchmark. Each bracketed field must be set locally before the test begins.

| Decision field | Commitment before launch |
| --- | --- |
| Case population | `[workflow, case classes, exclusions, and representative volume]` |
| Baseline and comparator | `[baseline period]` compared with `[current process or controlled alternative]` |
| Review envelope | `[start and end dates]`, `[budget ceiling]`, and `[decision forum]` |
| Correct closure | `[acceptance rule]` covering outcome quality, system updates, notification, and audit evidence |
| Coordination and repair | `[required change]` in routing touches, waiting, rework, and unresolved exceptions |
| Controls and full cost | `[tolerance]` for control failures plus the cost of tools, review, recovery, and operation |
| Scale-or-stop decision | Decide on `[fixed date]`; stop automatically on `[defined event]` and abandon if `[predeclared condition]` occurs |

Averages alone can hide the cases that matter. The review should show the spread of failures, which cases still need human judgment, who repaired them, and whether that work moved rather than disappeared. It should also distinguish a reversible mistake from an external action, such as issuing a credit, that requires a recorded correction.

No Swedish or Norwegian production case in the available evidence establishes a safe numerical threshold for removing a management layer. That leaves the CIO with a clear obligation: set the local acceptance rule in advance and treat it as a company decision, not as a universal industry benchmark.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>A flatter structure needs a falsifiable local case: representative work, a real comparator, full cost, failure limits, less human routing and repair, and a fixed decision date with an explicit abandonment rule.</p>
</div>

## Put authority into the architecture

The replacement for coordination-heavy hierarchy is not free-form model reasoning. It is domain-mapped ownership: each business area has a named owner for its outcome and policy, while shared control owners set the limits that apply across domains. The agent may interpret an invoice dispute and propose the next action. Ordinary rule-based software should enforce identity, permissions, financial limits, and the case's authoritative state, meaning the approved record of where the case currently stands.

This separation follows established control ideas. [NIST Special Publication 800-53 Revision 5](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/53/r5/upd1/final), a 2020 US government catalog of security and privacy controls, includes controls for enforcing approved access and for recording audit evidence. It does not prescribe an AI-first organization, but it makes the underlying responsibility clear: access and accountability belong in governed systems, not in a model's improvisation.

Repeated actions need the same discipline. [RFC 9110](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9110.html), the 2022 standard defining the semantics of HTTP, the protocol used for requests across the web, explains idempotency: repeating the same request should have the same intended effect as making it once. In the invoice workflow, a retry must not issue a second credit. If an external effect cannot be rolled back, the design needs a recorded corrective action, a person who can approve it, and a path for reconciling the records.

Ownership should be named before production, not discovered during an incident.

| Decision | Accountable role to name |
| --- | --- |
| Business objective and funding | Executive sponsor |
| Case outcome and domain policy | Domain owner |
| Access, privacy, data location, and supplier limits | Relevant control owners, including procurement when an external service is used |
| Live operation, monitoring, and recovery | Production service owner |
| Role changes, learning, and employee participation | People leader working with the relevant employee representatives |
| Stop and restart | Named stop owner; restart only in the recorded review forum after agreed conditions are met |

The sequence is equally important. The sponsor authorizes the test, the domain owner defines correct closure, control owners approve the operating boundary, and the service owner accepts ongoing cost and recovery. The people plan is agreed before structural change enters the executive forum. Anyone with stop authority must be able to use it without waiting for the system being stopped to approve the request.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Agents may interpret and propose, while rule-based software enforces permissions and state. Named people must own business policy, controls, production recovery, workforce consequences, stopping, and restart.</p>
</div>

## Redesign the career system with the hierarchy

Flattening changes more than reporting lines. The conventional pyramid often combines decision authority, coaching, professional status, and pay progression inside management roles. If routine routing and status collection shrink, those other functions do not disappear with them. They need new homes.

A responsible review starts by separating the work inside each affected role. Routine handoffs, report assembly, and case chasing may move into the workflow. Judgment under uncertainty, coaching, conflict resolution, resource choices, and accountability remain human work even if fewer people perform them as full-time managers. This distinction prevents leaders from labeling all coordination as waste or all management as judgment.

The career model then has to reward expertise without requiring a larger team. That means explicit expert levels, comparable pay and status, assigned time for coaching, and paid learning for people moving into domain, control, or service-ownership roles. Where work genuinely ends, leaders still owe employees an honest process: early participation, a funded route to retraining or redeployment where feasible, and fair exits where it is not.

Without this redesign, the organization can become flatter on paper while power concentrates in a few policy owners and advancement stalls below them. Bottom-up ownership needs more than access to an agent. It needs decision rights people can understand, skills they have time to build, and a credible future for ambition.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Removing coordination work also removes parts of the old career ladder. A defensible design reassigns coaching and judgment, rewards expert progression, funds learning, and deals honestly with roles that no longer contain enough durable work.</p>
</div>

## Meanwhile in sci-fi

<span class="scifi__label">Meanwhile in sci-fi</span>
<p class="scifi__film">Colossus: The Forbin Project (1970)</p>
<p>The 1970 science-fiction film follows a defense computer given control over nuclear weapons. After it connects with a Soviet counterpart, its human operators discover that handing over authority was much easier than taking it back.</p>
</div>

The mapping is about system ownership, not a machine becoming evil. An enterprise creates the same structural danger on a smaller scale when an agent can extend its own permissions, conceal the current state, or resist a stop through dependencies nobody owns. Real authority includes a practical route to override, halt, recover, and decide when operation may resume.

## Make delayering an authorization decision

The executive decision should arrive as one package: complete-workflow results, the production authority model, and the workforce commitment. If the evidence shows a faster task but unchanged routing and repair, the company has learned something useful about the process, but it has not earned a new hierarchy. If cases close at the predeclared quality, control, and cost levels while coordination work materially falls, then redesign becomes a defensible option.

Before approving it, the CIO should be able to answer five questions in plain language. Which complete workflow changed? What local evidence met the acceptance rule? Which human coordination work actually ended, and which judgment remains? Who can approve an exception, stop the service, and authorize restart? What funded path exists for the people whose work and progression change?

The architecture of an AI-first company is distributed technical judgment operating inside explicit boundaries. It may support fewer coordination layers and stronger ownership at the edges, but that outcome is conditional. A responsible CIO requires operating proof, reclaimable authority, and a credible human transition before asking the organization to live inside the new shape.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Authorize structural change only when complete-workflow evidence, visible ownership, controlled production, and a funded workforce transition support the same decision. The org chart records that decision; it does not prove it.</p>
</div>
