Two linked claims define the architecture of an AI-first company. Every business role acquires a technical dimension. And when agents absorb meaningful execution and coordination, authority can move closer to the work, making a flatter and more bottom-up organization possible.

[Arvind KC](https://openai.com/index/arvind-kc-chief-people-officer/), appointed Chief People Officer at OpenAI in February 2026, articulated the first claim in a [June 2026 interview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8atlQX8HTwQ&t=342s) with the business-interview channel DECODE. He described every future role as “fundamentally a technical role” applied to a particular domain. OpenAI, the AI research and product company behind ChatGPT, is an unusual organization rather than a transferable blueprint. KC’s observation still points to a broader shift: technical judgment is moving into sales, finance, operations, marketing, and support.

An AI agent, as used here, is software organized around a model that pursues a defined goal through approved tools and workflow steps with delegated autonomy. Conversational systems can also invoke tools, so the meaningful distinction is the amount of goal pursuit and workflow control being delegated, not whether the interface looks like chat. That delegation can eliminate handoffs. It can also create monitoring, repair, and supplier dependencies that were not there before.

The responsible sequence is therefore judgment, workflow evidence, decision rights, and workforce commitments. The org chart comes last.

<p>AI-first architecture can distribute judgment and reduce coordination, but flattening is an evidence-based redesign rather than a default effect of deploying agents.</p>

## Technical judgment moves into the business

Technical judgment is the ability to decide what an AI system may do within a business domain, what evidence it needs, where its capability boundary lies, and when a person must take over. It does not mean everyone must code.

Sales must know which commitments an agent may propose. Finance must distinguish routine reconciliation from a decision that changes risk exposure. Operations must define valid state changes and recovery paths. Marketing must assess whether source evidence supports a claim. Support must recognize ambiguity, vulnerability, and relationship consequences that make a standard response unsuitable.

Consider a hypothetical service credit after a customer-facing outage. An agent might assemble the incident record, read the contract, and propose a credit. The support specialist still needs to judge whether the incident record is complete, whether the clause applies, and whether the customer’s circumstances require commercial discretion. The agent provides leverage; the domain expert supplies the boundary.

The boundary matters because AI capability is uneven. In a [2026 *Organization Science* experiment](https://doi.org/10.1287/orsc.2025.21838), Fabrizio Dell’Acqua and coauthors studied 758 consultants using GPT-4, an OpenAI language model. On the selected task outside the tested capability frontier, AI users were 19 percent less likely to reach a correct answer. That result concerns one task, not an enterprise workflow, but it demonstrates why recognizing unsuitable work is part of competence. Article 4 of the [EU AI Act](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:02024R1689-20260727), the European Union’s 2024 risk-based AI regulation, similarly requires providers and deployers to support AI literacy in light of people’s knowledge, training, and use context. Neither point turns business workers into engineers. Both make uninformed delegation indefensible.

<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Every function needs enough technical judgment to set boundaries, test evidence, recognize failure, and escalate without becoming a software team.</p>
</div>

## The work is larger than the AI task

Business author Benjamin Simkin calls the common failure mode the Retrofit Trap in his 2026 book [*The AI First Company*](https://www.amazon.co.uk/dp/B0H45WZRX5). The label describes adding AI to an isolated activity while preserving the structure around it. The task becomes faster. The operating model remains intact.

Return to the service-credit example. Producing a recommendation is one activity. The complete workflow is the full path from the customer’s request through entitlement checks, approval, communication, financial recording, dispute handling, and final closure. If people still collect evidence, chase an approver, correct the recommendation, update three records, and repair a duplicate credit, the organization has accelerated text production rather than redesigned work.

Researchers Thomas Malone and Kevin Crowston defined [coordination](https://doi.org/10.1145/174666.174668) in 1994 as “managing dependencies between activities.” Organization-design scholar Jay Galbraith’s [1974 information-processing account](https://doi.org/10.1287/inte.4.3.28) explained why rules can handle familiar cases while hierarchy receives exceptions the rules cannot settle. Together, these ideas reveal the mechanism behind the legacy flattening thesis: agents may absorb information assembly, status collection, routine routing, and known-exception handling because those activities manage dependencies.

Managers also resolve conflict, allocate scarce resources, coach people, interpret weak signals, and remain accountable for difficult trade-offs. Those responsibilities do not disappear because status routing improves. The CIO’s job is to identify the exact coordination that left the workflow and the judgment that remains.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>A faster AI output changes the operating model only when the surrounding dependencies, exceptions, records, and repair work change with it.</p>
</div>

## Flattening must be earned in complete workflows

A useful evidence unit for organizational redesign is the correctly closed case. This is a proposed management test, not an established industry benchmark. A case is correctly closed when the full workflow meets its declared quality and policy conditions, updates the authoritative business record, counts every human intervention and repair, and leaves no hidden cleanup behind.

For each predeclared case class, the evidence should include:

- closure quality and elapsed time from trigger to final outcome;
- human routing and approval touches, repair time, reopening, and repeat contact;
- policy, privacy, security, and other control failures;
- total cost, including models, integration, monitoring, review, exceptions, and supplier fees;
- residual human judgment and work shifted into informal supervision.

The agent-supported design should face both the current process and the strongest reasonable conventional alternative, such as workflow software or business rules. Ordinary automation may remove structured routing more cheaply and reliably. Adverse cases—stale data, duplicates, unauthorized requests, disputes, and supplier failure—belong in the trial before it starts. An unfavorable result cannot be relabeled as out of scope after observation.

No validated Swedish or Norwegian production case identified to date establishes a numerical threshold for removing a management layer. A Nordic CIO therefore needs company-set evidence conditions, an explicit observation period, and predeclared failure conditions. These choices must be presented as local management decisions, not borrowed certainty.

If routing declines while repair, risk review, or frontline cleanup rises, coordination has moved rather than disappeared. Structural change becomes defensible only when the evidence shows which responsibility vanished, which moved closer to the domain, and which still requires human authority.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Judge complete, correctly closed cases against credible alternatives and count every new form of coordination before considering a flatter structure.</p>
</div>

## A flatter company needs a stronger core

Economists Philippe Aghion and Jean Tirole distinguished [formal authority from real authority](https://doi.org/10.1086/262063) in 1997: the assigned right to decide can differ from effective control over what happens. This distinction matters when decisions move toward domain teams. From the center, the change may look untidy because fewer questions travel up a reporting chain. The answer is clearer boundaries and enforceable decision rights, meaning explicit authority to propose, approve, reject, override, stop, or restart.

Domain-mapped ownership assigns outcomes and decisions to the business expertise closest to them. The domain owner defines the outcome, case classes, limits, and escalation rules. The agent interprets unstructured information and proposes the next step within that domain. Ordinary company-controlled software—the control core—checks identity, permission, authoritative state, policy limits, duplicate protection, and release conditions. A named human owns exceptions, overrides, stopping, restart, and the final decision. The system of record, the official business record, stores the approved action, actor, time, outcome, and recovery path. Authoritative state means the current approved status and version of the case, not whatever the model last read.

In the service-credit workflow, the agent may propose an amount while software verifies the current incident record, contractual limit, approver authority, and whether the action has already been released. [RFC 9110](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9110.html#section-9.2.2), a 2022 Internet Engineering Task Force standard, defines HTTP idempotency narrowly: repeating an identical request has the same intended server effect as making it once. Safe business retries also require the action identity, recorded outcome, and current case state to remain connected. A repeated command must return or reconcile with the earlier outcome rather than issue another credit.

Every consequential action needs an audit trail, and a named human needs stop authority: both the right and the practical mechanism to halt new actions. The audit trail records who or what acted, when, from which state, and with what outcome. The [US National Institute of Standards and Technology’s 2024 generative-AI profile](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.AI.600-1) is voluntary guidance, but its recommendation to monitor and document human overrides is useful here. Article 14 of the EU AI Act adds override and stop requirements only where a system meets the law’s high-risk definition; it does not apply automatically to every business agent. The [General Data Protection Regulation](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32016R0679), applicable since 2018, has separate scope for personal-data processing and certain solely automated decisions with significant effects. Internal risk labels do not replace those legal assessments.

Recovery must also reflect the real world. A rollback restores internal software or workflow configuration to a known safe version. An external commitment that has already been made may require a compensating action—a new action that corrects the effect—plus a pending state while records are reconciled. Supplier exit, meaning a tested ability to replace the provider or run the service manually, keeps policy, state, logs, and practical control with the company.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Push interpretation toward domain teams while company-controlled software enforces state and permissions and named people retain real authority over exceptions and consequences.</p>
</div>

## The old pyramid also carries careers

A management layer is not merely a box of routing tasks. It may carry coaching, pay progression, professional status, conflict resolution, and preparation for larger decisions. Removing the box without rebuilding those functions creates an organizational debt that appears later as attrition, weak supervision, or an informal hierarchy no one designed.

The [International Labour Organization](https://doi.org/10.54394/HETP0387), the United Nations agency for labor standards and research, emphasized in its 2025 analysis that exposure to generative AI does not imply the immediate automation of an entire occupation. Most roles contain a mixture of activities. A responsible transition identifies which activities shrink, which judgment responsibilities remain, and which new responsibilities emerge around evidence, exceptions, and service ownership.

Before changing the chart, leadership should establish expert progression that does not depend on headcount ownership, visible pay ranges, funded learning time, and named responsibility for coaching. Redeployment and selection rules need to be explicit. So does the treatment of people whose coordination-heavy work genuinely declines. Ambition will not disappear with the pyramid; it needs credible routes toward mastery, influence, and compensation.

Social dialogue means structured discussion among employers, workers, and their representatives. It should happen before a trial changes work design and again before evidence is used to change roles, pay, or reporting lines. In Sweden, Section 11 of the [Co-Determination in the Workplace Act](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-1976580-om-medbestammande-i-arbetslivet_sfs-1976-580/) requires negotiation before qualifying significant changes under its applicable conditions. Exact duties depend on the current law, collective agreements, and local circumstances. Participation also needs practical force: a named representative or control owner must be able to pause the organizational decision when agreed evidence or process conditions have not been met.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Rebuild progression, coaching, pay, learning, participation, and redeployment before removing a layer that currently carries those functions.</p>
</div>

## Put the org-chart decision behind a gate

The board does not need another forecast about inevitable flattening. It needs a decision record capable of producing a no.

Operational ownership comes first. Name the domain owner accountable for the outcome, the permanent service owner responsible after the trial, and the control owner empowered to block release. Name the executive sponsor separately. Then appoint an evidence owner with enough independence to reject the workflow team’s conclusion when the record does not support it.

The one-page record should specify:

- the complete workflow, predeclared case classes, current-process baseline, conventional-automation comparator, and evidence period;
- company-set acceptance conditions for correctly closed cases, routing, repair, quality, control failures, total cost, and residual judgment;
- permissions, consequential approvals, stop and restart authority, recovery paths, supplier dependencies, and manual service mode;
- workforce checkpoints, learning commitments, role consequences, and the people authorized to pause the structural decision.

The initial authorization is for a reversible workflow trial, not for removing a management layer. At its conclusion, leaders may stop, narrow, expand, or move the workflow into normal operations. Any organizational redesign is a separate proposal. It must show which responsibilities moved or disappeared, how remaining judgment is owned, and how careers, pay, coaching, participation, and accountability will work afterward.

If the evidence cannot survive independent review, keep the structure and improve the workflow. If it can, authority may move downward without becoming invisible. That is the leadership choice at the center of an AI-first company. The org chart comes last.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Authorize a reversible trial with independent evidence and explicit people commitments; revisit the hierarchy only after the complete workflow earns that decision.</p>
</div>
