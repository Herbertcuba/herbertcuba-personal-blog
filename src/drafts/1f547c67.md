Two claims define the architecture of an AI-first company. Every business role acquires a technical dimension. And when AI agents—software built around models that pursue defined goals through approved tools and steps—absorb enough routine execution and coordination, the organization can place more ownership near the work and may need fewer layers.

Those claims depend on each other. In a [June 2026 interview on DECODE](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8atlQX8HTwQ&t=342s), a business-interview channel, [Arvind KC, OpenAI’s Chief People Officer](https://openai.com/index/arvind-kc-chief-people-officer/), said that every role would become “fundamentally a technical role” applied to a particular domain. OpenAI is the AI research and product company behind the conversational service ChatGPT. Its experience is an observation from an unusual company, not proof that the same structure will work in a bank, manufacturer, public agency, or Nordic employer.

The operating consequence is conditional. An agent that produces a document while people continue chasing inputs, approvals, corrections, and records has not changed the organization. A responsible CIO considers a flatter structure only after complete workflows close correctly, decision rights remain enforceable, added complexity is owned, and the people whose work changes have a credible path forward. The reporting structure comes last.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>An AI-first company may distribute technical judgment and reduce coordination layers, but structural change must follow operating proof, visible authority, and a credible workforce transition.</p></div>

## Technical judgment belongs where the work is understood

Technical judgment is the ability to decide what an AI system may do inside a business domain, what evidence it needs, where its capability boundary lies, and when a person must take over. It does not require every employee to write software.

Sales needs this judgment when an agent extracts contract terms but a concession requires commercial authority. Finance needs it when routine reconciliation becomes a question about risk appetite. Support needs it when a familiar policy case turns into a vulnerable-customer or relationship decision. Operations needs it when a proposed action would move a case into a consequential state. Marketing needs it when fluent material lacks adequate sources or quality evidence.

Bottom-up ownership means that decisions and improvements can originate near the work, within declared limits, instead of waiting for a central AI team to translate every problem. That only works when domain specialists can recognize both useful capability and dangerous confidence.

The boundary is real. In a [2026 *Organization Science* experiment led by organization researcher Fabrizio Dell’Acqua](https://doi.org/10.1287/orsc.2025.21838), consultants used GPT-4, an OpenAI language model, on tasks selected to fall inside and outside its tested capability frontier—the boundary between work the model handled well and work it did not. Assistance helped on the inside tasks, but users were 19% less likely to produce a correct solution on the selected outside task. This was not a production-workflow or hierarchy study. It shows why domain judgment includes knowing when reliance on the system should stop.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Technical judgment puts capability, evidence, and escalation decisions inside each business function without turning every employee into a programmer.</p></div>

## Follow the work past the generated answer

Benjamin Simkin, a business author, calls one failure mode the Retrofit Trap in his 2026 field manual, [*The AI First Company: Engineering Your Business to Thrive in the AI Economy*](https://www.amazon.co.uk/dp/B0H45WZRX5). His formulation is simple: adding AI to the old structure can produce faster tasks and the same structural problems. The source gives the term its provenance; it does not establish how widespread the pattern is.

Consider supplier onboarding. An agent may read submitted documents, extract company details, and draft a recommendation in seconds. The workflow still includes collecting missing evidence, checking bank information, routing security and sanctions questions, obtaining the right approval, updating procurement and finance records, and resolving a failed or disputed outcome. The generated answer is one activity. The operating model is the complete dependency chain.

Researchers Thomas Malone and Kevin Crowston [defined coordination in 1994](https://doi.org/10.1145/174666.174668) as managing dependencies between activities. Organization-design scholar Jay Galbraith’s [1974 information-processing account](https://doi.org/10.1287/inte.4.3.28) explained why hierarchy handles exceptions when existing rules are insufficient. Managers also allocate resources, resolve conflict, coach people, and carry accountability, so management cannot be reduced to forwarding work. The useful question is narrower: which information, approval, and exception dependencies have actually left the workflow?

A complete workflow runs from its trigger through the business outcome, required records, exceptions, and closure. A correctly closed case is a proposed management unit for judging it: the outcome meets declared quality and policy conditions, the authoritative records are updated, every human intervention and repair is counted, and no cleanup remains hidden. The agent-supported design should be compared with both today’s process and the strongest reasonable design using ordinary workflow software and business rules.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The Retrofit Trap appears when AI accelerates a visible task while the surrounding dependencies, approvals, repair, and record work remain intact.</p></div>

## A layer disappears only when its work does

Before changing the chart, leadership should declare what would disprove the redesign. Case classes—the predefined types of ordinary work, exceptions, and failures—must be fixed before the trial begins. So must the evidence period, comparison processes, acceptance conditions, and stop conditions. An adverse result cannot be renamed out of scope after it appears.

The proposed scorecard should examine:

- correctly closed cases, quality, disputes, and reopenings by case class;
- elapsed time and every human routing or approval touch;
- repair effort, control failures, and work shifted into informal supervision;
- total cost, including integration, models, monitoring, review, exceptions, licenses, and suppliers;
- the human judgment that remains after routine coordination is removed.

These are decision categories, not universal benchmarks. No validated Swedish or Norwegian production case in the evidence assembled for this argument establishes a numerical threshold for safely removing a management layer. That gap belongs in the board paper. Magnus needs locally chosen conditions and an evidence owner with explicit authority to reject the workflow owner’s conclusion.

The flattening claim fails when coordination returns as frontline cleanup, additional risk review, informal supervision, or dependence on a supplier that controls the practical outcome. It also fails when conventional automation closes the same cases more reliably or cheaply. A layer is only gone when its responsibilities have disappeared or moved visibly—and the new arrangement holds for the declared period.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Flattening is a rejectable local hypothesis: complete-workflow evidence must show that coordination has durably left rather than moved somewhere harder to see.</p></div>

## Authority should get stronger at the edges

A flatter organization needs authority that is easier to inspect. Economists Philippe Aghion and Jean Tirole [distinguished formal and real authority in 1997](https://doi.org/10.1086/262063): formal authority is the assigned right to decide, while real authority is effective control over what happens. A domain team may hold the formal mandate while a vendor setting, inaccessible data source, model instruction, or hidden approval rule retains real control.

A decision right is explicit authority to propose, approve, reject, override, stop, or restart. Domain-mapped ownership assigns those rights to a bounded business area, such as customer renewals, together with responsibility for outcomes, exceptions, and limits.

For a renewal workflow, the operating boundary should read plainly. The agent interprets the renewal request and proposes a next action. Company-controlled software checks identity, permission, the current case state, policy limits, duplicate protection, and release conditions. A named human owns exceptions, overrides, stop decisions, restart, and accountability.

An authoritative state is the current approved status and version held in the system of record, the official business record. An audit trail records who or what acted, on which state, when, and with what outcome. Every consequential action needs an audit trail, and a named human needs stop authority: both the right and the practical mechanism to halt new actions.

Duplicate protection also needs a business contract. [RFC 9110](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9110.html#section-9.2.2), the Internet Engineering Task Force’s 2022 standard for HTTP semantics, defines idempotency as identical requests having the same intended effect as one request. A business workflow must add the unique identity and scope of the action, the handling of simultaneous duplicates, storage of the prior outcome, and the response to a retry. The current-state check and the local state update must succeed together.

| Renewal situation | Required response |
| --- | --- |
| Current version, authorized approver, within limit, unseen action identity | Release once and store the new state and outcome. |
| Proposal refers to an older case version | Reject it as stale and require a refreshed proposal. |
| Approver lacks the required authority | Reject the release and route it to the named exception owner. |
| The same action identity is retried | Do not release again; return or reconcile with the stored outcome. |
| Stop flag is active or audit recording is unavailable | Halt consequential releases until the named restart authority acts. |
| External commitment succeeded but internal confirmation failed | Record a pending state, avoid a blind retry, and invoke the named recovery path. |

Not every external effect can be erased. Rollback means returning software, configuration, or workflow rules to an earlier safe version. A compensating action is a new action that corrects an external commitment—such as a payment or binding message—that cannot simply be undone. Both need named owners and rehearsal.

An internal risk tier, meaning the company’s own classification of possible harm and required control, remains useful. It complements rather than replaces classification under the [EU AI Act, the European risk-based AI law adopted in 2024](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:02024R1689-20260727), review under the [GDPR, the EU personal-data law applicable since 2018](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32016R0679), and applicable Swedish or Norwegian employment and participation review. Legal applicability depends on the workflow and jurisdiction.

Finally, supplier exit means a tested ability to replace the provider or operate manually without losing control of policy, state, records, or service. If the organization cannot do that, the supplier may hold more real authority than the org chart admits.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Distributed ownership is defensible only when company-controlled systems enforce state and permission, named people can reject and stop actions, and recovery and supplier exit are real.</p></div>

## Rebuild the career system before removing rungs

Removing coordination work changes more than capacity. Management roles often combine routing with coaching, conflict resolution, resource allocation, accountability, and preparation for larger decisions. Leadership has to separate those activities before deciding that a role has disappeared.

Economists Raghuram Rajan and Julie Wulf studied [more than 300 large US firms during 1986–1998](https://www.nber.org/papers/w9633). Flatter structures coincided with wider executive spans and changes in authority, pay, and incentives for divisional managers. The evidence predates current generative AI and does not establish AI-caused delayering. Its value here is the reminder that hierarchy, authority, careers, and compensation move together.

The [International Labour Organization, the United Nations agency for labor standards, concluded in 2025](https://doi.org/10.54394/HETP0387) that exposure to generative-AI tasks does not mean an entire occupation will immediately be automated. Transformation is the more defensible planning assumption. That requires expert progression without direct reports, visible pay bands and status, named coaching responsibilities, funded learning time, transparent redeployment and selection rules, and a clear account of which judgment responsibilities remain.

It also requires social dialogue: structured discussion among the employer, employees, and their representatives. Participation should happen before a trial changes work design and again before evidence is used to change roles, pay, or reporting lines. An HR or employee-relations owner should convene the process, provide the decision record and workflow evidence, and hold authority to pause the structural decision when participation is incomplete.

In Sweden, Section 11 of the [Co-Determination in the Workplace Act of 1976](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-1976580-om-medbestammande-i-arbetslivet_sfs-1976-580/) requires negotiation before qualifying significant changes where its conditions apply. Collective agreements and local circumstances require current review. A Norwegian organization must examine the applicable Norwegian rules and agreements rather than infer them from Swedish law.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>A flatter structure needs a new employment bargain: credible progression, pay, coaching, learning, participation, redeployment, and authority to pause the change.</p></div>

## Give the board a decision it can reject

The board should not be asked whether it believes in an AI-first future. It should receive a bounded operating proposal whose result can be accepted, narrowed, or rejected. The following hypothetical renewal record uses company-set placeholders; it is a management instrument, not an established benchmark.

| Field | Hypothetical business-customer renewal trial |
| --- | --- |
| Workflow | Standard renewal from request through binding offer, authoritative customer and finance record updates, and resolved exceptions. |
| Named owners and signatories | Domain: `[Name — Commercial Director]`; technology and permanent service: `[Name — Head of Enterprise Applications]`; control: `[Name — Operational Risk/Compliance Lead]`; evidence: `[Name — Process Assurance Lead]`; sponsor: `[Name — CIO/COO]`. |
| Comparisons | Current human process and the strongest conventional workflow-and-rules design. |
| Case classes | Ordinary, commercial exception, stale data, duplicate request, unauthorized approver, disputed outcome, and supplier failure; fixed before launch. |
| Schedule and forums | `[Start–end dates]`; weekly operating review; evidence locked and executive decision taken on `[date]`. Observed failures may not be relabeled out of scope. |
| Evidence and cost | Correct closure, cycle time, human touches, repair, reopening, quality, control failures, residual judgment, and total cost under `[company-set ceiling]`, including integration, models, monitoring, review, exceptions, licenses, and supplier fees. |
| Authority and recovery | Agent proposes; company software enforces state and permission; the declared approver releases; the control owner can stop; restart requires `[named role]`; recovery paths are tested. |
| Workforce checkpoints | `[Name — HR/Employee Relations]` convenes representatives before work-design changes and before any change to roles, pay, or reporting lines; the structural decision pauses if participation is incomplete. |
| Supplier readiness | Contract, licensing, data use, logging access, data residency where required, model substitution, manual service mode, and exit owner are confirmed. |
| Structural stop | No layer change follows an unauthorized or stale release, unresolved duplicate behavior, missing audit record, uncontrolled personal data, absent fallback, incomplete workforce process, or rejection by the evidence owner. |
| Decision | Control and evidence owners may withhold approval. Choose expand, narrow, transfer into normal operations, or stop. Any org-chart redesign remains a separate later decision. |

A successful trial would establish an operating possibility, not an automatic mandate to remove a layer. Leadership would still need to identify which responsibilities disappeared, which moved into the domain, which became technology or control work, and whether the remaining leadership load is sustainable. If the evidence does not hold, the structure stays intact while the workflow is redesigned or stopped.

The AI-first company is therefore not defined by maximum automation or minimum management. It is defined by the ability to show where technical judgment sits, which coordination truly vanished, who can stop consequential action, how the organization recovers, and what future it offers the people carrying the remaining judgment. Those are the commitments a CIO should put before the board before anyone redraws the hierarchy.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Authorize a reversible, rejectable workflow trial first; revisit the org chart only after the evidence, authority, recovery, supplier, and workforce commitments all hold.</p></div>
