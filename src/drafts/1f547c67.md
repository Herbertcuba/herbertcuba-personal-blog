Two claims define the architecture of an AI-first company. Every business role acquires a technical dimension, and organizations can move more ownership toward the people closest to the work as AI agents absorb routine execution and coordination. An AI agent, in this context, is software that uses a generative AI model to interpret information, choose a next step, and act through business tools within defined limits.

[Arvind KC](https://openai.com/index/arvind-kc-chief-people-officer/), chief people officer at OpenAI, the company behind ChatGPT, made both claims concrete in a [DECODE technology interview published on 31 May 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8atlQX8HTwQ). He said that “every role as we go into the future is fundamentally a technical role,” then explained that the technical role is applied to a business domain. He also argued for innovation from the bottom up. That observation points toward a different organization, but it does not prove that management layers can safely disappear.

A hierarchy carries information, approvals, and exceptions. It also assigns accountability, resolves conflicts, coaches people, and holds authority when a case falls outside policy. My position is that an AI-first company can become materially flatter, but only after it replaces the coordination work and deliberately rebuilds the human functions that still matter.

<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Distributed technical judgment and bottom-up ownership make a flatter company possible. They do not make it automatic: agents must replace real coordination while human authority and development remain intact.</p>
</div>

## Technical judgment belongs in every business role

Calling every role technical does not mean everyone must code. It means that people need enough technical judgment to decide what AI can handle reliably, what evidence it needs, how failure becomes visible, and when a person must take over. This is a business capability because those answers depend on the domain, not only on the model.

A salesperson must know when AI-generated account research needs verification. Operations must distinguish a routine disruption from a safety exception. Finance must test whether a recommendation rests on complete records, while marketing must know the provenance and approval status of AI-generated creative content. Support teams need to recognize when a request has become a policy dispute rather than a classification task.

Once that judgment sits inside each function, domain teams can own more decisions without sending every case upward. Leadership still sets the outcome, risk boundary, and escalation path, then makes sure teams have the skill to act within them. To an organization accustomed to vertical approval lines, this can look disorderly. The order has simply moved into explicit boundaries, shared records, and visible ownership.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Technical judgment means knowing how AI behaves inside a business domain, including its limits and escalation points. That capability lets domain teams take more responsibility without confusing autonomy with uncontrolled action.</p>
</div>

## The Retrofit Trap preserves the old company

Benjamin Simkin, author of the 2026 business book [*The AI First Company: Engineering Your Business to Thrive in the AI Economy*](https://www.amazon.co.uk/dp/B0H45WZRX5), calls the mistake of adding AI to an unchanged operating structure the “Retrofit Trap.” The label matters because a faster task can leave the organization around it almost untouched.

Consider a customer-refund process. An agent may draft the reply quickly, yet a person still gathers the order record, another checks eligibility, a manager approves the exception, and someone repairs inconsistent entries across systems. The visible task is faster. The routing, waiting, and recovery work remains, so there is no basis for removing a coordination layer.

An AI-first redesign follows the case from request to closure. The agent can collect records, interpret the request, propose a resolution, route a genuine exception, and prepare the system updates. People retain authority over disputed facts, policy changes, high-consequence cases, and conflicts between customer, financial, and regulatory goals. When routine status gathering and approval chasing actually disappear, fewer handoffs and a flatter structure become plausible.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>The Retrofit Trap speeds up an isolated output while preserving manual routing and repair. Organizational redesign becomes defensible only when the complete workflow removes coordination rather than hiding it elsewhere.</p>
</div>

## A workflow must earn a flatter structure

The useful unit of evidence is a correctly closed case: the intended business outcome is reached, required controls are satisfied, records agree, and any customer or employee obligation is completed. A pilot should compare that result with the current process across routine, ambiguous, rare, and high-consequence cases. The measures should include human routing touches, repair effort, quality, control failures, elapsed time, full cost, and the judgment still required from people.

The evidence available for this article contains no Swedish or Norwegian production case that establishes a safe numerical threshold for removing a management layer. That absence does not invalidate the architecture thesis, but it makes flattening a bounded local hypothesis. A CIO should therefore approve the test and its decision rules before discussing headcount or reporting lines.

A compact pilot-to-production record should contain:

- **Scope and comparator:** the complete workflow, its boundaries, the current process, and a representative case mix.
- **Outcome evidence:** the local definition of correct closure, quality and control measures, human intervention, repair, time, and cost.
- **Authority:** the domain owner, permitted actions, exception owner, override rights, stop authority, and one transition the system must reject.
- **Value bridge:** coordination and repair costs removed, set against new service, control, supplier, transition, and residual human-capacity costs. A value bridge is simply a reconciliation of what disappears and what must be added.
- **Production conditions:** a named service owner, ongoing funding, handover date, supplier exit, model substitution, data-location boundaries, applicable risk review, and operational reversal.
- **People and decision:** employee consultation before role decisions are fixed, learning and redeployment commitments, locally declared acceptance and abandonment rules, and a dated choice to scale, revise, or stop.

These entries are not a universal benchmark. They are a decision record that makes management accountable for the evidence it chooses to accept.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Measure correctly closed workflows, including hidden human work and full cost. In the absence of a verified Nordic threshold for removing layers, predeclare a local evidence, control, supplier, and people contract before the test begins.</p>
</div>

## Authority has to become more visible

The replacement for managerial routing is a governed operating system, not an agent left to improvise authority. A business domain, meaning a bounded area such as refunds or supplier onboarding, has a human owner accountable for outcomes. Agents interpret evidence and propose actions within that domain. Case-management software records where each case stands, durable-workflow software resumes work after an interruption, and policy software applies explicit permissions. These established software categories provide a firmer control layer than free-form model reasoning.

For the refund case, the allowed path might be `received → evidence checked → proposed → approved → executed`. The agent may move the case from evidence checked to proposed. An explicit policy or an authorized person approves it, and the payment service executes it. The control layer rejects `received → executed` and blocks a second execution carrying the same unique case identifier.

That duplicate protection is called idempotency. The [Internet Engineering Task Force’s June 2022 HTTP Semantics standard, RFC 9110](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9110.html#section-9.2.2), published by the body that develops internet standards through Request for Comments documents, defines an idempotent request as one where repeated identical requests have the same intended effect as one request. In business terms, a retry must not produce a second refund.

The same clarity is needed around exceptional authority. An override is a recorded permission to depart from the normal rule. Stop authority names the person who can halt the workflow, while rollback returns reversible systems to a known safe state; an irreversible payment needs a separate corrective action instead. Every action should leave audit evidence showing the input, proposal, permission, result, and accountable owner.

Production approval is not permanent. The service owner should replay case histories, turn failures into evaluation tests, review changes in outcomes, and require fresh authorization after a material change to the model, tools, policy, or supplier. This is how domain teams gain real decision latitude without turning the organization’s edges into unowned risk.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Agents may interpret and propose, while ordinary software enforces state and permissions. Named people own exceptions, stopping, recovery, and renewed authorization when the production system materially changes.</p>
</div>

## Flattening changes the employment bargain

A management layer often contains routine coordination and genuine human value in the same role. Redesign starts by separating status collection, routing, reconciliation, and repair from judgment, conflict resolution, coaching, and accountability. Some coordination-heavy positions may shrink. Other people may become domain owners, service owners, control specialists, or senior practitioners whose authority no longer depends on managing a large team.

That shift breaks the old career pyramid unless leadership builds another path. Expert progression needs credible levels, pay, status, and access to consequential decisions. Coaching must have a named home and funded capacity rather than becoming spare-time work. Employees whose roles change need paid learning, a fair chance at redeployment, and a transparent process for exits when redeployment is not possible.

Consultation should begin while options remain open, before the future structure is presented as settled. The transition cost also belongs in the value bridge; otherwise the business case treats people as a cost outside the model. A flatter company is responsible only if it changes how learning, ambition, and reward work alongside the reporting lines.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Removing coordination work also removes parts of the career system. A defensible redesign funds expert progression, coaching, consultation, learning, redeployment, and fair exits as part of the operating model.</p>
</div>

## Put two decisions in front of the board

The first decision is whether to authorize a bounded workflow redesign with a named comparator, owner, budget, stop rule, and production handover. The second comes later: whether the resulting evidence supports changing roles or removing a coordination layer in that domain. Combining them turns a test into a restructuring decision before the facts exist.

For the second decision, I would require proof of correctly closed cases across the agreed case mix, explicit human ownership of authority and exceptions, reliable state and audit controls, a complete local value bridge, supplier and data reversibility, and a funded workforce transition. The answer may differ across domains. One unit may become flatter while another still needs the hierarchy because its exceptions, coaching load, or control burden remain high.

The architecture of an AI-first company is therefore more than an org chart with fewer boxes. It is an agreement about where judgment lives, what software may do, who can stop it, and what the organization owes people when the work changes. If that agreement cannot be made visible, the hierarchy is still carrying work the new model has not replaced.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Authorize workflow proof before organizational change. A board-defensible flattening decision requires complete-case evidence, visible authority, controlled operation, reversible dependencies, sound economics, and a credible settlement with the people affected.</p>
</div>
