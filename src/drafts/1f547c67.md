An AI-first company changes two things at once. Every role acquires a technical dimension, and ownership can move closer to the people doing the work as agents absorb part of the execution and coordination that hierarchy carries today.

By an AI agent, I mean software that uses a language model, the technology behind generative AI, to interpret a goal, choose steps and act through connected tools. This follows the practical distinction in [Anthropic’s guide to building AI agents](https://www.anthropic.com/research/building-effective-agents): an agent directs parts of its process instead of following only a fixed sequence.

The first claim is already a capability question. The second is an organizational hypothesis with much higher stakes. A fluent demo gives no CIO a defensible reason to remove a management layer. The case for a flatter company begins only when a complete workflow performs better, the remaining human authority is visible, and the people transition is credible.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>AI-first design links distributed technical judgment to the possibility of flatter ownership, but structural change has to be earned through workflow evidence, governed authority and a funded people plan.</p></div>

## Technical judgment belongs in the business

[Arvind KC](https://openai.com/index/arvind-kc-chief-people-officer/), chief people officer at OpenAI, an AI research and deployment company, put the capability shift plainly in a [2026 recorded interview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8atlQX8HTwQ): “every role as we go into the future is fundamentally a technical role.” He immediately added that the role is technical “applied to a particular domain,” meaning a bounded area of business knowledge and responsibility.

That qualification matters. Technical judgment is the ability to decide what an AI system may do reliably in a specific business setting, what evidence it needs, how failure will appear, and when a person must take over. It does not require everyone to write code. A salesperson needs to know whether an agent may assemble an offer within approved terms. Finance needs to understand when a proposed payment requires reconciliation. Marketing must know which data and claims are permitted, while support must recognize the cases in which policy and empathy outweigh speed.

KC’s accompanying organizational observation was that useful AI innovation at OpenAI is bottom-up: people across functions find applications in the domains they understand. That supports distributed discovery and ownership. It does not establish how many management layers another company can safely remove. My organizational hypothesis is narrower: when domain teams gain technical judgment and agents demonstrably remove recurring coordination, some ownership can move toward the edges and a specific part of the company may become flatter.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Every function needs enough technical judgment to set boundaries, assess evidence and recognize failure in its own domain. Bottom-up discovery supports local ownership, while flatter structure remains a separate claim that must be tested.</p></div>

## Find the coordination before removing the layer

Benjamin Simkin, author of the 2026 business book [*The AI First Company: Engineering Your Business to Thrive in the AI Economy*](https://www.amazon.co.uk/dp/B0H45WZRX5), calls the practice of bolting AI onto an old structure the **Retrofit Trap**. The term describes a real design error, not an established law: one task becomes faster while the surrounding routing, approvals and repair work remain intact.

Consider a customer refund. An agent may read the request and draft a reply in seconds, yet the full job still includes confirming the order, checking policy and payment status, securing approval for an exception, issuing the refund, updating the customer record and handling a failed payment action. If people still copy information between systems, chase approvals and correct the agent’s mistakes, the company has accelerated a task without changing its operating model.

This is where hierarchy enters the argument. A hierarchy is partly a system for moving information and authority: managers consolidate status, set priorities, approve exceptions, settle conflicts and make sure work crosses functional boundaries. Agents can potentially summarize evidence, track progress, route ordinary cases and propose the next action. Humans still own risk appetite, policy changes, contested trade-offs, employment decisions and accountability for consequential outcomes.

Removing a layer is therefore justified only when its recurring coordination work has disappeared or moved to a clearly governed system. If that work has merely shifted into a repair queue, extra meetings or the inboxes of senior specialists, the hierarchy has become less visible rather than flatter.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The Retrofit Trap speeds up an isolated task while preserving the coordination around it. Before changing the hierarchy, leaders must identify which routing and approval work has truly gone away and which human authority still has to remain.</p></div>

## Make the trial a decision record

The relevant unit of evidence is a **complete workflow**, from a valid request to a recorded business outcome, including normal cases and exceptions. A **correctly closed case** reaches that agreed outcome and passes its required quality and control checks. Measuring generated replies or agent actions is too narrow because it can hide human routing, rework and unresolved failures.

For the refund workflow, the trial should compare correctly closed cases by **case mix**, the proportions of ordinary, unusual and high-risk cases included. It should also record human touches, repair time, quality failures, control failures, total cost and the judgment that still remains with people. Averages alone can hide the exact cases that make delayering unsafe.

No Swedish or Norwegian production material reviewed for this article established a transferable numerical threshold for safely removing a management layer because of agents. The review question was specific: a before-and-after production case connecting agent-supported workflow results to safe structural removal. The evidence limit applies to this review, so each company must set local acceptance conditions rather than borrow a universal benchmark.

A portfolio owner can turn that requirement into a fillable trial charter. The blanks are deliberate; they force a local commitment before anyone sees the result.

| Decision field | Complete before the trial starts |
| --- | --- |
| Structural hypothesis | If the workflow removes **[named coordination work]**, we will consider **[specific role or layer change]**. |
| Scope and case mix | Workflow **[name]**; included and excluded cases **[list]**; ordinary and exception mix **[source]**. |
| Baseline and provenance | Comparison period **[dates]**; source systems **[names]**; evidence owner **[person]**; model and workflow versions **[IDs]**. |
| Trial boundaries | Maximum volume, spend, staff time and system access **[local limits]**. |
| Outcome measures | Correctly closed cases, human routing touches, repair effort, quality and control failures, total cost and residual human judgment **[definitions]**. |
| Authority map | Business owner, permission owner, exception owner, override approver and stop owner **[names]**. |
| Decision cadence | Monitoring dates **[dates]**; final review **[date]**; acceptance conditions **[local conditions]**. |
| People commitment | Affected roles, participation owner, paid learning, redeployment and employment options **[owners and budget]**. |
| Reversal path | How access is revoked, pending work is handled, records are preserved and the prior process is restored **[procedure]**. |

The final review should end with a signed choice: stop, extend the trial, redesign the workflow, or remove the specified coordination layer. Setting those choices and acceptance conditions in advance makes it harder to turn a promising demonstration into a predetermined restructuring decision.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Test complete workflows and correctly closed cases, including exceptions, repair, controls, cost and residual judgment. A precommitted trial charter makes the evidence, owners, decision date and reversal path usable in an executive or board review.</p></div>

## Keep authority outside the model

A flatter organization needs more explicit authority, not less. **Domain-mapped ownership** gives one accountable owner to each bounded business area, such as returns, supplier onboarding or employee access. **Decision rights** then state who may make which decision, for what resource, within which limit and for how long.

In the refund example, the agent may interpret the customer’s request and propose an action. Ordinary software should check the acting identity, current policy, amount limit and **case state**, the authoritative recorded position of that refund. The model cannot grant itself wider access. Each delegated permission should identify the agent’s service identity, the business owner on whose behalf it acts, allowed resources and actions, expiry, revocation behavior and what happens to work already in progress.

That separation also limits hostile instructions. [NIST AI 600-1](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.600-1.pdf), the US National Institute of Standards and Technology’s 2024 risk profile for generative AI, describes **indirect prompt injection**: instructions hidden in retrieved material can redirect a model. A customer message or attachment may therefore influence interpretation, but it must never expand the agent’s software-enforced authority.

Every consequential attempt also needs an **audit trail**, a durable history of what or who proposed, approved, attempted, rejected and completed each change. Before issuing a refund, the system should create an execution record with one identifier for that intended action. [RFC 9110](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9110.html), the 2022 internet standard for HTTP semantics, calls an operation **idempotent** when repeated identical requests are intended to have the same effect as one. In practice, the payment provider should receive the same action identifier on a safe retry rather than a fresh refund request.

That control does not eliminate the hardest gap. The provider may complete a refund just before the local system fails to record it. The workflow must then reconcile with the provider before trying again; if it cannot establish what happened, a named person with **stop authority**, the power to suspend further action, takes over. An **override** is a recorded, authorized departure from the normal rule. A **rollback** restores reversible internal changes, while an irreversible payment or message needs a defined corrective action instead.

This architecture keeps the creative part of the system flexible and the authority boundary inspectable. The agent interprets and proposes within a domain. Software enforces permissions and recorded state. Named humans own policy, exceptions, stops and recovery.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Agents may interpret evidence and propose actions, but ordinary software must enforce identity, permissions, limits and current recorded state. Audit, safe retries, reconciliation, human overrides, stop authority and recovery make distributed ownership governable.</p></div>

## Rebuild progression before shrinking the pyramid

Management roles rarely consist of coordination alone. They also carry coaching, conflict resolution, access to important decisions and the rungs through which people gain pay, status and broader responsibility. A flatter structure can remove waste while quietly damaging all of those functions.

Leaders should therefore decompose a role before deleting it. Routine status collection and case routing may disappear. Exception policy, professional judgment, coaching and domain stewardship may become more valuable. The new design needs progression for deep expertise as well as people leadership, with clear expectations, pay ranges, learning time and access to consequential work. Otherwise bottom-up ownership becomes extra responsibility without authority or reward.

The transition also has to begin while employees can still influence it. In Sweden, section 11 of the [1976 Co-Determination in the Workplace Act](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-1976580-om-medbestammande-i-arbetslivet_sfs-1976-580/) requires an employer bound by a collective agreement to initiate negotiations with the relevant employee organization before deciding on significant changes to its activities or employees’ working conditions. Exact duties depend on the organization, but the operating sequence should not: the accountable executive and HR or employee-relations owner convene the relevant representatives while trial scope, monitoring, role design and transition options remain open to change.

A credible workforce plan names the affected work and people, the skills that will still matter, the time and budget for learning, the routes into redesigned roles, and the terms for redeployment or exit when no suitable role exists. It also identifies who will coach early-career employees when fewer managers remain. These are operating-model costs and commitments, not communications added after the structural decision.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>A hierarchy also carries coaching, progression, pay and access to decisions. Any flatter design must replace those functions, involve employee representatives while choices remain open, and fund learning, redeployment and fair transition outcomes.</p></div>

## Ask the board for a bounded decision

The CIO should not ask the board to approve “an AI-first organization” as a destination. The decision should concern one workflow and one proposed structural change, supported by the completed trial charter: baseline evidence and its provenance, results by case mix, hidden repair and total cost, control failures, residual human judgment, named authority owners, workforce commitments and a tested reversal path.

That proposal should also state exactly where the remaining work moves. If a coordination layer is removed, who now resolves a policy conflict? Who coaches the team? Who may stop the workflow, and who reconciles an uncertain external action? A design that cannot answer those questions has concealed the hierarchy inside software, specialist overload or vendor dependence.

My conclusion is that AI-first companies can become materially flatter and more bottom-up. They earn that architecture locally, when technical judgment has moved into the domain, agents have removed complete-workflow coordination rather than displaced it, and authority and opportunity remain visible. If the evidence fails, keep the structure and redesign the workflow. If it holds, change the specified part of the organization, preserve the reversal path and continue measuring the work that remains.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The board decision is a reversible change to a named workflow, role or layer, backed by operating evidence, explicit authority and a funded people transition. The org chart follows the proof.</p></div>
