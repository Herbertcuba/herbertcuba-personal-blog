Two claims define the architecture of an AI-first company. Every business role acquires a technical dimension. And when AI agents absorb meaningful execution and coordination, decisions can originate closer to the work, making a flatter organization possible.

In a [June 2026 interview on DECODE](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8atlQX8HTwQ&t=342s), a business-interview channel, Arvind KC, [OpenAI’s Chief People Officer](https://openai.com/index/arvind-kc-chief-people-officer/), said every role was becoming “fundamentally a technical role” applied to a particular domain. OpenAI, the AI company behind the ChatGPT conversational service, is an unusual frontier organization. KC’s observation is useful, but it is not evidence that a bank, manufacturer, public agency, or Nordic employer should copy OpenAI’s structure.

Technical judgment means knowing what an AI system may do in a business domain, what evidence it needs, where its capability boundary lies, and when a person must take over. Bottom-up ownership means bounded decisions and improvements can originate near the work rather than only at the center. An AI agent, meanwhile, is software built around a model that pursues a defined goal through approved tools and steps.

Those ideas belong together, but neither justifies a new org chart on its own. A CIO should change the hierarchy only when complete-workflow evidence shows that coordination has durably left the work, while enforceable authority, controlled complexity, and a credible workforce transition remain intact.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>AI can support a flatter company, but distributed technical judgment and agent activity become an operating model only when the evidence, authority, and people architecture hold together.</p></div>

## The technical dimension belongs inside the work

This is not a mandate for everyone to code. It is a demand that each function understand enough about AI to make sound decisions in its own territory. Sales must know which commercial terms an agent may extract or propose. Finance must distinguish routine reconciliation from decisions that alter risk or create an external commitment. Marketing must judge whether the source evidence supports the claim. Operations and support must recognize ambiguity, policy conflicts, and cases where a customer relationship matters more than fluent output.

Consider a hypothetical service-recovery case. A customer reports a duplicate charge and requests a refund. An agent can read the message, collect order details, compare the case with policy, and propose a response. Yet support still defines what a fair outcome looks like. Finance knows whether the charge settled. Operations owns the official case state. Privacy and risk owners decide which data and exceptions the agent may handle.

The need for this judgment follows from AI’s uneven capability. A [2026 *Organization Science* experiment led by Fabrizio Dell’Acqua](https://doi.org/10.1287/orsc.2025.21838) found that AI helped participants on tasks inside its tested capability frontier but made them less likely to be correct on a selected task outside it. The study was not an enterprise workflow trial, but it makes the management point sharply: people near the work must be able to recognize when the system has crossed from useful assistance into unreliable confidence.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Technical judgment is domain literacy about capability, evidence, failure, and escalation. It belongs in business roles because AI performance depends on the case, not merely the tool.</p></div>

## The Retrofit Trap hides in the handoffs

Business author Benjamin Simkin gave a useful name to a common design error in his 2026 book, [*The AI First Company: Engineering Your Business to Thrive in the AI Economy*](https://www.amazon.co.uk/dp/B0H45WZRX5): the Retrofit Trap. It means adding AI to an existing activity while preserving the surrounding manual structure.

The refund recommendation may arrive in seconds. A supervisor still checks eligibility. Finance confirms the transaction. Another colleague copies the result into the customer record. A separate queue handles an unusual payment method. Someone notices that the agent used an outdated policy, repairs the answer, and contacts the customer again. The visible task became faster, while the dependency chain remained intact and acquired new review work.

An AI-first operating model starts with the whole outcome. It asks which handoffs exist because information used to be expensive to assemble, which approvals protect a legitimate decision right, which exceptions can follow explicit policy, and which cases require human judgment. Accelerating one document while leaving the rest untouched is tool adoption. It is not organizational redesign.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The Retrofit Trap appears when AI speeds up an isolated activity but leaves routing, approvals, repair, and record closure largely unchanged.</p></div>

## Hierarchy is a dependency machine

An org chart describes reporting lines, but the hierarchy also carries information, assigns approvals, and routes exceptions. In a 1994 research review, Thomas Malone and Kevin Crowston [defined coordination as managing dependencies between activities](https://doi.org/10.1145/174666.174668). Organization scholar Jay Galbraith had argued in 1974 that [hierarchy handles exceptions when existing rules are insufficient](https://doi.org/10.1287/inte.4.3.28). These theories predate generative AI. Their value here is that they reveal the mechanism an agent-supported workflow would have to change.

Some of that mechanism is routine: gathering facts, checking known conditions, chasing missing information, assigning the next step, reporting status, and updating records. Agents and conventional workflow software may absorb part of it. Other responsibilities remain with named people: changing risk appetite, resolving conflicting goals, making employment decisions, coaching colleagues, handling novel trade-offs, and accepting accountability for the outcome.

A flatter structure becomes plausible only when a layer’s actual coordination load declines. If the work returns as frontline cleanup, informal supervision, security review, supplier support, or exception queues, the organization has not removed coordination. It has moved it somewhere less visible.

From the center, stronger edge ownership may look less orderly because more decisions begin near the domain. The discipline does not disappear. It moves into explicit boundaries, shared evidence, and visible escalation paths.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Hierarchy can shrink only when its information, routing, and exception work genuinely declines; coaching, conflict resolution, and accountability cannot be wished away.</p></div>

## Flattening needs a falsifier

The correct evidence boundary is a complete workflow: the path from a triggering event through decisions, external effects, record updates, exceptions, and closure. In the refund example, closure means more than producing a persuasive answer. The customer receives the decision, the payment outcome is known, the customer and finance records agree, any exception is resolved, and no hidden repair remains.

Call this a correctly closed case: a complete outcome that meets predeclared quality and policy conditions, updates the authoritative business record, counts every human intervention and repair, and leaves no cleanup behind. This is a proposed management test, not an established industry benchmark.

A defensible trial should compare the agent-supported workflow with both the current process and the strongest reasonable conventional alternative, such as rule-based workflow software. It should measure:

- correctly closed cases by a case classification declared before the trial;
- elapsed time, human routing and approval touches, repairs, reopening, and repeat contact;
- outcome quality, privacy, security, policy, and control failures;
- total operating cost, including integration, models, monitoring, reviews, exceptions, and supplier fees;
- residual human judgment and work shifted into informal supervision.

The trial also needs predeclared ordinary, exception, stale-data, duplicate, unauthorized, disputed, and supplier-failure cases. Its acceptance and stopping conditions must be fixed before results arrive. An evidence owner, independent enough to challenge the workflow owner, must be able to reject the conclusion rather than explain away an adverse result.

The evidence available for this article contains no validated Swedish or Norwegian production case establishing a numerical threshold for safely removing a management layer. That absence is a board-level limit on the claim. It means the CIO needs local evidence, not a borrowed benchmark.

The falsifier is straightforward: if coordination reappears as cleanup, risk work, supplier dependence, or informal management, it has not left the workflow. The structural case fails even if one activity became dramatically faster.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Judge complete, correctly closed cases against declared comparators and failure conditions. If coordination merely changes location, the company has not earned a flatter structure.</p></div>

## Authority has to become visible

A decision right is explicit authority to propose, approve, reject, override, stop, or restart an action. Economists Philippe Aghion and Jean Tirole distinguished in 1997 between [formal authority, the assigned right to decide, and real authority, effective control over what happens](https://doi.org/10.1086/262063). An organization can delegate the former while a supplier, inaccessible configuration, central data owner, or hidden approval rule retains the latter.

The replacement architecture therefore has five parts:

1. Domain owners define the outcome, case classes, limits, and escalation rules.
2. The agent interprets messy input and proposes a next step.
3. Company-controlled software checks identity, permission, policy limits, and the authoritative state: the current approved status and version of the case.
4. A named human owns exceptions, overrides, restart, and accountability.
5. The system records what happened and keeps a tested recovery path.

The software in the third step should use predefined checks rather than free-form model interpretation. An override is an authorized decision to disregard or reverse the agent’s proposal, and it should be visible in the record. Every consequential action needs an audit trail, and a named human needs stop authority: both the right and the practical mechanism to halt new actions.

Duplicate protection needs equal care. The Internet Engineering Task Force’s [2022 HTTP standard defines idempotency](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9110.html#section-9.2.2) as identical requests having the same intended effect as one request. In a business workflow, a request identifier alone is insufficient. The organization must define the unique action and its scope, handle simultaneous duplicates, store the prior outcome, check the current state while recording the internal change, and make a retry return or reconcile with that outcome rather than issue another refund.

A compact state-and-authority contract makes the boundary inspectable:

| Situation | Required outcome |
|---|---|
| Current case version, valid approver, permitted amount, unseen action identity | Release once, then store the new state and outcome |
| Proposal refers to an older case version | Reject it as stale and require a refreshed proposal |
| Approver lacks the declared authority | Reject the release and route it to the named exception owner |
| The same action identity is retried | Do not refund again; return or reconcile with the stored outcome |
| The stop flag is active or audit recording is unavailable | Halt consequential releases until the named restart authority acts |
| The payment provider succeeded but internal confirmation failed | Enter a pending reconciliation state; do not repeat blindly |

An atomic state change means the relevant internal check and update complete together or not at all. It does not mean an external payment and unrelated internal systems can always commit as one transaction. Rollback returns software or workflow configuration to a known safe version. A compensating action is a new, owned step that corrects an external effect that cannot simply be erased.

The company also needs supplier exit: a tested ability to change provider or operate manually without losing policy, state, logs, or service continuity. Contract terms, data use, logging access, required residency, model substitution, manual service, and an exit owner belong in the operating design.

Finally, an internal risk tier is a company governance label. It complements rather than replaces classification and review under the [EU AI Act, Regulation (EU) 2024/1689](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:02024R1689-20260727), the [GDPR, the EU data-protection law applicable since 2018](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32016R0679), and applicable Swedish or Norwegian employment law and collective agreements.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Agents may interpret and propose, but company-controlled systems and named people must enforce state, permission, release, audit, stop, recovery, and legal review.</p></div>

## The career system is part of the architecture

The people decision begins before the trial, not after a successful presentation. The [International Labour Organization, the United Nations agency for labor standards, reported in 2025](https://doi.org/10.54394/HETP0387) that exposure to generative AI does not imply the immediate automation of an entire occupation. Roles contain different activities, and many still require human input.

That distinction matters for coordination-heavy jobs. A manager may spend time routing requests and assembling status, but also coach people, resolve conflict, interpret weak signals, protect standards, and prepare others for larger decisions. The workflow trial should map which activities shrink, which judgment moves closer to the domain, which responsibilities remain, and which new control work appears.

A flatter company also needs a new answer to ambition. If advancement, pay, and status depend mainly on acquiring more direct reports, removing layers removes the career ladder with them. Senior expert levels, broader decision rights, transparent pay ranges, funded learning time, and explicit coaching duties must be designed before the chart changes.

Employee participation belongs in the same architecture. Social dialogue means structured discussion among the employer, workers, and their representatives. The business owner and HR or employee-relations lead should convene representatives before a trial changes work design and again before evidence is used to change roles, pay, or reporting lines. Representatives need the trial evidence, job-impact analysis, redeployment and selection rules, learning commitments, and supplier dependencies. The control owner and the named employee-relations authority must be able to pause the organizational decision when the evidence or participation process is incomplete.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>A responsible redesign separates automated routing from human contribution, then rebuilds progression, pay, learning, coaching, redeployment, and employee voice before removing a layer.</p></div>

## Put the decision on one page

Before a CIO takes structural change to an executive committee or board, the proposal should fit into a decision record that is specific enough to reject. For a hypothetical service-recovery refund trial, with every bracketed value set locally, it could read:

- **Scope and outcome:** Eligible refund cases from inbound claim through customer confirmation, payment outcome, and aligned customer and finance records.
- **Named owners:** `[Head of Customer Operations]` owns the domain outcome; `[Director of Enterprise Applications]` owns the service during the trial and after production handover; `[Head of Operational Risk or Privacy]` can block release; `[Process Assurance Lead]` owns the evidence and can reject the workflow owner’s conclusion; `[HR or Employee Relations Lead]` convenes workforce participation and can pause role changes; `[CIO or COO]` sponsors and signs the final decision.
- **Trial contract:** `[company-set dates]`, `[company-set case volume]`, and `[company-set cost ceiling]`, with model, integration, monitoring, review, exception, supplier, and manual-fallback costs included. Comparators are the current process and the strongest rules-based workflow design.
- **Case and evidence rules:** Ordinary, exception, stale, duplicate, unauthorized, disputed, and supplier-failure cases are declared before launch. Adverse cases cannot be relabeled out of scope after observation. Correct closure, human touches, repair, reopening, quality, control failures, total cost, and residual judgment are reviewed.
- **Review cadence:** `[weekly or fortnightly operating forum]` examines incidents and exceptions; `[monthly executive evidence forum]` reviews cumulative results; `[final decision date]` closes the declared evidence period.
- **Authority and recovery:** The agent may extract and propose. Company software verifies identity, state, permission, action uniqueness, and release conditions. Pending-state reconciliation, rollback, compensating action, manual service, and restart authority are tested.
- **Stop conditions:** An unauthorized, stale, or duplicate refund; missing audit record; uncontrolled personal data; unresolved supplier failure; or incomplete workforce participation halts consequential releases or blocks structural change, even when speed improves.
- **Workforce checkpoints:** Employee representatives receive the work-design proposal before the trial and the full evidence and job-impact record before any decision about roles, pay, or reporting lines.
- **Supplier dependencies:** Contract and licensing terms, data use, logging access, required residency, model substitution, manual fallback, export of company records, and the named exit owner are resolved before normal operations.
- **Decision and signatures:** Expand, narrow, move into normal operations, or stop. Organizational redesign is a separate later decision requiring sign-off from the domain, service, control, evidence, workforce, and executive owners.

The first leadership decision is therefore a reversible operating trial, not a flatter org chart. The second decision comes only after the evidence shows that complete workflows close correctly, coordination has genuinely declined, authority remains enforceable, recovery works, permanent ownership is funded, and the people commitments are credible.

An AI-first company may become materially flatter. It earns that shape by proving what changed and accepting responsibility for what follows.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Authorize a rejectable, reversible workflow trial first. Revisit the hierarchy only after evidence, controls, ownership, supplier exit, and workforce commitments survive review.</p></div>
