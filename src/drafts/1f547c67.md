An AI-first company changes where technical judgment sits before it changes how many managers it has. People in sales, finance, operations, marketing and support need enough understanding of AI to shape reliable work in their own domains. Leaders, meanwhile, need proof that a new operating model has removed coordination rather than moved it somewhere less visible.

The architecture rests on two linked claims. First, every role acquires a technical dimension. Second, when AI agents absorb meaningful execution and coordination, ownership can move closer to the work and the company can become flatter. By an AI agent, I mean a model-driven system that can interpret a goal, use business tools and propose or carry out steps within granted permissions.

I would treat the two claims differently. Distributed technical judgment is a capability the company can build now. A flatter hierarchy is a structural option that must be earned through evidence, clear authority and a credible plan for the people whose work will change.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Build technical judgment across the business now, but make any move toward a flatter company conditional on proof that agents have removed real coordination work safely.</p>
</div>

## Technical judgment moves into every function

In a [June 2026 conversation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8atlQX8HTwQ), [Arvind KC](https://openai.com/index/arvind-kc-chief-people-officer/), Chief People Officer at OpenAI, the AI research and product company behind the ChatGPT generative AI assistant, said that “every role as we go into the future is fundamentally a technical role.” He immediately explained the point in domain terms: the technology is applied within a particular field.

Technical judgment, in this context, is the ability to match a business task with what AI can do reliably, recognize where it can fail, and decide where human judgment must remain. It does not require every employee to write software. A sales leader needs to know when an agent may summarize a customer conversation but cannot make a commercial commitment. A finance specialist must distinguish reliable extraction from an exception that needs interpretation. Support and marketing teams need equally concrete boundaries around resolution, escalation, evidence and approved claims.

That knowledge cannot live only in a central AI team because the meaning of a correct outcome lives inside each function. The centre should provide common platforms, security rules, evaluation methods and purchasing standards. Domain teams should own the definition of good work, important exceptions and proposed improvements.

This is what bottom-up ownership should mean: people close to the work can initiate and shape change inside visible boundaries. It may look less tidy than a programme in which every idea travels upward for approval, but it is not uncontrolled experimentation. The organizing principle is distributed initiative with named accountability.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Every function needs enough technical judgment to design and challenge AI-supported work. Central teams set shared boundaries; domain experts define correct outcomes and own improvement within them.</p>
</div>

## Flatten the coordination, then consider the hierarchy

Business author Benjamin Simkin calls the habit of attaching AI to an old operating structure the “Retrofit Trap” in [*The AI First Company: Engineering Your Business to Thrive in the AI Economy*](https://www.amazon.co.uk/dp/B0H45WZRX5). The label matters because a fast AI step can leave the surrounding approvals, handoffs and repair work untouched.

Consider supplier onboarding. An agent might read registration documents, compare bank details with submitted records and draft a risk classification. If an analyst still copies the result into another system, a manager approves every ordinary case, master-data staff re-enter the same fields and finance repairs mismatches later, the company has accelerated document review. It has not redesigned the operating model.

A hierarchy is also a coordination system. Managers gather incomplete information, interpret policy, approve action, reconcile competing priorities and route unusual cases upward. An agent-supported workflow may take over information gathering, routine policy checks, status chasing and the routing of known exceptions. Human authority remains necessary for policy choices, genuinely novel cases, conflicts between objectives and acceptance of consequences.

That mechanism sets the evidence standard. Measure the complete workflow from its business trigger to a correctly closed case: an outcome delivered with the right records and controls, without hidden rework. For supplier onboarding, that means measuring elapsed time, human routing touches, repair effort, error and control failures, total cost including suppliers and oversight, and the share of cases that still require human judgment. A faster first step says little if the rest of the work remains.

The comparison must be decided before the trial begins. Define the case mix, exclusions, baseline or matched comparator, review period, spending limit and local pass and stop thresholds in advance. The sources reviewed here include no Swedish or Norwegian production case that establishes a safe numerical threshold for removing management layers. Any such threshold is therefore a local decision rule, not an industry benchmark, and a successful workflow trial still does not prove that an entire layer can safely disappear.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>AI-first redesign starts with an end-to-end workflow, not an isolated task. Fewer layers become plausible only when predeclared evidence shows that routing, repair and residual judgment have declined across correctly closed cases.</p>
</div>

## Make authority visible in the system

Once a workflow has met its evidence gate, the replacement architecture needs a clear division of work. Domain-mapped ownership means assigning each business outcome and its exceptions to a named function rather than giving a general AI programme responsibility for everything. The agent interprets unstructured material and proposes a next action. Ordinary business software enforces permissions, records the authoritative state, and commits consequential actions.

Authoritative state is the official record of where a case stands, such as submitted, under review, approved, active or stopped. It should not depend on what a model infers from a conversation. The same separation applies to decision rights:

| Control object | Named owner | Required decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Correct outcome and ordinary exceptions | Domain executive | What counts as complete, and which cases leave the normal path |
| Permissions and policy limits | Business control owner | Who may propose, approve, override or reject each action |
| Case state and system action | Technology service owner | Which state changes are allowed and how they are recorded |
| Quality and control evidence | Independent risk, compliance or audit function | Who verifies results separately from the trial sponsor |
| Stop, restart and residual risk | Operations, risk and accountable executive | Who pauses the workflow, who approves restart, and who accepts the risk left after controls |

Return to supplier onboarding. The agent can extract information and recommend activation, but the business system should derive permission from the authenticated requester’s identity and the current policy version. It should check the expected supplier-record version and the requester’s authority in one indivisible operation before changing the record to active. If an external bank check times out, the case enters an “outcome unknown” state until reconciliation confirms what happened.

Retries need the same discipline. Every retry of one logical operation should reuse the same stable operation identifier, so repetition cannot create a second supplier record or another business effect. This property is called idempotency. The audit trail should preserve the input, agent and policy versions, recommendation, human intervention, final action and reason for any override.

Stop authority must form one inspectable chain. The workflow owner may pause operations when a declared trigger fires. The service owner and control owner approve restart after the fault is understood and tested, while the accountable executive accepts any residual risk, meaning the risk that remains after the controls. Rollback must have an owner, a tested path and a defined limit beyond which reversal is no longer possible.

Supplier contracts are part of this architecture. Procurement, privacy and security owners should cover processing location, subprocessors, notice of model changes, access to logs, service failures, price changes, data export and deletion, and funded exit support. Otherwise, important authority remains with a vendor precisely when the company needs to inspect or leave the system.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Agents may interpret and propose, while ordinary software owns permissions, official case state and consequential actions. Named people must own exceptions, verification, stopping, restart, residual risk, rollback and supplier exit.</p>
</div>

## Redesign careers before removing roles

If agents absorb routine coordination, coordination-heavy roles will change. Some work may shrink, including status collection, approval chasing and standard exception routing. Other management work remains: coaching, conflict resolution, capability building, resource choices and accountability for outcomes. A responsible redesign separates those activities before it treats a job title as redundant.

The old hierarchy is also a career system. It distributes progression, pay, access to information, learning opportunities and professional status. Removing a layer without replacing those functions narrows ambition for specialists and weakens the route through which future leaders learn to lead.

The people design therefore belongs inside the authorization case. It should map work that disappears, work that remains and new work created by domain ownership. It should publish specialist and leadership paths with clear pay and progression, protect learning time, fund redeployment, and set fair selection principles for changed roles. For a Nordic organization, I would include HR and employee representatives while the design can still change, rather than presenting them with a finished chart.

This transition will contain conflict. Some people will want the new roles, some will need time to learn, and some may not have a place in the resulting structure. Leadership owes them clarity about timing, criteria, support and who decides. Calling that friction “resistance” would avoid the actual management duty.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>A flatter structure changes careers as well as coordination. Role mapping, pay and progression, learning, redeployment, transition funding and early employee participation are conditions of the redesign, not follow-up work.</p>
</div>

## What the board should authorize

The first proposal should ask for a bounded operating-model trial, not advance approval for a flatter company. Its authorization sheet should force local definitions into the open:

| Decision field | Entry required before work begins | Accountable role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Workflow boundary | Trigger, correct closing state, case cohort, exception mix and exclusions | Domain executive |
| Trial boundary | Start and end dates, spending cap, assigned capacity and review cadence | Executive sponsor |
| Comparator and horizon | Baseline or matched comparison, measurement window and data owner | Finance or analytics owner |
| Evidence gate | Numeric local pass and stop values for quality, control failures, routing, repair, total cost and residual judgment | Sponsor, challenged by independent verifier |
| Authority chain | Permission source, exception owner, override path, stop trigger and holder, restart approver, residual-risk accepter and rollback owner | Business, technology and risk owners |
| Supplier gate | Processing, change notice, logs, failure, cost, portability, deletion and exit terms | Procurement, privacy and security owners |
| Workforce gate | Role map, career and pay design, learning and redeployment funding, participation process and the CHRO checkpoint that can block chart change | CHRO and accountable executive |
| Permitted outcome | Scale the workflow, extend evidence collection, stop it, or return with a separate structural proposal | Executive committee or board |

Terms such as “representative,” “repeated” and “material” cannot remain movable adjectives. Representative should name the normal and exceptional cases included. Repeated should state how many consecutive review periods must pass. Material should be expressed as the local numerical change required for each measure. The evidence owner and verifier should be different people, and failure at the workflow, authority, supplier or workforce gate should produce an explicit outcome: no structural change.

This preserves the original architectural possibility without treating it as a conclusion. Technical judgment can spread through the business, agents can remove routine coordination, and ownership can move toward the domains. The org chart changes only after the work, controls and human commitments make that decision defensible.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Authorize a bounded trial with fixed scope, thresholds, owners and stop conditions. Change the hierarchy only after complete-workflow evidence, inspectable authority, supplier control and the workforce gate all support a separate structural decision.</p>
</div>
