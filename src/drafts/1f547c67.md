Two claims sit at the heart of an AI-first company. Every role acquires a technical dimension. And when agents absorb enough execution and coordination, ownership can move closer to the work and the organization can become flatter. The first claim changes roles now. The second may justify a structural decision later.

In a June 2026 interview with DECODE, a business-interview video channel, [Arvind KC](https://openai.com/index/arvind-kc-chief-people-officer/)—appointed chief people officer of OpenAI in February 2026—said that every future role would be “fundamentally a technical role” applied to a particular domain. OpenAI is an AI research and product company with an unusual operating environment. KC’s observation is a useful role-design signal, not proof that its structure transfers to a bank, manufacturer, public agency, or Nordic employer.

The second claim is the legacy article’s architectural proposition: bottom-up ownership means that more decisions originate near the work instead of waiting for the center. Here, an AI agent means software that uses a generative model to pursue a defined goal through approved tools and steps. If agents remove routine execution and dependency management, some coordination-heavy layers may become less necessary.

From the center, that can resemble disorder. The difference between an empowered edge and concealed chaos lies in complete-workflow evidence, explicit decision rights, enforceable controls, and a workforce transition people can believe in.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Distribute technical judgment now. Treat a flatter organization as a conditional operating decision, not an automatic consequence of adopting agents.</p></div>

## Put technical judgment where the work lives

Technical judgment is the ability to decide what an AI system may do within a business domain—a bounded area of responsibility—what evidence it needs, where its capability ends, and when a person must take over. Coding may help some roles, but it is not the threshold.

Consider a hypothetical disputed supplier invoice. An agent can compare the invoice with a purchase order, extract the disputed line, and propose a resolution. Procurement still understands the commercial terms. Finance knows which adjustment can be released and which creates a material commitment. The business owner knows whether the service was delivered. Technical judgment connects those facts to the system’s permitted action.

The same pattern appears elsewhere. Sales judges which contract concessions require legal or commercial authority. Operations defines valid state changes and recovery paths. Marketing tests whether sources support a claim. Support recognizes ambiguity, vulnerability, and relationship consequences. Domain expertise becomes more valuable because it now includes the ability to shape and challenge machine-supported work.

That challenge matters. A [2026 *Organization Science* experiment](https://doi.org/10.1287/orsc.2025.21838) led by organization researcher Fabrizio Dell’Acqua tested 758 consultants using GPT-4, the OpenAI model selected for the study. On one task outside the model’s measured capability frontier—the set of tasks it could handle reliably in that experiment—AI users were 19 percent less likely to reach the correct answer. The result does not describe every model or workflow. It shows why fluency cannot substitute for task-specific judgment.

[Article 4 of the EU AI Act](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:02024R1689-20260727), the European Union’s 2024 regulation for AI systems, reinforces this direction. It requires providers and deployers to take measures supporting AI literacy according to people’s knowledge, experience, training, and use context. AI literacy here means enough practical understanding to use, question, and escalate the system appropriately.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Every function needs domain-specific judgment about AI capability, evidence, failure, and escalation. That is a business capability, not a demand that everyone become a programmer.</p></div>

## Follow the work past the AI output

Benjamin Simkin, a business author, introduced the term Retrofit Trap in his 2026 book [*The AI First Company: Engineering Your Business to Thrive in the AI Economy*](https://www.amazon.co.uk/dp/B0H45WZRX5). The term describes adding AI to an isolated task while preserving the surrounding process. It gives this failure a useful name; it does not establish how prevalent the failure is.

Return to the disputed invoice. The agent reads the documents and drafts a recommendation in seconds. People may still have to verify delivery, find the current purchase order, route a pricing exception, secure approval, update the accounts-payable record, notify the supplier, and reconcile the ledger. The output arrived faster. The dependency chain survived.

In 1994, researchers [Thomas Malone and Kevin Crowston](https://doi.org/10.1145/174666.174668) defined coordination as managing dependencies between activities. Organization-design scholar [Jay Galbraith’s 1974 information-processing account](https://doi.org/10.1287/inte.4.3.28) explained why established rules can handle routine cases while hierarchy receives exceptions that those rules cannot resolve. Agents may absorb information assembly, status collection, routine routing, and known-exception handling. Managers also allocate resources, reconcile conflicting goals, coach people, interpret weak signals, and remain accountable for difficult choices. Those responsibilities do not vanish with the routing work.

The correct unit of evidence is therefore the complete workflow: the path from trigger through decision, action, record update, exception handling, and closure. For this article’s management model, a correctly closed case is a complete outcome that meets declared quality and policy conditions, updates the authoritative business record, counts every human intervention and repair, and leaves no hidden cleanup behind. This is a proposed decision unit, not an established industry benchmark.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>An AI-first operating model changes the dependency chain, not merely the speed of one output. Judge the complete workflow through correctly closed cases.</p></div>

## Let the workflow earn the flatter structure

Before removing a reporting layer, run a bounded workflow trial whose result can be rejected. Define ordinary, disputed, stale-data, duplicate, unauthorized, and supplier-failure cases before observing the results. Compare the agent-supported design with the current process and the strongest reasonable conventional alternative, such as workflow software applying predefined business rules. Select that comparator before the evidence arrives.

Measure elapsed time from trigger to closure, human routing and approval touches, repair effort, rework, reopening, and repeat contact. Then examine outcome quality, policy and security failures, privacy incidents, total operating cost, and the human judgment that remains. Model fees, integration, monitoring, review, exceptions, and supplier charges all belong in the cost. So does work that moves into informal supervision or frontline cleanup.

The task-local research found no validated Swedish or Norwegian production case establishing a numerical threshold for safely removing a management layer. That is an evidence gap, not proof that no relevant case exists. It prevents a CIO from borrowing a reassuring number and calling the structural decision settled.

The trial must answer a sharper question: which responsibility disappeared, which moved closer to the domain, and which still requires human authority? A layer becomes a candidate for redesign only when routine coordination has durably left the workflow without returning as repair, risk work, ceremonial approval, or supplier dependence.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Predeclare the cases, comparator, measures, and rejection conditions. Flattening becomes defensible only when complete-workflow evidence shows that coordination has genuinely declined.</p></div>

## Give every action an owner and a state

In 1997, economists [Philippe Aghion and Jean Tirole](https://doi.org/10.1086/262063) distinguished formal authority—the assigned right to decide—from real authority, the practical ability to determine what happens. A flatter company fails when a domain team holds the title while a vendor setting, inaccessible model instruction, centralized data owner, or hidden approval rule holds the real decision.

Decision rights must therefore name who may propose, approve, reject, override, stop, and restart. A governed edge has four parts:

- Domain owners define outcomes, case classes, limits, and escalation rules.
- An agent interprets unstructured information and proposes a next step.
- Company-controlled software checks identity, permission, current case state, policy limits, duplicate protection, and release conditions.
- A named human owns exceptions, overrides, stop decisions, restart, and accountability.

The company-controlled software should apply predefined logic rather than free-form model interpretation. The agent may understand a messy invoice and suggest a credit. It should not decide whether the requester has authority, whether the invoice version is current, or whether the same credit has already been released.

Suppose the release request times out. The authoritative state is the current approved status and version of the case. A stable action identifier tells the system which intended credit is being attempted. The Internet Engineering Task Force, the open standards body for Internet protocols, defines idempotency in its [2022 HTTP standard RFC 9110](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9110.html#section-9.2.2): repeated identical requests have the same intended effect as one request. In this business workflow, the practical contract should return or reconcile the stored outcome instead of issuing a second credit.

If the supplier received the credit notice but the internal ledger update is uncertain, the case enters a visible pending state. A named owner reconciles it before another release. Rollback returns internal software or workflow rules to a previous safe version. When an external commitment cannot simply be erased, a compensating action is a new, authorized action that corrects the effect.

An audit trail records the actor, action, time, source state, outcome, and recovery path. Every consequential action needs an audit trail, and a named human needs stop authority: both the right and the practical mechanism to halt new actions.

Legal review remains specific to the workflow. The EU AI Act’s override and stop provisions apply where a system meets the regulation’s legal definition of high-risk AI; they do not automatically cover every business agent. The [General Data Protection Regulation](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32016R0679), the EU privacy regulation applicable since 2018, governs personal-data processing and certain solely automated decisions with legal or similarly significant effects. An internal risk label does not replace either legal classification.

Operational control also requires supplier exit: a tested ability to change provider or use a manual service mode without losing business state, policy, logs, or the ability to recover. The exit path needs an owner before the pilot becomes normal operations.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Push interpretation toward the edge while keeping permissions, authoritative state, duplicate protection, audit, recovery, and stop mechanisms under visible company control.</p></div>

## Build a career system for the flatter company

When routine coordination shrinks, some coordination-heavy roles will change. That does not make every management responsibility redundant. Coaching, conflict resolution, resource allocation, difficult judgment, and accountability still need capable owners. The organization must separate those responsibilities from forwarding, chasing, translating, and assembling status.

The [International Labour Organization](https://doi.org/10.54394/HETP0387), the United Nations agency for labor standards and research, concluded in its 2025 generative-AI exposure analysis that exposure to AI-supported tasks does not imply the disappearance of an occupation. Transformation is more likely because most occupations retain work requiring human input. That finding does not promise a painless transition. It tells leaders to redesign work at the activity level before making staffing assumptions.

A credible people architecture states which activities shrink, which judgment responsibilities remain, and how redeployment and selection decisions will be made. It funds learning time. It creates expert progression, status, and pay growth without requiring headcount ownership. It names who will coach people when fewer managerial roles remain. Ambition needs somewhere credible to go when promotion no longer means managing a larger team.

Participation must begin before the trial changes work design and happen again before evidence changes roles, pay, or reporting lines. Social dialogue means structured discussion among the employer, employees, and their representatives, with enough information and time to influence the decision. In covered Swedish settings, [Section 11 of the Co-Determination Act of 1976](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-1976580-om-medbestammande-i-arbetslivet_sfs-1976-580/) can require negotiation before qualifying significant changes. Applicability depends on current law, collective agreements, and local review; a Norwegian organization must examine its own statutory and collective arrangements.

Employee representatives also need a practical escalation path and the authority to request a pause when the evidence or transition commitments are incomplete. Otherwise participation becomes another ceremonial approval in a supposedly flatter company.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Redesign progression, pay, learning, coaching, participation, and redeployment before removing organizational rungs. The people architecture is part of the operating model.</p></div>

## Make structural change a second decision

Put the trial contract on one page before work begins. It should contain:

- **Workflow and closure:** the trigger, complete outcome, authoritative records, case classes, and definition of a correctly closed case.
- **Ownership:** named domain, technology, control, executive, and evidence owners, plus the person accountable after the workflow enters normal operations. The evidence owner must be independent enough to reject the delivery owner’s conclusion.
- **Test design:** the current baseline, strongest conventional comparator, company-set evidence period, adverse cases, and a rule preventing failed cases from being relabeled out of scope.
- **Decision rights and controls:** who may propose, approve, release, override, stop, restart, reconcile, and authorize compensating action.
- **Failure and exit:** stop conditions, rollback and recovery paths, audit requirements, manual service mode, supplier dependencies, and the owner of provider exit.
- **People commitments:** affected activities and roles, learning time, selection and redeployment rules, career and pay design, coaching ownership, participation dates, and pause authority.

At the review, choose whether to expand, narrow, move the workflow into normal operations, or stop it. Treat organizational redesign as a separate decision. A successful document-generation trial cannot authorize delayering. Neither can a faster median time if difficult cases, hidden repair, or control failures have been excluded.

The executive and board discussion can then use three rejectable gates. Workflow proof asks whether complete cases close correctly with less net coordination. Authority proof asks whether permissions, state, exceptions, audit, stop, recovery, and supplier exit work in practice. People proof asks whether the organization has a funded, participatory, and credible transition for roles, careers, pay, and coaching.

An AI-first company may become flatter because routine coordination no longer needs an organizational home. Its deeper architecture is judgment at the edges, enforceable controls at the core, named humans at consequential boundaries, and a career system people can still believe in. If any proof gate fails, keep the chart and improve the workflow. If all three hold across adverse cases and the declared evidence period, structural change becomes a defensible consequence of operating reality rather than an act of faith.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Authorize a reversible workflow trial first. Change the org chart only after workflow, authority, and people proof all support a separate structural decision.</p></div>
