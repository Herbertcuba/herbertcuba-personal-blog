An AI-first company begins with two linked propositions: every business role gains a technical dimension, and ownership can move closer to the work as agents absorb routine execution and coordination. The first changes who needs judgment. The second changes what hierarchy is for.

In a June 2026 interview, [Arvind KC](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8atlQX8HTwQ&t=342s), appointed Chief People Officer of [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/arvind-kc-chief-people-officer/) that February, said future roles would be “fundamentally a technical role” applied to a particular domain. OpenAI is the AI research and product company behind ChatGPT. Its experience is instructive, but one frontier company cannot prove how a bank, manufacturer, public agency, or Nordic employer should organize.

The mechanism still matters. An AI agent is more than a chatbot returning an answer: it is software built around a model that pursues a defined goal through approved tools and steps. When agents take over enough routine execution and dependency management, decisions can move toward the people who understand the domain. That creates room for flatter structures and more bottom-up ownership.

Room is not proof. A responsible leader changes the hierarchy only when complete-workflow evidence shows that coordination has actually disappeared, authority remains visible, failure is controllable, and the people affected have a credible path forward.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Distributed technical judgment and agent-absorbed coordination can move ownership toward the edge, but structural change must follow operating evidence.</p></div>

## Put technical judgment where the facts live

Technical judgment is the ability to decide what an AI system may do in a business domain, what evidence it needs, where its capability boundary lies, and when a person must take over. It does not mean that everyone must learn to code.

Sales must know which contract facts an agent may extract and which concessions require commercial authority. Finance must separate routine reconciliation from decisions that change risk or create an external commitment. Support must distinguish a standard policy case from one involving vulnerability, ambiguity, or relationship damage. Marketing must evaluate sources and claims rather than accept fluent copy. Operations must define valid states, failure conditions, and recovery paths.

This judgment cannot sit entirely in a central AI team because the decisive context lives inside the function. The central team can provide platforms, controls, and specialist support. The domain still owns what acceptable work means.

The boundary matters because AI capability is uneven. In a 2026 experiment involving 758 consultants, [Fabrizio Dell’Acqua and his coauthors](https://doi.org/10.1287/orsc.2025.21838) found that participants using GPT-4 were 19 percent less likely to reach a correct solution on one selected task outside the model’s tested capability frontier. Knowing when not to rely on the system is part of the job.

The [EU AI Act](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:02024R1689-20260727), the European Union’s 2024 AI regulation, reinforces this direction. Article 4 requires providers and deployers to support an appropriate level of AI literacy while considering people’s knowledge, training, experience, and use context. That is role-appropriate competence, not universal engineering training.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Technical judgment belongs with domain accountability: people must know where AI helps, what evidence it needs, and when escalation is necessary.</p></div>

## Follow the case until nothing is hiding

Benjamin Simkin, a business author and coach, calls a common design error the “Retrofit Trap” in his 2026 book [*The AI First Company*](https://www.amazon.co.uk/dp/B0H45WZRX5): AI accelerates an activity while the old structure around it remains. The name describes a design problem, not a measured prevalence rate.

Consider a hypothetical customer billing dispute. An agent reads the invoice and contract, identifies a likely discrepancy, and drafts an explanation in seconds. Yet people may still locate the latest commercial terms, confirm service delivery, approve a credit, update the ledger and customer record, send the response, and resolve an appeal. The generated answer was faster. The operating model was not necessarily different.

A complete workflow is the whole path from the triggering event through decisions, exceptions, external effects, official records, and closure. In 1994, coordination researchers [Thomas W. Malone and Kevin Crowston](https://doi.org/10.1145/174666.174668) defined coordination as “managing dependencies between activities.” Organization-design scholar [Jay R. Galbraith](https://doi.org/10.1287/inte.4.3.28) had explained in 1974 why hierarchy often enters when established rules cannot settle an exception.

Judge the trial by correctly closed cases. A correctly closed case meets its declared quality and policy conditions, updates the authoritative business records, counts every human intervention and repair, and leaves no concealed cleanup. This is a proposed management contract, not an established industry benchmark. Its value is that it makes the organizational claim falsifiable.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>A faster AI output is not an AI-first operating model; the evidence unit is a complete case closed correctly, with routing, repair, and records included.</p></div>

## Let evidence—not enthusiasm—remove coordination

A management role is rarely just a bundle of handoffs. Managers also reconcile conflicting priorities, allocate resources, coach people, interpret weak signals, carry accountability, and decide novel exceptions. Agents may reduce status assembly, routine routing, standard approvals, and known-exception handling. They do not inherit judgment over risk appetite, employment decisions, unresolved conflict, or consequential trade-offs merely because they can move information faster.

A workflow trial therefore has to separate visible output from displaced work. Measure correctly closed cases by predeclared case class, elapsed time, quality, and reopening. Separately count human routing, approvals, repair effort, control failures, residual judgment, and total cost—including integration, models, monitoring, review, exceptions, and supplier fees.

Compare the agent-supported design with both the current process and the strongest conventional alternative. Rules-based workflow software moves cases through predefined states; robotic process automation repeats predefined interface actions. Either may remove structured coordination more cheaply and reliably than an agent. The agent should earn its place where interpreting unstructured or changing information adds value.

No validated Swedish or Norwegian production case in the available evidence supplies a numerical threshold for removing a management layer. That does not prove that delayering will fail. It means the CIO cannot borrow a benchmark and call the decision evidence-based.

A layer becomes a candidate for redesign only when named responsibilities have disappeared or moved sustainably toward the domain. If coordination returns as frontline cleanup, informal supervision, risk review, or supplier dependence, the organization has moved the work rather than removed it.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Flatten only after a complete-workflow comparison shows that coordination declined without resurfacing as hidden labor, weaker control, or higher total cost.</p></div>

## Put freedom inside firm boundaries

A flatter edge needs a more explicit center. Domain-mapped ownership means assigning a defined business outcome and its difficult decisions to people with the relevant expertise. A decision right states who may propose, approve, reject, override, stop, or restart an action.

Economists [Philippe Aghion and Jean Tirole](https://doi.org/10.1086/262063) distinguished formal authority—the assigned right to decide—from real authority, the effective control over what happens, in 1997. That distinction becomes urgent when a domain team appears empowered but a vendor setting, inaccessible model instruction, central data owner, or hidden approval rule still determines the outcome.

The replacement architecture should be inspectable:

- Domain-mapped ownership starts with a named business owner for the outcome, not a committee around the tool.
- The agent may interpret the complaint and propose a credit.
- Company-controlled software should verify identity, permission, the authoritative state—the current approved case version—and the financial limit before any credit is released.
- A named human owns exceptions, overrides, stopping, restarting, and the final accountability.
- Every consequential action needs an audit trail, and a named human needs stop authority: both the right and the practical mechanism to halt new actions.

An audit trail records the actor, action, time, relevant state, and outcome so that a decision can be reconstructed. Stop authority is meaningful only when the owner has enough information, competence, time, and system access to use it.

Duplicate protection deserves the same precision. The Internet Engineering Task Force, which publishes open internet standards, defines idempotency in its 2022 [RFC 9110](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9110.html#section-9.2.2): repeating an identical request has the same intended server effect as making it once. In the billing case, a retry must not post the same credit twice. A stable action identity, a state check, and a stored outcome are therefore part of the business control, not optional implementation detail.

Real-world effects also resist neat reversal. If a credit is issued but an internal confirmation fails, the workflow should enter a pending reconciliation state instead of retrying blindly. Rollback returns internal software or workflow rules to an earlier safe version. A compensating action is a new, authorized step that corrects an external effect that cannot simply be erased.

Finally, real authority must survive a supplier failure. If the supplier disappears, the company must retain its records, policy, manual operating mode, and a tested exit path.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Agents may interpret and propose, while company-controlled software enforces permissions and state and named people retain exception, stop, recovery, and accountability rights.</p></div>

## Rebuild the career bargain

The old pyramid is not only a coordination structure. It is also a career system. Management layers provide pay progression, status, coaching, access to consequential decisions, and preparation for broader responsibility. Removing a layer without replacing those functions turns an operating-model change into a broken promise.

The [International Labour Organization](https://doi.org/10.54394/HETP0387), the United Nations agency for labor standards and research, concluded in 2025 that exposure to generative-AI-capable tasks does not imply immediate automation of an entire occupation. Transformation is more likely because occupations continue to contain work requiring human input.

Start with the role, not a headcount target. Identify which routing activities may shrink, which judgment remains, and which new responsibilities appear in evidence review, exception analysis, domain stewardship, coaching, and permanent service ownership. Then publish the selection and redeployment rules before people have to infer them from an altered reporting line.

A credible career architecture needs expert levels that do not require managing headcount, transparent pay bands, funded learning time, named coaching responsibilities, and access to larger decisions. Employees should not have to choose between professional ambition and staying close to their craft.

It also needs social dialogue: structured discussion and negotiation among employers, workers, and their representatives. In Sweden, Section 11 of the [Co-Determination in the Workplace Act](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-1976580-om-medbestammande-i-arbetslivet_sfs-1976-580/), enacted in 1976, requires negotiation before qualifying significant changes to activities or working and employment conditions. Applicability depends on the organization, collective agreements, and current local advice.

Use two workforce checkpoints. The first comes before a trial changes how work is performed. The second comes before evidence is used to change roles, pay, or reporting lines. Employee representatives need usable evidence and a practical way to pause the structural decision when the transition commitments are incomplete.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>A flatter organization requires a replacement career system covering progression, pay, coaching, learning, participation, selection, and redeployment before roles are changed.</p></div>

## Make two decisions, not one

The executive paper should not ask for an AI-first reorganization. It should ask for a reversible workflow trial with a separate, later decision about structure.

Put the first decision on one page:

- Define the complete workflow, its predeclared case classes, and the outcome that counts as correct closure.
- Name the domain owner, technology and permanent service owner, control owner, independent evidence owner, and executive sponsor.
- Fix the evidence period and compare the trial with the current process and the strongest conventional automation design.
- Track workflow outcomes, hidden human work, residual judgment, control failures, recovery performance, supplier dependence, and total cost.
- State permissions, approval limits, exception owners, stop and restart conditions, audit requirements, and the tested recovery path.
- Record the workforce checkpoints, learning commitments, redeployment principles, and who may reject a structural conclusion.

The workflow decision can expand, narrow, move into normal operations, or stop. The evidence owner must be independent enough to reject a favorable interpretation when adverse cases say otherwise.

Only then should leadership consider the second decision: whether specific responsibilities have moved or disappeared sufficiently to change reporting lines, roles, pay, and management scope. That decision needs its own employee participation, risk review, and accountable owner. A successful workflow trial is evidence for discussion, not automatic permission to restructure.

A company is not AI-first because its org chart has fewer boxes. It is AI-first when technical judgment sits close to the domain, authority can be seen and exercised, controls survive failure, and people can see a credible future in the new structure. If those conditions justify fewer layers, change the chart. If they do not, keep the hierarchy and continue redesigning the work.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Authorize a reversible operating trial first. Revisit the hierarchy only when workflow evidence, control ownership, and workforce commitments make the separate restructuring decision defensible.</p></div>
