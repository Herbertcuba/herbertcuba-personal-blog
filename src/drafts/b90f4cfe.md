After 650 proposed ideas had failed, who had the authority to fund another round?

That question sits beneath [Anthropic's account](https://www.anthropic.com/research/riemann-zeta) of an unreleased research version of Claude, the AI company's model. Anthropic reports that the project used two sessions in Claude Code, its tool-using environment for coding and research, and produced 31 million output tokens. An output token is a unit of text generated by a model. The company also reports an initial 650 ideas that did not work, followed by roughly 60 subagents, meaning delegated AI runs working on parts of the problem, over about a day and a half.

These numbers are not a price tag. Anthropic has not published the input-token count, model settings, full tool and compute use, human effort, or a denominator of comparable failed campaigns. Still, the case reveals a consequential shift: an organization can now budget for far more attempts without asking one person to carry every dead end. I think this makes the right to continue more important than the ability to continue.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Machine persistence can now be funded in batches, but the reported scale is an incomplete cost record. The leadership question is who may keep the search running, and on what evidence.</p></div>

## The result is narrow, real, and still under review

The Riemann zeta function is a mathematical function closely connected to the distribution of prime numbers. In 1859, German mathematician Bernhard Riemann proposed that all its relevant, or “nontrivial,” zeros lie on a location called the critical line. That unproved claim is the Riemann hypothesis. A lower bound proves only a minimum share of zeros on that line as mathematicians count farther up the function, using a size measure called height.

The [August 13, 2026 arXiv preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13637v1), posted to an open repository for scholarly papers, reports an unconditional lower bound of at least two thirds, optimized to 0.6725. “Unconditional” means the proof does not assume the Riemann hypothesis is true. The previous record was 5/12, or about 41.7%, and had stood since 2020. Number theorist J. B. Conrey had crossed two fifths in a [peer-reviewed 1989 paper](https://doi.org/10.1515/crll.1989.399.1), so the popular 37-year framing describes a long research lineage, not 37 years without human progress.

The current provenance matters. The arXiv version lists Anthropic mathematicians Levent Alpöge and Ralph Furman and says Claude discovered the proof while the listed authors verified and communicated it. Anthropic says outside number theorists Brian Conrey and Dan Goldston also examined the work on short notice. A [public Lean 4 companion](https://github.com/anthropics/zeta-23-lean) encodes the argument for checking by a proof-assistant language. Formal verification can catch gaps relative to a precisely stated theorem and its declared assumptions; it does not replace independent replication, judgment about significance, or conventional peer review. Those broader forms of acceptance had not yet occurred.

This is therefore a serious and inspectable result with a deliberately limited status. It also cannot establish that 650 failures caused the discovery. The proof drew on decades of human mathematics and a new linear-algebraic reading of earlier work. One successful campaign tells us that scaled search can surface a valid result in a favorable setting, not that another batch of attempts will rescue any problem.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Claude advanced a proven minimum share of certain zeta zeros; it did not prove the Riemann hypothesis. The fresh preprint, human checks, and formal artifact strengthen the claim, while conventional peer review and independent replication remain open.</p></div>

## More attempts move the bottleneck

Organizations have always paid for thinking through salaries, laboratories, and research grants. What changes with agent systems is the granularity of the purchase. A leader can authorize another thousand machine trajectories, run many in parallel, and preserve their traces without requiring a researcher to stake time and reputation on every attempt. Fatigue, embarrassment, and career risk were not measured in this project, but they are human limits that a compute budget does not experience in the same way.

That does not make truth cheap. The zeta problem was unusually suitable because proposed proofs could be checked numerically, challenged by specialists, compared with prior literature, and encoded in a proof assistant. A product strategy or acquisition thesis has no equivalent critical line. If success is vague, tireless agents can spend months producing polished support for the assumption they were asked to test.

Evidence from ordinary work gives the same warning from two directions. A [2023 National Bureau of Economic Research working paper](https://www.nber.org/papers/w31161), based on 5,179 customer-support agents, found that access to an AI assistant raised issues resolved per hour by 14% on average, with larger gains among novice workers. In a different setting, a [2025 randomized study by METR](https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/), an independent AI research group, found that 16 experienced open-source developers took 19% longer on 246 real software issues when allowed to use early-2025 AI tools. METR now treats that result as a historical snapshot rather than a forecast for newer systems. Neither study concerns frontier mathematics, but together they show why output volume cannot stand in for economic value.

The relevant measure is full cost per independently accepted outcome. That includes model and tool use, reviewer time, delay, replication, and the opportunity cost of questions left unfunded. Once machine search expands, verification capacity often becomes the scarce resource.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Buying more attempts shifts scarcity toward clear tests and independent review. Persistent output becomes valuable only when accepted results justify the complete campaign cost.</p></div>

## Give every search an expiry date

A stopping rule written after enthusiasm fades is usually a justification for whatever has already been spent. The launch decision should instead create a short search mandate that expires by default.

A useful mandate can fit on one page:

- **Question and comparator:** What answerable question is being funded, and how will the campaign be compared with the current process?
- **Evidence gate:** What would count as success, disconfirmation, novelty, and independent acceptance?
- **Budget and capacity:** What are the caps on full cost, time, tool permissions, and reviewer hours?
- **Decision rights:** Who owns delivery, who verifies results, and who alone may renew the mandate? The delivery team and its agents may not renew themselves.
- **Decision date and closure duty:** When does permission expire, what records must be archived, and who closes access and credentials?

The state should be equally plain. **ACTIVE** permits work only until the recorded expiry. **PAUSED** blocks new runs while evidence or capacity is reviewed. **CLOSED** is final for that mandate; reopening requires a new record and a new decision. Before any batch launches, the system should read the current authoritative state. An approval copied before its expiry, or a renewal submitted by the delivery agents themselves, must fail at that boundary.

For a first enterprise pilot, use a six-week horizon and fill in the thresholds before launch. Require at least **[X]** independently accepted outcomes or **[Y]%** improvement over the current process, keep full cost below **[Z]**, and cap the verification queue at **[N] reviewer-hours**. Pause when two consecutive batches produce no new testable hypothesis or when the review queue breaches its cap. At the decision date, compare accepted outcomes, reviewer delay, unsupported renewals, later-reversed closures, and complete campaign cost. If the evidence gate is not met, close the mandate and preserve the rejected paths so the next search does not pay for the same mistakes.

The brackets matter because no universal threshold follows from one mathematics case. Leaders must choose values that reflect the decision's stakes and the next-best use of the budget. Humans still contribute ideas, context, relationships, and judgment. Their distinctive responsibility is to decide when more thinking deserves more capital, and to make that decision before tirelessness starts to look like proof.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Launch persistent agents with an expiring mandate, separate delivery from acceptance and renewal, enforce ACTIVE, PAUSED, and CLOSED states, and compare the full campaign with today's process.</p></div>
