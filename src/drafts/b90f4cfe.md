An organization can now make a decision that used to be partly made by a researcher's body: pay for one more attempt.

In August 2026, [Anthropic, the AI company behind the Claude AI system](https://www.anthropic.com/research/riemann-zeta), reported that an unreleased research version of Claude tried 650 ideas that did not work before finding a new result in mathematics. The project used 31 million output tokens, meaning metered units of text generated by the model, and about 60 subagents, delegated AI runs working on parts of the problem. Anthropic says the work took roughly a day and a half across two sessions in Claude Code, its coding-agent environment.

Because Anthropic has published neither the project's full cost nor a controlled comparison, the figures cannot tell us which part of the setup caused the result. Their strategic importance lies elsewhere: repeated search can be provisioned, measured, and extended after a human researcher might have run out of time, attention, or institutional support. I think that moves the hardest leadership question upstream. Before asking how long an AI system can continue, we need to decide which problem deserves the next attempt.

The case still demonstrates that leaders can allocate large-scale persistence, making question selection and stopping part of capital discipline.</p></div>

## A narrow theorem reveals a broad change

The [Riemann zeta function](https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/564f962e60643842f5fcb4a17c9dbc8f608f1c37.pdf) is a mathematical function whose zeros carry information about how prime numbers are distributed. In 1859, German mathematician Bernhard Riemann proposed that all the special, or “nontrivial,” zeros relevant to his hypothesis sit on the critical line, where the real part of the input is one half. The Riemann hypothesis remains unproved. A lower bound is narrower: it establishes the minimum share of those zeros known to lie on that line.

Claude's preprint raises that proven minimum from 5/12 to at least 2/3, a change of 25 percentage points, and reports an optimized constant of 0.6725. The previous 5/12 record had stood since 2020. The popular “37-year record” description reaches back to number theorist J. B. Conrey's [1989 peer-reviewed result](https://doi.org/10.1515/crll.1989.399.1), although mathematicians improved the bound between his paper and 2020.

The mechanism matters more than the headline. The successful argument drew on decades of prior mathematics, including [a peer-reviewed 2024 paper by four number theorists](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04799), and added a new way to read an existing expression through linear algebra. During the search, Claude's agents wrote scripts, ran numerical checks, looked for counterexamples, reviewed one another's work, and downloaded 54 papers to check whether the idea was already known. Failure became useful because it was connected to tests and feedback.

The evidence also has levels. Anthropic released a preprint, says internal mathematicians validated it and outside number theorists examined it on short notice, and published a [Lean 4 formalization](https://github.com/anthropics/zeta-23-lean). Lean 4 is a proof-assistant language that checks a precisely encoded statement and its logical dependencies. That artifact makes the proof easier to inspect, while journal peer review and independent replication remain future tests.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Claude advanced a specific lower bound rather than proving the Riemann hypothesis. Its search combined prior human mathematics, a conceptual step, repeated testing, formal checking, and human review; persistence worked inside an evidence-rich system.</p></div>

## The first batch needs an entry decision

Mathematics is unusually friendly to persistent search because a proposed proof can be attacked line by line. Most enterprise questions have softer edges. An agent asked to “find a growth opportunity” can keep producing plausible narratives without ever encountering decisive evidence that it is wrong.

Results from ordinary work reinforce that caution. A 2023 [National Bureau of Economic Research study](https://www.nber.org/papers/w31161), from a U.S. economics research organization and based on 5,179 customer-support agents, found that access to a conversational assistant raised issues resolved per hour by 14% on average. In a different setting, a 2025 [randomized study by the independent AI evaluator METR](https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/) found that 16 experienced open-source developers took 19% longer on 246 real software issues when allowed to use then-current AI tools. The settings are too different to combine into one productivity score. Together, they show why activity and confidence cannot stand in for measured value.

Before approving an agent search, the organization's existing portfolio forum, the group that ranks competing investments, should require a short Tireless Intelligence Charter: a versioned mandate for one finite campaign. It should name the decision the work could improve, compare its expected value with the best unfunded alternative, and state a baseline plus a result that would count as meaningful progress. It should also record what evidence would disprove the working hunch and reserve an independent reviewer before the first batch begins.

The same entry decision must bound authority. The charter should specify permitted tools, data classification, retention and residency, a reversible way to leave the supplier, and the person with power to stop the work. Where a result could affect employees or customers, it also needs a route for them to contest the decision. If leaders cannot supply a measurable test, reviewer capacity, bounded access, and an accountable sponsor, the question is not ready for tireless search.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>A worthy search starts with a material decision, a baseline, possible disproof, an available independent reviewer, bounded data and tools, and a named sponsor. Admission belongs in the normal portfolio process because every approved campaign displaces another use of money and attention.</p></div>

## Continuation has to earn its budget

Once admitted, the campaign should receive a batch rather than an open tap. Its budget includes model usage, elapsed time, tool permissions, and reviewer hours. At expiry, the portfolio forum should examine independently accepted outcomes, full cost including review and replication, reviewer delay, renewals granted without new evidence, and stops later judged premature. These measures reveal whether the organization is learning or merely producing more material than it can check.

Stage gates, predeclared tests, circuit breakers, and access controls are established management and engineering practices. The AI-specific pressure comes from speed: agents can generate candidate answers and polished reasons to continue faster than independent reviewers can verify either. A renewal should therefore require new evidence that could prove the current hunch wrong, an improved validated measure, or successful reproduction by a method with a different failure mode. The campaign pauses when the same failure recurs without a new idea, reviewers cannot reproduce the result, required access exceeds the charter, or another portfolio candidate has a stronger expected return on the next batch.

The rule also needs a technical boundary. Store the mandate in the authoritative workflow record. Before every new subagent or tool action, the orchestration layer, meaning the software that coordinates the agents, should make one fail-closed check of the mandate's version, expiry, and concurrency limit, the maximum number of tasks allowed at once. If the record is missing, stale, expired, or self-renewed by the delivery team, the software refuses the next dispatch. A stop should cancel queued work and withdraw temporary credentials, while the charter states in advance whether work already running is cancelled or allowed to finish safely.

Finally, keep the record honest. Board-facing evidence should link the exact preprint version and the exact saved version, or commit, of the formal-proof repository. It should label the present review status and state which event would change it, such as independent replication, journal review, a correction, or withdrawal. Failed hypotheses, tests, and sources should remain in an archive so a stopped campaign leaves reusable knowledge instead of inviting another system to rediscover the same dead ends.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>A renewal earns funding through accepted results, full-cost evidence, available review capacity, and a valid mandate. Runtime controls refuse unauthorized continuation, while a versioned evidence record makes both success and failure auditable.</p></div>

## What remains human

People still do far more than hold the budget. Human mathematicians built the research frontier, people chose the challenge, specialists interpreted the result, and reviewers still determine how much confidence it deserves. Technical insight, domain judgment, relationships, and ethical responsibility remain part of the work.

Leadership now has to own two kinds of error. Stopping too early may abandon a valuable line of inquiry, so premature stops should be reviewed and the appeal path should be real. Continuing too easily consumes money, reviewer attention, and authority while the system manufactures increasingly persuasive reasons to persist. Neither risk disappears because the agents are tireless.

The economic shift is that another attempt can be purchased without asking a person to absorb all of its fatigue and career exposure. The attempt remains costly and uncertain, but the choice becomes visible, fundable, and accountable. The organizations that benefit will be the ones willing to say, before the search begins, what evidence earns another round and who must say no when it does not arrive.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Tireless systems widen the space of questions an organization can pursue, but they cannot own the trade-off. Humans remain responsible for choosing the question, interpreting the evidence, protecting affected people, and ending a campaign that no longer earns its next attempt.</p></div>
