On August 10, 2026, Anthropic, the AI company behind Claude, [reported](https://www.anthropic.com/research/riemann-zeta) that an unreleased research version of its system had generated and tried 650 ideas that did not work. After that initial search, it coordinated roughly 60 subagents, delegated AI processes assigned narrower parts of the work. Across two sessions in Claude Code, Anthropic's environment for agents that can use files and run software tools, the effort consumed 31 million output tokens, units of model-generated text used to meter usage.

The result concerns the Riemann zeta function, a mathematical object whose zeros reveal patterns in the distribution of prime numbers. In 1859, German mathematician Bernhard Riemann proposed what became the Riemann hypothesis: all of the function's nontrivial zeros, special inputs where its value becomes zero, lie on the critical line, a precise line in the number system used by the function where each input's real component is one-half. Claude did not prove that hypothesis. Its [paper](https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/564f962e60643842f5fcb4a17c9dbc8f608f1c37.pdf) raised the unconditional lower bound, the minimum share mathematicians can prove lies on the line without assuming the hypothesis, from 5/12 to at least 2/3. The claim that every zero lies there remains open.

Persistence mattered, but it shared the stage with decades of human mathematics, a new interpretation of earlier work, computation, and several kinds of checking. I think the deeper change is economic. A large search that once would have competed directly with scarce human attention can now be provisioned and metered as computing capacity. Fatigue, shame, and career risk becoming a budget is a useful metaphor for that shift, not a psychological finding from Anthropic's project. Leadership now has to decide which questions deserve this capacity, what will count as evidence, and when the spending must end.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Claude advanced a specific mathematical lower bound rather than solving the Riemann hypothesis. The strategic change is that sustained search can now be funded as a metered resource, making question choice, evidence standards, and termination central leadership decisions.</p></div>

## What the 650 failures actually bought

Anthropic says the agents ran 2,400 commands that launched scripts and checks, wrote hundreds of Python programs, tested calculations against known zeta zeros, searched for counterexamples, and downloaded 54 papers from arXiv, an open scholarly repository that hosts work both before and after journal review. The literature search helped test whether the finding was already known; it could not settle novelty by itself. In the project materials reviewed here, Anthropic does not define exactly how one of the 650 ideas was counted or explain how duplicate paths were controlled. Even with that limit, the public account describes more than a stream of guesses because candidate ideas repeatedly met tools, calculations, and criticism.

For leaders, the useful unit is therefore a traceable cycle: a candidate hypothesis enters a bounded tool run, an outcome check or counterexample challenges it, and the evidence or rejection is recorded before independent review. A failed attempt has economic value only when it changes the next move or prevents a later campaign from buying the same dead end. Otherwise, 650 failures are simply 650 expenses.

Scale alone cannot explain the proof. Number theorist J. B. Conrey's peer-reviewed [1989 paper](https://doi.org/10.1515/crll.1989.399.1) established that more than two-fifths of the zeros lie on the critical line, and later human work improved the guarantee to 5/12 by 2020. Claude's preprint, a research paper released before journal review, says its distinctive step was a “linear-algebraic reading” of a [2024 result](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04799) about how zeta zeros are spaced. In plain language, it found a new way to use algebra to interpret an existing mathematical relationship and open a route to a stronger proof. The popular 37-year frame measures the time since Conrey's paper, not 37 years in which mathematicians made no progress.

The reported validation process included numerical tests, counterexample searches, review by Anthropic's own mathematicians, and examination by two outside number theorists on short notice. Anthropic also released a [Lean 4 formalization](https://github.com/anthropics/zeta-23-lean). Lean 4 is a theorem-proving programming language, and formal verification means a proof assistant checks a precisely encoded statement and its dependencies under declared logical assumptions. This makes the result more open to inspection, although it does not establish novelty or replace normal journal peer review.

I don't know whether half the agents or a tenth of the tokens would have reached the same result. The published project materials reviewed here contain no controlled comparison, public invoice, or comparison showing how often similar campaigns fail completely. I think the defensible conclusion is narrow but important: an organization can provision a large, bounded search around a clear target and strong checks. One remarkable case demonstrates a capability, not an expected return on every 31 million tokens.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The search budget purchased breadth, testable work, and repeated scrutiny, while the successful proof still depended on a human-built research frontier and a conceptual move. Without a controlled comparison or a record of comparable failures, the case establishes possibility rather than a dependable rate of return.</p></div>

## Persistence becomes capital, and capital can be wasted

A person who fails repeatedly pays in attention, opportunity, and sometimes reputation. Anthropic did not measure those pressures in this project, so they should not be smuggled into the result as data. Yet the run makes a structural difference visible: another machine attempt can be purchased without asking one person to absorb the full human cost of trying again. I think this is a useful meaning of productivity becoming capital. Capacity can be provisioned, while judgment must decide where it belongs.

The zeta project was unusually favorable for this arrangement because it had a precise theorem and several ways to check a proof. Most enterprise questions arrive in softer language: improve the product, understand the market, reduce risk. Before a persistent campaign begins, leaders have to translate that language into an observable test. A product claim might face a controlled experiment, a forecast might be scored on unseen cases, and a software change might be judged by an independently measured defect rate. Without such a test, a campaign can keep producing polished reasons to continue until activity starts to resemble evidence.

Two workplace studies show why the metric must fit the work. A [2023 working paper](https://www.nber.org/papers/w31161) from the National Bureau of Economic Research, a U.S. economics research organization, studied 5,179 customer-support workers and found that access to an AI assistant raised issues resolved per hour by 14% on average, with larger gains among less experienced workers. In a different setting, a [2025 randomized study](https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/) by METR, an independent organization that evaluates AI capabilities, found that experienced open-source developers took 19% longer on its measured tasks when allowed to use early-2025 AI tools. These results do not cancel each other out. They support measuring value in the actual task instead of treating model activity as a universal productivity score.

That makes the economic thesis testable inside a portfolio. Each new funding tranche should produce a testable hypothesis, evidence that survives an independent check, or a fast rejection that releases resources for better work. If spend rises while false positives, apparent successes that fail checking, and failed replications remain above declared limits, or if no new hypothesis survives review, persistence is no longer compounding knowledge. My take is that tirelessness magnifies the quality of the objective. A weak hunch without an evidence gate becomes an endless campaign with better prose.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Purchasable persistence moves part of the cost of repeated failure from human endurance into computing capacity, but it does not make every campaign valuable. Leaders need task-specific tests and portfolio signals that reveal whether another tranche is producing validated learning or merely more persuasive activity.</p></div>

## Give every tireless campaign a persistence warrant

Leadership begins before the first attempt. I think any persistent-agent campaign asking for money or expanded authority should carry a one-page **Persistence Warrant**. The warrant is a decision record that makes another batch conditional rather than automatic:

- **Decision and owner:** Name the business or research decision, the accountable domain owner, and the executive who can terminate the campaign.
- **Testable hypothesis:** State the claim being tested, what would disprove it, and what would count as a genuinely new path after failure.
- **Evidence gate and verifier:** Define the result required for the next tranche and appoint a reviewer with a different failure mode, such as an external dataset, a controlled test, a proof assistant, or a domain expert who did not generate the answer.
- **Budget and authority:** Limit tokens or attempt paths, elapsed time, human review, data access, tool permissions, and consequential actions. Define how access can be withdrawn or an action reversed.
- **Stop condition:** Pause when the same failure recurs without a new hypothesis, independent review cannot reproduce the result, authority would exceed the warrant, or the next tranche is worth less than the best alternative in the portfolio.

In a generic enterprise workflow, rather than a claim about Anthropic's process, the domain owner brings this warrant to the organization's existing AI governance forum. Finance compares the campaign with other uses of the budget; legal, risk, worker representatives, and the adopting team join when data rights, employee impact, or consequential decisions are involved. The forum controls new data access and tool permissions, while the named executive retains the authority to stop the work. The [2023 AI Risk Management Framework](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework) from the U.S. National Institute of Standards and Technology, the federal measurement and standards agency, offers useful verbs for this loop: govern, map, measure, and manage.

Engineering already has durable workflows, systems that save task state and resume work after interruption, as well as evaluation harnesses, repeatable suites that test outputs against set criteria. Those practices can manage retries, records, and tests. The Persistence Warrant adds the portfolio layer: who may keep spending, who may widen the system's authority, and who must end the campaign. That distinction matters because technical durability can make a poorly governed objective more durable too.

At a monthly portfolio review, compare cost per validated outcome with the next-best candidate, including reviewer time, false positives, failed replications, and the value of rejected paths. Release another tranche only when the named owner can show a new falsifiable hypothesis, independent evidence from the previous tranche, and a reversible path if the campaign is wrong. After repeated failure, extending the budget without those three conditions should be forbidden by the operating model, not defended as optimism.

In Doug Liman's 2014 science-fiction film *Edge of Tomorrow*, a soldier relives the same fatal battle and uses each reset as feedback. AI systems can supply more resets, but repetition becomes learning only when evidence changes the next move and someone remains accountable for ending the loop. The scarce act is no longer trying again. It is deciding whether another try still deserves the organization's money and authority.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>A Persistence Warrant gives a campaign an owner, testable claim, independent evidence gate, staged budget, authority boundary, and prewritten exit. Tireless systems make another attempt easy to request; leadership earns its role by deciding when another attempt no longer deserves the organization's money or authority.</p></div>
