Your website is on its way to becoming irrelevant as the center of your digital business.

The site will remain useful for trust, brand and direct customer experiences. What is changing is its position in the journey. A customer can ask an AI system a question, accept its answer and soon let an agent take the next step, all without entering the front door you designed.

That opens a verification gap: the distance between what an AI system says about your company and what your company can prove, update or withdraw. Imagine a 5,000-person manufacturer whose expired safety claim is repeated in an external AI answer because nobody owns the approved version. The business impact extends beyond a missed page view into operational risk.

I think the strategic response is to move investment beneath the page. The next durable asset is a knowledge catalog that makes company knowledge usable wherever the customer conversation happens, while keeping the company in control of what it is prepared to stand behind.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The website is becoming one touchpoint among many. As AI systems answer and act elsewhere, verified company knowledge becomes an operational asset.</p></div>

## Search is becoming a conversation that can act

A [2024 analysis by marketing research firm SparkToro, using anonymized browsing activity from data provider Datos](https://sparktoro.com/blog/2024-zero-click-search-study/) found that 58.5% of US Google searches and 59.7% of EU searches ended without a click. Those figures include people who stopped searching and people who changed their query, so they do not show that AI caused the behavior. They do show that a website visit was already far from guaranteed.

The AI-specific evidence is more direct. [Pew Research Center, an independent US research organization](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/) analyzed browsing data from 900 US adults in March 2025. People clicked a traditional result in 8% of visits that included a Google AI summary, compared with 15% when no summary appeared. Sources cited inside the summary received clicks in only 1% of those visits. Pew also found that about 60% of searches beginning with a question word produced an AI summary, which helps explain why search increasingly feels like a conversation rather than a list of links.

Now that conversation is gaining the ability to act. An AI agent is software that pursues a goal through several steps, such as checking a requirement, finding an option and requesting a booking. At Google Cloud Next ’25, Google’s annual enterprise technology conference, the company announced [Agent2Agent, or A2A](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/a2a-a-new-era-of-agent-interoperability/), an open set of rules that allows agents to exchange information and coordinate work across different business applications.

I don’t know whether agent-led buying becomes normal in two years or six. Trust, payments, liability and customer habits still create friction. But I think the direction already changes the investment question, because the path from demand to decision no longer has to pass through your website.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>AI summaries reduce the need to visit a result, while A2A gives software a path from answering a question to coordinating an action. The company website no longer controls the whole journey.</p></div>

## A knowledge catalog closes the verification gap

In a June 2026 video for Phlash Consulting, a marketing consultancy, presenter [Phil Risher described a “knowledge catalog”](https://youtu.be/xAIeA3ewRbo) as a verified, structured set of information about a business. The phrase is useful, although it is not the name of a Google product in the official material reviewed for this article. I use it for a company-owned layer of approved claims, with each claim connected to its source, accountable owner, review point, access rule and change history.

This is different from a chatbot. A chatbot is a customer interface; a knowledge catalog is the controlled claims layer that a website, chatbot or outside agent may be allowed to use. It does not replace product systems, policy documents or a content platform. Those systems still hold the original records. The catalog states which version the business has approved for which purpose.

Return to the manufacturer. A current certificate in its quality system can support an approved claim that a valve meets a specific safety standard. An agent may repeat that claim only while the certificate is valid and the customer has permission to receive it. When the evidence expires, the claim owner withdraws approval, and the permitted answer becomes: “We cannot confirm that standard.” This visible chain from source to claim to answer turns governance into something a leader can see and test.

At board level, a knowledge catalog should be understood as operational infrastructure for controlling claims rather than as an autonomous customer channel. It reduces the chance that an old policy, unsafe specification or unsupported promise travels faster than the company can correct it.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>A knowledge catalog links every reusable claim to current evidence, ownership and permission. It lets many interfaces use company knowledge without detaching the answer from company accountability.</p></div>

## Structured content creates a practical head start

Structured content stores a fact in a clearly named field instead of burying it inside a designed page. A field called “service eligibility,” for example, lets a website and an authorized agent retrieve the same approved value. Machine-readable information simply means software can recognize what a value represents without guessing from the layout around it.

In May 2025, [Google Search Central, Google’s guidance service for website owners](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/05/succeeding-in-ai-search/) said structured data helps businesses share machine-readable information that its systems can consider. The same guidance stressed unique and useful content. Structure makes knowledge accessible, but it cannot make a generic claim valuable or an outdated claim true.

This is where a modern headless content management system, or headless CMS, becomes strategically important. It separates reusable content from website design and supplies that content to different channels through standard software connections. Companies that already have sensible content models, approval steps and change histories hold much of the raw material for a knowledge catalog.

I think that advantage is real, but it is not automatic. A headless CMS filled with long page copy and unclear ownership is still a cleanup project. The useful head start comes from well-defined fields, distinctive company knowledge and a workflow that can show who approved a claim and when it should be checked again.

Risher calls content “training data for the future internet.” As a metaphor, that captures the stakes, but the near-term mechanism is usually retrieval, where an AI system looks up a current approved fact while forming an answer. This also changes measurement. Search rankings still matter, while mention rate, how often an AI answer names the company across a fixed set of questions, and AI share of voice, its presence relative to selected competitors, become useful diagnostic signals. Neither replaces a business outcome such as more qualified customer enquiries, faster service or fewer unsupported answers.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Structured content gives software facts it can recognize and reuse. A well-governed headless CMS provides a head start, while business outcomes remain more important than new visibility metrics.</p></div>

## The near-term investment belongs below the interface

The same independent consultancy that built your content platform may be a sensible candidate to extend it, because it already understands the content model and approval routes. Familiarity is not evidence, though. My take is that leaders should choose the team through a vendor-neutral test of capability rather than through its relationship with a platform.

Ask for evidence of headless-CMS implementation capability and knowledge governance, including how claims receive sources, owners and review points. Put usable export rights, data location and role-based access controls, which limit viewing and editing by job responsibility, into the contract. Require disclosure of commissions, referral fees or financial stakes in platforms, then keep project fees independent of license incentives. Finally, test the handover: your own team should be able to export a representative set of knowledge and restore it in another approved environment. That exercise does not prove perfect portability, but it shows whether practical control exists.

Supplier evidence should then carry into a bounded acceptance test. Trace a small set of important questions from original source to approved claim, permitted answer and withdrawal. Name an executive sponsor for the business result, a domain owner who approves the claims and a platform owner who controls access and can restore the previous working state. Before any outside agent receives access, assess where the data sits and what the agent may decide on the company’s behalf. The project must also comply with the applicable requirements of the [European Union’s General Data Protection Regulation](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj), its personal-data law applied since 2018, and the [EU AI Act](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj), its 2024 risk-based law for artificial intelligence.

Put the business result first. Mention rate and AI share of voice can help diagnose visibility, but the investment should earn its place by increasing supported answers, shortening service time or producing more qualified enquiries without creating unsafe commitments. If controlled claims do not improve a defined outcome, do not scale the program. I think this is the right balance between moving early and refusing to turn an agent experiment into an uncontrolled customer channel.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Treat the catalog as an operational-risk and resilience investment. Choose a supplier through evidence, preserve control of the data and scale only when governed knowledge improves a defined business result.</p></div>

## Generated interfaces make the knowledge layer decisive

*[The Digital Singularity Shift](/books/the-digital-singularity-shift/)*, my October 2024 book about business in an age of abundant AI creation, argues that features and interfaces become easier to copy and value moves toward meaning, story and belief. The recent changes in search and agents fit that direction. When answers and actions are assembled outside the website, the visible experience becomes easier to reproduce while trustworthy company knowledge becomes harder to fake.

Generative user interface, or generative UI, pushes this further. It means software builds an interface for a particular request instead of serving every person the same fixed screen. In April 2026, Google released [A2UI v0.9](https://developers.googleblog.com/a2ui-v0-9-generative-ui/), an open specification that lets an AI agent request suitable buttons, forms or tables while the receiving application keeps control of the approved design components.

The closest science-fiction image is the holodeck in *Star Trek: First Contact*, the 1996 film in which a computer creates a setting on demand. The room changes with the mission, but the knowledge and operating rules underneath decide what can happen. Generative UI applies the same strategic logic: the screen becomes temporary, while the facts, permissions and identity beneath it carry the company from one interface to the next.

I think design and brand will continue to matter wherever people choose to engage directly. They simply stop being the only durable center. When an interface can be created for each question and an agent can act through it, the most important move now is to own the verified knowledge both depend on.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Generative UI makes presentation more fluid and widely available. The next strategic move is the knowledge catalog: the company-owned foundation every future interface and permitted agent will need.</p></div>
