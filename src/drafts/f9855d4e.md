Your website is losing its position as the default route into your company. People will still use it to compare offers and build confidence. Yet more discovery ends inside an AI answer, while agents begin carrying requests into company systems.

[Phil Risher’s argument](https://youtu.be/xAIeA3ewRbo) points toward a new centre of gravity. I think leaders should manage the approved knowledge beneath the site as a business capability, because software increasingly needs to find, check and use it without loading a homepage.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The website still serves people, while approved knowledge becomes available wherever decisions happen.</p></div>

## Search is becoming an answer and an action

[SparkToro and Datos](https://sparktoro.com/blog/2024-zero-click-search-study/) found that 59.7% of EU Google searches and 58.5% of US searches ended without a click in 2024. The study includes ended sessions and changed searches, so the implication is narrow: referral traffic is less dependable, while websites continue to serve the people who arrive.

[Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/) later observed traditional-result clicks in 8% of visits with an AI summary, compared with 15% without one. Google then launched [Agent2Agent, or A2A](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/a2a-a-new-era-of-agent-interoperability/), a shared way for agents to exchange information and coordinate work. The protocol carries requests between systems; each company sets approval, liability and human escalation around it. I don’t know whether broad adoption takes two years or six. The direction already deserves executive attention.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>AI answers weaken the search-to-site path. A2A lets software coordinate requests under company controls.</p></div>

## A knowledge catalog gives agents approved facts

I use “knowledge catalog” as a business term for a governed, machine-readable set of approved company facts. These can cover product limits, pricing rules, policies, credentials and recurring customer questions. Each record has a source, owner, review date and rule for software use.

Suppose a buyer’s agent asks whether an industrial valve works at minus 30 degrees and can arrive in Stockholm next week. The catalog returns its certification and current delivery promise. If either fact expires, the request goes to a person. I think every automated answer should lead back to a claim the company can defend today.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>A knowledge catalog turns company claims into traceable records that agents can use within clear limits.</p></div>

## Structured content separates knowledge from presentation

Structured content stores facts in named fields, separate from a finished page. “Operating temperature” becomes a reusable value rather than a sentence copied across brochures. In [Google’s search guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/05/succeeding-in-ai-search), structured data means machine-readable markup that helps its systems interpret a page.

The connection is machine readability, yet the investments differ. Page markup exposes selected information to search. Structured content supplies reusable facts with ownership, freshness and approval rules. Risher calls content “training data for the future internet,” a useful strategic metaphor. AI systems can also retrieve current information while answering, a process separate from model training.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Search markup helps machines read a page. A catalog governs reusable facts and keeps their sources visible.</p></div>

## A headless CMS creates a head start

A headless content management system stores reusable content models and delivers them through APIs, software connections that let the same approved fields feed a site, app or agent. Companies using Sanity, Hygraph or Payload may already have content models, approval workflows and owners.

The lead is genuine only when the content is governed. Current terms and availability often live in sales or operations systems, so the catalog must connect those sources without losing ownership. The CMS supplies the foundation; the operating model completes it.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>A governed headless CMS shortens the build through reusable fields and approval workflows.</p></div>

## Generative UI makes the deeper shift visible

[Google’s A2UI work](https://developers.googleblog.com/a2ui-v0-9-generative-ui/) lets agents generate interface elements in real time. Generative UI resembles the holodeck in *Star Trek*: the visible environment is assembled for the situation. An answer can become a comparison, form or booking flow made for one request.

That makes the bridge to *[The Digital Singularity Shift](/books/the-digital-singularity-shift/)* concrete. The book argues that as AI makes creation cheap, functional differences shrink and value moves toward meaning, story and belief. If presentation is assembled on demand, the stable competitive work moves into the governed knowledge beneath it. I think the forecast is becoming operational reality.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>When interfaces are generated per request, current and accountable knowledge becomes the stable foundation.</p></div>

## Choose a builder whose incentives match yours

Begin with the consultancy that knows your content platform, then test its incentives. Require headless-CMS and knowledge-governance experience, full export rights, fees separate from software licences, clear control of access and data location, and a handover your team can run.

My take: every named firm must pass that test. Within [Eidra](https://eidra.com/), [Curamando](https://www.curamando.com/) describes its role around AI transformation, while [Umain](https://www.umain.com/) focuses on product and technology implementation. Assess the assigned team against the same portability, fee and handover terms in writing.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Choose with evidence and contract terms: platform skill, portable data, aligned fees and tested handover.</p></div>

## Start with one decision this quarter

Choose one question buyers repeatedly ask, such as the valve’s certified limits and delivery window. Over four weeks, give each underlying fact a source, owner and review date. Let approved systems read those facts first, while named people retain the rights to change claims, approve exceptions and make commitments.

Measure verification time, unclear requests, qualified demand and operating cost against today’s baseline. Add mention rate, how often AI answers name you, and share of voice, how your presence compares with competitors. Keep those measures only when movement coincides with qualified demand or a measurable fall in verification time.

I think one controlled decision is enough. Once the company can supply an answer that software and people can trust, it has the pattern for the catalog that will replace the website as its only digital hub.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Build one governed slice now, prove that it improves a recurring buyer decision, and expand from evidence rather than ambition.</p></div>
