Your website is becoming irrelevant as the center of your digital business.

That does not mean it disappears. Companies will still need places to explain complex offers, express a point of view and build direct customer relationships. What is weakening is the assumption that every meaningful digital journey must pass through a search result, a click and a page you designed.

I think many leadership teams are still treating the page as the permanent asset. It is not. The more durable asset is the knowledge underneath it: what the company sells, what it promises, where those promises apply and which evidence makes them trustworthy.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The website will remain useful, but it is losing its position as the required doorway into a business. Verified company knowledge retains its value when discovery, comparison and action happen through other interfaces.</p></div>

## The customer journey is moving outside your website

This movement began before the current wave of artificial intelligence. A [2024 study by marketing analytics company SparkToro and browsing-data provider Datos](https://sparktoro.com/blog/2024-zero-click-search-study/) estimated that 58.5% of US Google searches and 59.7% of EU searches ended without a click. “Zero-click” includes people who found enough information on Google, stopped searching or changed their query, so the figure does not prove that AI caused the behavior. It does show that a website visit was already far from guaranteed.

AI-generated summaries have made the effect easier to isolate. In March 2025, [Pew Research Center, a nonpartisan US research organization, studied the Google browsing activity of 900 American adults](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/). People clicked a traditional result in 8% of visits when Google showed an AI summary, compared with 15% when it did not. Sources cited inside the summary received clicks in only 1% of those visits. Pew also found that about 60% of searches beginning with words such as who, what, when and why produced a summary, which helps explain why search increasingly feels like a conversation rather than a list of pages.

The next change is from answers to delegated tasks. An AI agent is software given a goal and permission to carry out several steps for a person or organization. At Google Cloud Next ’25, Google’s annual enterprise technology conference, the company introduced [Agent2Agent, or A2A](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/a2a-a-new-era-of-agent-interoperability/), an open protocol that lets agents exchange information and coordinate work across different business systems. In plain language, agents made by different organizations now have a common way to work together. The protocol enables coordination; it does not decide which facts are true or what an agent is allowed to do.

I don’t know how quickly customers will delegate major purchases. Trust, payment, liability and habit remain serious barriers. But an agent does not need to complete the entire purchase to alter the customer journey. If it can compare suppliers, remove unsuitable options or arrange the next step, commercial progress can happen before the customer sees a company website.

Even visibility is changing. [Semrush, a search analytics company, analyzed 200,000 keywords in September 2024](https://www.semrush.com/blog/ai-overviews-study/) and found that a top-ten traditional ranking did not guarantee inclusion in a Google AI Overview. Rankings still matter, but I think leaders should also watch mention rate, meaning how often AI answers name the company, and AI share of voice, meaning how visible it is compared with competitors.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Many searches already end without a website visit, AI summaries reduce clicks further, and agents can move a task forward across business systems. Companies must increasingly earn a place inside the answer and the action, not only in a list of links.</p></div>

## A knowledge catalog gives AI something trustworthy to use

In a June 2026 video, [Phil Risher, a marketing consultant at Phlash Consulting](https://youtu.be/xAIeA3ewRbo), describes a knowledge catalog as a verified set of information about a business that can be deployed through an agent. The phrase is useful, although available Google documentation does not establish it as the name of an official product or standard. I use it for a company-owned body of knowledge arranged so software can use it without guessing what each fact means.

A knowledge catalog could include services, product limits, locations, credentials, pricing rules, policies and the questions customers repeatedly ask. Verification means an important claim can be traced to an approved source and an accountable owner. Structure means that its parts have consistent labels, while machine-readable simply means software can identify those parts without relying on the visual layout of a web page.

Consider a warranty. A normal page may bury its duration, eligible products and regional exceptions in several paragraphs. A structured record keeps those facts separate but connected, along with the approved policy and review date. The website can turn that record into polished prose, while a service tool or agent can answer a narrow question without quietly dropping a condition.

This distinction matters because a conversational interface can present an answer or trigger an action, but the knowledge catalog is the governed information layer behind what the organization permits it to say or use. Risher calls content “training data for the future internet.” I think the strategic idea is right, although the near-term mechanism will often be retrieval: the AI looks up relevant company information while forming an answer instead of permanently learning it through model training.

The catalog therefore has a different job from a larger pile of content. It helps the business prove, update and withdraw the claims it is prepared to stand behind. Without that control, an outdated price or policy can travel through an AI answer faster than the company can correct it.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>A knowledge catalog is verified, company-owned information arranged so people and software can use it consistently. Its value comes from connecting an answer to the source, meaning and organizational authority behind it.</p></div>

## Structured content creates the head start

Structured content stores an idea in named parts instead of treating a finished page as one inseparable block. A service record might hold the service name, eligible region, pricing rule and review date in separate fields. Because each part keeps its meaning, one approved record can serve a website, an employee tool and an agent without being copied into conflicting versions.

This is not merely a theory promoted by content-platform vendors. In May 2025, [Google Search Central, the company’s official guidance service for website owners](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/05/succeeding-in-ai-search/), said structured data helps organizations share information in a machine-readable form its systems can consider. The same guidance stressed original, useful material. Structure makes knowledge easier for machines to handle, but it cannot make a generic statement distinctive or an outdated statement true.

Organizations with a well-run headless content management system have a practical head start. A headless CMS separates reusable content from the design of one website and sends it through the software connections that feed websites, apps and internal tools. If product facts, approval rights and publishing flows are already modeled that way, much of the raw material exists. A modern platform filled with unstructured page copy does not create the same advantage.

The platform market recognizes the direction. Sanity and Contentful, companies that provide headless content systems, and Optimizely, a digital-experience software company, introduced agent-facing or AI-visibility capabilities during 2025 and 2026. Their releases are vendor evidence, not neutral proof, but they show where the category is moving.

The consultancy that built the current content platform may therefore be a sensible partner for the next phase. Valtech, a global digital consultancy, is one example; independent implementation consultancies in the Contentful, Sanity and Optimizely partner networks are others. These names are examples, not an automatic shortlist. My take is to favor a consulting firm whose advice does not depend on making one software license permanent, while preserving company ownership of the knowledge model and the ability to move the information elsewhere.

I think this is a stronger near-term priority than another broad redesign. Better structured knowledge can improve the website while also serving search, customer service, employee tools and agents. A redesign improves one expression of the business.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Structured content lets one approved fact travel without losing its meaning. A mature headless CMS and an independent implementation consultancy can accelerate the shift, provided the company retains ownership and portability.</p></div>

## Generative UI reveals what comes next

*[The Digital Singularity Shift](/books/the-digital-singularity-shift/)*, my 2024 business book about AI making digital creation abundant, argues that functional features become easier to reproduce and value moves toward the meaning beneath them. AI answers and acting agents now make part of that thesis visible. Generative user interface, usually shortened to GenUI, adds the final piece: software can assemble a screen for a particular request instead of relying on one interface designed in advance for everyone.

In November 2025, [Google introduced Gemini 3 experiments that generated an interface when a user submitted a prompt](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/gemini/gemini-3-gemini-app/). In April 2026, it published [A2UI](https://developers.googleblog.com/a2ui-v0-9-generative-ui/), a proposed open standard through which an agent can request suitable interface elements while an application uses its approved design components. These developments do not make design irrelevant. They make the form more adaptable while increasing the importance of the facts and permissions underneath it.

The useful science-fiction comparison is the holodeck in *Star Trek: First Contact*, the 1996 film in which a computer creates a setting around the immediate mission. GenUI points toward a restrained business version of that idea: one customer receives a comparison, another a form and a third a dashboard, each assembled around the task at hand. When the scenery can change on demand, the source material behind it carries more of the strategic weight.

The website will remain one important expression of a company, but it will no longer carry the whole digital strategy. GenUI is the spectacular glimpse of what comes next; I think the knowledge catalog is the investment that matters now.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>GenUI makes interfaces more adaptive and temporary, in line with the direction described in <em>The Digital Singularity Shift</em>. The immediate strategic priority is the verified knowledge that every future screen and agent will need.</p></div>
