Your website is becoming irrelevant to the moment a customer decides.

[Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/) found that people clicked a traditional result in 8% of visits with a Google AI summary, compared with 15% without one. Sources inside the summary received a click in only 1% of visits.

I think the website will survive for proof and brand. Its old role as the compulsory stop between question and decision is fading.

Consider a hypothetical facilities director choosing a heat-pump maintenance provider for twelve sites. An assistant could compare coverage and booking before the director sees a homepage. The supplier's data may enter the decision before its design does.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The website keeps a role, but it no longer controls the first answer. Business knowledge must work wherever the conversation starts.</p></div>

## Search is becoming delegation

The route from query to company page is getting shorter.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Search increasingly answers questions itself. Agent protocols let software carry work across companies.</p></div>

[SparkToro and Datos](https://sparktoro.com/blog/2024-zero-click-search-study/) estimated that 58.5% of US Google searches and 59.7% of EU searches in 2024 ended without a click. Their measure includes ended sessions and changed queries. Pew provides the AI-specific signal.

At Google Cloud Next 2025, Google announced the open [Agent2Agent protocol](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/a2a-a-new-era-of-agent-interoperability/), backed by more than 50 technology partners. A2A lets agents exchange information and coordinate work. [ServiceTitan](https://www.servicetitan.com/blog/webinar-recap-ai-virtual-agents-call-booking) says its AI agents can book or reschedule jobs.

I don't know how quickly people will delegate high-value purchases. Liability and habit will slow them down. The direction is visible: ask once, then let software gather the answer.

## A knowledge catalog gives the agent a source

An agent cannot rely on a polished page when the facts beneath it are vague or stale.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>A knowledge catalog gives software verified business information without forcing it to guess from page copy.</p></div>

[Phil Risher describes](https://youtu.be/xAIeA3ewRbo) a knowledge catalog as a verified dataset an agent can use. I think of it as the machine-readable version of what a company knows and is prepared to promise. The cited Google material defines no product category with that name.

For the maintenance provider, it would contain service areas, response rules, certifications, price logic and booking conditions. Every claim needs an owner and review date.

## Structured content is the dividing line

A web page wraps business facts inside presentation.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Structured content gives each fact a defined field, making it easier to retrieve, update and govern.</p></div>

Google's [guidance for AI search](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/05/succeeding-in-ai-search) says structured data shares information in a machine-readable form its systems can consider.

"Fast service across Sweden" leaves room for interpretation. A structured record can specify covered postal areas, a contractual response time and its expiry date. The agent retrieves the approved answer. An expired record triggers human review.

Risher calls content "training data for the future internet." I think its immediate value is simpler: AI gets a current source instead of a sentence it must interpret.

## Generative UI changes the value of the frontend

Google is experimenting with interfaces assembled for the request at hand.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Generated interfaces change quickly. Verified knowledge can serve every surface.</p></div>

Google's [A2UI project](https://developers.googleblog.com/introducing-a2ui-an-open-project-for-agent-driven-interfaces/) lets agents send interface descriptions that an application can render. Our facilities director might receive a comparison card inside an assistant.

The emerging web feels closer to the computer in *Star Trek* than to browsing a directory. You state an intent, and the system assembles an answer. A website becomes one useful surface among many.

## Headless CMS creates a head start

Companies that separated content from presentation have completed part of the work.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>A headless CMS supplies modeled content. The catalog adds ownership, freshness and rules for agent use.</p></div>

A headless CMS stores a service area or certification as a reusable object instead of burying it in one page. I think that gives disciplined content teams a structural advantage. The argument starts in [The quiet truth about headless CMS](/posts/the-quiet-truth-about-headless-cms/).

The provider still has to name the owner of its response-time claim and decide which system wins when records disagree. The CMS supplies raw material. Governance makes it dependable.

## The prediction becomes operational

This shift follows the mechanism behind my broader thesis.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>As interfaces become easier to produce, advantage moves toward proprietary knowledge and accountability.</p></div>

In [The Digital Singularity Shift](/books/the-digital-singularity-shift/), I argue that AI collapses functional differentiation toward zero and moves competition toward meaning, story and belief. The thesis is broader than CMS architecture.

A knowledge catalog brings that mechanism into content operations. Many systems can generate a supplier comparison. Fewer companies can provide current coverage rules and stand behind the answer. I think the interface becomes more disposable while verified knowledge becomes more consequential.

## Choose the partner by its incentives

The firm that built your content platform may already understand your information model.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Use specialists for contained platform work. Choose cross-platform incentives when the catalog spans several systems.</p></div>

A consultancy such as [Valtech](https://www.valtech.com/), or a qualified firm from the [Contentful](https://www.contentful.com/partners/), [Sanity](https://www.sanity.io/agency-partners) or [Optimizely](https://www.optimizely.com/partners/) partner networks, can extend an existing content foundation.

My take is practical. A specialist can move quickly inside one product. When the catalog crosses the CMS, customer records and scheduling, require portable schemas and a clear exit path. The commercial model should reward sound architecture instead of a larger license footprint.

## Start with one decision

Begin where a wrong answer would create a visible business cost.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Build around one customer decision, test the answers, then expand when ownership routines work.</p></div>

For the maintenance provider, start with service coverage and emergency booking. Connect each answer to its source and give a business owner the power to pause it. Keep search rankings in the report, then add AI mention rate and share of voice. [Optimizely offers](https://www.optimizely.com/agents/competitive-ai-share-of-voice-agent/) a product around the latter metric.

Agents will form answers from the sources they can reach. Put your verified facts within reach before somebody else's summary becomes the version your customer hears.
