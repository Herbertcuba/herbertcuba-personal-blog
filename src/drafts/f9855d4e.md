A website bundles two very different assets. One is the interface a company presents to people. The other is the knowledge carried inside it: services, prices, policies, credentials and answers to customer questions. For most of the web's history, those assets traveled together.

AI is pulling them apart. A customer can receive an answer in a search service, then let an AI agent, software that pursues a goal and takes authorized steps, continue the journey. The website remains valuable for depth and reassurance, but the visit itself is becoming optional. I think the strategic center is therefore moving from the page to the company knowledge beneath it.

That knowledge needs its own form. A knowledge catalog is a verified, structured body of company information that software can find, interpret and use without guessing what a page was supposed to mean. Building it now is more important than treating the next website redesign as the default answer to every digital problem.

## Your website has been hiding two assets

[Phil Risher of Phlash Consulting, a marketing consultancy for service businesses, describes a knowledge catalog in a June 2026 video](https://youtu.be/xAIeA3ewRbo) as a verified set of information about a company that can be made available through an agent. I think the phrase gives leaders a useful picture of the asset they need to build. It is still a working term, however: the Google material reviewed for this article does not establish an official Google product or standard with that name.

The contents are ordinary business knowledge made reliable. A catalog could include where the company operates, which products fit which needs, how prices are calculated, what warranties cover and which qualifications support a claim. Each important record should retain its approved source, accountable owner, market, effective date and next review. Without those details, an AI system may repeat a true statement in the wrong country or continue using it after the business has changed.

Verification is only half of the control. The same catalog must state what an agent may do with a fact. Knowing that an appointment is available does not authorize software to confirm it, and knowing the listed price does not authorize a discount. The agent is the actor; the catalog supplies the approved knowledge and the boundaries for action.

Risher asks companies to treat content as "training data for the future internet." That works as a strategic metaphor because every published answer can influence how an AI system represents the business. Technically, many current systems look up fresh material while composing an answer rather than permanently adding each page to a model. The practical implication is the same: incomplete, generic or contradictory content gives the system weak evidence.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>A knowledge catalog turns familiar company information into governed records with sources, owners, dates and market scope. It also keeps factual accuracy separate from permission to act, so an agent can know something without automatically gaining authority to commit the company.</p>
</div>

## The interface no longer owns the journey

The traffic data shows why this separation matters. [SparkToro, an audience-research company, and Datos, a provider of browsing data, published a clickstream study in July 2024](https://sparktoro.com/blog/2024-zero-click-search-study/) that put the share of Google searches ending without a click at 58.5% in the United States and 59.7% in the European Union. Those numbers include sessions that ended and searches that were reformulated, so AI did not cause the whole effect. They establish a simpler point: the website visit was already far from guaranteed.

AI-generated summaries strengthen that pattern. [Pew Research Center, a nonpartisan US research organization, observed the Google browsing of 900 American adults in March 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/). People clicked a conventional search result during 8% of visits with an AI summary and 15% of visits without one. A cited source inside the summary received a click during only 1% of relevant visits. About six in ten searches phrased as questions produced an AI summary, which connects conversational behavior directly to fewer opportunities for a company to explain itself on its own page.

Answers are now beginning to lead into actions. At Google Cloud Next '25, Google's annual conference for business technology, the company introduced [Agent2Agent, or A2A, an open communication protocol through which AI agents built by different organizations can exchange information and coordinate tasks](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/a2a-a-new-era-of-agent-interoperability/). More than 50 technology partners supported its launch. Protocols do not create trust by themselves, but they remove one obstacle to a customer's agent checking a supplier's availability or passing an approved request to another system.

The visible interface is becoming more fluid as well. A generative user interface, often called GenUI, is a screen assembled by AI around the user's immediate request. [Google's Gemini 3 AI model presented experiments with prompt-generated interfaces in November 2025](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/gemini/gemini-3-gemini-app/), and Google followed in April 2026 with [A2UI v0.9, an open specification for interface elements produced by agents](https://developers.googleblog.com/a2ui-v0-9-generative-ui/). A company may therefore supply the meaning while somebody else's system decides how that meaning appears.

This direction is consistent with [*The Digital Singularity Shift*, my 2024 book about increasingly adaptive and personal digital systems](https://cubagarcia.com/books/the-digital-singularity-shift/). Search answers detach knowledge from pages, A2A lets agents coordinate, and GenUI assembles presentation at the moment of need. I think these are three visible parts of the same movement from fixed digital destinations toward systems that organize themselves around the individual.

I don't know when most customers will trust an agent with a costly purchase or a sensitive exception. Liability, fraud and habit may slow the transition for years. Even so, I think leadership teams should respond to the demonstrated change in information flow now, because cataloging approved knowledge creates value whether agents advance quickly or slowly.

<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Zero-click behavior, AI summaries, agent communication and generated interfaces are removing the compulsory website visit one layer at a time. Adoption will be uneven, but companies already need knowledge that remains accurate when another system controls the answer, action or screen.</p>
</div>

## Headless content changes the starting line

Structured content stores meaning in reusable fields and components. A price can carry its currency, market and valid-from date, while a policy can carry its owner and review status. Structured data is the machine-readable labeling used when that material is published. [Google Search Central, Google's guidance service for website owners, wrote in May 2025](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/05/succeeding-in-ai-search) that structured data helps convey content in a form its systems can process, and it paired that advice with a call for original, useful information.

A modern headless content management system, or headless CMS, keeps content separate from the website's visual layer and delivers it to different interfaces through software connections. That architecture creates a substantial head start because the organization has already begun separating knowledge from presentation. It can add ownership and verification to existing content models, then make approved records available to websites, service tools and agents from the same source.

The direction of the platform market supports this analysis, even though vendor roadmaps are not neutral evidence of business results. [Sanity, a structured-content platform, released tools for AI-assisted content workflows in May 2025](https://www.sanity.io/blog/agent-actions-ai-building-blocks-for-structured-content). [Contentful, another headless content platform, made its hosted Model Context Protocol connection generally available in July 2026](https://www.contentful.com/blog/remote-mcp-now-generally-available). Model Context Protocol, or MCP, is a standard way for AI assistants and agents to connect to external tools and information under defined access controls.

A CMS will rarely contain every approved fact. Product details may sit in a product-information system, contractual limits in policy documents and live availability in an operational platform. The catalog should connect those sources while preserving the owner of each record. I think the advantage of headless content is architectural, not automatic: clean models and software access reduce the work, while weak content and unclear accountability remain weak in any platform.

It provides useful raw material for a knowledge catalog, but leaders still have to connect other systems and assign accountability for every important record.</p>

## Make one customer promise portable

My take is to begin with one customer promise whose failure has a visible cost. It could be service eligibility, a warranty decision or appointment availability. Give a small cross-functional team 90 days to make the supporting knowledge portable across the website and at least one AI-assisted journey.

The first implementation needs four pieces of work:

1. Collect the real questions customers ask before that decision and identify the currently approved answers.
2. Convert those answers into structured records with sources, owners, markets, dates and permitted uses.
3. Test common questions, awkward edge cases and requests the agent must refuse or hand to a person.
4. Add AI visibility to the existing report. Mention rate measures how often a fixed set of AI answers names the company, while AI share of voice compares those mentions with a defined group of competitors.

Search rankings and website conversion still matter because customers continue to use both. The additional measures reveal whether the business appears inside answers that produce no visit. [Optimizely, a digital-experience software company, now offers a Competitive AI Share of Voice agent](https://www.optimizely.com/agents/competitive-ai-share-of-voice-agent), which shows that vendors are productizing this type of measurement. Its business value still has to be tested against outcomes such as qualified demand, correct answers and completed service.

The consultancy that helped build the content platform may be able to lead the work because it already understands the content model and editorial process. Candidates could include [Valtech, a global digital consultancy](https://www.valtech.com/), or experienced firms in the [Contentful](https://www.contentful.com/partners/), [Sanity](https://www.sanity.io/partners/) and [Optimizely](https://www.optimizely.com/partners/) partner networks. These names are examples for evaluation, not endorsements.

Platform expertise is valuable, but choose a consultancy whose economics do not depend on expanding one software license. Ask whether it can preserve systems that already work, connect authoritative sources outside the CMS and recommend a smaller architecture when that serves the company. Deep platform knowledge should inform the design without deciding its boundaries.

Do not wait for a universal definition of knowledge catalog or a settled market for agents. Use the next digital initiative to make one important customer promise structured, verified and safe for software to use. Then expand from evidence, because the company that governs its knowledge will remain understandable even when the website is no longer the route customers take.

<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Start now with one valuable customer promise, a 90-day test and explicit rules for accuracy and action. Measure whether AI systems represent the company correctly, choose an implementation partner able to work across platforms, and let proven knowledge governance become the foundation for every interface that follows.</p>
</div>
