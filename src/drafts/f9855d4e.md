A company can correct its website on Monday and have no guarantee that every AI answer changes on Tuesday. The problem is no longer only whether the page is accurate. It is whether an approved company answer keeps its source, scope and permissions after it leaves the page.

I call the difference between those two states the **answer-control gap**. It appears when Google, an AI assistant or an agent answers on the company’s behalf through an interface the company did not design. Hallucinations attract attention, but a factually correct answer can be just as dangerous when it applies to the wrong product, market or customer, or when software turns it into a promise nobody authorized.

Websites will remain useful places to explain, persuade and serve customers. I think their strategic role is changing from the hub that contains the whole journey to one interface among many. The asset underneath should be a **knowledge catalog**: a governed way to connect approved company knowledge to its evidence, owner, valid context and permitted use so that different systems can consume it without stripping away its meaning.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The website can be accurate while an answer elsewhere is stale, out of context or unauthorized. Closing that answer-control gap requires company knowledge that carries its boundaries beyond the page.</p></div>

## The answer has left the page

The change is already visible in search behavior. [Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/), an American nonpartisan research organization, published an analysis in July 2025 based on the March browsing activity of 900 US adults. A traditional result drew a click in 8% of visits that contained a Google AI summary, against 15% when no summary appeared. A cited source inside the summary received a click in just 1% of visits, while about 60% of searches phrased as questions produced an AI summary.

That observational study cannot establish AI as the cause of every missing click. It still shows that an answer may shape a decision before the company receives a visit. [Audience-research firm SparkToro and browsing-data provider Datos estimated in 2024](https://sparktoro.com/blog/2024-zero-click-search-study/) that no click followed 58.5% of American Google searches and 59.7% of searches in the European Union. Their totals also count sessions that ended and queries that were revised, which makes them evidence of zero-click behavior rather than a measure of AI’s effect.

Now the interface itself is becoming fluid. A **generative user interface**, often shortened to GenUI, creates a screen around the request at hand rather than choosing a finished page from a fixed library. Google showed this capability when it released the Gemini 3 AI model in [November 2025](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/gemini/gemini-3-gemini-app/). Its [A2UI v0.9 format, published in April 2026](https://developers.googleblog.com/a2ui-v0-9-generative-ui/), allows an AI system to declare what a screen should do while the receiving application chooses and renders approved components. Neither release proves broad use, but both make the direction concrete: the screen can increasingly be assembled after the question is asked.

The following step is action. An **AI agent** is software that works through a sequence on the way to a goal, perhaps gathering options before requesting information or coordinating a task. Google used its annual Cloud Next conference in April 2025 to launch [Agent2Agent, or A2A](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/a2a-a-new-era-of-agent-interoperability/), as an open protocol backed by contributions and support from more than 50 technology partners. In ordinary language, a protocol is an agreed method of communication. A2A gives separately built agents a way to exchange information securely and coordinate work across business applications.

I don’t know when customers will trust software with high-stakes purchases. Fraud, liability and habit still stand in the way, and a communication protocol does not grant an agent commercial authority. I think the launches matter because they separate the customer interaction from any one company page. Once an outside agent can ask for information or initiate a task, the company needs to supply the right answer and enforce what may happen next.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Search often ends without a visit, generative interfaces can build the screen around a request, and A2A lets agents coordinate across systems. The evidence supports a shift in where answers and actions happen, even though the speed of adoption remains uncertain.</p></div>

## A catalog gives every fact a boundary

[Phil Risher of digital-marketing firm Phlash Consulting](https://youtu.be/xAIeA3ewRbo) offered a useful plain-language definition in June 2026: a knowledge catalog is “a verified data set of information about your business.” The name also refers to a specific product. [Google Cloud, Google’s cloud-computing division, launched Google Cloud Knowledge Catalog in April 2026](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/data-analytics/introducing-the-google-cloud-knowledge-catalog) to give agents governed context about data and AI assets. Here, metadata is information that describes data, including its meaning, origin and owner.

The Google Cloud product and Risher’s broader business definition overlap, but they are not identical. I think leaders need to treat any catalog product as one possible component of a larger operating model. The business still has to decide which claims are approved, where their evidence lives, who can change them, where they apply and whether an agent may merely state a fact or use it to take an action.

Consider a manufacturer that has verified that one model no longer contains a restricted chemical after a production change. That statement needs the model, serial-number range, market, effective date, laboratory evidence and accountable owner attached to it. An AI system may be allowed to repeat the approved statement for an eligible unit, yet it should not issue a regulatory certificate or extend the claim to older units. The words can be true somewhere and misleading in the answer in front of the customer.

A knowledge catalog should therefore connect existing sources rather than become a new master database. Product-information systems hold product attributes. Master-data systems keep shared records such as product and customer identities consistent. Enterprise data catalogs show which datasets exist and who owns them; content-management systems hold publishable explanations; knowledge graphs map relationships between facts. The knowledge catalog draws an approved answer and its boundaries from those systems, then makes the result usable by websites, assistants and agents.

This also makes ownership a board issue rather than a content-cleanup exercise. A business owner must approve the claim, content and data teams must maintain the connection to its sources, and legal or security leaders must set limits on access and action. For European organizations, those decisions should connect to existing controls under the [General Data Protection Regulation](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj), the European Union’s 2016 law for personal-data processing, and the [EU AI Act](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj), its 2024 rulebook for AI systems. Neither law is a ready-made catalog design, but both make ownership, access and auditability difficult to dismiss.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>A knowledge catalog is not simply another database or the name of one Google Cloud product. It is the operating discipline that links an approved answer to existing source systems, business context, accountable owners and limits on what software may do with it.</p></div>

## Structured content is the head start

**Structured content** represents a business statement as named, reusable fields rather than one block of finished page copy. The manufacturer’s chemical statement could be stored as the claim, affected model, evidence, market, effective date and approval status. Because each part retains its meaning, the same approved record can support a product page, a service conversation or an agent response without being rewritten from scratch.

This is different from **web structured data**, also called schema markup, which adds standardized labels to visible page content so search systems can classify it. In [guidance for its AI search experiences published in May 2025](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/05/succeeding-in-ai-search), Google says its systems consider information shared in that machine-readable form. The same guidance asks publishers for unique, non-commodity material. Both requirements matter: better labels cannot rescue generic or outdated knowledge, while distinctive knowledge is difficult to govern when it remains buried in page copy.

A **headless content management system**, or headless CMS, keeps the material it manages independent from the website design, then makes that material available to other channels through software interfaces. I think organizations with mature content models, reliable ownership and clean interfaces have a substantial head start because the platform already keeps knowledge apart from presentation. This is an architectural judgment, not a measured market statistic. A modern platform full of duplicates and stale claims is still a poor foundation.

The suppliers themselves show where the category is moving. In May 2025, enterprise content platform [Sanity introduced Agent Actions](https://www.sanity.io/blog/agent-actions-ai-building-blocks-for-structured-content), AI building blocks designed around structured content models. In July 2026, enterprise content platform [Contentful made its Remote MCP connector generally available](https://www.contentful.com/blog/remote-mcp-now-generally-available); the Model Context Protocol is a standard for connecting AI assistants to tools and managed information. These are vendor announcements, so they show product direction rather than independent proof of customer adoption.

[*The Digital Singularity Shift*](/books/the-digital-singularity-shift/), my 2024 book, examines what happens when AI makes functions easier to copy and durable value shifts toward meaning, story and belief. I think the recent interface releases make one part of that thesis operational. When a useful screen can be assembled for each request, the screen itself becomes less durable as a differentiator. Verified knowledge, distinctive judgment and the trust attached to them become more important, and structured content is how that value travels without becoming detached from its source.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Structured content preserves the meaning and context of a claim beyond one page. A well-run headless CMS can provide much of the raw material, but the advantage comes from current, distinctive knowledge and clear ownership rather than the platform label.</p></div>

The mapping is the loss of a shared front door. When every customer can receive a machine-assembled path, a company can no longer depend on one page to preserve the context around an answer. The knowledge underneath must carry its source, scope and permissions into whichever interface appears.

## Start with one answer, not another platform

I think the first investment should be a six-week answer-control test, not an enterprise software procurement. Choose one product family, one market and one customer question whose wrong answer has a visible cost. Freeze a test set of twelve realistic ways customers ask that question, then record the current answers across the website, sales and service material, any company assistant and a small selection of public AI services.

Give the test one executive sponsor and one accountable business owner. Add the people who maintain the content and source data, plus a legal or risk reviewer who can reject an unsafe use. Their weekly decision meeting should settle conflicting claims and permissions. Record the hours and external cost of doing the work, because a catalog that improves answers but creates an unaffordable review burden has not solved the management problem.

Make the test repeatable. Run every fixed question twice on the same two days each week, recording the service, displayed model version, date, market and every result rather than selecting the best one. Change one approved fact during the test and measure how long every owned interface takes to use it. My proposed gate is demanding but simple: every owned interface must use the newly approved answer, the update time must fall by at least half from the baseline, and no response may turn the fact into an unauthorized commitment. Public AI services should be monitored, but they should not determine the pass result because the company does not control their update cycle.

Keep search rankings, then add two limited visibility measures. **Mention rate** is the proportion of responses in the fixed test that name the company. **AI share of voice** is the company’s percentage of all brand mentions in that test, measured against a preselected competitor group. These are not universal standards and they do not prove revenue; they show whether the company is present in the answers being observed and whether that presence changes after the catalog record improves.

The consultancy that built the current content platform may be the best place to start because it already knows the content model and integrations. [Valtech](https://www.valtech.com/), a global experience and transformation consultancy, and consultancies in the [Contentful partner network](https://www.contentful.com/partners/), [Sanity agency directory](https://www.sanity.io/agency-partners) or network of [digital-experience software provider Optimizely](https://www.optimizely.com/partners/) are possible sources for a shortlist, not endorsements. A partner badge demonstrates a platform relationship; it says nothing by itself about independence or delivery quality.

My take is to prefer a consultancy that can work across platforms and disclose every license, resale or investment incentive behind its recommendation. Ask how the catalog records, test set and audit history can be exported; how supplier access can be removed; and how an unsafe change can be reversed. Deep platform expertise is useful, but the architecture and the operating model must remain yours.

Start with the answer whose loss of context would cost the business most. Scale only when the test closes the answer-control gap at an acceptable operating cost, pause when access or auditability fails, and stop when the governed record performs no better than the page it was meant to outlive. The durable digital asset is becoming the company truth that survives without the company screen.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Use a six-week, one-question test with fixed prompts, named owners, logged effort and a pre-agreed pass gate. Choose an implementation partner for independence and portability, then expand only when approved answers move faster without losing their context or authority limits.</p></div>
