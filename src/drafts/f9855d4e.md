Your website is on its way to becoming irrelevant as the center of your digital business.

It will not disappear. People will still visit websites for trust, brand and experiences a company wants to shape directly. What is disappearing is the assumption that discovery, evaluation and action must pass through that front door.

The evidence is already visible in a March 2025 study of 900 American adults by [Pew Research Center, an independent US research organization](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/). Google’s AI summary is a generated answer displayed on the search-results page. People clicked a traditional result in 8% of visits when that summary appeared, compared with 15% when it did not. Sources cited inside the AI answer received clicks in just 1% of those visits.

I think business leaders should read this as an investment signal, not merely a search-ranking emergency. As answers, actions and even screens move into AI systems, the durable asset becomes a knowledge catalog: verified, structured company information that software can use without guessing what is current, permitted or true.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The website will remain a valuable channel, but it is losing its position as the required center of the customer journey. Verified company knowledge is becoming more important than control of the page.</p></div>

## Search can answer, and agents can act

This change began before today’s answer-generating AI reached the mainstream. A [2024 study by marketing research firm SparkToro, using browsing data from analytics provider Datos](https://sparktoro.com/blog/2024-zero-click-search-study/) found that 58.5% of US Google searches and 59.7% of EU searches ended without a click. Those figures include abandoned searches and rewritten queries, so they do not prove that AI caused zero-click behavior. They show that a website visit was already optional.

AI answers push the market further in that direction. The same Pew study found that about 60% of searches beginning with a question word produced an AI summary. Search is moving from matching a few keywords with pages toward interpreting a question and assembling an answer. That matters because the customer increasingly evaluates the answer before seeing its source.

The next step is an AI agent, software that can pursue a goal through several actions rather than provide one response. At Google Cloud Next ’25, Google’s annual enterprise technology conference, the company announced [Agent2Agent, or A2A](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/a2a-a-new-era-of-agent-interoperability/). It is a published set of technical rules that lets agents exchange information and coordinate work across different business applications. The protocol does not give an agent authority to buy, book or disclose data, but it creates a common route through which permitted actions can happen.

I don’t know whether delegated buying becomes normal in two years or six. Trust, payments, liability and customer habits remain real constraints. Yet apps and social media were additional destinations; agents can become intermediaries across destinations. That is why this prediction of the website’s decline deserves more attention than earlier claims about its death.

Visibility is changing with it. Rankings still matter, but businesses are also beginning to track mention rate, how often an AI answer names them across a defined set of questions, and AI share of voice, their presence compared with selected competitors. In a 2025 study of 200,000 AI summaries, [search-marketing software company Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/blog/ai-overviews-study/) found that a top-ten position among unpaid search results did not guarantee inclusion in the AI answer. The old scoreboard no longer captures the whole market.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Search increasingly answers questions before a visit, while A2A gives authorized agents a way to coordinate the next action. Rankings remain useful, but they no longer show whether a business is present in the answer customers actually receive.</p></div>

## A knowledge catalog is the new control layer

In a June 2026 video for Phlash Consulting, a marketing consultancy, [Phil Risher described a knowledge catalog](https://youtu.be/xAIeA3ewRbo) as a verified set of information about a business that can be deployed through an agent. I think the term is useful, with one important correction: official Google material reviewed for the research behind this article does not name a product or standard called a knowledge catalog. It is better understood as a strategic model that a company owns.

The catalog sits beneath websites, chatbots and agents. It holds approved claims about services, pricing rules, policies, locations, credentials and customer questions in a form software can read. Each important claim remains connected to evidence, an accountable owner, a review date and rules about who may use it. A chatbot presents an answer; the catalog determines which answer the organization is prepared to stand behind.

Consider a manufacturer that states a component can operate at a certain temperature. In page-based content, that claim may be copied into brochures, product pages and service documents until nobody knows which version is current. In a knowledge catalog, the claim points back to the approved test report and its expiry date. If the evidence expires, an agent should stop repeating the claim instead of turning an old sentence into a new promise.

Risher describes content as “training data for the future internet.” My take is that the strategic idea is right, although the near-term mechanism is often retrieval, which means an AI system looks up approved information while forming an answer. The distinction matters. A company does not need to train the world’s AI models to gain influence; it needs to make its best knowledge clear, distinctive and reliable enough to be found and trusted.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>A knowledge catalog is a company-owned control layer for reusable claims. It lets many interfaces use the same knowledge while keeping each answer tied to evidence, ownership, validity and permission.</p></div>

## Structured content determines who has a head start

Structured content stores information in clearly named fields instead of burying it inside a designed page. A price, service area or policy date can then be recognized and reused by different systems without asking software to infer its meaning from headings and layout. This is what machine-readable information means in practical terms.

In May 2025, [Google Search Central, Google’s guidance service for website owners](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/05/succeeding-in-ai-search/) said structured data helps businesses share machine-readable information that Google’s systems can consider. The same guidance stressed that the underlying content must be unique and useful. Structure can make knowledge available, but it cannot make a generic claim valuable or an outdated claim true.

This gives organizations with a modern headless content management system, or headless CMS, a real advantage. A headless CMS separates reusable content from the design of a particular website and delivers it to many channels through software connections. If the organization already has clear content fields, approval flows and ownership, much of the raw material for a knowledge catalog exists. If its CMS contains little more than long blocks of page copy, the advantage is mostly theoretical.

The platform market is confirming the direction. Contentful, Sanity and Optimizely, three providers of enterprise content technology, released agent-facing or AI-visibility capabilities during 2025 and 2026. Their product claims are not neutral proof of customer adoption, but their roadmaps show where content infrastructure is moving: away from publishing pages alone and toward supplying knowledge to AI systems.

I think this is where near-term digital investment should shift: toward the company-owned content model, clear links from knowledge back to its sources, and the ability to deliver that knowledge beyond one interface. Another website redesign may improve the current channel, but it does not address the shift in where digital value sits.

The same consultancy that built a company’s content platform may be well placed to extend it because the team already understands how its content is organized and approved. Examples include [Valtech, a global digital consultancy](https://www.valtech.com/), and independent partners in the Contentful, Sanity or Optimizely ecosystems. Familiarity alone is not enough, though. I would prefer a consultancy that can work across platforms over a supplier whose financial upside depends on selling more licenses, because the goal is control of the company’s knowledge rather than deeper dependence on one product.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Structured content is the bridge between company knowledge and AI use. A well-run headless CMS creates a practical head start, and investment should favor company-owned knowledge over another cycle of interface renewal or platform dependence.</p></div>

## Generative interfaces reveal the durable asset

*[The Digital Singularity Shift](/books/the-digital-singularity-shift/)*, my October 2024 book about business in an age of abundant AI creation, argues that features and interfaces become cheaper to produce as AI improves, while meaning, story and belief carry more of the value. The market is now exposing the technical mechanism behind that shift. AI answers loosen discovery from the website, agents loosen action from the website, and generative interfaces loosen the experience from a fixed screen.

A generative user interface, or generative UI, is a screen assembled for a particular request instead of designed in advance for every visitor. Google demonstrated this direction in [its Gemini AI application in November 2025](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/gemini/gemini-3-gemini-app/), when the Gemini 3 release introduced interfaces generated at the moment of a prompt. In April 2026, Google released [A2UI v0.9](https://developers.googleblog.com/a2ui-v0-9-generative-ui/), a published set of rules that lets an agent request appropriate buttons, forms or tables while the receiving application keeps control of approved design components.

The closest science-fiction image is the holodeck in *Star Trek: First Contact*, a 1996 film in which a computer creates a setting on demand. The room can change for each mission because the stable intelligence sits beneath the scenery. Generative UI follows the same logic: the screen becomes temporary, while knowledge, permission and identity determine what the experience can say and do.

I think websites will remain important wherever people want a direct relationship with a company. But the economics are changing. When a useful interface can be assembled at the moment of need, pouring the next investment into a fixed website experience creates less durable value. The more important asset now is the verified, structured knowledge that future interfaces and permitted agents will need.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Generative UI turns the screen into a temporary expression of deeper company knowledge. The knowledge catalog is the durable investment because it can support the website that exists today, the agents arriving next and interfaces that have not yet been designed.</p></div>
