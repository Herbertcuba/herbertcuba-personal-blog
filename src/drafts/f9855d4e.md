Your website is becoming less relevant as your company’s main digital hub. People will still visit to compare offers and decide whether they trust you, while discovery and service increasingly happen inside AI interfaces.

[Phil Risher calls the emerging foundation a knowledge catalog](https://youtu.be/xAIeA3ewRbo). I think leaders should appoint an executive owner for approved company claims and set rules for who updates them and how software uses them.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The website remains useful, while governed company knowledge becomes the foundation for discovery and service beyond it.</p></div>

## Search is turning into conversation and action

A [SparkToro and Datos study](https://sparktoro.com/blog/2024-zero-click-search-study/) found that 59.7% of EU Google searches and 58.5% of US searches ended without a click in 2024. The figures include ended sessions and revised searches, so AI explains only part of them. [Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/) later found traditional-result clicks in 8% of visits with an AI summary, compared with 15% without one.

Google’s [Agent2Agent protocol, or A2A](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/a2a-a-new-era-of-agent-interoperability/), lets software agents exchange information and coordinate work. A buyer’s agent could ask whether the industrial valve can arrive Friday. Company controls decide which system may reveal availability and when a sales operations manager must approve the promise, with the exchange logged. I don’t know whether widespread adoption takes two years or six. The control problem belongs on the executive agenda.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>AI answers weaken the search-to-site path, while A2A enables controlled action across systems.</p></div>

## A knowledge catalog turns facts into accountable claims

A knowledge catalog is a governed, machine-readable set of claims a company is prepared to defend. Product databases, master records and document search may supply facts and evidence. The catalog adds an accountable owner, an expiry date, permitted uses and an escalation path to each claim.

Consider an industrial valve rated for minus 30 degrees. Its catalog record would name the certification as evidence, the product owner, the review date and whether software may quote the limit. A price exception or delivery commitment would still go to an authorized employee. I think every automated answer should lead back to a claim the company can prove today.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>A knowledge catalog adds evidence, ownership, expiry and permission to company facts.</p></div>

## Structured content makes knowledge reusable

Structured content stores information in named fields, separate from a finished page. “Operating temperature” becomes one reusable value across websites and brochures. Page markup helps a search system interpret a published page, while structured content gives the company facts it can govern across channels.

[Google’s guidance for AI search](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/05/succeeding-in-ai-search) recommends machine-readable structured data and distinctive content. Risher describes content as “training data for the future internet,” which works as a strategic metaphor. In practice, current facts can also be retrieved while an AI answers, through a process separate from model training.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Structured content separates approved facts from any single page, so people and software can use the same current information.</p></div>

## A headless CMS gives you a practical head start

A headless content management system stores content in reusable models and delivers it through software connections called APIs. I think companies with a modern headless CMS can move faster because some fields, workflows and connections already exist. That advantage becomes real when every important fact has a source, an owner and a review cycle, including facts held in sales or operations systems outside the CMS. The platform supplies raw material; governance makes the knowledge dependable.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>A headless CMS shortens the build when its reusable content is connected to named sources, owners and review cycles.</p></div>

## Generative interfaces change where value sits

[Google’s A2UI work](https://developers.googleblog.com/a2ui-v0-9-generative-ui/) shows agents generating interface elements in real time. Generative UI resembles the holodeck in *Star Trek*: the visible environment is assembled for the request. A question can produce a tailored comparison, form or booking flow.

This makes *[The Digital Singularity Shift](/books/the-digital-singularity-shift/)* more concrete. Cheap interface creation can shrink functional differences and move value toward meaning, story and belief. I think the second-order fault line is ownership of the customer relationship and decision data when another company’s agent assembles the interface. Direct brand experiences will remain important for high-stakes choices. Yet every interface still needs current product rules, evidence and service commitments it can present safely.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Generated interfaces increase the value of knowledge a company can prove and control.</p></div>

## Choose a builder through evidence

The consultancy that built your content platform may be a sensible candidate. Test it through a written selection checklist: demonstrated headless-CMS implementation capability, a clear approach to knowledge governance, full export rights, stated data locations, fees independent of platform incentives, role-based access controls and a handover your team must pass in a practical test.

My take: the assigned team should prove each point first. Ask the consultancy, or an internal owner, to deliver a bounded pilot whose results determine whether the work expands, pauses or ends.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Select a builder through proven capability, aligned terms, portable data and tested handover.</p></div>

## Start with one decision this quarter

Choose one recurring decision, such as whether the valve meets its certified limit and delivery window. Name the executive, content and operations owners, cost ceiling and stop-or-scale threshold in a one-page charter. For four weeks, record the source, evidence, review date, permitted use and escalation path for each claim.

Only named systems may expose claims. Log every exchange and require human approval for prices, delivery promises and contract exceptions. Measure verification time, qualified demand and service effort against today’s baseline. Track mention rate and AI share of voice alongside rankings with fixed questions, retaining them only when they connect to business outcomes.

I think one controlled decision can expose weak ownership and test the operating model. Companies that show what they know, who owns it and when software may act will stay visible wherever the decision begins.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Test one governed slice within firm limits, then expand when business evidence supports it.</p></div>
