Your website can keep attracting visitors and still lose its old role in the customer journey. The important change is happening before the visit: an AI system can answer the question, compare the options and eventually help complete the task without making your homepage the center of the experience.

This is already visible in search. [Pew Research Center, a nonpartisan US research organization, tracked the Google activity of 900 American adults in March 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/). When an AI summary appeared, people clicked a traditional result in 8% of visits, compared with 15% when no summary appeared. Only 1% clicked a source cited inside the summary.

I think the leadership question is therefore changing. It is no longer enough to ask whether customers can find your website. You also need to ask whether the systems standing between the customer and your company can find, understand and correctly use what your company knows. The answer begins with a knowledge catalog: a maintained body of verified business information that software can consume without having to interpret a finished web page.

## Discovery no longer guarantees a visit

The decline of the click started before today's AI boom. A [July 2024 study by audience-research company SparkToro and browsing-data provider Datos](https://sparktoro.com/blog/2024-zero-click-search-study/) estimated that 58.5% of US Google searches and 59.7% of European Union searches ended without a click. Those figures include people who stopped searching or changed their query, so they do not measure an AI effect. Pew's later study supplies the more specific signal: when Google answers on the results page, visits to other sites fall sharply.

Questions are also replacing compact keyword searches. In Pew's March 2025 sample, roughly 60% of searches beginning with a question word produced an AI summary. That matters because a conversation gives the system enough context to compare, qualify and recommend, which moves it closer to doing work on the user's behalf.

An AI agent is software that can pursue a goal and use approved tools, such as checking an inventory system or requesting a booking, rather than only producing an answer. At Google Cloud Next '25, Google's annual business-technology conference, the company launched [Agent2Agent, or A2A](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/a2a-a-new-era-of-agent-interoperability/), an open protocol that lets independently built agents exchange information and coordinate actions. More than 50 technology partners supported the launch.

I don't know how quickly customers will trust agents with expensive or sensitive purchases. Fraud, liability, payment rules and simple human habit remain real brakes. But the strategic direction does not depend on a precise adoption year. As soon as an intermediary can answer part of the buying question, your information has to perform before your website does.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Zero-click behavior is established, AI summaries reduce visits further, and agent protocols are extending discovery from answering questions toward coordinating permitted actions.</p></div>

## What a knowledge catalog must hold

Phil Risher, founder of service-business marketing consultancy Phlash Consulting, described a knowledge catalog in his [June 2026 video](https://youtu.be/xAIeA3ewRbo) as a verified set of company information that can be made available through an agent. I think the phrase is useful, with an important clarification: Google does not document an official product or standard by that name. It is a working business concept for the knowledge layer your company controls.

Consider a manufacturer that sells the same machine in several markets. A useful catalog would not contain one paragraph claiming that the machine has a five-year warranty. It would hold the warranty term, the relevant product model, the country where it applies, its effective date, the approved source and the person responsible for reviewing it. The same pattern would cover compatible parts, delivery conditions, certifications, service areas, pricing rules and answers to recurring customer questions.

That structure turns a statement into something an AI system can use with context. It also makes correction possible. If the warranty changes in one market, the owner updates one governed fact instead of hunting through pages, PDFs, sales material and support scripts that may all tell different stories.

A catalog also needs a boundary between knowledge and authority. Knowing an approved price does not give an agent permission to offer a discount, place an order or commit the company. Those rights belong in separate business rules, with limits and approval paths. I think this distinction will become as important to executives as the accuracy of the underlying content.

Risher calls content "training data for the future internet." Read literally, that overstates the control a company has over how external AI models are trained. Read as strategic shorthand, it lands: every clear, original and consistent fact gives retrieval systems better material from which to represent the business.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>A knowledge catalog keeps each business fact attached to its scope, source, review owner and validity, while permissions to transact remain separately governed.</p></div>

## Structured content gives you a head start

Structured content is information stored in defined fields and relationships, such as product, market, price and effective date. It differs from a page-shaped block of copy because each part can be retrieved and reused on its own. Structured data is the machine-readable description attached to published material; [Google Search Central, Google's guidance service for site owners, said in May 2025](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/05/succeeding-in-ai-search) that it helps Google's systems understand content, while also stressing the need for original and useful information.

A headless content management system, or headless CMS, keeps content separate from the page that displays it and makes that content available through software connections. Companies using one may already have product types, reusable fields, links between records and publishing workflows. I think that creates a meaningful head start because the first catalog work is organizational: identify what can be trusted, assign an owner and expose it in a consistent form.

The platform is only a starting point. A headless CMS full of generic, stale or duplicated material gives an agent efficiently delivered confusion. The advantage becomes real when proprietary knowledge, including actual policies, product logic and recurring customer questions, has clear ownership and review dates.

At the same time, the visible interface is becoming more fluid. A generative user interface is a screen assembled by AI for the task at hand rather than a permanent layout designed in advance. Google introduced real-time generated interfaces with Gemini 3 in November 2025 and published [A2UI, an open project for agent-generated interfaces](https://developers.googleblog.com/introducing-a2ui-an-open-project-for-agent-driven-interfaces/), the following month. When the screen can be created for each request, the reusable facts beneath it carry more long-term value than any single arrangement of those facts.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Modern headless CMS architecture provides useful raw material, but the durable advantage comes from original structured content with owners, context and reliable review processes.</p></div>

## The Digital Singularity changes the center

The Digital Singularity, my 2024 book about digital systems becoming adaptive and increasingly generated, offers a useful frame for this shift. The relevant prediction was not that every website would vanish. It was that fixed digital destinations would matter less as software began assembling experiences around individual intent.

The evidence from 2025 and 2026 now points in that direction. Search produces composed answers, agents are gaining a common way to coordinate, and interfaces can be generated at the moment of need. These developments come from different parts of the technology market, yet they place the same pressure on companies: separate durable knowledge from the temporary form in which a customer sees it.

I think the website will remain valuable for deep research, brand expression, legal disclosure and direct customer relationships. What changes is its status. It stops being the universal hub from which every digital journey must begin and becomes one managed presentation of a knowledge base that must also serve channels the company does not design.

That shift changes investment logic. A redesign can still improve an experience, but it cannot repair missing product facts, conflicting policies or unclear ownership. Improving the knowledge layer raises the quality of the website and every AI-mediated interaction built on the same material.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The Digital Singularity is showing up as a separation between durable company knowledge and interfaces generated for a particular person, channel or moment.</p></div>

## Build the first catalog around one decision

Do not begin with an enterprise-wide repository or a new platform purchase. Choose one consequential customer decision, such as selecting the correct product configuration, and map the facts required to support it. For each fact, record its source, scope, owner, review date and permitted uses. Then test whether an internal assistant can answer a controlled set of real questions accurately and show where each answer came from.

Your existing content partner may be well placed to do this work because the team already understands your content models, integrations and publishing governance. That could be Valtech, a global digital consultancy, or an independent specialist in the partner ecosystem of a content platform such as Contentful, Sanity or Optimizely. The logo matters less than the incentive. I would ask whether the adviser can work across platforms, improve the information model you already own and recommend against a license when the license adds no value. Product expertise is useful; advice tied to selling one product is harder to trust.

The measures must change with the journey. Keep rankings, but add mention rate, the share of tracked questions in which an AI answer names your company, and AI share of voice, your appearances relative to competitors across the same question set. Accuracy, correct qualification and source use matter too. Visibility is worthless when an agent confidently presents the wrong policy.

I think the practical move is to fund a bounded catalog pilot now, before a large redesign absorbs the same attention and budget. The companies best prepared for agent-mediated business will be those that can make their knowledge clear, current and usable wherever the customer's next interface happens to appear.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Start with one customer decision, extend the structured platform you already have, use an adviser whose incentives fit your architecture, and judge success by accurate AI representation as well as visibility.</p></div>
