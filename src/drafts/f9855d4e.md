What happens when a customer can get an answer, compare an offer and prepare an action without visiting your website?

That journey is now technically possible. Search systems can answer questions directly, while an AI agent, software that can pursue a goal and take permitted steps for a person, can coordinate with other systems. The website still matters for trust, complex decisions and direct relationships, but it is no longer safe to treat it as the only doorway to the business.

I think this changes the asset that leaders should protect. A page is one presentation of company information. The deeper asset is the approved knowledge beneath it, arranged so that a website, an employee or an AI system can use it without inventing the missing context. I will call that asset a knowledge catalog.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Customers can increasingly receive answers and begin tasks before they reach a company page. The leadership response is to govern the knowledge beneath the website so it remains useful wherever the journey begins.</p></div>

## Search is turning into answers and actions

The loss of the compulsory click began before the current wave of AI products. In July 2024, [SparkToro, an audience-research company, and Datos, a browsing-data provider](https://sparktoro.com/blog/2024-zero-click-search-study/), estimated that 58.5% of US Google searches and 59.7% of EU searches ended without a click. The figures came from browsing-panel data and included people who ended the session or changed their query, so they do not show that AI caused every zero-click search or that every user received a satisfactory answer.

AI summaries provide more specific evidence. [Pew Research Center, a nonpartisan US research organization, examined the Google browsing activity of 900 American adults in March 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/). People clicked a traditional search result in 8% of visits when Google showed an AI summary, compared with 15% when it did not. A source cited inside the summary received a click in only 1% of those visits. Pew also found that about 60% of searches beginning with question words produced an AI summary, which is why ordinary-language questions matter alongside conventional keywords.

The next step is coordination between agents. At Google Cloud Next ’25, Google’s annual enterprise technology conference, the company launched [Agent2Agent, or A2A](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/a2a-a-new-era-of-agent-interoperability/), with support from more than 50 technology partners. A2A is an open set of rules that lets independently built AI agents exchange information and coordinate actions across business systems. A customer’s buying agent could ask a supplier’s agent about availability, then prepare an order for human approval.

A2A does not decide what an agent is authorized to see or do. Each organization still controls access, permitted actions and approval points. I don’t know which agent protocol or customer habit will dominate, and high-stakes purchases will require more than technical compatibility. Leaders do not need to predict the winner, however, to recognize that company information will be requested outside the path of a designed web page.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Most measured Google searches in the 2024 SparkToro and Datos study produced no click, Pew observed fewer clicks when AI summaries appeared, and A2A lets software agents coordinate work. Visibility now includes the answers and actions that happen before a website visit.</p></div>

## A knowledge catalog gives every important claim a home

In a June 2026 video, [Phil Risher of the marketing consultancy Phlash Consulting](https://youtu.be/xAIeA3ewRbo) describes a knowledge catalog as verified information about a business that can be made available through an agent. The phrase is useful, but it needs careful attribution: neither Google’s April 2025 A2A announcement nor its [May 2025 guidance for AI search](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/05/succeeding-in-ai-search/) defines an official Google product or standard called a knowledge catalog. I use it here as an operating model for business information.

A knowledge catalog is a company-owned body of approved facts arranged so people and software can use them without guessing their meaning or authority. It can cover products, service areas, pricing rules, policies, credentials and real customer questions. For every important claim, the company records the approved wording, source policy, responsible business owner, applicable product or market, review date and current status.

Take the promise “delivery within 24 hours.” In ordinary web publishing, versions of that promise may appear in campaign copy, help articles and country pages. A catalog record would connect it to eligible products and markets, the policy that authorizes it, the person responsible for review and whether it is still active. If the customer’s country is unknown, the correct response is to ask rather than apply a US promise to a customer in Sweden.

This is also why a catalog is more than a larger content repository. The approved version must be traceable, its conditions must travel with it, and the company must be able to withdraw it. Risher calls content “training data for the future internet,” but the phrase is a metaphor rather than a complete technical description. AI systems can also use retrieval, which means looking up external information while composing an answer instead of permanently adding that information to the underlying model.

I think the executive test is straightforward: can the company explain where a commercial claim came from, where it applies, who can change it and how it is removed? If the answer depends on finding the right page owner, the knowledge is still governed as pages rather than as a reusable business asset.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>A knowledge catalog is a governed source of company facts, not an official Google product. It connects every important claim to its scope, approved source, owner, review date and status so the claim can be used, checked and withdrawn.</p></div>

## Structured content supplies the starting material

Structured content stores information in named, reusable parts instead of trapping it inside the layout of one finished page. The delivery promise becomes connected information about time, country, eligible product, approving policy and review date. Because those parts keep their meaning, the same approved record can be delivered to a website, a service tool or an agent without rewriting it for each interface.

Structured content is related to, but broader than, structured data. In Google Search, structured data is standardized markup that helps a machine identify what a web page contains. Structured content is the underlying business information model that can exist independently of any page. [Google Search Central, the company’s official guidance service for website owners, said in May 2025](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/05/succeeding-in-ai-search/) that structured data helps share information in a machine-readable form its systems can consider. The same guidance emphasized original, useful content, because structure cannot make a generic claim distinctive or an expired policy true.

A modern headless content management system, or headless CMS, separates reusable content from the design of a single website and supplies it to different channels through software connections. If products, policies and approval flows are already modeled that way, some of the work needed for a knowledge catalog has been done. If the system mainly holds page-shaped copy, the platform name offers little help.

The vendors’ own roadmaps support this direction, although their announcements are marketing evidence rather than neutral measures of adoption. [Sanity, a structured-content platform, announced AI tools for structured-content workflows in May 2025](https://www.sanity.io/blog/agent-actions-ai-building-blocks-for-structured-content). [Contentful, another content platform, made its secured connection for AI assistants and agents generally available in July 2026](https://www.contentful.com/blog/remote-mcp-now-generally-available). [Optimizely, a digital-experience platform provider, offers an agent that reports a company’s share of mentions in AI answers](https://www.optimizely.com/agents/competitive-ai-share-of-voice-agent).

My take is that a headless CMS creates a useful starting condition only when the organization also has clear owners, approved sources and working review routines. Software can expose a fact. The business decides whether that fact is current and safe to use.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Structured content preserves the meaning of reusable information beyond one page, while a headless CMS can provide models and workflows already suited to that job. The head start is conditional: company ownership and review still determine whether the information deserves trust.</p></div>

## Generated interfaces make the deeper shift visible

The 2024 book *[The Digital Singularity Shift](/books/the-digital-singularity-shift/)* argues that AI makes digital functions easier to reproduce and moves strategic attention toward the meaning beneath them. Recent interface developments make that argument concrete. A generative user interface, often shortened to GenUI, is a screen assembled by AI for the request at hand rather than chosen from a fixed path designed in advance.

In November 2025, [Google’s Gemini 3 AI model introduced experiments that generated a custom interface from a user’s prompt](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/gemini/gemini-3-gemini-app/). In April 2026, Google released version 0.9 of [A2UI, an open proposal for agent-driven interfaces](https://developers.googleblog.com/a2ui-v0-9-generative-ui/). A2UI lets an agent request suitable interface elements while the receiving application controls how those elements look and behave.

Think of the holodeck in *Star Trek: The Next Generation*, the science-fiction television series launched in 1987. Its fictional room renders a responsive environment around the user. GenUI is a modest business equivalent: one customer might receive a comparison while another receives a form, each assembled for the immediate task. When the screen can change on demand, the hard governance question becomes which company claims the system is permitted to use.

Design still matters for trust, accessibility and recognition. I think its role changes from being the container of all digital value to being one controlled expression of the business. That is the useful connection to *The Digital Singularity Shift*: generated function increases the need to control meaning.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>GenUI allows the visible interface to adapt to each request, a direction anticipated by <em>The Digital Singularity Shift</em>. The more flexible the screen becomes, the more important it is to specify which company facts may appear and under what conditions.</p></div>

## Start with ten claims before buying another platform

The consultancy that modeled and connected your current content may be a sensible candidate for the next step. [Valtech, a global digital consultancy](https://www.valtech.com/), and implementation firms in the Contentful, Sanity or Optimizely partner networks are examples to evaluate, not endorsements. Product knowledge is valuable, but a partner badge does not prove commercial independence. Ask whether the firm is being paid to solve your information problem or is also rewarded for increasing the number, scope or cost of licenses you buy.

Put three matters into the contract. Your company should own the information model and be able to export its content in a usable form. The agreement should state where company and customer data may be stored or processed, who maintains approval and review routines after launch, and how the supplier hands over the work if the relationship ends. Access should remain limited to what each person or agent needs, with sensitive actions requiring explicit approval and permissions that can be withdrawn.

Then run a bounded 30-day discovery exercise rather than starting with a large platform program. Choose one high-volume, high-risk set of facts, such as delivery promises, return policies or product eligibility. Select ten claims and record their approved wording, applicable market, source policy, owner, review date, current status and withdrawal path. Trace where each claim has been copied, and document any stale, conflicting or ownerless version you find.

Use a small scorecard for the exercise. Record how many claims have an approved source and owner, how many are within their review date, whether test questions produce the correct answer for the correct market, and whether a withdrawn fact disappears from the channels you control. Keep search rankings, then add mention rate, how often a fixed set of AI answers names the company, and AI share of voice, how those mentions compare with selected competitors. These visibility measures are signals, not proof of answer quality.

At the end of 30 days, leadership can decide whether to expand, change the method or stop before a major commitment. I think that is a better decision point than another website redesign brief built on the assumption that every customer will arrive through the same front door. Before authorizing more systems to speak for the company, establish who owns the facts they will speak with.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Audit ten valuable claims before making a large technology commitment, and require clear ownership, export rights, data-location terms, controlled access and a workable exit from any partner. The immediate board decision is not whether to abandon the website; it is whether anyone owns the facts that every website, employee and agent will reuse.</p></div>
