Your website is becoming a less dependable digital hub. It will still matter for trust and direct customer experiences, but customers can now receive an answer without visiting, and software agents can carry a request into company systems.

That creates a risk larger than lost traffic. When an AI interface operated outside your company becomes the first customer touchpoint, it may make a claim about your products or policies before your company can prove, update or withdraw it. I think the strategic investment must move beneath the page, toward company knowledge that every permitted interface can use under clear rules.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The website still matters, but governed company knowledge is becoming the foundation for discovery and service across AI systems.</p></div>

## Search is moving from answers to action

A [2024 analysis of observed browsing activity by marketing research firm SparkToro and data provider Datos](https://sparktoro.com/blog/2024-zero-click-search-study/) found that 58.5% of US Google searches and 59.7% of EU searches ended without a click. The figure includes people who stopped searching and people who changed their query, so it does not show that AI caused those results. It does show that a search can end before any website receives a visit.

The AI-specific evidence is narrower and more recent. [Pew Research Center, an independent US research organization](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/) tracked the browsing of 900 US adults in March 2025. Traditional search results were clicked in 8% of visits with a Google AI summary and 15% without one, while sources cited inside the summary received clicks in only 1% of visits. About 60% of question-form searches produced a summary, which is where keyword search starts to look more like a conversation. This is an observed association, not proof that the summary caused every missed click, but it makes the direction difficult to ignore.

The next change is from answering to acting. An AI agent is software that pursues a goal through several steps, such as checking requirements, finding an option and requesting a booking. At Google Cloud Next ’25, Google’s annual enterprise technology conference, the company announced [Agent2Agent, or A2A](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/a2a-a-new-era-of-agent-interoperability/), an open set of rules that lets agents exchange information and coordinate work across different applications.

A2A can carry a buyer’s request into a supplier’s system. It does not decide whether the buyer may see a fact, whether that fact is current or who may approve a commercial commitment; the company still needs identity checks, permissions and accountable owners. I don’t know whether this becomes normal in two years or six, but I think leaders already need to decide which answers and actions their companies will authorize.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>AI summaries are associated with fewer visits to search results, while agent protocols create a path from a customer question into company systems. Proof and permission remain the company’s responsibility.</p></div>

## A knowledge catalog closes the verification gap

In a June 2026 Phlash Consulting video, presenter [Phil Risher used “knowledge catalog”](https://youtu.be/xAIeA3ewRbo) for verified, structured information about a business. The term is useful, but it is not a documented Google product or a settled industry standard. I use it more narrowly for a controlled set of claims the company is prepared to stand behind, with each claim linked to evidence, an accountable owner, a review date, access rules and a record of changes.

The catalog does not replace product systems, policy documents or the content platform. Those remain the places where the original facts are maintained. The catalog points to approved facts from those sources and makes the checked version available to websites, assistants or agents. When an original fact changes, the related claim can be reviewed, corrected or withdrawn before it is reused.

Consider an industrial valve. Its catalog record could contain the operating temperature, current certificate, certificate owner and expiry date. If the certificate has expired, the system should say that it cannot confirm compliance and send the question to a person. I think an evidence-backed answer and a clear refusal will both become more valuable than fluent wording.

This leads to a falsifiable prediction. Given the same ten buyer questions, a company with owned and reviewed claims should produce more supported answers and fewer unsafe ones than a company that merely publishes better pages. If a controlled pilot cannot show that difference, the investment should not expand.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>A knowledge catalog makes company claims usable beyond the website without cutting them loose from their sources, owners, review dates or permissions.</p></div>

## Structured content creates the head start

Structured content stores information in named fields instead of burying it inside a designed page. An “operating temperature” field lets a website and an authorized agent retrieve the same value. Machine-readable simply means that software can recognize what the value represents without guessing from layout or surrounding prose.

In May 2025, [Google Search Central, the company’s guidance service for website owners](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/05/succeeding-in-ai-search/), said structured data helps businesses share machine-readable information that Google’s systems can consider. The guidance also stressed unique and useful content. Structure makes information accessible to software, but it cannot make a generic claim distinctive or an outdated claim true.

A headless content management system, or headless CMS, already separates reusable content from website design and supplies it to different channels through standard software connections. I think companies with well-designed fields, approval steps and change histories have a practical head start. They still need to find missing evidence, assign owners and decide which fields an outside system may use.

Risher calls content “training data for the future internet.” That works as a metaphor, but model training and live retrieval are different. Training changes a model using a body of data; retrieval looks up a current approved fact while an answer is being produced. The near-term investment is the retrievable source of truth your company can review and control, especially for proprietary knowledge such as product limits, service eligibility and policy rules.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Structured content and a headless CMS provide reusable fields and workflows, but only distinctive knowledge with current evidence, clear ownership and access rules becomes dependable.</p></div>

## Generated interfaces shift value beneath the page

Generative user interface, or generative UI, means software assembles an interface for a particular request instead of serving the same fixed screen to everyone. In April 2026, Google released [A2UI v0.9](https://developers.googleblog.com/a2ui-v0-9-generative-ui/), an open specification that lets an AI agent describe the buttons, forms and tables needed for a task. The receiving application then renders those elements from its own approved design components, which makes a request-specific interface possible without giving the agent unrestricted control of the screen.

This is a little like the holodeck in *Star Trek: First Contact*, the 1996 science-fiction film in which a computer creates a setting on demand. The room changes with the mission, but its knowledge and operating rules determine what can happen safely. A generated interface works on the same strategic principle: the visible layer can change quickly because the dependable assets sit below it.

*[The Digital Singularity Shift](/books/the-digital-singularity-shift/)*, published in October 2024, is my book about business when AI makes creation abundant. It proposed that functional differences become easier to copy and value moves toward meaning, story and belief. I think generated interfaces make one part of that argument concrete: when presentation can be assembled for each request, a company’s durable difference depends more on what it knows, what it can prove and why people should trust it.

The website remains a direct surface the company can shape. My take is that the market is moving toward many possible surfaces, some owned by the company and some generated elsewhere, so investment must also reach the facts and rules beneath them.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>As interfaces become generated and distributed, checked knowledge and clear operating rules carry more strategic weight than any single fixed presentation.</p></div>

## Build the first catalog as a controlled investment

The consultancy that built your content platform may be a strong candidate because it knows the existing fields and approval routes, but familiarity is not proof of capability. Ask every candidate to demonstrate a previous headless-CMS implementation in which content was moved from fixed pages into reusable fields. It should also show how it gives a claim made available to outside systems a source, owner, review date and permission rule.

Technical capability is not enough because a supplier’s commercial incentives can shape its recommendation. Your contract should give the company full rights to export the content in a usable format, state the country and hosting provider where the data is stored, and name who can view or change it. If a consultancy resells a platform, owns a financial stake in one or receives referral fees or commissions, require written disclosure and project fees that do not depend on license sales or referrals.

Handover should be tested rather than promised. Before final payment, your own team should operate a sample, export it and restore it in another approved environment. This proves that the company can leave and recover its knowledge if the relationship or platform changes.

The supplier demonstration should then become the basis of a four-week pilot built around ten buyer questions and one system that holds the original facts. Start with low-risk product facts or service eligibility. Keep live prices, contracts and decisions governed by law or industry regulation out of scope until the controls have worked under test.

Ownership must be concrete. Name an executive owner who is accountable for the business result, a content owner who approves the claims and review dates, and a technical platform owner who manages access and can restore the previous working state if the pilot fails. That controlled return to the earlier state is often called a rollback.

Set the baseline before changing anything. Ask the same ten questions in three named AI services, creating a fixed sample of 30 answers. For this pilot, mention rate is the number of those 30 answers that name your company at least once, divided by 30. To calculate AI share of voice, count each company no more than once per answer, then divide your company’s count by the combined count for your company and the selected competitors across the same 30 answers. If one answer names your company and two competitors, it adds one to your count and three to the total. These measures show relative visibility, not revenue, and should sit beside rather than replace search rankings during the transition.

The expansion rule should also be agreed in advance. Choose eight questions that should receive current, evidence-backed answers and two that should be refused and sent to a person. Expand only if all ten behave as expected, no unsupported claim appears and one business measure, such as handling time or qualified enquiries, improves beyond a threshold set from the baseline.

I think leaders should begin now without handing consequential decisions to an agent. The first investment is the controlled ability to answer ten important questions with evidence, ownership and the power to say, “we cannot confirm that.”

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Select a vendor-neutral builder through evidence, test a low-risk domain with named owners and fixed acceptance criteria, and expand only when checked answers improve a business result without creating unsupported commitments.</p></div>
