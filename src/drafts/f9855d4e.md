Your website is becoming a secondary interface. Customers will still use it to compare and build trust, but AI answers can end a search before a visit, while agents carry requests into company systems.

[Phil Risher describes the emerging foundation as a knowledge catalog](https://youtu.be/xAIeA3ewRbo). I think the direction is right: the durable asset is company knowledge that software can find, verify and use under clear rules.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The website remains useful, while governed knowledge becomes more important wherever discovery and service happen.</p></div>

## Search is becoming conversation and action

A [SparkToro and Datos study](https://sparktoro.com/blog/2024-zero-click-search-study/) found that 59.7% of EU Google searches and 58.5% of US searches ended without a click in 2024. The study includes ended and revised searches, so it cannot isolate AI. [Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/) later found traditional-result clicks in 8% of visits with an AI summary, versus 15% without one.

Google’s [Agent2Agent protocol, or A2A](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/a2a-a-new-era-of-agent-interoperability/), lets agents exchange information and coordinate work. It carries a request, while each company controls which systems answer, which actions need a person and what gets logged. I don’t know whether this becomes normal in two years or six, but I think the control question already belongs with leadership.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>AI answers weaken the path from search to website, while agent protocols create a controlled path from a question to an action.</p></div>

## A knowledge catalog makes claims accountable

A knowledge catalog is a governed set of company claims for people and software. Product systems and approved documents supply the facts. The catalog adds proof, an accountable owner, a review date, permitted use and an escalation route.

Imagine a valve approved to operate at minus 30 degrees. Its record names the value, certification, owner, next review and whether software may quote it. I think every automated answer should lead back to a claim the company can defend today.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>A knowledge catalog turns scattered facts into approved claims with evidence, ownership, review dates and limits on automated action.</p></div>

## Structured content creates the head start

Structured content stores facts in named fields. Page labels help Google interpret a finished page; a managed “operating temperature” field lets the company approve and reuse one value anywhere. “Machine-readable” means software can recognize that label and value reliably.

[Google recommends structured data for its AI search experiences](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/05/succeeding-in-ai-search). Risher calls content “training data for the future internet,” a useful strategic metaphor. An AI can also retrieve a current approved fact while answering, a process separate from model training.

A headless CMS separates reusable content from page design and supplies it through APIs, standard software connections. I think this gives companies a practical head start because some content models and workflows may already exist. Governance makes that raw material dependable.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Structured content makes facts reusable, and a governed headless CMS provides useful foundations for an agent-facing catalog.</p></div>

## Generated interfaces depend on governed knowledge

Google’s work on [A2UI](https://developers.googleblog.com/a2ui-v0-9-generative-ui/) shows generative UI, where an agent assembles an interface for the request in real time. It resembles the holodeck in *Star Trek*: the visible environment changes with the mission, while knowledge and rules make the scene possible.

*[The Digital Singularity Shift](/books/the-digital-singularity-shift/)* predicted that cheap AI creation would erode functional differences, moving value toward meaning and belief. Generated interfaces make that prediction concrete. When another company’s agent presents the answer, it may own the first customer interaction and learn from the decision. Direct brand experiences still carry weight, but verified product and service claims are required before an interface can represent the company responsibly.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>As interfaces become easier to generate, verified knowledge and control over customer decisions become more durable sources of value.</p></div>

## Choose a builder through evidence

A knowledge catalog joins content design with governance, so the partner choice shapes both technology and ownership. The consultancy that built your content platform is a candidate if it proves headless-CMS delivery and a clear way to assign evidence, owners and reviews.

Contract tests should cover full export rights, named data locations and access controls that limit what each person or system may see and change. Require a handover test that your team must pass. Fees should be independent of license sales or platform incentives, since a provider tied to one platform may favor choices that expand it. Evidence remains necessary for every provider.

My take: let the checklist decide. Use its requirements as pilot acceptance tests, connecting supplier promises to working proof.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Select a builder through proven capability, control over data, fees independent of platform sales and a handover your team can demonstrate.</p></div>

## Start with one decision now

Run a four-week pilot around one decision, such as whether the valve meets a buyer’s operating limit. A one-page charter names the executive owner for the outcome, the content owner for the claim and the operations owner for permitted actions. Set a maximum cost. Expand if verification gets faster without more wrong or expired answers; stop if evidence or access controls fail.

During the test, allow only named systems to expose claims, log every exchange and require human approval for prices, delivery promises and contract exceptions.

Measure verification time with qualified demand or service effort. Mention rate is how often the company appears in answers to fixed buyer questions. AI share of voice is its portion of those mentions against named competitors. Fixed questions make periods comparable; keep both measures only when they connect to business outcomes.

I think leaders should begin now, with one decision and firm controls. If software increasingly mediates customer decisions, governed knowledge gives your company a credible place in the conversation.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Test one governed decision for four weeks, expand on business evidence and keep consequential commitments under human control.</p></div>
