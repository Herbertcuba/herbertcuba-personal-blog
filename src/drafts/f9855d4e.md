Your website is not disappearing. It is losing its right to organize your entire digital strategy.

For years, the commercial path was easy to picture: a person searched, clicked, entered a website and acted. Search engines can now answer before the click, while AI agents can research options and start a task without following the journey your company designed.

That changes where durable value sits. I think the strongest near-term investment is a **knowledge catalog**: a governed collection of verified company facts that websites, assistants and AI agents can all use. The website remains an important channel, but the catalog becomes the shared source beneath it.

<p>The website still matters, but verified and reusable business knowledge is becoming the more durable digital asset.</p>

## Discovery is moving ahead of the website

A [2024 study](https://sparktoro.com/blog/2024-zero-click-search-study/) by marketing research company SparkToro and clickstream-data provider Datos estimated that 58.5% of searches made through Google in the US and 59.7% in the EU ended without a click. A zero-click search can mean that someone found an answer, gave up or changed the query, so the figures do not prove that AI caused the shift.

The AI-specific evidence is clearer. [Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/), a nonpartisan US research organization, examined the March 2025 browsing activity of 900 adults. People clicked a traditional result in 8% of visits when Google showed an AI summary, compared with 15% when it did not. Links cited inside the summary received clicks in only 1% of visits containing one.

An AI agent pushes the change from answers into action. An agent is software that can plan and perform parts of a task for a user. Google launched its open [Agent2Agent protocol](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/a2a-a-new-era-of-agent-interoperability/) in April 2025 with more than 50 technology partners, allowing participating agents to exchange information and coordinate work. In July 2026, Google expanded [Gemini Spark](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-app/gemini-spark-updates-july-2026/), a personal agent that can research alternatives and begin a browser booking with permission, while returning sensitive steps such as payment to the person.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Discovery is shifting from links to answers, and agents extend that shift by beginning tasks before a customer reaches a company website.</p>
</div>

## A knowledge catalog turns facts into infrastructure

“Knowledge catalog” is not an established Google product or shared technical standard. [Phil Risher](https://youtu.be/xAIeA3ewRbo), owner of digital marketing firm Phlash Consulting, used the term in a June 2026 video for verified information about a business that can be made available to an agent. I use it because it names an asset many companies have not yet managed as one.

A useful catalog could hold services, pricing rules, policies, locations, credentials and answers to real customer questions. Each important fact needs a source, an owner, a market, a period of validity and an approval status. Those details let a system distinguish a current Swedish return policy from an expired US one instead of treating both as interchangeable text.

Control has limits. A company can require its own agent to follow access rules and withdraw outdated facts, but it cannot force an outside agent to use the catalog or repeat it correctly. The catalog therefore improves the quality and availability of authoritative information; it does not guarantee an answer. Rankings and clicks still matter, but leaders should also watch mention rate, meaning how often the company appears in relevant AI answers, and AI share of voice, its portion of those mentions compared with competitors.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>A knowledge catalog makes approved business facts reusable and traceable, while recognizing that external AI systems remain outside the company’s control.</p>
</div>

## Structured content creates the head start

Structured content stores information in defined fields and relationships rather than burying it inside a finished page. A price can be identified as a price, tied to a product and market, and given an effective date. Google’s [May 2025 guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/05/succeeding-in-ai-search) says structured data helps systems understand content in a machine-readable form, while also stressing that the underlying information must be useful and original.

This is why companies with a modern **headless content management system** have a head start. A headless CMS keeps content separate from a particular page design and delivers it to different channels through software connections called APIs. That separation creates options, an architectural point explored further in the article [The Quiet Truth About Headless CMS](/posts/the-quiet-truth-about-headless-cms/). It does not create truth: weak facts, unclear ownership and duplicate records remain weak in any architecture.

The same distinction should guide supplier selection. Look for an independent implementation partner with proven headless-CMS capability, and define knowledge governance as named ownership and regular review of important facts. Then test export rights, data location, access controls, fees structured independently of platform incentives, and a handover demonstrated in practice.

This market direction is consistent with the [Digital Singularity](/posts/mckinsey-digital-singularity/), my framework for the convergence of content, software and AI into experiences assembled around the immediate need. The recent evidence does not settle the adoption timeline. It does show why the same approved fact must be able to serve a website, an assistant and a future interface without being recreated each time.

<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Structured content and a headless CMS provide reusable raw material, but governance, independence and verified facts determine whether that head start has business value.</p>
</div>

## Invest in what survives the interface

Generative UI makes the investment choice sharper. **Generative user interface** means a screen assembled for a particular request instead of designed once for everyone. Google demonstrated this direction in its Gemini 3 AI model in November 2025 and introduced [A2UI](https://developers.googleblog.com/introducing-a2ui-an-open-project-for-agent-driven-interfaces/) in December 2025 as an open project for agent-generated interfaces.

The first executive move is to give one business owner accountability for the highest-value facts and fund their inventory, verification and maintenance before a controlled pilot. If that pilot later lets an agent act, the investment must also cover data responsibility, access rules, supplier risk and a human right to stop the system.

Keep funding a good website where customers need one. But do not let another redesign consume the budget required to make the company’s knowledge usable beyond that site. As generative UI makes each web interface more temporary, the knowledge catalog becomes the part worth building to last.
