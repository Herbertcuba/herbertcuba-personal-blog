A customer can now receive an AI-generated answer about your price, warranty or delivery terms without visiting the page where your company approved the wording. An AI agent, software that can pursue a goal and take permitted steps for a person, may then use that answer to prepare a purchase somewhere else.

The website will remain useful for explanation, persuasion and conversion. But I think leaders should stop treating it as the compulsory hub and start treating it as one interface among several. That creates a harder question than how much search traffic you might lose: who owns the commercial truth when another system becomes the interface?

I call the capability needed here **claim control**. It means being able to show where an important business statement came from, where it applies, who approved it, how company-controlled systems may use it and how quickly those systems can correct it. A **knowledge catalog** is the operating model I propose for that capability.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>An AI-generated answer no longer requires a website visit, and technical rules now let AI programs coordinate permitted work across business systems. Leaders therefore need control over the source, scope, ownership and permitted use of important business statements after those statements leave the page.</p></div>

## The click is no longer guaranteed

In a 2024 clickstream study, a majority of the measured Google searches ended without a click. [SparkToro, an audience-research company, and Datos, a browsing-data provider](https://sparktoro.com/blog/2024-zero-click-search-study/), estimated that the shares were 58.5% in the United States and 59.7% in the European Union. Those figures include people who stopped searching or changed their query. They do not show that AI caused every zero-click search, or that every searcher received a satisfactory answer.

AI summaries offer more direct evidence. [Pew Research Center, a nonpartisan US research organization, studied the Google browsing activity of 900 American adults in March 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/). People clicked a traditional result in 8% of visits when Google showed an AI summary, compared with 15% when it did not. A source cited inside the summary received a click in only 1% of those visits. Pew also found that about 60% of searches beginning with question words produced an AI summary. I think that pattern is consistent with discovery becoming more conversational, although the study did not measure a general move away from keywords over time.

Google has also published a technical route for agents to coordinate actions. At Google Cloud Next ’25, Google’s annual enterprise technology conference, the company launched [Agent2Agent, or A2A](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/a2a-a-new-era-of-agent-interoperability/), with support from more than 50 technology partners. A2A is an open set of rules that lets independently built AI agents exchange information and coordinate work across business systems. A customer’s agent could ask a supplier’s agent about availability, for example, and prepare an order for human approval.

I don’t know which protocol or customer habit will dominate, especially for expensive or high-risk decisions. A2A provides a way for systems to communicate; it does not certify that a supplier’s information is true or give an agent commercial authority. I think leaders should therefore stop assuming that every customer will reach a company-controlled screen before company information shapes the decision.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Independent studies found that many Google searches end without a click and that traditional-result clicks were less common when an AI summary appeared. Google’s Agent2Agent rules also let independently built AI programs coordinate permitted work across business systems.</p></div>

## A knowledge catalog keeps conditions attached

In a June 2026 video, [Phil Risher of Phlash Consulting, a marketing consultancy for service businesses](https://youtu.be/xAIeA3ewRbo), describes a knowledge catalog as verified company information that can be made available through an agent. I find the phrase useful, but the Google materials cited here do not establish an official product or standard by that name. In this article, it means a company-owned collection of approved knowledge that people and software can use without guessing what a fact means.

The catalog can contain product details, service areas, pricing rules, policies, credentials and answers to real customer questions. A sensitive statement should carry its source, accountable owner, applicable market and product, review date, current status and limits on use. The wording alone is not enough because those conditions determine whether it is true.

A public Nordic policy difference shows the problem. [Konsumentverket, Sweden’s national consumer agency, says on a page reviewed in November 2025](https://www.konsumentverket.se/konsumentratt-process/reklamera-vara/) that consumers in Sweden have three years to complain about a faulty product. [Forbrukerrådet, Norway’s consumer council](https://www.forbrukerradet.no/forside/feil-ved-vare/), says the Norwegian period is two or five years depending on how long the product is meant to last. If a Nordic retailer released the generic answer “you have three years” to a Norwegian customer, it would give a valid Swedish rule the wrong geographic reach. The Norwegian market owner should be able to stop that answer until the local condition and approved source travel with it.

A compact catalog record could make the boundary visible:

| Field | Illustrative value |
| --- | --- |
| Statement | A consumer may complain about a faulty product within three years |
| Market | Sweden only |
| Approved source | Current Swedish consumer policy and internal legal approval |
| Accountable owner | Nordic customer-policy director |
| Review rule | Recheck after a legal or policy change |
| Permitted use | May answer an information request; refunds require separate approval |

The final row is essential. Permission to retrieve a policy does not include permission to use personal data, promise a refund or amend an order. Each action needs its own limited and reversible authority.

Risher calls content “training data for the future internet.” I think that works as a strategic metaphor, rather than a complete technical description. Many AI systems use **retrieval**, which means looking up relevant company information while composing an answer instead of permanently adding it to the underlying model. In either case, the company needs evidence of which source was used and a reliable route for replacing it in the systems it controls.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>A knowledge catalog is a company-owned operating model for approved business knowledge, not an official Google standard in the sources used here. It keeps each statement connected to its evidence, local conditions, accountable owner and action limits so a correct fact does not become a false promise when reused.</p></div>

## Structured content is the key

**Structured content** stores information in named, reusable parts instead of leaving it only inside the layout of a finished page. A return policy becomes connected information about the market, eligible product, time limit, approving source and review date. Because those parts retain their meaning, the same approved record can serve a website, a customer-service tool or an agent without being rewritten for every interface.

Structured content is related to **structured data**, but they describe different layers. In Google Search, structured data is standardized code added to a page so a machine can identify what the page contains. Structured content is the underlying model of the business information. [Google Search Central, Google’s official guidance service for website owners, said in May 2025](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/05/succeeding-in-ai-search/) that structured data helps share information in a machine-readable form its systems can consider. The guidance also emphasized original, useful content because structure cannot make a stale policy true or generic copy distinctive.

A modern **headless content management system**, usually called a headless CMS, keeps reusable content separate from the design of one website and supplies it to different channels through software connections. If your company already models products, policies, markets and approvals this way, it has useful raw material for a catalog. It does not need to extract that information from page-shaped copy before the catalog work can begin. That is a concrete architectural head start, although the headless label proves nothing if ownership is unclear and the content cannot be exported cleanly.

For a large organization, the catalog should connect authoritative systems rather than replace them. Shared customer and supplier identities may remain in a master-data system, which keeps core business entities consistent. Product specifications may remain in a product-information system built to maintain product attributes, while live stock and prices stay in operational systems. The catalog can expose approved views, record which source has authority and leave regional teams in control of local conditions.

This also gives leaders a practical investment rule. Extend the current content system when it already supports reusable fields, market scope, approval history, usable exports and delivery through software connections. Add separate catalog capacity when important statements depend on several authoritative systems, change in real time or require different access rights. Structured content supplies the knowledge model; identity controls, access policies and answer monitoring supply necessary boundaries around it.

Vendor roadmaps support the direction, although they cannot prove adoption or safety. [Sanity, a structured-content platform, announced AI tools for structured-content workflows in May 2025](https://www.sanity.io/blog/agent-actions-ai-building-blocks-for-structured-content). [Contentful, another content platform, made its hosted Model Context Protocol connection generally available in July 2026](https://www.contentful.com/blog/remote-mcp-now-generally-available), providing a secured route between AI assistants and structured-content workflows. My take is that the useful advantage comes from modeled meaning and assigned responsibility, not from the platform logo.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Structured content keeps business information in reusable parts with their meaning intact. A content management system that already separates those parts from one website’s design may provide useful raw material, while source ownership, access rules and connections to authoritative systems still have to be designed.</p></div>

## Generated interfaces still need dependable meaning

The connection to *[The Digital Singularity Shift: The Rise of Metamodern Narratives in Business](/books/the-digital-singularity-shift/)*, my 2024 book about AI making digital functions easier to reproduce and increasing the importance of the meaning beneath them, can now be stated more precisely. A **generative user interface**, often shortened to GenUI, is a screen assembled by AI for the request at hand instead of selected from one fixed path designed in advance.

In November 2025, [Gemini 3, an AI model from Google, introduced experiments that generated a custom interface from a user’s prompt](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/gemini/gemini-3-gemini-app/). In April 2026, Google released version 0.9 of [A2UI, an open specification for agent-driven interfaces](https://developers.googleblog.com/a2ui-v0-9-generative-ui/). It lets an agent describe the interface elements needed for a task while the receiving application controls how they look and behave.

Think of the holodeck in *Star Trek: The Next Generation*, the science-fiction television series that began in 1987. Its fictional room creates a responsive environment around the person inside it. GenUI is a modest business version of that idea: one customer may receive a comparison while another receives a form, each assembled for the immediate request. The presentation can change while the price, policy and authority underneath it must remain dependable.

This does not remove the need for design. Companies still need recognition, trust and accessibility in the experiences they control. I think leaders should nevertheless assign more strategic weight to the governed meaning beneath the screen, because companies do not control every surface where their information can appear. That is how I apply the thesis from *The Digital Singularity Shift* here: as a decision lens, rather than proof that generated interfaces have already made fixed front ends less valuable across the market.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>A generative user interface is a screen assembled by AI for a particular request. When one body of company knowledge can appear through many different screens, leaders should treat the dependable meaning and authority underneath them as the more reusable asset.</p></div>

## Test ten statements before buying more technology

Do not begin with another platform purchase. I would start with a 30-day exercise around ten high-volume or high-risk statements, such as delivery promises, return rights or product eligibility. My proposed minimum is one named pilot lead with roughly two days a week protected, four or five specialists contributing a few hours each week, and an executive sponsor joining a short weekly decision meeting. This is a starting proposal, not a measured industry standard.

Make the decision rights explicit:

| Role | Work and authority |
| --- | --- |
| Business owner | Approves what is true and where it applies; remains accountable for the statement |
| Information maintainer | Keeps the record, source and review date current; runs correction tests |
| Local market owner | Confirms local scope and can block release when a regional rule is unclear |
| Legal or compliance specialist | Reviews evidence and sensitive commitments; can require the statement to remain unavailable |
| Security and technology specialist | Controls system access and tests that reading information cannot become an unauthorized action |
| Executive sponsor | Resolves ownership conflicts and decides whether to scale, pause or stop |

First record how long it takes to find the authority behind each statement, how many versions conflict and how many lack an owner. Then create the ten catalog records, connect them to one controlled assistant and run a correction test outside production. For the pilot, I think a useful service target is to remove an expired answer from the company website and controlled assistant within four business hours of an approved change. The board-ready measure is simple: time from changed business promise to corrected answer in every channel the company controls.

The boundary matters as much as the target. Your company can test its own website, assistant, service tool and stored copies used to speed responses. It cannot guarantee immediate removal from a public Google summary, a customer’s saved chat or an external AI system that has already copied an answer. If internal correction becomes faster while customer-facing answers remain persistently inconsistent, the catalog is better content maintenance, but it has not yet become the strategic asset described here.

Test failure, not only the happy path. Ask the assistant for an expired statement and confirm that it refuses or retrieves the replacement. Give it a Norwegian customer with a Swedish market condition and check that it does not mix the policies. Then place a malicious instruction in an outside source and verify that this **prompt injection**, an attempt to manipulate an AI system through untrusted text, cannot increase its access or turn a policy lookup into an unauthorized refund. These tests expose stale information, wrong-market matching and unauthorized action before customers do.

Visibility can sit beside correction in the same exercise. **Mention rate** is how often a fixed set of AI answers names the company. **AI share of voice** compares those mentions with a defined competitor set. If the company is absent from accurate answers about an important product category, leaders may prioritize missing evidence. If it is frequently mentioned with an expired policy, correction comes first. Attention without accuracy increases the risk.

At day 30, require three deliverables: a register of the ten statements with owners and sources, a record of the correction and permission tests, and a recommendation for the next investment decision. Scale when every statement has an accountable owner, the correction target holds in controlled systems and the permission tests pass. Pause when evidence or regional authority remains disputed. Stop or redesign the approach when stale answers survive repeatedly or the assistant crosses from reading to acting without approval.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Start with a staffed 30-day test of ten important business statements. Measure correction time in company-controlled channels, test expired and wrong-market information, prevent unauthorized action, and end with a documented decision to scale, pause or stop.</p></div>

## Choose a partner that can recommend less platform

Only after the operating model is clear should the supplier question enter. The consultancy that modeled and connected your current content may be a sensible candidate because it already understands the information structure. [Valtech, a global digital consultancy](https://www.valtech.com/), and implementation firms in the partner networks of Contentful, Sanity and [Optimizely, a digital-experience platform](https://www.optimizely.com/partners), are examples to evaluate, not endorsements.

Platform knowledge has real value, and a partner badge does not guarantee independence. Ask whether the firm is paid only to solve your information problem or also benefits when you buy more licenses. Require it to explain when the existing content system is enough and when an additional connection, policy or monitoring layer is justified. A credible consultancy should be able to recommend less platform when less platform is the right answer.

Put control into the contract. Your company should own the information model, receive usable exports, decide where data is stored and processed, restrict access for each person and AI system, and get a tested handover if the relationship ends. Those conditions matter more than a vendor logo because the catalog must remain usable when an interface or supplier changes.

Start with the business promises that cause the most harm when they are wrong. Before more AI systems answer and act on your behalf, make sure the truth can leave the website without leaving its conditions behind.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The consultancy that built a modern content platform may be well placed to extend it, provided its incentives and contract preserve company ownership, usable exports and access control. Define the knowledge and decision rights first, then choose a partner that can justify both more technology and less.</p></div>
