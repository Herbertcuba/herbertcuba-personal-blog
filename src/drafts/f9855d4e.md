Your next customer may choose your company while your homepage sits unopened.

Imagine an office manager asking an AI agent to find a supplier that can survey a Gothenburg site for 12 EV chargers next month. The agent could check coverage and request a slot. The homepage stays closed.

That journey is still emerging. I think the website remains valuable for brand and proof, while its old job as the mandatory hub is fading.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Customers are beginning to get answers and initiate work through AI interfaces. The durable asset moves below the page, into company knowledge that software can retrieve safely.</p></div>

## Search is becoming a transaction layer

The familiar path was search, click, website, form. Companies could improve each page and measure the visits that reached it.

A [SparkToro study using Datos clickstream data](https://sparktoro.com/blog/2024-zero-click-search-study/) found that 58.5% of US Google searches and 59.7% of EU searches ended without a click in 2024. The measure includes ended sessions and changed queries, and it predates broad AI-answer adoption.

[Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/) gives us the AI-specific signal. In its March 2025 sample, traditional results received clicks in 8% of visits with an AI summary, compared with 15% without one. Sources inside the summary received clicks in 1% of visits.

Google launched the open [Agent2Agent protocol, or A2A](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/a2a-a-new-era-of-agent-interoperability/) at Cloud Next in April 2025 with support and contributions from more than 50 technology partners. A2A lets agents exchange information and coordinate across systems. Applications still have to enforce authorization and validate bookings.

[ServiceTitan says its AI virtual agents](https://www.servicetitan.com/blog/webinar-recap-ai-virtual-agents-call-booking) can book or reschedule field-service jobs. Its product covers a narrower flow than the fictional EV journey. I think the strategic break arrives when an answer can start work. The protocol question goes deeper in [Who owns the agent layer?](/posts/who-owns-the-agent-layer/).

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Search already keeps many answers away from company websites. A2A extends the path into coordinated action, while authorization remains the company's responsibility.</p></div>

## A knowledge catalog creates answer contracts

Phil Risher describes a [knowledge catalog](https://youtu.be/xAIeA3ewRbo) as a verified dataset about a business that an agent can use. The Google pages cited here stop short of defining a product category with that name. I use it for maintained business facts that software can retrieve without scraping a page.

For the fictional EV supplier, one entry might look like this. Every value is illustrative.

```yaml
fact_id: workplace_ev_site_survey
service_area: "Gothenburg municipality"
site_size_limit_ports: 20
survey_fee_sek: 4900
valid_until: 2026-09-30
source_version: commercial_policy_v8
fact_owner: commercial_ops@company.example
reader_scope: public_agent
allowed_action: request_site_survey
quote_authority: human_only
stale_action: escalate
```

The agent can confirm coverage and request a survey because both are explicit. A human retains authority to issue a binding quote. Once the entry expires, the agent escalates.

Treat each entry as an answer contract. It states the source, owner, lifetime and permitted use of a business claim. A normal CMS entry rarely carries that decision boundary.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>A knowledge catalog turns business facts into answer contracts. Each contract connects a claim to its source, owner, lifetime and permitted use.</p></div>

## Structured content makes the agent loop inspectable

A webpage might say, "We install workplace chargers across western Sweden." An agent still has to infer whether Gothenburg is covered, whether 12 chargers fit and whether the price is current.

Structured content gives those details separate fields. Google's [guidance for AI search](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/05/succeeding-in-ai-search) calls structured data a machine-readable way to share content information. Risher calls content "training data for the future internet." With retrieval, the agent can fetch the current entry at request time, so a policy change does not require model retraining.

The EV example makes four engineering terms concrete.

**ReAct** combines reasoning with actions. The model decides that it needs the service area, calls the catalog tool and uses the result for its next step. The name comes from the [ReAct paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629).

**[LangGraph](https://github.com/langchain-ai/langgraph/blob/main/libs/langgraph/langgraph/graph/state.py)** is a Python library for stateful agent workflows. A **StateGraph** connects nodes that update shared state. A **[TypedDict](https://docs.python.org/3/library/typing.html#typing.TypedDict)** describes the keys and value types expected in a Python dictionary. Type checkers can catch a missing field, although Python leaves runtime enforcement to your code.

```python
from typing import Literal, TypedDict
from langgraph.graph import StateGraph

class SurveyState(TypedDict):
    postcode: str
    catalog_version: str | None
    decision: Literal["answer", "request_survey", "escalate"]

flow = StateGraph(SurveyState)
```

One node retrieves the entry, and another checks its expiry and permissions. I think a junior engineer should be able to show which version produced an answer and which rule permitted the next action.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Structured fields let code inspect a claim before a model uses it. ReAct describes the tool loop, while LangGraph, StateGraph and TypedDict expose the state and decision path.</p></div>

## Headless CMS teams start closer to the answer

A headless CMS stores content separately from the frontend and delivers it through APIs. If service areas and price rules already live as fields in Contentful, Sanity or Optimizely, the EV supplier has useful raw material.

The team still needs owners, expiry behavior and action permissions. [Sanity's Agent Actions](https://www.sanity.io/blog/agent-actions-ai-building-blocks-for-structured-content) and [Contentful's Remote MCP](https://www.contentful.com/blog/remote-mcp-now-generally-available) show the platform direction. These vendor releases offer directional evidence rather than neutral proof.

I think organizations with mature content models have a structural advantage because knowledge is already separate from presentation. That argument begins in [The quiet truth about headless CMS](/posts/the-quiet-truth-about-headless-cms/).

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>A headless CMS supplies modeled content and APIs. The knowledge catalog adds authority and failure rules before that content can support agent action.</p></div>

## Generative UI makes the screen temporary

Generative UI, or GenUI, creates an interface for the request at hand. Google's open [A2UI project](https://developers.googleblog.com/introducing-a2ui-an-open-project-for-agent-driven-interfaces/) lets agents send interface descriptions to different renderers.

Our office manager might hear the coverage answer through a car speaker, then receive a generated survey card on a phone. The same answer contract supports both.

I don't know whether websites lose their hub role in three years or seven. Separating company knowledge from the screen pays under either timeline.

## The Digital Singularity reaches the content layer

In [The Digital Singularity Shift](/books/the-digital-singularity-shift/), I argue that AI collapses functional differentiation toward zero and moves competition toward meaning, story and belief. The book's thesis is broader than CMS architecture. A knowledge catalog is my operational reading of that mechanism at the content layer.

Generated interfaces make frontend function easier to reproduce. Verified knowledge still needs judgment and accountability. The human surface carries meaning; the machine layer carries claims the company is prepared to honor.

I think this is one concrete form of the shift described in the book. Content becomes governed input for systems that answer and act.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Cheap generation reduces the scarcity of interface function. Value moves into human meaning and company knowledge that stays trustworthy elsewhere.</p></div>

## Choose the partner by incentive

The consultancy that built your content platform may already understand its models and approval paths. Valtech is one example. The [Contentful](https://www.contentful.com/partners/), [Sanity](https://www.sanity.io/agency-partners) and [Optimizely](https://www.optimizely.com/partners/) directories can help you find others.

My take is to prefer an independent consultancy when the catalog crosses platforms or reaches pricing and booking. A platform specialist can move faster inside one product, while a license incentive can quietly shape the architecture.

Ask whether your company can export the catalog, revoke access without supplier help and retain the schema history. Then ask how the partner gets paid when the best design uses fewer platform features.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Your existing content partner can shorten the start. Inspect exit rights and commercial incentives before giving any supplier control of the answer layer.</p></div>

## Start with twenty questions and one forbidden action

Use the fictional EV supplier as a six-week pilot. Pick twenty customer questions about one service area. Keep the endpoint read-only, with a binding quote as the forbidden action.

Name a business owner, a content steward, an AI engineer and a risk reviewer. Meet weekly to resolve disputed answers. Give the business owner authority to pause the endpoint.

Spend two weeks locating sources and owners, two weeks modeling a versioned endpoint, then two weeks connecting the agent and testing expired data.

| Control | Pilot pass condition |
| --- | --- |
| Source | Every response includes `source_version` |
| Freshness | An expired entry always escalates |
| Authority | The agent cannot issue a binding quote |
| Trace | Every decision returns a trace ID and catalog version |

Keep search rankings in the report. Add coverage of priority questions, stale-answer rejection rate and AI mention rate. Share of voice is available in tools such as [Optimizely's Competitive AI Share of Voice Agent](https://www.optimizely.com/agents/competitive-ai-share-of-voice-agent/), although a spreadsheet is enough here.

I think the first useful knowledge catalog should feel small. If the agent breaks the agreed controls on twenty narrow questions, fix the source data before giving it authority to act.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Begin with a read-only catalog, explicit owners and a forbidden customer commitment. The next front door is an agent asking for a fact. Give it an answer your company is prepared to own.</p></div>
