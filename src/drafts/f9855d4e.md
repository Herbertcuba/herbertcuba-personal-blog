Your website is losing its monopoly on the customer promise.

It will still explain, persuade and convert. Yet a customer can now receive an AI-generated answer about your price, warranty or delivery terms without seeing the page where you approved the wording. An AI agent, software that can pursue a goal and take permitted steps for a person, may then use that answer to prepare a purchase somewhere else.

That creates a harder question than how much search traffic you might lose: who owns the commercial truth when an outside AI system becomes the interface? I call the capability needed here **claim control**. It is the ability to show where an important fact came from, where it applies, who approved it, how controlled systems may use it and how quickly your company can correct it.

I think this is the board-level shift. The website remains a channel, while the governed knowledge behind every channel becomes the strategic asset. A **knowledge catalog** is a practical operating model for that asset.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The website will remain useful, but it is no longer guaranteed to be the entrance to a company. Leaders now need control over the meaning, ownership and permitted use of important commercial facts after those facts leave the page.</p></div>

## Search is becoming an answer and an action

The compulsory click was weakening before the current wave of AI products. In July 2024, [SparkToro, an audience-research company, and Datos, a browsing-data provider](https://sparktoro.com/blog/2024-zero-click-search-study/), estimated that 58.5% of US Google searches and 59.7% of EU searches ended without a click. These figures include people who stopped searching or changed their query, so they do not prove that AI caused every zero-click search or that every searcher found a satisfactory answer.

AI summaries provide more direct evidence. [Pew Research Center, a nonpartisan US research organization, studied the Google browsing activity of 900 American adults in March 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/). People clicked a traditional result in 8% of visits when Google showed an AI summary, compared with 15% when it did not. A source cited inside the summary received a click in only 1% of those visits. Pew also found that about 60% of searches beginning with question words produced an AI summary, which helps explain why discovery increasingly resembles a conversation rather than a list of keywords.

Answers are now being joined by actions. At Google Cloud Next ’25, Google’s annual enterprise technology conference, the company launched [Agent2Agent, or A2A](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/a2a-a-new-era-of-agent-interoperability/), with support from more than 50 technology partners. A2A is an open set of rules that lets independently built AI agents exchange information and coordinate work across business systems. A customer’s agent could ask a supplier’s agent about availability, for example, and prepare an order for human approval.

I don’t know which protocol or customer habit will dominate, especially for expensive or high-risk decisions. A2A also does not establish whether a supplier’s information is true or whether an agent has authority to act on it. Still, the direction is clear enough to change an executive assumption: company information can shape a decision before the customer reaches any company-controlled screen.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>SparkToro and Datos measured a majority of Google searches ending without a click, Pew observed fewer clicks when AI summaries appeared, and A2A lets software agents coordinate work. Discovery can now produce an answer or begin an approved action before a website visit.</p></div>

## A knowledge catalog keeps conditions attached

In a June 2026 video, [Phil Risher of Phlash Consulting, a marketing consultancy for service businesses](https://youtu.be/xAIeA3ewRbo), describes a knowledge catalog as verified company information that can be made available through an agent. The phrase is useful, but it is not an official Google product or standard in Google’s A2A announcement or its guidance for AI search. I use it here for a company-owned body of approved knowledge that people and software can use without guessing what a fact means.

The catalog can contain product details, service areas, pricing rules, policies, credentials and answers to real customer questions. A sensitive claim should include its source, business owner, applicable markets and products, review date, current status and action limits. The sentence alone is not enough because its conditions determine whether it is true.

A public Nordic policy difference shows why this matters. [Konsumentverket, Sweden’s national consumer agency, says on a page reviewed in November 2025](https://www.konsumentverket.se/konsumentratt-process/reklamera-vara/) that consumers in Sweden have three years to complain about a faulty product. [Forbrukerrådet, Norway’s consumer council](https://www.forbrukerradet.no/forside/feil-ved-vare/), says the Norwegian period is two or five years depending on how long the product is meant to last. A Nordic retailer that releases the generic answer “you have three years” would therefore give a valid Swedish rule the wrong geographic reach. The Norwegian market owner should be able to block that answer until the market condition and approved local source travel with it.

A compact catalog record could make the boundary visible:

| Field | Illustrative value |
| --- | --- |
| Claim | A consumer may complain about a faulty product within three years |
| Market | Sweden only |
| Approved source | Current Swedish consumer policy and internal legal approval |
| Business owner | Nordic customer-policy director |
| Review | Recheck after a legal or policy change |
| Use | May answer an information request; refunds require separate approval |

The final row matters. An agent may retrieve the policy because access to that fact has been approved. It may not use personal data, promise a refund or amend an order unless the company grants separate and reversible permission for that action.

Risher calls content “training data for the future internet.” I think that metaphor captures the stakes, although it is not a complete technical description. Many AI systems use **retrieval**, meaning they look up company information while composing an answer rather than permanently adding it to the underlying model. Either way, the business needs evidence of which source was used and a reliable route for replacing it in the systems it controls.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>A knowledge catalog is a company-owned operating model, not an official Google standard. It keeps an approved fact connected to its evidence, local conditions, accountable owner and action limits so a correct statement does not become a false promise when reused.</p></div>

## Structured content provides the foundation

**Structured content** stores information in named, reusable parts instead of leaving it only inside the layout of a finished page. A return policy becomes connected information about the market, eligible product, time limit, approving source and review date. Because those parts retain their meaning, one approved record can serve a website, a service tool or an agent without being rewritten for each interface.

Structured content is related to **structured data**, but the terms describe different layers. In Google Search, structured data is standardized code added to a page so a machine can identify what the page contains. Structured content is the underlying model of the business information itself. [Google Search Central, Google’s official guidance service for website owners, said in May 2025](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/05/succeeding-in-ai-search/) that structured data helps share information in a machine-readable form its systems can consider. The same guidance emphasized original, useful content because structure cannot make a generic claim distinctive or an expired policy true.

A modern **headless content management system**, or headless CMS, separates reusable content from the design of one website and supplies it to different channels through software connections. If products, policies, markets and approvals are already modeled this way, the company has useful raw material for a catalog. If the CMS mainly holds page-shaped copy with unclear ownership, the architecture label offers little advantage.

In a company with 5,000 employees, the catalog should not replace every existing source. Core customer and supplier identities may remain in a master-data system, which keeps shared business entities consistent. Product attributes may remain in a product-information system, while live inventory and prices stay in operational systems. The catalog connects approved views of that knowledge, records which system has authority and gives regional teams control over local conditions. This is central governance without pretending that one central team owns every fact.

That distinction also tells leaders when the current CMS is enough. Extend it when it already supports reusable fields, market scope, approval history, export and delivery through **application programming interfaces**, or APIs, which are software connections between systems. Add catalog capacity around it when important facts live in several authoritative systems, change in real time or require different access rights for each agent. Content structure is only one layer of an agent-ready system; APIs, identity controls, access policies and answer monitoring are also necessary.

Two vendor roadmaps show that agent access is becoming part of the structured-content category. [Sanity, a structured-content platform, announced AI tools for structured-content workflows in May 2025](https://www.sanity.io/blog/agent-actions-ai-building-blocks-for-structured-content). [Contentful, another content platform, made a secured agent connection generally available in July 2026](https://www.contentful.com/blog/remote-mcp-now-generally-available). These announcements show what two suppliers are building, not market-wide adoption or proof of safety. My take is that a headless CMS creates a head start only when the organization has modeled meaning, assigned responsibility and tested access.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Structured content preserves the business meaning of information beyond one page, and a mature headless CMS may already provide models, approvals and delivery connections. Large organizations still need to join authoritative systems, regional ownership, API access and security rules rather than force all knowledge into one platform.</p></div>

## Generated interfaces make meaning more valuable

The connection to *[The Digital Singularity Shift: The Rise of Metamodern Narratives in Business](/books/the-digital-singularity-shift/)*, my 2024 book about how AI makes digital functions easier to reproduce and shifts attention toward the meaning beneath them, is becoming concrete. A **generative user interface**, often shortened to GenUI, is a screen assembled by AI for the request at hand rather than selected from one fixed path designed in advance.

In November 2025, [Gemini 3, an AI model from Google, introduced experiments that generated a custom interface from a user’s prompt](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/gemini/gemini-3-gemini-app/). In April 2026, Google released version 0.9 of [A2UI, an open specification for agent-driven interfaces](https://developers.googleblog.com/a2ui-v0-9-generative-ui/). It lets an agent describe the interface elements needed for a task while the receiving application controls how they look and behave.

Think of the holodeck in *Star Trek: The Next Generation*, the science-fiction television series that began in 1987. Its fictional room renders a responsive environment around the person inside it. GenUI is a modest business version of that idea: one customer may receive a comparison while another receives a form, each assembled for the immediate request. A fixed page gives way to many possible presentations, which makes the rules beneath them more important.

I think this is the prediction that now matters from *The Digital Singularity Shift*. As interfaces become easier to generate, the scarce capability moves toward trusted company meaning and the authority to use it. Design still matters for recognition, trust and accessibility, but the company will not design every place where its price or policy appears. The strategic job is to make sure the conditions survive the journey.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>GenUI makes the book’s argument tangible: AI can assemble different screens for different requests, while the company still needs one dependable meaning underneath them. As presentation becomes fluid, verified conditions and authority become more valuable.</p></div>

## Test ten claims before buying a platform

Do not begin with another technology purchase. I would start with a 30-day exercise around ten high-volume or high-risk claims, such as delivery promises, return rights or product eligibility. A credible minimum is one working lead with roughly two days a week protected for the exercise, four or five specialists contributing a few hours each week, and an executive sponsor joining a short weekly decision meeting. That is a proposed starting capacity, not an industry benchmark.

Responsibility should be explicit from the beginning:

| Role | Work and authority |
| --- | --- |
| Business claim owner | Approves what is true and where it applies; accountable for the claim |
| Information maintainer | The person who keeps the record, source and review date current; runs the tests |
| Local market owner | Confirms local scope and can block release when the market rule is unclear |
| Legal or compliance | Advises on law, evidence and sensitive commitments; can require a claim to remain unavailable |
| Security and technology | Controls system access and tests that reading a fact cannot become an unauthorized action |
| Executive sponsor | Resolves ownership conflicts and makes the final scale, pause or stop decision |

First record the baseline: how long it takes to find the authority behind each claim, how many versions conflict and how many lack an owner. Then create the ten records, connect them to one controlled assistant and run a correction test in a non-production environment. For the pilot, I think a useful service target is to remove an expired answer from the company website and controlled assistant within four business hours of the approved change. Measure the time from a changed business promise to a corrected answer in every controlled channel.

That target must have an honest boundary. Your company can test its own website, assistant, service tool and caches, meaning stored copies used to speed up a response. It cannot guarantee immediate removal from a public Google summary, a customer’s saved chat or another external AI system that has already copied the answer. The catalog is strategically useful when it speeds correction in controlled systems and makes the limits elsewhere visible. If correction becomes faster internally while customer-facing answers remain persistently inconsistent, the catalog is still better content hygiene, not yet a strategic asset.

The failure tests should be as serious as the happy path. Ask the assistant for an expired claim and confirm that it refuses or retrieves the replacement. Give it a Norwegian customer with a Swedish market condition and check that it does not mix the policies. Then place a malicious instruction in an outside source and verify that this **prompt injection**, an attempt to manipulate the agent through untrusted text, cannot increase its access or move it from reading a policy to issuing a refund. These tests expose stale information, wrong-market matching and unauthorized escalation before customers do.

Visibility belongs in the exercise, but it should not become another decorative dashboard. **Mention rate** is how often a fixed set of AI answers names the company. **AI share of voice** compares those mentions with a defined set of competitors. If the company is absent from accurate answers about an important product category, leaders may prioritize a missing evidence set. If it is frequently mentioned with an expired policy, the priority is correction, not more visibility. Attention without accuracy creates a larger liability.

At day 30, the team should deliver a claim register with owners and sources, a correction-and-withdrawal test record, and a recommendation to scale, pause or retire the exercise. Scale when owner coverage is complete, the four-hour target holds in controlled systems and permission tests pass. Pause when sources or regional authority remain disputed. Retire or redesign the approach when stale claims survive repeatedly or an agent crosses from reading to acting without approval. I think these conditions turn an AI initiative into a reversible business decision.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>A useful first move is a staffed 30-day test with ten claims, named decision rights, a four-hour correction target for controlled systems and deliberate failure cases. The result should be a claim register, a test record and an explicit decision to scale, pause or stop.</p></div>

## Choose a partner after you define control

Only after the operating model is clear should the supplier question enter. The consultancy that modeled and connected your current content may be a sensible candidate because it already understands the information structure. [Valtech, a global digital consultancy](https://www.valtech.com/), and implementation firms in the partner networks of Contentful, Sanity or [Optimizely, a digital-experience platform](https://www.optimizely.com/), are examples to evaluate, not endorsements.

Platform knowledge has real value, and a consultancy badge does not guarantee independence. Ask whether the firm is paid to solve your information problem or also rewarded when you buy more licenses. Ask it to prove when the existing CMS can be extended and when a separate connection, policy or monitoring layer is necessary. A useful partner should be able to recommend less platform when less platform is the right answer.

Put control into the contract. Your company should own the information model, export its content in a usable form, decide where data is stored and processed, limit access for each person and agent, and receive a tested handover if the relationship ends. Those terms matter more than a vendor logo because the catalog will outlive individual interfaces.

Start with the promises that can hurt the business when they are wrong. Before more systems speak and act for your company, make sure the conditions can travel farther than the website.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The consultancy that built a modern content platform may be well placed to extend it, provided its incentives and contract preserve your ownership, export rights and access control. Before agents speak for the business, the truth behind each important promise must be able to leave the page without leaving its conditions behind.</p></div>
