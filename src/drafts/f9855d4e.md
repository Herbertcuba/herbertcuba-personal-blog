Your next customer may make a decision about your company before your website loads once. They can ask an AI assistant whether you serve their area, compare your terms with a competitor and request the next available appointment. The answer may be assembled in somebody else's interface, and an AI agent may carry the request further.

That does not make the website useless. People will still visit it for reassurance, detail and direct service. Its old role as the center of the digital estate is weakening, however, because discovery and action no longer require a visit. I think the leadership question has changed with it: if the page is absent from the journey, where does an AI system get the company information needed to represent you accurately?

The answer is a knowledge catalog: verified, structured company knowledge prepared for use by software as well as people. Building one is becoming more important than another round of visual redesign.

<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>The website remains useful, but it can no longer carry the whole customer journey. Companies need a reliable knowledge source beneath every site, assistant and agent that may speak or act on their behalf.</p>

## Discovery is separating from traffic

The break started before the current wave of AI assistants. A [July 2024 analysis by SparkToro, an audience-research company, and Datos, a browsing-data provider](https://sparktoro.com/blog/2024-zero-click-search-study/) found that 58.5% of US Google searches and 59.7% of EU searches ended without a click. Those figures include people who stopped searching or changed their query, so they do not show that AI caused the behavior. They show something more basic: Google could satisfy or end a customer journey without distributing traffic.

AI summaries intensify that separation. The [Pew Research Center, a nonpartisan US research organization, examined browsing by 900 American adults in March 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/) and found that people clicked a conventional search result in 8% of visits with an AI summary, compared with 15% when no summary appeared. Sources cited inside the summary received clicks in only 1% of those visits. The customer still asks a question, but the answer increasingly arrives above the website.

Now the answer can lead to action. An AI agent is software that pursues a goal through several steps, using approved tools and systems along the way. At Google Cloud Next '25, Google's annual business-technology conference, the company introduced [Agent2Agent, or A2A](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/a2a-a-new-era-of-agent-interoperability/), an open protocol that lets independently built agents exchange information and coordinate work. Google launched it with support from more than 50 technology partners. By July 2026, Google was rolling out Gemini Spark, a personal AI agent designed to browse, research and help complete bookings.

I don't know how quickly people will delegate major purchases. Trust, payment disputes and liability remain real barriers. Yet the strategic direction is visible already: search is becoming conversational, and conversations can now hand work to software.

<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Zero-click search separated discovery from website traffic, AI summaries widened the gap, and agent protocols now connect answers to actions. The adoption pace is uncertain, but a company can no longer assume that customer intent will arrive as a page view.</p>

## A knowledge catalog makes the business understandable to machines

Phil Risher, founder of the service-business marketing consultancy Phlash Consulting, used "knowledge catalog" in a [June 2026 video](https://youtu.be/xAIeA3ewRbo) for a verified data set about a business that can be made available through an agent. The phrase is useful, with one important correction: the available Google material does not establish it as an official Google product or standard. I think it is better treated as a practical business model.

A knowledge catalog contains the information required to answer and support real customer requests: services, eligibility rules, prices, credentials, policies and the questions customers actually ask. Each item carries enough context for a machine to use it correctly. Consider same-day installation. The useful record includes eligible products, covered locations, the booking cutoff, current charges and the internal source responsible for each value. A sentence hidden in an old campaign page cannot provide those boundaries reliably.

Verification alone is also incomplete. A catalog may confirm that same-day installation exists while withholding authority to promise a slot. Availability must come from the booking system, and the agent needs explicit permission before it commits the company. This distinction between knowledge and authority is where a convenient AI experience becomes a governable one.

Risher describes content as "training data for the future internet." I think that works as a strategic warning, provided we use *training* loosely. Some AI systems will crawl published material; others will retrieve an approved record at the moment of a question or connect through controlled software. In every case, vague, duplicated or stale content gives the system poor material from which to form an answer.

<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>A knowledge catalog is a maintained body of company information with the context machines need to apply it correctly. It must keep factual accuracy separate from permission to book, buy or make a commitment.</p>
</div>

## Structured content creates a head start

Structured content stores meaning in reusable fields, such as service name, location, price condition and effective date. Structured data adds machine-readable labels that help external systems interpret published material. The distinction matters because an AI system needs the fact and its business context, not the page layout wrapped around them. [Google Search Central, Google's guidance service for site owners, said in May 2025](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/05/succeeding-in-ai-search) that structured data helps share information in machine-readable form, while also stressing the value of original and useful content.

A modern headless content management system separates content from the screen on which it appears and delivers it through software connections. Companies using one may already have content models, approval workflows and reusable records. That is a meaningful starting advantage, although it is no guarantee. A headless system filled with generic copy and unclear ownership simply makes weak material easier to distribute.

The platform market shows where this is going. Sanity and Contentful, two structured-content platforms, released agent-facing capabilities during 2025 and 2026. Optimizely, a digital-experience platform, has also added tools for visibility in AI answers. These vendors have an interest in promoting the category, so their announcements do not prove widespread adoption. They do show that the content stack is being adapted for machine consumers.

The interface is changing at the same time. A generative user interface is assembled by AI for the request at hand, rather than designed in advance as one fixed screen. Google introduced real-time generated interfaces with its Gemini 3 AI model in November 2025 and published A2UI, an open project for agent-driven interfaces, the following month; [version 0.9 followed in April 2026](https://developers.googleblog.com/a2ui-v0-9-generative-ui/). When the visible experience can be created on demand, the dependable asset is the structured knowledge feeding it.

This is where the current shift meets *The Digital Singularity*, my 2024 book. Its forecast that AI mediation and generated experiences would reduce the power of fixed frontends is becoming concrete. My take is that the transition is arriving through ordinary infrastructure: search summaries, content models, software protocols and approval rules. The future rarely announces itself as one dramatic product launch.

<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Structured content gives facts reusable meaning, and a headless CMS may already provide the models and workflows a knowledge catalog needs. As interfaces become generated for each request, that underlying knowledge retains value even when the screen changes.</p>

## Start with one customer decision

A knowledge catalog should begin as a focused operating change, not an enterprise-wide content migration. Choose one valuable customer decision, such as checking service eligibility, and collect the approved information needed to support it. Give every item a responsible business owner, define when it expires and connect it to the system that can confirm live conditions. Keep the first agent read-only until the company has tested where it answers well, where it must ask for help and where it must refuse.

The consultancy that built your content platform may be well placed to do this work because it already understands the content model and integration landscape. Valtech, a global digital consultancy, and qualified firms in the Contentful, Sanity or Optimizely partner networks are examples of the kind of provider to assess. A partner badge proves product familiarity, though it says little about commercial independence. Ask each firm to disclose license incentives and show how its design would preserve your ability to change platforms later.

Measurement must follow the new journey. Mention rate is the share of tracked AI answers in which your company appears. AI share of voice compares that presence with selected competitors across a defined set of customer questions. These are emerging measures sold by vendors, so they need stable prompts and careful interpretation. They belong beside rankings, conversion and customer outcomes. A 2025 [study by Semrush, a search-marketing software company, of 200,000 AI Overviews](https://www.semrush.com/blog/ai-overviews-study/) found that a top-ten organic position did not guarantee inclusion in the AI answer, which is why rank alone no longer describes visibility.

I think leaders should start now, but start narrowly. The first goal is to prove that one important decision can draw from current, owned and correctly bounded company knowledge. Once that works, the catalog can expand across products, markets and agents without surrendering control. In an internet shaped by generated answers, the company that can supply dependable knowledge becomes easier for machines to choose.

<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Begin with one customer decision, a read-only use case and named business owners. Use an independent consultancy that understands the existing content platform, disclose its commercial incentives, and measure whether AI systems represent the company accurately before expanding the catalog.</p>
