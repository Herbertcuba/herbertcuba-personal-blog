Your next customer may compare your offer, rule it out or begin a purchase without ever seeing your homepage. Search engines can answer inside their results. AI agents, software that can plan and perform parts of a task for users, can research options or begin tasks for them. A new layer is forming between companies and customers.

This does not make the website useless. It changes what should sit at the center of digital investment. I think the more durable asset is a knowledge catalog: a governed publishing layer of verified company facts that websites, search systems, assistants and agents can all use.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The website remains useful, but customer access is moving into answers and agents. Companies need verified knowledge that can travel beyond any single interface.</p></div>

## Customers can act before they arrive

A [2024 analysis by SparkToro and Datos](https://sparktoro.com/blog/2024-zero-click-search-study/), respectively a marketing research company and a clickstream-data provider, estimated that 58.5% of US searches and 59.7% of EU searches made through Google Search ended without a click. The figures include abandoned and reformulated searches, so they do not prove that AI caused the behavior. They show that the route from search to website was already incomplete.

The AI-specific evidence is clearer. [Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/), a nonpartisan US research organization, examined the March 2025 browsing activity of 900 adults. People clicked a traditional result in 8% of visits with a Google AI summary, compared with 15% without one. Sources cited inside the summary received clicks in only 1% of visits containing one.

Agents extend that change from answers to action. At Google Cloud Next, the company’s annual enterprise technology conference, Google [launched the open Agent2Agent protocol](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/a2a-a-new-era-of-agent-interoperability/) in April 2025 with more than 50 technology partners. Usually shortened to A2A, it lets participating agents exchange information and coordinate work. In July 2026, Google expanded [Gemini Spark](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-app/gemini-spark-updates-july-2026/), a personal agent that can research flight options and begin a browser booking with permission, while returning payment to the person.

Trust, liability and customer habits will slow adoption. Even so, a company can increasingly be evaluated before its own interface appears.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>AI summaries reduce visits, and agents can begin a task before a customer reaches the site. Company information therefore has to work outside the company’s own interface.</p></div>

## The catalog should not become another database

Phil Risher, owner of the digital marketing firm Phlash Consulting, used “knowledge catalog” in a [June 2026 video](https://youtu.be/xAIeA3ewRbo) for verified business information made available to an agent. It is not an established Google product or shared technical standard. I use it for a governed publishing layer that draws approved facts from the systems that already own them.

Product, price, customer and policy systems should remain authoritative in their respective domains. The catalog makes selected facts reusable and adds source, market, language, version, validity and approval status. When sources disagree, named owners and conflict rules determine what may be published.

Structured content stores information in defined fields and relationships instead of trapping it inside page copy. A [headless content management system](/posts/the-quiet-truth-about-headless-cms/) separates content from its visual presentation and supplies it through application programming interfaces, or APIs, which are defined connections between software systems. Existing models, workflows and APIs give headless-CMS users a head start. Google’s [May 2025 guidance for site owners](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/05/succeeding-in-ai-search) confirms that structured data helps systems read content, but structure does not make a fact true, and a headless CMS is not required.

Controls should follow the risk. Public facts can be distributed broadly. Personal data requires protected access under the European Union’s General Data Protection Regulation, its privacy law, while delegated decisions may also bring obligations under the EU AI Act, its risk-based AI regulation. Authenticated APIs and rules enforced by company systems should control access. Consequential actions need logs, approval and a named person authorized to pause or override the system. These controls govern company systems, not external agents that may cache or paraphrase published facts.

A supplier cannot create accountability through a license. External partners should be judged on headless-CMS implementation capability, knowledge governance and commercial independence. Ask whether referral revenue or resale arrangements reward a platform recommendation, then require export rights, clear data location, access controls and a tested handover.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>A knowledge catalog is a governed layer above existing authoritative systems, not a replacement for them. Structured content helps, while ownership, access controls and supplier independence determine whether it can be trusted.</p></div>

## The next interface is made for the moment

Generative UI means software creates an interface for a particular request instead of sending every customer through the same prebuilt pages. Google demonstrated this direction with Gemini 3, its AI model released in November 2025, and A2UI, an open project announced in December 2025 and updated in April 2026 so agents can send interface descriptions to compatible applications. The technology is early, but presentation can already be assembled from knowledge and rules.

This is [The Digital Singularity](/posts/mckinsey-digital-singularity/) in practical form. The term describes a market in which digital experiences become adaptive outputs rather than fixed destinations. Human design still matters for trust, accessibility and brand, but any one screen becomes less durable when software can create another for each request.

The investment case needs restraint. The evidence supports a shift in discovery and interfaces; it does not prove enterprise-scale returns from a knowledge catalog. AI share of voice, how often a company appears across AI answers, and mention rate, how often it is named, are useful signals. Business value follows only when accurate answers lead to completed tasks, stronger conversion or lower service cost. Until then, the catalog is a testable investment thesis rather than a guaranteed payoff.

Websites will continue to serve people who want to inspect a company directly. Yet as generative UI makes the screen more changeable, the information underneath carries more long-term value. The knowledge catalog is the asset worth moving toward now; the fixed interface is one expression of it.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Generative UI makes interfaces easier to create and replace, while verified knowledge must remain accurate across all of them. That makes the knowledge catalog the stronger near-term investment when its value is tied to customer and operating outcomes.</p></div>
