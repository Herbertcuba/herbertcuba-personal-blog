Your website can be well designed, technically sound and full of useful content, yet still lose strategic importance. The reason sits outside the site: more customers receive an answer before they reach it.

The [Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/), an independent US research organization, tracked the browsing of 900 adults in March 2025. When Google, the dominant search platform, displayed an AI summary, people clicked a traditional result on 8% of visits, compared with 15% when no summary appeared. They clicked a source cited inside the summary only 1% of the time.

This extends an older trend rather than proving that AI caused it. A [2024 study](https://sparktoro.com/blog/2024-zero-click-search-study/) by audience-research firm SparkToro and clickstream provider Datos found that 58.5% of US Google searches and 59.7% of EU searches ended without a click. Websites will remain useful for trust, service and transactions, but they can no longer be treated as the only route between demand and the business.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>A strong website can still lose reach because search platforms increasingly satisfy the customer's need before a visit occurs.</p></div>

## From answers to actions

The change begins with conversation. In Pew's sample, AI summaries appeared on about 60% of searches phrased as questions, compared with 18% of searches overall. A customer can describe an outcome in ordinary language and let the search system assemble an answer, rather than translate that need into keywords and inspect several pages.

The next step is an AI agent: software that can pursue a goal and use other systems with permission, instead of only producing text. At Google Cloud Next, Google's annual cloud conference, in April 2025, the company launched the open [Agent2Agent protocol](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/a2a-a-new-era-of-agent-interoperability/), usually shortened to A2A. It gives agents a common way to exchange information and coordinate actions across business applications.

Trust, fraud, liability and payment rules will slow fully autonomous commerce, so the timetable remains uncertain. The market signal is still clear: discovery is gaining an intermediary that can compare options and act before a customer opens a company's interface.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Search is moving from finding pages to resolving intent, while A2A provides infrastructure for agents to continue from an answer into an action.</p></div>

## The knowledge layer becomes the asset

Phil Risher, founder of the US marketing firm Phlash Consulting, uses a useful term for what that intermediary needs: a **knowledge catalog**. It is a verified, machine-readable body of business knowledge, including offerings, pricing logic, locations, policies, credentials and approved answers to real customer questions. Unlike a folder of documents, it records which claim is current, who owns it and where it may be used.

The foundation is **structured content**: information stored in labeled, reusable fields instead of trapped inside the design of a page. An agent can reliably distinguish a service area from a price or an eligibility rule because each item has an explicit meaning. The same approved information can then support a website, a search answer or an internal assistant without being rewritten for every channel.

In May 2025, [Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/05/succeeding-in-ai-search), Google's guidance service for site owners, advised businesses to combine machine-readable structured data with unique, non-commodity content. This is why a modern **headless content management system**, which stores content separately from page design and supplies it to different interfaces, offers a real head start. It already contains models and distribution paths, although stale or generic content remains stale or generic regardless of the technology beneath it.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>A knowledge catalog turns proprietary business knowledge into governed, structured information that both human interfaces and AI systems can use reliably.</p></div>

The relevant mapping is the intermediary. When a trusted agent becomes the customer's main interface, a business competes through the knowledge and actions that agent can access, not only through the destination a person might visit.

## Invest beneath the interface

This shifts the investment case. Rankings still matter, but leaders also need **mention rate**, how often relevant AI answers mention the business, and **AI share of voice**, its presence relative to competitors across those answers. These measures ask whether the company is represented accurately where decisions are increasingly formed, rather than only where its page ranks.

The implementation partner should be selected without turning the catalog into another form of lock-in. Require proven headless-CMS implementation capability, knowledge-governance expertise, clear export rights, known data location, access controls, fees independent of platform incentives and a tested handover. The company should own the source data, content model, access policy and export path; a pilot should prove that those assets still work when the partner's private tools are removed.

The final market movement is **generative UI**, an interface assembled by AI for the user's immediate request. Google introduced real-time “generative interfaces” with its Gemini 3 AI model in November 2025, then advanced [A2UI](https://developers.googleblog.com/a2ui-v0-9-generative-ui/), an open format for agent-generated interfaces, in April 2026. The screen can now be composed after the question is asked.

This fits [The Digital Singularity](/posts/mckinsey-digital-singularity/), my term for the point at which digital experiences become fluid and increasingly assembled around intent. GenUI will attract attention because it is visible. The knowledge catalog deserves the investment now because every generated interface is only as useful, accurate and trustworthy as the knowledge underneath it.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>As interfaces become generated and temporary, investment should move toward owned, governed knowledge that can serve every interface and agent.</p></div>
