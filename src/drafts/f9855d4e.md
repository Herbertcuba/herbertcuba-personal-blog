Your next customer may compare your offer, understand your policies, and ask an AI system to act without ever entering a channel you own. The website still has a job, but it is losing its role as the place where discovery, explanation, and conversion naturally meet.

This is visible in user behavior. In March 2025, the [Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/), a nonpartisan US research organization, tracked the browsing of 900 adults. When Google displayed an AI summary, meaning a generated answer above the usual search results, users clicked a traditional result in 8% of visits. Without one, they clicked in 15%. Sources cited inside the summary received a click in just 1% of visits.

For business leaders, the consequence is larger than a traffic problem. When the intermediary can answer for your company, the quality and accessibility of the knowledge beneath that answer become strategic assets.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Customers increasingly reach answers before they reach websites, shifting value from the page they visit to the company knowledge an AI system can find and trust.</p>
</div>

## Discovery is moving beyond the click

Generative AI accelerated a change that was already underway. A [2024 study by SparkToro and Datos](https://sparktoro.com/blog/2024-zero-click-search-study/), an audience-research company and a browsing-data provider, found that 58.5% of US Google searches and 59.7% of EU searches ended without a click. These “zero-click” searches also included people who stopped or changed their query, so AI did not cause the entire figure. Pew provides the more specific signal: about 60% of question-shaped searches in its 2025 sample produced an AI summary.

The next change is from answers to action. An AI agent is software that can choose steps, use approved tools, and carry out a task for a person or company. At Google Cloud Next in April 2025, Google introduced [Agent2Agent, or A2A](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/a2a-a-new-era-of-agent-interoperability/), an open protocol that lets agents exchange information and coordinate work across different business systems. By July 2026, Google was also rolling out Gemini Spark, a consumer agent that can browse, research, and book on a user’s behalf.

Trust, fraud, liability, and payment rules will slow agent-led commerce. Even so, the market direction is clear: a growing share of the customer journey will be mediated by systems that need reliable facts rather than a polished navigation path.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Search is moving from links to answers and then toward delegated action. That makes reliable company data more important even while the timing of widespread agent commerce remains uncertain.</p>
</div>

## The asset beneath every answer

I use **knowledge catalog** as a practical name, not as an official Google product category. It is the verified, structured, machine-readable body of knowledge about a business: services, locations, pricing rules, policies, credentials, and real customer questions with approved answers. “Machine-readable” simply means the facts are stored so software can identify and reuse them without guessing what a webpage means.

The key is **structured content**. Instead of burying a delivery area or cancellation rule inside a page, the company stores it as a named fact with a clear relationship to the relevant product, market, and date. Google’s [Search Central guidance for site owners](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/05/succeeding-in-ai-search) said in May 2025 that structured data helps its systems understand content in a machine-readable form. The same guidance stresses unique, useful material, because perfect structure cannot rescue generic copy.

Measurement changes with the distribution model. Search ranking still matters, but leaders also need **mention rate**, how often an AI answer names the company for relevant questions, and **AI share of voice**, its portion of relevant answers compared with competitors. These measures are imperfect and still developing, yet they reveal visibility that a rankings report alone cannot show.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>A knowledge catalog turns proprietary company facts into governed, reusable content. Structure makes the facts accessible to machines; distinct and current knowledge gives those machines a reason to use them.</p>
</div>

## Put investment into the company-owned layer

Companies with a modern **headless content management system**, or headless CMS, have a meaningful head start. In this architecture, content is stored separately from the website’s visual presentation and supplied to different channels through software connections. The material may already be divided into reusable fields rather than trapped in pages. That advantage follows from the architecture; it is not a measured guarantee of success.

Software alone does not create trustworthy knowledge. A named business owner must control definitions, approval rules, update responsibility, and usage rights, while the company retains its content model and source records. This turns the investment from another publishing project into a governed business asset.

External support can be useful, especially from an independent implementation consultancy that already understands the content platform. Selection should cover headless-CMS implementation capability and knowledge governance, along with clear export rights, known data location, strong access controls, and a tested handover. Fees should also be independent of incentives to sell a particular platform. Deep product knowledge has value, but ownership must remain with the company.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Headless CMS architecture can shorten the path, but the real investment is portable, governed knowledge under company control, supported by partners whose incentives and handover terms can be examined.</p>
</div>

Today’s agents are far narrower than the film’s system, but they already point toward that change in market access.

## The interface can now be made on demand

The final signal is **generative UI**, short for generative user interface. Rather than sending every user through the same fixed page structure, an AI model assembles an interface for the request at hand. Google demonstrated this with Gemini 3 in November 2025 and followed with A2UI, its open project for agent-driven interfaces, which reached version 0.9 in April 2026.

This is the direction I call *The Digital Singularity*: AI absorbs more of the interface and coordination layer, while value moves toward the verified knowledge underneath. Websites remain useful for brand expression, complex decisions, and public accountability. They increasingly become one presentation of company knowledge rather than its master form.

That distinction should guide near-term investment. Generative UI will attract attention because it changes what customers see. The knowledge catalog matters more now because it determines what those interfaces and agents can say, and whether the business can stand behind the answer.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>As interfaces are generated for each request, fixed frontends become less durable. The strategic asset is the verified knowledge that lets every human-facing interface and machine agent represent the business accurately.</p>
</div>
