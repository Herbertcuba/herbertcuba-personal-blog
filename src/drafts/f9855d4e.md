Your website is on its way to becoming irrelevant as the center of your digital business.

That does not mean it will disappear. Websites will still carry brand, build trust and support experiences a company wants to control directly. What is fading is the assumption that customers must pass through the website to discover an offer, compare it and take action.

The evidence is already visible. In March 2025, [Pew Research Center, an independent US research organization, studied the browsing behavior of 900 American adults](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/). When Google displayed an AI summary, a generated answer above the traditional results, people clicked a result in 8% of visits, compared with 15% when no summary appeared. They clicked a source cited inside the summary in just 1% of visits.

I think business leaders should read this as an investment signal rather than a search-marketing emergency. When answers, actions and even screens can be produced outside your website, the durable asset is the knowledge beneath them: verified company information that software can understand and the organization is prepared to stand behind.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The website will remain a valuable channel, but it is losing its position as the required center of the customer journey. The strategic asset is shifting from control of the page to control of the knowledge used beyond it.</p></div>

## Search is becoming conversation and action

This shift began before AI answers became common. A [2024 study by marketing research firm SparkToro, using browsing data from analytics provider Datos](https://sparktoro.com/blog/2024-zero-click-search-study/), found that 58.5% of US Google searches and 59.7% of EU searches ended without a click. Those totals include people who stopped searching as well as those who rewrote their query, so they do not show that AI caused zero-click behavior. They show that visiting a website was already optional.

AI summaries push the market further in the same direction. Pew found that about 60% of Google searches beginning with a question word produced an AI summary. Search is moving from matching short keyword phrases with pages toward interpreting a question and assembling an answer. The customer can now form an opinion before seeing the company that supplied the information.

The next change is from answering to acting. An AI agent is software given a goal and permission to complete several steps on a person's behalf. In July 2026, Google began rolling out Gemini Spark, a consumer agent that can browse to research options and make bookings for its user. At Google Cloud Next '25, Google's annual enterprise technology conference, the company had already introduced [Agent2Agent, or A2A](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/a2a-a-new-era-of-agent-interoperability/), a common set of technical rules that lets agents exchange information and coordinate work across different business systems.

A2A makes communication possible, but it does not make an action truthful or authorized. Companies still need separate controls for identity, permission, records of what happened and the ability to reverse an action. I don't know whether delegated buying becomes ordinary in two years or six because trust, payments, liability and customer habits remain genuine constraints. The direction still matters: apps and social networks created more destinations, while agents can become intermediaries across all of them.

That changes what visibility means. Search rankings remain useful, but leaders also need mention rate, how often an AI answer names the company across a defined set of questions, and AI share of voice, how that presence compares with selected competitors. In 2025, [search software company Semrush studied 200,000 Google AI summaries](https://www.semrush.com/blog/ai-overviews-study/) and found that a top-ten position in traditional results did not guarantee a place in the AI answer. Rankings alone no longer show whether the business appears in the answer the customer receives.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Search can now answer before a visit, and authorized agents can carry the journey into action. This weakens the website's role as gatekeeper and makes presence inside AI answers a business measure alongside traditional rankings.</p></div>

## A knowledge catalog closes the verification gap

In a June 2026 video, [Phil Risher, a marketing consultant at Phlash Consulting](https://youtu.be/xAIeA3ewRbo), described a knowledge catalog as a verified set of information about a business that can be deployed through an agent. I think the term is useful, with one important qualification: the available Google documentation does not establish a Google product or standard by that name. It is better understood as a strategic model that a company owns.

A knowledge catalog is the structured, machine-readable body of information the business wants other systems to use. It can cover services, product facts, pricing rules, policies, locations, credentials and real customer questions. Machine-readable simply means each item has a clear label and format, so software does not have to infer its meaning from the visual layout of a page.

Verification makes the catalog more than a cleaner content library. Each important statement should remain connected to its source, a responsible owner, a validity date, rules about who may receive it and a status showing whether it has been withdrawn. Imagine a product record that says a component can operate at a certain temperature. The claim points to an approved test report, belongs to an engineering owner and expires when that evidence requires review. Once it expires, an agent can block the claim instead of turning an old web sentence into a new commercial promise.

This is the verification gap businesses now face. An external AI system can repeat a company claim at great speed, while the company may have no reliable way to prove, update or withdraw it. A chatbot only presents an answer; the knowledge catalog defines which answer the organization is willing to support.

Risher calls content “training data for the future internet.” My take is that the strategic point is sound, although the immediate mechanism is often retrieval: the AI system looks up approved information while composing an answer rather than retraining its underlying model. A company does not need to train the world's AI to benefit. It needs to make its distinctive knowledge clear enough to find and reliable enough to use.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>A knowledge catalog is a company-owned source of verified claims, not another chatbot. It lets websites, agents and future interfaces reuse the same information while preserving evidence, ownership, validity and permission.</p></div>

## Structured content creates the head start

Structured content stores information in named fields instead of burying it inside a finished page. A service area, policy date or product limit can then travel to a website, an app or an agent without losing its meaning. In May 2025, [Google Search Central, Google's guidance service for website owners](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/05/succeeding-in-ai-search/), said structured data helps businesses share machine-readable information that its systems can consider. The same guidance stressed that the content itself must be original and useful. Good structure can expose knowledge, but it cannot make a generic statement valuable or an outdated one true.

This is why organizations with a modern headless content management system, or headless CMS, have a practical lead. A headless CMS keeps reusable content separate from the design of a particular website and delivers it to different channels through software connections. If a company already has clear content fields, approval flows and accountable owners, much of the raw material for a knowledge catalog exists. If the system contains only long blocks of page copy, the technical label offers little advantage.

The providers' roadmaps indicate where their products are moving. During 2025 and 2026, Contentful, Sanity and Optimizely, three providers of enterprise content technology, released capabilities for agent access or visibility in AI answers. Their announcements are vendor evidence, not neutral proof that the whole market has changed. Even so, they show that content platforms are preparing to supply AI systems, not merely publish pages.

I think the consultancy that built a company's content platform may be well placed to extend it, because it should already understand the content model, approval process and surrounding systems. [Valtech, a global digital consultancy](https://www.valtech.com/), and independent partners working with Contentful, Sanity or Optimizely are examples of the type of firm to consider, not an automatic shortlist. The important distinction is incentive. A consultancy should help the company own and export its knowledge, preserve the option to change platforms and test that withdrawn claims actually disappear. A supplier financially tied to one platform has a reason to make the license central to the answer.

Near-term investment should therefore favor the company-owned content model, links from claims back to evidence, permissions for different users and systems, and reliable delivery beyond one interface. Another website redesign may improve today's channel. It does much less to prepare the business for a market in which the interface itself can change from one request to the next.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Structured content is the bridge between company knowledge and AI use. A well-run headless CMS provides a real head start, but the lasting advantage comes from owning the model, evidence and permissions rather than depending on one page or platform.</p></div>

## Invest in the knowledge catalog now

*[The Digital Singularity Shift](/books/the-digital-singularity-shift/)*, my 2024 book about business in an age of abundant AI creation, argues that interfaces and digital features become cheaper to produce as AI improves, while the meaning and trusted knowledge beneath them carry more of the value. The market is now revealing the mechanism. AI answers loosen discovery from a website, agents loosen action from it, and generative interfaces loosen the experience from a fixed screen.

A generative user interface, or generative UI, is a screen assembled for a particular request rather than designed in advance for every visitor. In November 2025, [Google's Gemini 3 release introduced interfaces generated when the user submitted a prompt](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/gemini/gemini-3-gemini-app/). In April 2026, Google published [A2UI v0.9](https://developers.googleblog.com/a2ui-v0-9-generative-ui/), a set of rules that lets an agent request suitable buttons, forms or tables while the receiving application retains control of its approved design components.

The closest science-fiction image is the holodeck in *Star Trek: First Contact*, a 1996 film in which a computer creates a setting on demand. The scenery can change for each mission because the stable intelligence sits beneath it. Generative UI follows the same logic: the screen becomes temporary, while knowledge, identity and permission determine what the experience can say and do.

Design will still matter. So will accessibility, brand and the direct relationship a company creates on its own website. But I think the next unit of durable digital value sits below that surface. A verified product rule can support today's page, tomorrow's agent and a screen nobody has designed yet; a fixed page cannot travel in the same way.

My take is that leaders should begin funding the knowledge catalog now, without waiting for every agent standard or buying habit to settle. The winning investment is not a speculative replacement for the website. It is the verified, structured company knowledge that makes every current and future interface more useful. Start building that asset now, because in a market of generated interfaces, the company that can supply trusted knowledge can shape the experience even when it no longer owns the screen.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Generative UI makes the screen a temporary expression of deeper company knowledge, which is the shift anticipated in <em>The Digital Singularity Shift</em>. Invest in the knowledge catalog now so the business can remain useful and trusted across interfaces it does not control.</p></div>
