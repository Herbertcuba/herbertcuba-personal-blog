Your website is becoming irrelevant as the compulsory front door to your business.

A customer can already ask a question and receive an answer inside Google’s search engine without visiting you. The next step is an AI agent, software that can pursue a goal and take permitted steps for a person, asking other systems about availability, terms or service. The customer may reach your website later, but the first comparison and part of the decision can happen elsewhere.

This does not make the website worthless. It changes the assumption that every meaningful digital journey must pass through it. When an outside system gives the wrong price, warranty or delivery promise, the urgent question is no longer which page needs an edit. It is whether anyone can trace the claim to an approved source, correct it and withdraw the old version.

I think that correction problem points to the next important digital asset. A page is one presentation of company information. Underneath it, leaders need a governed body of knowledge that a website, an employee or an AI system can use without inventing missing context. I will call that asset a knowledge catalog.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The website is losing its position as the required entrance to a company. Leaders now need to govern the facts beneath every interface so those facts remain traceable and correct wherever a customer receives an answer.</p></div>

## Search is turning into answers and actions

The compulsory click was already weakening before the present wave of AI products. In July 2024, [SparkToro, an audience-research company, and Datos, a browsing-data provider](https://sparktoro.com/blog/2024-zero-click-search-study/), estimated that 58.5% of US Google searches and 59.7% of EU searches ended without a click. Their browsing-panel figures included people who ended the session or changed their query, so they do not prove that AI caused every zero-click search or that every searcher found a satisfactory answer.

AI summaries provide more direct evidence of the new behavior. [Pew Research Center, a nonpartisan US research organization, studied the Google browsing activity of 900 American adults in March 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/). People clicked a traditional result in 8% of visits when Google showed an AI summary, compared with 15% when it did not. A source cited inside the summary received a click in only 1% of those visits.

Keywords have not disappeared, but the form of discovery is changing. Pew found that about 60% of searches beginning with question words produced an AI summary. A person who asks a complete question is more likely to encounter a composed answer, which means a company can be represented before its carefully designed page appears.

Answers are now being joined by actions. At Google Cloud Next ’25, Google’s annual enterprise technology conference, the company launched [Agent2Agent, or A2A](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/a2a-a-new-era-of-agent-interoperability/), with support from more than 50 technology partners. A2A is an open set of rules that allows independently built AI agents to exchange information and coordinate work across business systems. A customer’s buying agent could ask a supplier’s agent whether a product is available, then prepare an order for human approval.

A2A does not decide whether the product claim is true, nor does it transfer authority or responsibility from the companies involved. Each organization still has to control what an agent may see, what it may do and when a person must approve the next step. I don’t know which protocol or customer habit will dominate, especially for expensive or high-risk decisions. The evidence is already strong enough, however, to stop treating the designed web journey as the only place where company information will be interpreted.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>SparkToro and Datos measured a majority of Google searches ending without a click, Pew observed fewer clicks when AI summaries appeared, and A2A lets software agents coordinate work. Discovery now includes answers and permitted actions that can happen before a website visit.</p></div>

## A knowledge catalog gives every claim an owner

In a June 2026 video, [Phil Risher of Phlash Consulting, a marketing consultancy for service businesses](https://youtu.be/xAIeA3ewRbo), describes a knowledge catalog as verified information about a company that can be made available through an agent. The phrase is useful, but it is not an official Google product or standard in Google’s A2A announcement or its May 2025 guidance for AI search. I use it here as an operating model for company knowledge.

A knowledge catalog is a company-owned body of approved facts arranged so people and software can use them without guessing their meaning, scope or authority. It can contain product details, service areas, pricing rules, policies, credentials and answers to real customer questions. Each important claim carries its approved wording, source policy, responsible business owner, applicable product and market, review date, current status and withdrawal path.

Consider the promise “delivery within 24 hours.” Versions of it may sit in campaign copy, help articles, sales documents and country pages. A catalog record would connect the promise to the eligible products and markets, the policy that authorizes it, the owner who reviews it and whether it remains active. If the customer’s country is unknown, the system should ask for it rather than apply a US promise to a customer in Sweden.

That refusal matters. A repository can store thousands of documents and still leave an AI system to guess which sentence has authority. A catalog must make the approved version traceable, carry its conditions with it and allow the company to withdraw it. When sources conflict or a required condition is missing, “I cannot answer yet” can be more valuable than a fluent invention.

Risher calls content “training data for the future internet.” I think the metaphor captures the stakes, although it is not a complete technical explanation. AI systems may instead use retrieval, which means looking up company information while composing an answer rather than permanently adding it to the underlying AI model. In both cases, weak source material produces weak representation.

The executive test is concrete: how long does it take to find the authority behind a commercial claim, identify where it applies and remove it from every channel the company controls? That result can be measured. As AI-mediated journeys grow, companies with unowned claims should expect slower corrections and more contradictory answers than companies that can trace and withdraw the same information.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>A knowledge catalog is a governed source of company facts, not an official Google standard. It connects each important claim to its source, scope, owner, review date and withdrawal path, making correction a managed capability rather than a search through scattered pages.</p></div>

## Structured content creates a practical head start

Structured content stores information in named, reusable parts instead of leaving it only inside the layout of a finished page. The delivery promise becomes connected information about time, country, eligible product, approving policy and review date. Because those parts retain their meaning, the approved record can be supplied to a website, a service tool or an agent without being rewritten for every interface.

Structured content is related to structured data, but the terms describe different layers. In Google Search, structured data is standardized code added to a page so a machine can identify what the page contains. Structured content is the underlying model of the business information itself, which can exist independently of any page. [Google Search Central, Google’s official guidance service for website owners, said in May 2025](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/05/succeeding-in-ai-search/) that structured data helps share information in a machine-readable form its systems can consider. The same guidance emphasized original, useful content, because structure cannot make a generic claim distinctive or an expired policy true.

This is where a modern headless content management system, or headless CMS, can offer a head start. It separates reusable content from the design of one website and supplies that content to different channels through software connections. If a company already models products, policies, markets and approvals this way, it has some of the raw material for a knowledge catalog. If the system mainly contains page-shaped copy with unclear owners, the platform label does not solve the problem.

The platform market is moving in the same direction, although vendor announcements show product intent rather than neutral proof of adoption. [Sanity, a structured-content platform, announced AI tools for structured-content workflows in May 2025](https://www.sanity.io/blog/agent-actions-ai-building-blocks-for-structured-content). [Contentful, another content platform, made a secured connection for AI assistants and agents generally available in July 2026](https://www.contentful.com/blog/remote-mcp-now-generally-available). These releases matter because they make existing content easier for agents to reach; they do not guarantee that the content is approved, current or safe.

My take is that a headless CMS creates an advantage only when the organization has also done the less glamorous work of modeling meaning and assigning responsibility. The software can expose a fact in milliseconds. The business still has to decide whether that fact deserves to be used.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Structured content preserves the meaning of reusable information beyond one page, while a headless CMS can provide models and workflows suited to that job. The head start is real only when approved sources, business owners and review routines already exist.</p></div>

## Generated interfaces make correction more valuable

The connection to *[The Digital Singularity Shift: The Rise of Metamodern Narratives in Business](/books/the-digital-singularity-shift/)*, my 2024 book about how AI makes digital functions easier to reproduce and shifts attention toward the meaning beneath them, is becoming concrete. A generative user interface, often shortened to GenUI, is a screen assembled by AI for the request at hand rather than selected from one fixed path designed in advance.

In November 2025, [Gemini 3, an AI model from Google, introduced experiments that generated a custom interface from a user’s prompt](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/gemini/gemini-3-gemini-app/). In April 2026, Google released version 0.9 of [A2UI, an open specification for agent-driven interfaces](https://developers.googleblog.com/a2ui-v0-9-generative-ui/). It lets an agent describe the interface elements needed for a task while the receiving application controls how they look and behave.

Think of the holodeck in *Star Trek: The Next Generation*, the science-fiction television series that began in 1987. Its fictional room renders a responsive environment around the person inside it. GenUI is a modest business version of that idea: one customer may receive a comparison while another receives a form, each assembled for the immediate request. The interface becomes more adaptable, while the permitted claims underneath it still need firm boundaries.

Generation will be cheap; reliable correction will remain hard. I think that is the strategic implication hiding behind the excitement about adaptive screens. Design still matters for trust, accessibility and recognition, but a fixed front end can no longer contain all digital value when answers and interfaces can be assembled elsewhere.

This gives leaders a falsifiable prediction rather than a slogan. Over the next year, test whether teams with a governed claim set can trace a wrong answer to its source, correct it and withdraw it from controlled channels faster than teams working from scattered pages and documents. If there is no meaningful difference, the investment case weakens. If correction time falls and contradictory answers decline, the catalog is becoming an operational asset.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>GenUI lets the visible interface adapt to each request, which makes the argument in <em>The Digital Singularity Shift</em> tangible. As digital presentation becomes easier to generate, the ability to trace, constrain and correct company meaning becomes more valuable and can be tested through correction time.</p></div>

## Start with ten claims before buying another platform

The consultancy that modeled and connected your current content may be a sensible candidate for the next step. [Valtech, a global digital consultancy](https://www.valtech.com/), and implementation firms in the Contentful, Sanity or Optimizely partner networks are examples to evaluate, not endorsements. Product knowledge is useful, but a partner badge does not prove commercial independence. Ask whether the firm is being paid to solve your information problem or is also rewarded when you buy more licenses.

The selection test should be written into the contract. Your company should own the information model and be able to export the content in a usable form. The agreement should state where company and customer data may be stored or processed, who maintains approval and review routines, how access is limited to what each person or agent needs, and how the work is handed over if the relationship ends. Sensitive actions should require explicit approval, and permissions should be reversible. A2A can coordinate agents; it does not give a supplier authority over your business decisions.

I would make the first move a bounded 30-day discovery exercise rather than a large platform program. Choose one high-volume, high-risk set of facts, such as delivery promises, return policies or product eligibility. Select ten claims and record the approved wording, market, source policy, business owner, review date, current status and withdrawal path. Then trace where each claim has been copied and find at least one stale, conflicting or ownerless version.

Use a small scorecard. Record how many claims have an approved source and owner, how many remain within their review date, whether test questions return the correct answer for the correct market, and whether a withdrawn fact disappears from the channels you control. Keep search rankings in the report, then add mention rate, how often a fixed set of AI answers names the company, and AI share of voice, how those mentions compare with selected competitors. Use the same questions and comparison set each time so the baseline means something; these visibility measures are signals, not proof that an answer is correct or commercially useful.

At the end of 30 days, leadership can expand the scope, change the method or stop before making a major commitment. I think that is the right near-term investment decision because it tests both the information problem and the organization’s ability to govern it. A redesigned website can still follow, but it should draw from facts that already have owners.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Audit ten valuable claims before making a large technology commitment, and require clear ownership, export rights, data-location terms, controlled access and a tested handover from any partner. Before more systems speak for the business, someone must own the facts they use.</p></div>
