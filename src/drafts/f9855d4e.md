Your website is becoming irrelevant as the compulsory front door to your business.

That is not the same as saying the website is dead. It can still explain, persuade and convert. But a customer can now receive an AI-generated answer about your price, warranty or delivery promise without seeing the page where you published it. Soon an AI agent, software that can pursue a goal and take permitted steps for a person, may use that answer to prepare a transaction somewhere else.

The deeper risk is therefore larger than lost traffic. A company may own every word in its content system and still lose control of the promise when an outside interface removes its conditions. I call the capability needed here **claim control**: the ability to prove where a commercial fact came from, constrain where it applies, correct it and withdraw it across every interface you control.

I think this is the board-level version of the argument: the website remains a channel; the strategic asset is the governed claim set behind every channel. A **knowledge catalog** is the operating model for that asset.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The website will remain a useful channel, but it is losing its role as the required entrance to a company. Claim control protects the source, scope, authority and withdrawal path of every important promise wherever that promise appears.</p></div>

## Search is becoming an answer and an action

The compulsory click was weakening before the current wave of AI products. In July 2024, [SparkToro, an audience-research company, and Datos, a browsing-data provider](https://sparktoro.com/blog/2024-zero-click-search-study/), estimated that 58.5% of US Google searches and 59.7% of EU searches ended without a click. Those figures include people who ended the session or changed the query, so they do not prove that AI caused every zero-click search or that every searcher was satisfied.

AI summaries provide more direct evidence. [Pew Research Center, a nonpartisan US research organization, studied the Google browsing activity of 900 American adults in March 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/). People clicked a traditional result in 8% of visits when Google showed an AI summary, compared with 15% when it did not. A source cited inside the summary received a click in only 1% of those visits. Pew also found that about 60% of searches beginning with question words produced an AI summary, which helps explain the shift from short keywords toward composed questions and answers.

Answers are now being joined by actions. At Google Cloud Next ’25, Google’s annual enterprise technology conference, the company launched [Agent2Agent, or A2A](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/a2a-a-new-era-of-agent-interoperability/), with support from more than 50 technology partners. A2A is an open set of rules that lets independently built AI agents exchange information and coordinate work across business systems. A customer’s agent could ask a supplier’s agent about availability and prepare an order for human approval.

I don’t know which protocol or customer habit will dominate, especially for expensive or high-risk decisions. A2A also says nothing about whether a supplier’s claim is true or whether an agent has permission to act on it. Yet the direction is clear enough to challenge the old assumption: company information can shape a decision before the customer reaches a company-controlled interface.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>SparkToro and Datos measured a majority of Google searches ending without a click, Pew observed fewer clicks when AI summaries appeared, and A2A lets software agents coordinate work. Discovery increasingly includes answers and permitted actions that happen before a website visit.</p></div>

## A knowledge catalog creates claim control

In a June 2026 video, [Phil Risher of Phlash Consulting, a marketing consultancy for service businesses](https://youtu.be/xAIeA3ewRbo), describes a knowledge catalog as verified company information that can be made available through an agent. The phrase is useful, but it is not an official Google product or standard in Google’s A2A announcement or its May 2025 guidance for AI search. I use it here as an operating model for company knowledge.

A knowledge catalog is a company-owned body of approved facts arranged so people and software can use them without guessing their meaning, scope or authority. It can contain product details, service areas, pricing rules, policies, credentials and answers to real customer questions. Each important claim needs an approved source, an owner, the markets and products where it applies, a review date, a current status and a tested way to withdraw it.

Here is a compact illustrative record:

| Field | Example value |
| --- | --- |
| Claim | Delivery within 24 hours |
| Market and product | Sweden; stocked replacement parts |
| Source policy | Approved logistics policy, version 6 |
| Business owner | Service operations director |
| Review date | 30 September 2026 |
| Status and withdrawal | Active; suspend when the carrier commitment changes |
| Action permission | May be retrieved; placing or amending an order needs separate, reversible approval |

The record makes a crucial boundary visible. An agent may retrieve the delivery policy because access to a fact has been approved. It may not use a customer’s address, place an order or change an existing order unless the company grants a separate permission for that action. Owning the sentence is insufficient if its scope and authority do not travel with it.

Risher calls content “training data for the future internet.” I think that metaphor captures the stakes, although it is not a complete technical description. Many AI systems use **retrieval**, meaning that they look up company information while composing an answer instead of permanently adding it to the underlying model. In either case, the company needs to know which source the system used and how to replace it when the fact changes.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>A knowledge catalog is a governed source of company facts, not an official Google standard. Claim control comes from attaching source, scope, owner, review status, withdrawal state and action limits to each important promise.</p></div>

## Structured content is the practical foundation

**Structured content** stores information in named, reusable parts instead of leaving it only inside the layout of a finished page. The delivery promise becomes connected information about time, country, eligible product, approving policy and review date. Because those parts retain their meaning, the same approved record can serve a website, a service tool or an agent without being rewritten for every interface.

Structured content is related to **structured data**, but the terms describe different layers. In Google Search, structured data is standardized code added to a page so a machine can identify what the page contains. Structured content is the underlying model of the business information itself. [Google Search Central, Google’s official guidance service for website owners, said in May 2025](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/05/succeeding-in-ai-search/) that structured data helps share information in a machine-readable form its systems can consider. The same guidance emphasized original, useful content because structure cannot make a generic claim distinctive or an expired policy true.

A modern **headless content management system**, or headless CMS, separates reusable content from the design of one website and supplies it to different channels through software connections. If a company already models products, policies, markets and approvals this way, it has some of the raw material for a knowledge catalog. If the system mainly contains page-shaped copy with unclear owners, the platform label offers little protection.

The market is moving in this direction. [Sanity, a structured-content platform, announced AI tools for structured-content workflows in May 2025](https://www.sanity.io/blog/agent-actions-ai-building-blocks-for-structured-content). [Contentful, another content platform, made a secured agent connection generally available in July 2026](https://www.contentful.com/blog/remote-mcp-now-generally-available), allowing approved assistants to retrieve or work with stored content under access controls. These are vendor roadmaps, not neutral proof of adoption or safety. My take is that a headless CMS creates a head start only when the organization has also modeled meaning and assigned responsibility.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Structured content preserves the meaning of reusable information beyond one page, while a headless CMS can provide useful models and delivery connections. The advantage exists only when approved sources, business owners and review routines already exist.</p></div>

## Generated interfaces make governed meaning more valuable

The connection to *[The Digital Singularity Shift: The Rise of Metamodern Narratives in Business](/books/the-digital-singularity-shift/)*, my 2024 book about how AI makes digital functions easier to reproduce and shifts attention toward the meaning beneath them, is becoming concrete. A **generative user interface**, often shortened to GenUI, is a screen assembled by AI for the request at hand rather than selected from one fixed path designed in advance.

In November 2025, [Gemini 3, an AI model from Google, introduced experiments that generated a custom interface from a user’s prompt](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/gemini/gemini-3-gemini-app/). In April 2026, Google released version 0.9 of [A2UI, an open specification for agent-driven interfaces](https://developers.googleblog.com/a2ui-v0-9-generative-ui/). It lets an agent describe the interface elements needed for a task while the receiving application controls how they look and behave.

Think of the holodeck in *Star Trek: The Next Generation*, the science-fiction television series that began in 1987. Its fictional room renders a responsive environment around the person inside it. GenUI is a modest business version of that idea: one customer may receive a comparison while another receives a form, each assembled for the immediate request. The interface changes, but the permitted claims underneath it still need firm boundaries.

I think this is the second-order consequence that matters. Companies can own their content and still lose authority over customer promises when external systems assemble the answer, remove the market condition or connect a fact to an unauthorized action. Design remains important for trust, accessibility and recognition. Claim control becomes more important because the company no longer designs every place where the claim is used.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>GenUI makes the argument in <em>The Digital Singularity Shift</em> tangible: interfaces can be assembled for each request, while company meaning still needs stable boundaries. The more presentation can change, the more valuable portable source, scope and authority become.</p></div>

## Start with ten claims and accountable owners

Do not begin with another platform purchase. I would start with a 30-day exercise around ten high-volume or high-risk claims, such as delivery promises, return policies or product eligibility. Give it one named executive sponsor and a small working group with representatives from service or operations, a local market, content, legal or compliance, and technology or security. The point is not to create a committee. It is to put every decision-maker needed for one bounded test in the same weekly forum.

The operating model can be simple. A business claim owner approves what is true. An information steward maintains the record, review date and evidence of where it appears. Legal, compliance and security set the boundaries for personal data and sensitive actions. Market representatives confirm local scope. If two functions disagree, the sponsor owns the escalation and decides whether the claim remains unavailable until the conflict is resolved.

For a concrete European example, imagine a Swedish customer asking an agent for a Norwegian supplier’s delivery terms. The agent may retrieve the approved policy for Sweden, but it should not send the customer’s address to another system or place an order until the company has approved the processing location, any required consent and a reversible action permission. Teams should map that flow against [the General Data Protection Regulation, the European Union’s data-protection law](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj), and [the EU AI Act, its risk-based law for AI systems](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj), before the pilot touches personal data or regulated decisions.

First record the current baseline: how long it takes to find the authority behind each claim, how many versions conflict and how many lack an owner. Then create the ten catalog records, test them through one controlled assistant and withdraw one test claim in a non-production environment. A successful withdrawal should remove the old answer from every controlled route without giving the assistant permission to take a related commercial action.

The monitoring method should be equally concrete. Use a fixed set of questions, state the market and customer context, write the approved expected answer, test the same AI systems on a regular schedule and keep traceable evidence of the answer and cited source. **Mention rate** measures how often those answers name the company. **AI share of voice** compares those mentions with a fixed set of competitors. These visibility measures should influence investment only when they sit beside stronger owner coverage, fewer conflicting claims and faster correction; attention without accuracy is another liability.

Expand the exercise when every selected claim has a source and owner, correction and withdrawal work faster than the baseline, and action permissions hold under testing. Stop or redesign it when ownership remains disputed, a withdrawn claim survives in a controlled channel, or an agent crosses from reading a policy to acting without separate approval. I think those tests make the decision both serious and reversible.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>A useful first move is a sponsored 30-day test with ten claims, named owners, a cross-functional working group and a repeatable answer baseline. Expand only when correction improves and action limits hold; stop when ownership, withdrawal or authorization fails.</p></div>

## Choose a partner after you define control

Only after the operating model is clear should the supplier question enter. The consultancy that modeled and connected your current content may be a sensible candidate because it already understands the information structure. [Valtech, a global digital consultancy](https://www.valtech.com/), and implementation firms in the partner networks of Contentful, Sanity or [Optimizely, a digital-experience platform](https://www.optimizely.com/), are examples to evaluate, not endorsements.

Platform knowledge has value, and commercial independence is never guaranteed by a company label. Ask whether the consultancy is paid to solve your information problem or also rewarded when you buy more licenses. Put the answer into the contract: your company owns the information model, can export the content in a usable form, controls where data is stored and processed, limits access for each person and agent, and receives a tested handover if the relationship ends.

Start building the catalog now, but begin with the promises that can hurt the business when they are wrong. Before more systems speak and act for your company, make every claim portable with its proof, limits and off switch.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The same independent consultancy that built a modern content platform may be well placed to extend it, but incentives and contract terms matter more than partner badges. Before more systems speak for the business, every important promise needs portable proof, limits and an off switch.</p></div>
