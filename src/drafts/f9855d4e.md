Your next website program may solve the wrong problem. It can improve a destination even though some customer questions are already resolved before anyone arrives.

The website will continue to explain, reassure and serve. But I think the management question has changed from “How do we bring everyone to our interface?” to “Can every approved channel or software assistant get the same correct answer from us?” That shift moves investment toward the company’s knowledge, not away from its customers.

In a [June 2026 video](https://youtu.be/xAIeA3ewRbo), Phil Risher, a consultant at Phlash Consulting, calls this asset a **knowledge catalog**, defined as “a verified data set of information about your business.” I use it here as a working term, not as the name of a Google product or official standard: approved company knowledge organized so it can be used beyond the page where it was written.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>A knowledge catalog is an approved, reusable body of company information. It gives leaders a way to keep answers consistent when customers encounter the business through search pages, websites or software assistants.</p></div>

## The journey is being assembled before the visit

[Datos supplied clickstream records for a study published by audience-research company SparkToro in July 2024](https://sparktoro.com/blog/2024-zero-click-search-study/). The no-click rate in that sample was 59.7% for EU Google searches and 58.5% for US Google searches. The category combined people who stopped searching with people who rewrote their query, which means the figures cannot be attributed to AI. They show that a search result has not guaranteed a website visit for some time.

Evidence specific to AI-written answers makes the change clearer. In a [July 2025 analysis, the nonpartisan Pew Research Center examined March browsing data from 900 US adults](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/). A traditional result drew a click in 8% of visits to a page with a Google AI summary, versus 15% where no summary appeared. A citation embedded in the summary drew a click in 1% of visits where a summary was present. The study observed behavior rather than testing causation, but it shows why traffic is no longer a complete measure of visibility.

Presentation is also becoming request-specific. **Generative user interface**, or GenUI, means that AI composes the screen in response to what one person asks, rather than serving a layout prepared in advance. [Google’s November 2025 Gemini 3 release, an update to its Gemini AI system](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/gemini/gemini-3-gemini-app/), included experiments that generated interfaces from prompts. Google then published [A2UI v0.9](https://developers.googleblog.com/a2ui-v0-9-generative-ui/) in April 2026, an open specification that lets an AI system express what a screen should contain while the receiving application renders approved components.

These releases demonstrate a capability rather than the end of websites: software can construct a useful screen after receiving a request. I think that raises the strategic weight of the information behind the screen, because presentation can vary while product facts, policies and evidence still have to remain coherent.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>In observed Google visits, traditional-result clicks were less common when an AI-written answer appeared, while recent Google releases allow screens to be assembled for individual requests. The website remains useful, but it can no longer be the only place where company knowledge makes sense.</p></div>

## Agents turn unclear content into operating risk

Software qualifies here as an **AI agent** when it can pursue a goal and carry out allowed steps, rather than merely return text. Google used its annual cloud conference in April 2025, Google Cloud Next ’25, to announce the open [Agent2Agent protocol, or A2A](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/a2a-a-new-era-of-agent-interoperability/). More than 50 technology partners supported it at launch. In plain language, a protocol is an agreed communication format; under A2A, participating agents can communicate, exchange information securely and coordinate actions across different business systems.

Agents can coordinate messages without vouching for a supplier’s claims or receiving authority to spend money. I don’t know whether customers will delegate routine purchases quickly or remain cautious for years. The relevant preparation is the same in either case: a company needs reliable answers before it can safely expose them to software that may act on them.

Consider a hypothetical manufacturer whose customer asks whether a component fits a particular machine. The answer may depend on model, production year, market and safety certification. I think the catalog record should hold those conditions along with the source, accountable owner, effective date and review status. That is my proposed operating model, not an industry standard. Calling the record “verified” means an accountable person approved it and the supporting evidence remains traceable; it does not mean a machine has pronounced it true.

The same record also needs a boundary around its use. A catalog can tell an agent which component fits; identity, access and contract controls separately decide whether the agent may reveal a negotiated price, offer a discount or place an order. Reliable knowledge and permission to transact are different management responsibilities.

This is why **structured content** matters. It stores meaning in named fields and reusable records instead of leaving every condition buried in a finished page. By contrast, **structured data** labels the information displayed on a web page using a standardized format machines can read. In May 2025, [Google Search Central, the company’s official guidance service for site owners](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/05/succeeding-in-ai-search), said this markup helps its systems consider page information and should agree with what a visitor can see. Structured content makes the underlying answer reusable; structured data helps a search service interpret one published page.

Risher describes content as “training data for the future internet.” I read that as a business metaphor. Publishing a page does not automatically retrain every AI model, but clearer company evidence can be retrieved, compared or cited by systems looking for an answer.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>A knowledge catalog keeps approved answers with their evidence, scope, owner and review status. Software assistants that can take permitted steps raise the cost of ambiguity, so accurate information and authority to act must be governed separately.</p></div>

## A headless CMS changes the starting line

With a **headless content management system**, or headless CMS, content is managed independently of the website’s visual design and delivered to different channels through defined interfaces. When products, policies and support information already exist as structured records, the organization can assess and extend that material rather than extract every fact from finished pages.

That conclusion follows from how the system is built; it is not a measured market advantage. My take is that the starting position can still be substantially better when the records are distinctive, current and clearly owned. A modern platform filled with duplicated or generic copy offers fast access to weak information, which does not solve the trust problem.

This pattern also fits the strategic prediction in [*The Digital Singularity Shift* (2024)](/books/the-digital-singularity-shift/), my book about what happens when AI makes functional digital advantages easier to reproduce. The prediction concerns value, not a specific protocol: when competent interfaces become cheaper to create, meaning, story and belief carry more of what remains hard to copy. The book did not forecast A2A by name, and current product launches do not prove its whole thesis.

They do reveal one of its mechanisms. A screen can be generated for a request, but an organization still has to decide what it knows, which claims it can prove and why it deserves trust. I think a knowledge catalog puts that work into a form that can travel across interfaces without surrendering ownership of the answer.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>A headless content system stores information separately from website design, which can provide useful starting material when the records are current and owned. <em>The Digital Singularity Shift</em> (2024) predicted that easier digital function would move lasting value toward meaning and belief; reusable company knowledge makes that shift practical.</p></div>

## Make governance the buying brief

The consultancy that built the current content environment belongs on the shortlist because it may already understand how the information is organized and where it connects to other systems. It should not receive the work by default. Valtech, a global business-transformation consultancy, and agencies in the partner networks of Contentful, Sanity and Optimizely, three providers of content-management platforms, illustrate the field to examine. A partner listing confirms a platform relationship; it does not establish independence, delivery quality or fit.

I think leaders should favor a consultancy that can recommend reuse, integration or no new license at all. Ask candidates to disclose resale income, ownership ties and commercial targets connected to each platform. Then evaluate who owns the information, who can access it, whether it can move to another provider and what happens at handover. Platform expertise is useful, but the supplier’s incentives should not quietly decide how the company organizes and connects its knowledge.

Give the chosen team one narrow test. Select a business answer whose inconsistency creates real cost, find every current source, resolve conflicts and appoint an accountable owner. Record the conditions that change the answer, expose only the information an external system should receive, and check whether a customer-facing page, an employee and a permitted software assistant all reach the same approved result.

Measurement should follow the new path without pretending to prove more than it can. Keep rankings. For a fixed set of business questions, **mention rate** records the proportion of AI responses in which the company appears. **AI share of voice** compares its mentions with those of selected competitors across the same systems, markets and period. I am using practical definitions rather than claiming an accepted standard, and both measures describe visibility in selected responses rather than sales. Optimizely already [lists software for comparing AI share of voice](https://www.optimizely.com/agents/competitive-ai-share-of-voice-agent/), which shows that vendors have started selling tools around this measure.

Start now, but start with the answer rather than the software. Once one important piece of company knowledge is provably current, reusable and limited by clear permission rules, the organization has tested the working method the rest of the catalog will need.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Test suppliers for both skill and financial incentives, then put one costly business answer under clear ownership and expose it within defined limits. Technology should follow the knowledge model, because the company must control the answer even when it does not control the interface.</p></div>
