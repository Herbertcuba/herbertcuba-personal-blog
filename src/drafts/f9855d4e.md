Your website is becoming irrelevant to a growing share of customer decisions. It will still explain products, serve customers and close business, but the answer that shapes a decision may now appear in search, an AI assistant or an agent before the customer sees a page.

That creates an **answer-control gap**: the distance between the approved company record you can govern and the public answer you can only observe and influence. The boundary matters. You can control your source records and the path into interfaces you own, but you cannot force an external AI service to update on your schedule, use your data or respect your permissions.

I think the strategic response is a **knowledge catalog**, a governed body of company knowledge designed to work beyond any single page. Its purpose is concrete: move approved answers through owned channels faster, preserve the context that makes a fact safe to use and prevent software from making commitments nobody authorized.

## The website is no longer the whole journey

The shift is already visible in search. [Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/), a nonpartisan American research organization, published a July 2025 analysis of browsing activity from 900 US adults during March of that year. People clicked a traditional Google Search result in 8% of visits that included an AI summary, compared with 15% when no summary appeared. They clicked a source cited inside the summary in only 1% of visits.

Pew also found that 60% of queries beginning with “who,” “what,” “when” or “why” produced an AI summary. That is a specific subset, not every question asked in search, but it shows why conversational behavior matters: the more a query resembles a request for an answer, the more likely the interface is to provide that answer directly.

A second study shows that the change did not begin with generative AI. [SparkToro, an audience-research company, and Datos, a browsing-data provider](https://sparktoro.com/blog/2024-zero-click-search-study/), estimated in July 2024 that 58.5% of American Google searches and 59.7% of searches in the European Union ended without a click. Those figures include both ended sessions and revised searches, so they do not prove that AI caused the missing clicks. They do show that website traffic was already an incomplete measure of whether a company entered the customer's consideration.

The interface is changing at the same time. A **generative user interface**, often shortened to GenUI, creates a screen for the request at hand instead of choosing a finished page from a fixed library. Google demonstrated request-specific interfaces when it released its Gemini 3 AI model in [November 2025](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/gemini/gemini-3-gemini-app/). In [April 2026](https://developers.googleblog.com/a2ui-v0-9-generative-ui/), it published A2UI v0.9, a format in which an AI system describes the interface it needs while the receiving application renders approved components. These releases show a product direction, not universal adoption, but the mechanism is now real: the screen can be assembled after the question is asked.

The next step is action. An **AI agent** is software that works through several steps toward a goal, such as comparing options, collecting information and requesting a booking. At Google Cloud Next, Google's annual cloud-computing conference, the company launched [Agent2Agent, or A2A, in April 2025](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/a2a-a-new-era-of-agent-interoperability/) with support and contributions from more than 50 technology partners. A protocol is an agreed method of communication; A2A lets separately built agents exchange information securely and coordinate work across business applications.

A2A is transport, not authority. It does not make an outside model read your catalog, obey your permissions or gain the right to sign a contract. I don't know when customers will trust agents with high-stakes purchases, because fraud, liability and habit remain serious constraints. I think A2A still matters because interaction and action can now move away from the company page, making the quality of the knowledge underneath more important than the route used to reach it.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Search often supplies an answer without a visit, GenUI can assemble a screen around a request, and A2A lets agents coordinate. Adoption will vary, but the customer journey is already separating from the company page.</p></div>

## A knowledge catalog makes company knowledge usable

[Phil Risher of Phlash Consulting](https://youtu.be/xAIeA3ewRbo), a digital-marketing firm for local service businesses, offered a useful definition in June 2026: a knowledge catalog is “a verified data set of information about your business.” I think that is the right starting point, although an enterprise needs more than a collection of correct facts. Every important claim also needs its source, owner, scope, effective date, approval state and permitted use.

The term also has a narrower product meaning. [Google Cloud, Google's cloud-computing division, introduced Google Cloud Knowledge Catalog in April 2026](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/data-analytics/introducing-the-google-cloud-knowledge-catalog) to provide governed context about enterprise data and AI assets. That product may become one component of a solution. The broader knowledge catalog described here is an operating model that connects business decisions, records and delivery paths, regardless of which software holds each part.

Imagine a manufacturer that has verified that one current product model no longer contains a restricted chemical. The claim record should point to the laboratory report, identify the model and serial-number range, name the markets where the statement applies, carry an effective date and assign an accountable owner. Approval may allow a customer assistant to answer a buyer's question, while explicitly rejecting a request to issue a regulatory certificate or extend the claim to an older unit. A fact can be correct and still be unsafe when software loses its boundaries.

The delivery path is what turns that record into an answer the company can govern. When an owned assistant receives a question, it requests the current record from the catalog. The catalog checks which user or application is asking, the relevant market and the action being requested before returning the approved fields. The interface shows the answer, while the system logs the record version, the access decision and the output.

If the approval is withdrawn, the owner can restore the previous version or require the assistant to refuse safely. The audit trail shows what changed, who approved it and which answers used that version. This is where control is real. Once a public AI service creates an answer outside this path, the company can publish better evidence, watch the result and seek corrections, but it cannot guarantee the output.

A knowledge catalog should connect existing systems rather than pretend to replace them. Product systems hold product details; master-data systems keep shared core records such as product codes and legal names; content-management systems hold publishable explanations; data catalogs show where data lives and who owns it; knowledge graphs describe relationships between facts. The new agreement is organizational: which source wins, who decides, what context travels with an answer and which actions remain forbidden.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>A knowledge catalog connects an approved claim to its evidence, boundaries and allowed uses, then carries it through a checked and auditable path into interfaces the company owns.</p></div>

## Structured content creates the head start

**Structured content** stores an idea as named, reusable fields instead of burying it inside a finished block of page copy. In the manufacturer example, the claim, product model, market, evidence, effective date and approval status remain separate. A website, service assistant or internal agent can then use the same approved record without guessing which sentence contains the rule.

This is different from **web structured data**, also called schema markup, which adds standard labels to visible page content so search systems can classify it. In [May 2025 guidance for its AI search experiences](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/05/succeeding-in-ai-search), Google said its systems consider information shared in machine-readable structured data and asked publishers to create unique, non-commodity content. Both parts matter. Better labels cannot rescue generic or stale knowledge, and valuable knowledge is difficult to govern when it remains trapped inside finished pages.

A **headless content management system**, or headless CMS, stores managed content separately from the website design and makes it available to other channels through software interfaces. I think organizations with mature content models, named owners and reliable integrations have a substantial head start because much of their knowledge is already separated from presentation. This is an architectural judgment, not a measured market advantage. A modern CMS filled with duplicates, outdated claims and unclear ownership offers very little advantage.

This is also where the shift meets [*The Digital Singularity Shift*](/books/the-digital-singularity-shift/), my 2024 book about what happens when AI makes functional differences easier to copy. Its argument was that durable competition would move toward meaning, story and belief as those functional differences collapsed. The book did not name A2A or prescribe a knowledge catalog. What it anticipated was the deeper direction: when an interface can be generated for each request, presentation becomes easier to reproduce, while distinctive company knowledge and the trust attached to it remain harder to copy. Structured content is how that value travels without losing its origin.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Structured content preserves the meaning and limits of a claim beyond one page. A mature headless CMS supplies useful raw material, but the real advantage comes from distinctive knowledge, clear ownership and reliable delivery paths.</p></div>

## Prove one answer before funding a transformation

The evidence above is not a Nordic enterprise case, and American click behavior should not be treated as a forecast for every Swedish or Norwegian business. The honest response to that evidence gap is a local, reversible test. Choose one product family, one market and one customer question whose wrong answer creates a visible cost, such as a correction, a compliance escalation or an unauthorized offer.

Use a six-week planning envelope. The executive sponsor needs about four decision hours in total. An accountable business owner contributes half a working day each week; a content lead and an integration lead contribute one day each per week; legal and security share about twelve review hours. Using eight-hour working days, that is roughly 136 internal hours.

Set an external-fee ceiling as well. My proposed limit is SEK 500,000 with no new platform license. These numbers are decision limits, not industry benchmarks. If one governed answer cannot produce useful evidence within them, a wider program has not earned approval.

Decision rights should be just as clear. The sponsor owns the budget and the scale-or-stop decision. The business owner approves the claim and resolves conflicts between source systems. Content and integration leads own the record and delivery path, while legal or security can veto an unsafe disclosure or action. The implementation partner can build and advise, but it cannot approve the business claim it is paid to move.

Make the evaluation repeatable. Select twenty real customer questions from search or service logs and prepare two ordinary phrasings of each, so the team can see whether wording rather than the catalog caused a change. Run every phrasing three times before and after one approved fact update. Keep the prompt set fixed, and record the service, displayed model version, date, market and every output.

The business owner should grade accuracy and scope against a written answer key, while the risk reviewer grades whether each response stayed within its permitted use. Preserve the frozen source record, approvals, outputs, grader decisions and proof of rollback under the company's normal audit schedule. That record makes the result reproducible and keeps a later provider update from being mistaken for an improvement in the catalog.

My take is to define the **baseline** as the time and effort required by the current approved-update path. Pass only if the changed fact reaches every owned test interface in no more than half that time, creates no unauthorized commitment in the complete test set, reduces correction or review effort and survives a tested rollback. Public AI services belong in the observation set, not the pass gate, because their models and update cycles sit outside company control.

Keep search rankings, but add two bounded measures for those public services. **Mention rate** is the share of a fixed set of prompts whose answers name the company. **AI share of voice** is the company's share of all brand mentions across a fixed competitor set. Neither is a universal standard or proof of revenue, so pair them with a business result such as fewer corrections, fewer escalations or more qualified inquiries.

The consultancy that built the current content platform is a sensible place to start because it already knows the content model and integrations. I think the selection should remain vendor-neutral and evidence-based. Require proof of headless-CMS implementation skill and a working governance model with named owners, approval rules and review dates. Secure full export rights, a declared storage location and access controls tied to each role. Separate service fees from license resale, require disclosure of platform investments or sales incentives, and test the handover before the pilot closes.

Start with the answer whose loss of context would cost the business most. Scale only when the governed path moves that answer faster, blocks forbidden uses and lowers the cost of correction. The durable digital asset is approved company knowledge that can travel, be checked and be withdrawn when the interface is no longer yours.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Run a six-week, one-answer pilot with explicit decision rights, a fixed resource cap, repeatable tests and a business outcome beyond visibility. Use an implementation partner for delivery, but keep the knowledge records, audit evidence, export rights and supplier choice under company control.</p></div>
