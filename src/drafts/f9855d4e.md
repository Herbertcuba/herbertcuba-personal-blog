Your website is not about to disappear. Its privileged role as the center of your digital business is weakening because a customer can now receive an answer without visiting it, see an interface made for one request and, in some cases, let software prepare the next action.

I think traffic loss is only the visible effect. The strategic problem is the **answer-control gap**: the distance between an answer your company has approved and the answer a customer receives from a website, search service or AI assistant.

That gap has two different parts. Your company can control answers on interfaces it operates, but it can only monitor and influence what outside AI services say. The executive question is whether your current systems can keep approved knowledge accurate, traceable and within scope across the first group while making dependable source material available to the second.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The website is becoming one interface among many. Leaders need to govern the approved knowledge beneath those interfaces and separate the answers they control from the external answers they can only influence.</p></div>

## The answer is leaving the page

[SparkToro](https://sparktoro.com/blog/in-2026-less-than-one-third-of-google-searches-still-send-a-click/), an audience-research company, estimated that 68.01% of US Google searches from January through April 2026 ended without a click. The estimate came from a desktop-and-mobile browser panel supplied by Similarweb, a company that measures digital behavior. A **zero-click search** ends without the user opening a result, whether the person found an answer, abandoned the session or changed the query.

The limits matter. The panel excluded Google's mobile app, and its coverage differs from SparkToro's [2024 European Union estimate](https://sparktoro.com/blog/2024-zero-click-search-study/) of 59.7%, so the two figures do not form a clean trend line. Only 0.34% of searches in the 2026 panel reached AI Mode, Google's conversational search interface. Zero-click behavior is common in this panel, but it is not evidence that people have already handed ordinary commerce to agents.

[Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/), a nonpartisan US research organization, observed fewer clicks on visits where an AI summary appeared. It examined March 2025 browsing activity from 900 American adults. A traditional result received a click in 8% of Google visits with an AI summary and 15% of visits without one. A source inside the summary received a click in only 1% of visits where a summary appeared. This is an association in observed visits, not proof that the summary alone caused the difference.

The interface itself can also change. A **generative user interface**, or GenUI, creates a screen for a particular request instead of showing everyone the same fixed page. Google demonstrated the capability with its Gemini 3 AI model in [November 2025](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/gemini/gemini-3-gemini-app/). When software can assemble the screen at the moment of need, the webpage becomes one presentation of the knowledge beneath it.

An **AI agent** is software that carries out several steps toward a goal. On [April 9, 2025](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/a2a-a-new-era-of-agent-interoperability/), Google announced the open **Agent2Agent protocol**, or A2A, with support and contributions from more than 50 technology partners. It gives agents built by different organizations a common way to exchange information and coordinate actions. The launch establishes an interoperability standard, although it does not establish broad commercial adoption.

I don't know how quickly customers will trust agents with expensive, sensitive or regulated decisions. I think the defensible conclusion is narrower: discovery can now move from page to answer, and software can begin to move from answer to action. Both developments weaken the website's claim to be the single hub.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Search often ends without a website visit, request-specific interfaces are now possible, and agents have a standard for coordinating work. The capabilities are real even though mass agent adoption remains unproven.</p></div>

## What a knowledge catalog adds

Phil Risher, owner of Phlash Consulting, a marketing firm for local-service businesses, described a **knowledge catalog** in a [June 2026 video](https://youtu.be/xAIeA3ewRbo) as “a verified data set of information about your business.” Google Cloud, Google's enterprise cloud business, introduced [a narrower product with the same name](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/data-analytics/introducing-the-google-cloud-knowledge-catalog) in April 2026 for governing enterprise data and context. No verified Google source defines Risher's broader business model in the same way, so I use the term here as a practical synthesis rather than an established product category.

For a company, the catalog is a governed set of approved claims: facts, pricing rules, policies, credentials and answers, each connected to its source, owner, market, effective date and permitted use. Those fields preserve where a statement is valid and who may change it. That makes the catalog an operating model before it becomes a technology purchase.

Consider a hypothetical record: “Consumer returns are accepted within 30 days.” The record names the approved policy as its source, commerce operations as its owner, Sweden as its market and public consumer support as its permitted use. If an agent asks about Norway or a business contract, the record is out of scope and the system should refuse to extend it. A useful catalog governs the boundary of an answer as carefully as the answer itself.

Your organization may already have several pieces. A content management system, or CMS, manages editorial material. A product information management system keeps product attributes, a data catalog helps people find datasets, a knowledge graph records relationships, and retrieval software selects relevant material for an AI response. The knowledge catalog adds a shared approval contract across those systems, so source, scope, ownership and expiry travel with each claim wherever it is used.

This also defines the boundary of control. The catalog can directly govern answers supplied by your website, app, service desk or company agent. Publishing the same claims in a **machine-readable** format, meaning a structure software can reliably parse, may give an outside AI service better evidence. It cannot make that service use the evidence or repeat it correctly.

**Structured content** provides the raw material by storing meaning in named, reusable fields instead of burying it inside finished page copy. It is broader than webpage structured data, often called schema markup, which adds labels that software can read to information on a page. In [May 2025 guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/05/succeeding-in-ai-search), Google Search Central, the company's documentation for website owners, advised publishers to use structured data and create original, useful material rather than information competitors can simply copy. Clear fields help machines interpret a claim; distinctive company knowledge gives the claim value.

A [**headless CMS**](https://www.sanity.io/headless-cms) stores content separately from website design and supplies it to different interfaces through software connections called APIs. I think an organization with mature content models, named owners and dependable update processes has a substantial head start because much of the catalog's raw material already exists. This is an architectural judgment, not a measured market result. Poorly governed content remains poor input, however modern the CMS may be.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>A knowledge catalog adds an approval and scope contract across systems the company may already use. Structured content makes approved claims reusable, and a mature headless CMS can provide a head start without guaranteeing quality.</p></div>

## The Digital Singularity shifts the scarce asset

[*The Digital Singularity Shift*](/books/the-digital-singularity-shift/), my 2024 book about AI making functional differences easier to copy, argues that meaning, story and belief become the remaining battleground. The current shift extends that mechanism into the interface through which customers meet a company.

GenUI makes presentation easier to reproduce because software can assemble a useful screen for each request. AI-mediated search chooses which knowledge appears in that screen, while agents may act on the resulting answer. I think authority is therefore the next fault line: which source deserves trust, where its claim applies and who is accountable when it changes.

A carefully designed website still matters when a person chooses to visit. Its strategic value changes when presentation becomes temporary and the same approved claim must survive in search, an assistant, a sales tool and an interface that did not exist when the claim was written. Trusted meaning that can travel without losing its limits then becomes scarcer than one perfect frontend.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>As AI makes interfaces easier to generate, verified meaning and accountable authority become harder to reproduce. That is the strategic connection between The Digital Singularity and the knowledge catalog.</p></div>

## Prove the case before buying the platform

The research assembled for this article contains no measured Swedish or Norwegian enterprise case with a published baseline, cost and outcome. US search data and vendor announcements cannot fill that gap. My take is to treat the knowledge catalog as a testable Nordic business hypothesis and make the first pilot produce the local evidence that is currently missing.

Start with a six-week test of an interface you own. Choose one policy family, one market and one customer-service interface. Before changing anything, run 100 predefined questions through the current setup, including paraphrases, expired wording, wrong-market requests and requests beyond the user's authority. One hundred questions will not estimate all customer behavior; it creates a manageable diagnostic sample that can expose several failure types and be repeated after a source changes.

For the owned interface, I would set four starting limits. Every answer must be supported by an approved record. Wrong-market, expired or unauthorized answers are critical defects, with zero accepted. An approved change must appear within one business day. Finally, allow no more than five noncritical defects per 100 questions, such as a correct but incomplete answer, a broken source link or wording outside the agreed style; define those categories before recording the baseline.

Scale only if the current setup first misses those limits and the pilot then meets all of them. Stop if the existing systems already pass, a critical defect remains or the work exceeds its agreed capacity. Rollback means disconnecting the pilot delivery path and restoring the previous source while retaining the test record.

Run a separate external-visibility test because outside AI services are beyond your control. Choose 50 fixed customer questions and two named services, record a baseline, then publish the approved source in a format software can reliably parse and repeat the test weekly for 12 weeks. Track answer accuracy, citations and **mention rate**, the percentage of tested answers that name your company. Add **AI share of voice**, your share of mentions across a fixed competitor group, alongside search rankings rather than treating it as revenue.

Predeclare what would disprove the external case. For a first test, I would require at least a ten-percentage-point increase in answers that both name the company and state the tested claim correctly, sustained across three weekly checks. If that result does not appear, reject the claim that this intervention improved outside answers. The business case for consistent answers on owned interfaces must then stand on its own.

Put the work inside an economic boundary: no more than 60 internal hours, €25,000 in external fees and no new software license for the first scope. These are proposed decision limits, not a market price. Name a permanent business owner, delivery owner and risk owner before starting. If the capability scales, the business owner should chair a monthly review, claims should be checked whenever a source policy changes and at least quarterly, the risk owner should be able to block unsafe use, and expired claims should have a named retirement decision.

Begin with the consultancy that already understands your content platform. [Valtech](https://www.valtech.com/), which describes itself as a global business-transformation agency, is one example. The agency directories of headless-CMS providers [Contentful](https://www.contentful.com/partners/) and [Sanity](https://www.sanity.io/agency-partners/), and the solution-partner network around [Optimizely's](https://www.optimizely.com/partners/) CMS and digital-experience platform, are other places to look. A partner badge does not prove independence, so require disclosure of license-resale income, full export rights and a written reason for every proposed new platform.

Publish the baseline, final result, internal hours and external cost. I think leaders should begin now, but the first objective is to learn whether existing systems can preserve an approved answer when the interface changes. If they can, keep them. If they cannot, the evidence will tell you what your knowledge catalog actually needs to become.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Test owned-answer control and external visibility separately, with fixed questions, acceptance limits, cost, ownership and rollback. Build a knowledge-catalog capability only when current systems fail and the pilot proves that the gap can be closed.</p></div>
