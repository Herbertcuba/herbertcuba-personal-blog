Your website is on its way to becoming irrelevant as the center of your digital business. It will still explain products and close business, but more decisions will be shaped before a customer opens a page.

Traffic loss is the visible problem. I think the deeper problem is quieter: your company approves one answer while a search service or AI assistant gives customers an old, incomplete or wrong-market version. I call the distance between those two states the **answer-control gap**.

That gap gives leaders a practical test. If your current systems can keep approved answers accurate, current and within scope across every interface you control, build no new layer. If they cannot, start building a knowledge catalog.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The website is becoming one interface among many. The strategic risk is the gap between what the business has approved and what customers are told.</p></div>

## The answer is leaving the page

[SparkToro](https://sparktoro.com/blog/in-2026-less-than-one-third-of-google-searches-still-send-a-click/), an audience-research company, analyzed Similarweb's US desktop-and-mobile web panel in June 2026. Similarweb measures digital behavior. SparkToro estimated that 68.01% of Google searches from January through April 2026 ended without a click. A **zero-click search** ends without the user opening a result, whether the user found an answer, gave up or changed the query.

The browser-only panel excluded Google's mobile app. Its result cannot be compared directly with the 59.7% European Union estimate in [SparkToro's 2024 analysis of panels from Datos](https://sparktoro.com/blog/2024-zero-click-search-study/), a browsing-data provider, because the coverage differs.

Zero-click behavior does not prove that agents have taken over. Only 0.34% of searches in the early-2026 panel reached AI Mode, Google's conversational search interface. Yet [Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/), a nonpartisan US research organization, found a related pattern in the March 2025 browsing of 900 American adults. Traditional-result clicks appeared in 8% of Google visits with an AI summary and 15% without one; a link inside the summary received a click in only 1% of visits with a summary.

The interface is moving too. A **generative user interface**, or GenUI, creates a screen for a particular request; Google demonstrated this with its Gemini 3 AI model in [November 2025](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/gemini/gemini-3-gemini-app/). An **AI agent** is software that takes several steps toward a goal. At Google Cloud Next, its enterprise technology conference, Google announced the open **Agent2Agent protocol**, or A2A, on [April 9, 2025](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/a2a-a-new-era-of-agent-interoperability/) with more than 50 technology partners. A2A lets separately built agents exchange information and coordinate actions, while each organization controls what its agents may see and do.

I don't know how quickly people will trust agents with expensive or regulated decisions. I think the direction is clear enough to act on: discovery is moving from page to answer, while software is beginning to move from answer to action.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Zero-click search is widespread, but direct use of Google's AI Mode was still small in early 2026. The evidence supports a shift away from fixed pages, not a claim that agent adoption is complete.</p></div>

## A knowledge catalog closes the gap

Phil Risher, owner of [Phlash Consulting](https://youtu.be/xAIeA3ewRbo), a marketing firm for local-service businesses, described a **knowledge catalog** in June 2026 as “a verified data set of information about your business.” [Google Cloud, Google's enterprise cloud division](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/data-analytics/introducing-the-google-cloud-knowledge-catalog), introduced a narrower product with the same name in April 2026. Here the term means a broader, vendor-neutral operating model, not a required product or an industry standard.

A company knowledge catalog is a governed body of facts, pricing rules, policies, credentials and approved answers. It links every claim to its source, owner, market, effective date and permitted use, so meaning does not disappear when the answer leaves its page.

The boundaries matter. A product information management system can remain the source for product facts, while a content management system holds approved explanations. The catalog connects those sources to each claim and its limits. Separate identity and policy controls govern access and action, delivery software supplies the answer, and an audit record preserves what was used. The catalog does not inherit those jobs.

Suppose one current model meets a Swedish product-safety rule. The catalog links that claim to the model, Sweden, its evidence and expiry date. A policy control can reject its use for Norway or an older model, while the audit record preserves the version used. A fact can be correct and still become unsafe when software loses its boundaries.

This model is falsifiable. Set a limit for **update lag**, the time between an approved change and a consistent answer across interfaces you own. Measure an **exception rate** with fixed tests for stale, conflicting, wrong-market and unauthorized answers. These are proposed management measures, not industry standards. If existing systems stay within your limits, no separate catalog is necessary.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>A knowledge catalog preserves an approved claim together with its source, owner and limits. It earns a place only when existing systems cannot do that reliably.</p></div>

## Structured content creates the head start

**Structured content** stores meaning in named, reusable fields instead of burying it inside finished page copy. It is broader than **web structured data**, often called schema markup, which adds machine-readable labels to information on a webpage. In [May 2025 guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/05/succeeding-in-ai-search), Google Search Central, the company's documentation for website owners, said its systems consider structured data and urged publishers to create unique, non-commodity content. Machines need clear fields, but the knowledge inside them must still be worth using.

A **headless content management system**, or headless CMS, stores content separately from website design and sends it to different interfaces through software connections. I think organizations with mature content models, named owners and reliable update processes have a substantial head start because the catalog's raw material already exists. That is an architectural judgment, not a measured market result. A headless CMS filled with generic or poorly governed content merely moves ambiguity faster.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Structured content makes approved knowledge reusable beyond the webpage. A mature headless CMS helps only when clear ownership and distinctive business knowledge already sit behind it.</p></div>

## The Digital Singularity has reached the interface

[*The Digital Singularity Shift*](/books/the-digital-singularity-shift/), my 2024 book about AI making functional differences easier to copy, argues that meaning, story and belief become the remaining battleground. I think GenUI extends that pressure to the frontend. When software can assemble a useful interface for each request, polished presentation becomes cheaper to reproduce while distinctive knowledge and the trust attached to it remain harder to copy.

This changes the role of content. A webpage is one presentation of what the company knows, not the asset itself. The durable edge comes from preserving meaning when the same claim appears in search, an assistant, a sales tool or an interface that did not exist when the content was approved.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>AI can increasingly generate the presentation layer on demand. The more interfaces become temporary, the more valuable verified meaning becomes.</p></div>

The mapping is the separation between interface and authority. A spoken request may replace a menu, but the computer still needs current records and rules governing access and action. A knowledge catalog creates that connection for a company's approved answers without pretending to control outside AI services.

## Start with a test, not a platform

My take is to run a six-week pilot around one important answer, one market and one interface you own. On a one-page charter, name a permanent business owner for meaning and upkeep, a technology owner for connections and delivery, and a risk owner for permissions. Review results weekly and set the success limits and scale-or-stop date in advance. If the work scales, the business owner sets the review cadence and retires expired claims.

Test ordinary questions alongside stale, conflicting, out-of-market and unauthorized cases, recording the source and version behind each response. A small pilot cannot predict every agent failure. There is no measured Swedish or Norwegian enterprise case in this research, so the pilot must create local evidence rather than invented certainty.

The consultancy that built your content platform may be the right place to start because it knows your models and connections. Candidates include [Valtech](https://www.valtech.com/), a global business-transformation agency, or independent partners in the Contentful, Sanity and Optimizely platform ecosystems. These are examples, not endorsements. Ask bidders to disclose license-resale incentives, grant full export rights and prove why another platform is needed.

Keep rankings, then add two measures for outside AI services. **Mention rate** is the share of fixed questions whose answers name your company. **AI share of voice** is your share of mentions across fixed competitors. Neither proves revenue or control over an external model, so connect both to corrections, escalations and qualified inquiries.

I think leaders should start building the capability now, even when the first result is proof that current systems are enough. The website can remain; the strategic center must move to the knowledge that survives when the interface changes.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Measure the answer-control gap with a bounded pilot, assign lasting ownership and buy no unnecessary layer. Start building the knowledge-catalog capability now.</p></div>
