Your website is becoming irrelevant as your digital hub. It remains useful for trust, yet buyers increasingly receive answers before they visit and agents can carry requests into company systems.

I think the asset moving upstream is governed company knowledge: facts software can retrieve, trace to evidence and use within rules you control.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The website will keep a role, while verified company knowledge becomes the foundation for discovery and service across AI systems.</p></div>

## Search becomes an answer, then an action

A [SparkToro and Datos study](https://sparktoro.com/blog/2024-zero-click-search-study/) found that 58.5% of US Google searches and 59.7% of EU searches ended without a click in 2024. Because that includes ended and revised searches, it does not isolate AI. [Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/) observed traditional-result clicks in 8% of visits with an AI summary, versus 15% without one.

Answers reduce visits; agents carry the next request into a business system. At Cloud Next ’25, Google announced its [Agent2Agent protocol, or A2A](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/a2a-a-new-era-of-agent-interoperability/), so agents can exchange information and coordinate work across applications.

A buyer’s agent could ask whether a valve meets a temperature requirement and is available. A2A can standardize the exchange, but the supplier’s system must control identity, access, evidence and approval. I don’t know whether this becomes normal in two years or six, but I think leaders already need to decide which answers and actions their company will authorize.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>AI answers shorten the path from question to response, while agent protocols can extend that path into company systems under rules the company must design.</p></div>

## A knowledge catalog makes claims usable

[Phil Risher describes this foundation as a knowledge catalog](https://youtu.be/xAIeA3ewRbo). I use the term for a governed set of approved company claims. Product systems and content platforms remain the sources. The catalog links each claim to evidence, an owner, a review date, access policy and audit record.

For the valve, it would store the approved operating temperature, certificate, owner and expiry date. If the certificate has expired, the system should return “cannot confirm” and route the question to a person. I think every automated answer should be defensible when it is given.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>A knowledge catalog connects approved claims to evidence, ownership, freshness and permission, so software can answer safely and fail closed when proof is missing.</p></div>

## Structured content creates the head start

Structured content stores facts in named fields outside the page design. An “operating temperature” field lets the website and an agent retrieve the same approved value. Machine-readable means software can recognize that field and value reliably.

[Google recommends structured data](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/05/succeeding-in-ai-search) as a way to share information its systems can consider. Risher’s phrase “training data for the future internet” is a useful metaphor. In the near term, fund a current, approved source that AI retrieves while answering. Model training is a separate, less controllable use of public content.

A headless CMS separates reusable content from presentation. I think sound content models and approval workflows provide a practical head start because existing fields can be extended. Structure supplies the raw material; governance makes it dependable.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Structured content turns page-bound information into reusable fields, while a governed headless CMS supplies much of the raw material for an agent-ready catalog.</p></div>

## Generated interfaces move value upstream

Google’s [A2UI work](https://developers.googleblog.com/a2ui-v0-9-generative-ui/) illustrates generative UI: an agent assembles an interface for a specific request in real time. Think of the holodeck in *Star Trek*: the visible room changes with the mission, while reliable knowledge and firm rules determine what the environment can safely do.

*[The Digital Singularity Shift](/books/the-digital-singularity-shift/)* predicted that cheap AI creation would erode functional differences and move value toward meaning and belief. Generated interfaces make that prediction concrete because another company’s agent may become the first place a customer sees your offer. Such an interface needs checked facts and clear limits to represent your company responsibly.

The website still gives the brand a place it controls directly. Investment must also reach beneath it, into the claims and rules every interface may need.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>As interfaces become generated and distributed, value moves toward verified knowledge and the authority rules behind each customer interaction.</p></div>

## Invest through a controlled pilot

The consultancy behind your content platform may be a strong candidate because it knows your workflows. My take is to let evidence decide. Require proven headless-CMS implementation capability and knowledge governance that assigns a source, owner and review process to every exposed claim.

The contract should give your company full export rights, name the data location and enforce access controls. Fees should be independent of platform incentives. Require a tested handover in which your team operates, exports and restores a sample. Turn each requirement into a pilot acceptance test, creating a direct bridge from supplier promises to working proof.

Run a four-week pilot around one consequential question and one source system. Name an executive owner for the outcome, a content owner for claim quality and a platform owner for system controls. Budget one day a week for each of them, with scheduled security and legal reviews.

Record time to an evidence-backed answer, the rate blocked by expired proof and a related business outcome, such as qualified enquiries or resolved service requests. Expand when verification becomes faster, access can be revoked and the team can roll back safely.

Track mention rate, how often the company appears in answers to fixed buyer questions, and AI share of voice, its portion of mentions against named competitors. Treat both as directional visibility measures alongside business results.

One board-level control should remain firm: an agent may retrieve an approved claim, while any commercial or contractual commitment requires an accountable human. I think leaders should invest here now. When the next buyer-facing system asks on a customer’s behalf, your company should return a checked answer while retaining approval over every consequential commitment.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Choose a builder through evidence, test one governed decision with named owners, and expand only when the company can retrieve checked answers while retaining control of consequential commitments.</p></div>
