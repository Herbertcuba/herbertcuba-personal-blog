Your website is becoming irrelevant as the center of your digital business. It can still explain products and close business, but customers will increasingly form decisions elsewhere.

I think traffic loss is the visible problem. The deeper problem is the **answer-control gap**: the distance between an answer your company has approved and the answer a search service or AI assistant gives a customer.

The test is simple. If current systems keep approved answers accurate, current and within scope across every interface you control, add nothing. If they cannot, start building a knowledge catalog.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The website is becoming one interface among many. The strategic risk is the gap between what the business has approved and what customers are told.</p></div>

## The answer is leaving the page

[SparkToro](https://sparktoro.com/blog/in-2026-less-than-one-third-of-google-searches-still-send-a-click/), an audience-research company, used a US desktop-and-mobile web panel from Similarweb, a digital-data company, to estimate that 68.01% of Google searches from January through April 2026 ended without a click. A **zero-click search** ends without the user opening a result, whether the user found an answer, gave up or changed the query.

The panel excluded Google's mobile app. It is not directly comparable with SparkToro's [2024 estimate](https://sparktoro.com/blog/2024-zero-click-search-study/) of 59.7% for the European Union because the provider and coverage changed. Only 0.34% of searches in the 2026 panel reached AI Mode, Google's conversational search interface, so zero-click behavior does not prove AI or agent adoption.

AI answers still change behavior where they appear. [Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/), a nonpartisan US research organization, studied March 2025 browsing activity from 900 American adults. Traditional-result clicks occurred in 8% of Google visits with an AI summary and 15% without one. A source inside the summary received a click in only 1% of visits with a summary.

The interface is changing too. A **generative user interface**, or GenUI, creates a screen for a specific request; Google demonstrated one with its Gemini 3 AI model in [November 2025](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/gemini/gemini-3-gemini-app/). An **AI agent** is software that takes several steps toward a goal. On [April 9, 2025](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/a2a-a-new-era-of-agent-interoperability/), Google announced the open **Agent2Agent protocol**, or A2A, with more than 50 technology partners. A2A lets separately built agents exchange information and coordinate actions, while access and authority remain with the participating organizations.

I don't know how quickly people will trust agents with expensive or regulated decisions. The direction is clear enough to prepare for: discovery is moving from page to answer, and software is beginning to move from answer to action.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Zero-click search is widespread, while use of Google's AI Mode was still small in early 2026. The evidence supports a shift away from fixed pages, not a claim that agent adoption is complete.</p></div>

## What a knowledge catalog actually is

Phil Risher, owner of [Phlash Consulting](https://youtu.be/xAIeA3ewRbo), a marketing firm for local-service businesses, described a **knowledge catalog** in June 2026 as “a verified data set of information about your business.” Google Cloud, Google's enterprise cloud business, introduced [a narrower product with the same name](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/data-analytics/introducing-the-google-cloud-knowledge-catalog) in April 2026. I think the useful executive model is broader and vendor-neutral.

A company knowledge catalog is a governed body of facts, pricing rules, policies, credentials and approved answers. It keeps each claim connected to its source, owner, market, effective date and permitted use. A statement can be correct for one product and country, yet become unsafe when an AI system repeats it for another.

This does not require a new database. Product and content systems can remain the sources. The catalog preserves each claim and its limits; identity and policy controls decide who may see or do what; delivery software supplies the answer; and an audit record preserves what happened. Keeping those jobs separate makes accountability visible.

I think the model should be falsifiable. Measure **update lag**, the time between an approved change and a consistent answer across interfaces you own. Define an **exception rate** as the share of fixed tests that return stale, conflicting, wrong-market or unauthorized answers. If existing systems stay within limits set by the business, a separate catalog is unnecessary.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>A knowledge catalog preserves an approved claim together with its source, owner and limits. It earns a place only when existing systems cannot do that reliably.</p></div>

## Structured content creates the head start

**Structured content** stores meaning in named, reusable fields instead of burying it inside finished page copy. It is broader than **web structured data**, often called schema markup, which adds machine-readable labels to a webpage. In [May 2025 guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/05/succeeding-in-ai-search), Google Search Central, the company's documentation for website owners, said its systems consider structured data and urged publishers to create unique, non-commodity content. Machines need clear fields, but the knowledge must still be worth using.

A **headless content management system**, or headless CMS, stores content separately from website design and sends it to different interfaces through software connections. I think organizations with mature content models, named owners and dependable update processes have a substantial head start because the catalog's raw material already exists. That is an architectural judgment, not a measured market result. A headless CMS filled with generic or poorly governed content only moves ambiguity faster.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Structured content makes approved knowledge reusable beyond the webpage. A mature headless CMS helps only when clear ownership and distinctive business knowledge sit behind it.</p></div>

## The Digital Singularity reaches the interface

[*The Digital Singularity Shift*](/books/the-digital-singularity-shift/), my 2024 book about AI making functional differences easier to copy, argues that meaning, story and belief become the remaining battleground. I think GenUI extends that pressure to the customer-facing interface. When software assembles an interface for each request, polished presentation becomes cheaper to reproduce while distinctive knowledge and the trust attached to it remain harder to copy.

A webpage is therefore one presentation of what the company knows, not the asset itself. The durable edge comes from preserving meaning when the same claim appears in search, an assistant, a sales tool or an interface that did not exist when the content was approved.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>AI can increasingly generate what customers see on demand. As interfaces become temporary, verified meaning becomes more valuable.</p></div>

The mapping is the separation between interface and authority. A spoken request may replace a menu, but the computer still needs current records and rules for access and action. A knowledge catalog creates that connection for approved answers without claiming control over outside AI services.

## Start with one controlled answer

My take is a six-week pilot around one approved answer, one market and one interface you own. The one-page charter names a lasting business owner, a delivery owner and a risk owner; sets a weekly review, test limits and a scale-or-stop date; and includes stale, conflicting, out-of-market and unauthorized cases. Continued work needs funded reviews and a rule for retiring expired claims.

This research contains no measured Swedish or Norwegian enterprise case with a published baseline and outcome. The pilot must therefore create local evidence.

Start with the consultancy that built your content platform rather than the platform vendor itself. [Valtech](https://www.valtech.com/), a global business-transformation agency, and agency or solution partners working with Contentful, Sanity and Optimizely, three headless-CMS vendors, are examples rather than endorsements. Do not assume independence from a partner badge. Require disclosure of license-resale incentives, full export rights and a clear reason for any new platform.

Keep rankings, then add two bounded measures for outside AI services. **Mention rate** is the share of fixed questions whose answers name your company. **AI share of voice** is your share of mentions across fixed competitors. Neither proves revenue or control over an external model, so connect both to corrections, escalations and qualified inquiries.

I think leaders should start building this capability now, even when the first result is proof that current systems are enough. The website can remain, but the strategic center must move to the knowledge that survives when the interface changes.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Measure the answer-control gap with a bounded pilot, assign lasting ownership and buy no unnecessary layer. Start building the knowledge-catalog capability now.</p></div>
