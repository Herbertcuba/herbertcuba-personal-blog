Picture a facilities director asking an AI assistant to find an EV-charging supplier for 20 offices. The assistant checks coverage and requests a site assessment. A supplier can lose that opportunity before its homepage appears.

I think the website is starting to lose its role as a required stop in this journey. It will still carry brand and proof. More of the decision now happens inside the answer.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Customers can reach decisions without a click. Business facts need to travel without the page.</p></div>

## Search is becoming a conversation

Pew found that traditional links were clicked about half as often when an AI summary appeared.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Search answers more questions itself, so visibility often begins before a company page opens.</p></div>

[SparkToro and Datos found](https://sparktoro.com/blog/2024-zero-click-search-study/) that 58.5% of US Google searches and 59.7% of EU searches ended without a click in 2024. Their measure also counts stopped or changed searches. [Pew's March 2025 sample](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/) isolates AI summaries more clearly: traditional links received clicks in 8% of visits with a summary and 15% without one. Cited sources received clicks in 1%.

Our facilities director may get a shortlist from one conversation. I think visibility now starts before the visit.

## Agents can continue past the answer

Google launched its open Agent2Agent protocol in April 2025 with support from more than 50 technology partners.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>A2A lets agents coordinate across systems. Discovery can then lead to a business action.</p></div>

[Google says A2A](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/a2a-a-new-era-of-agent-interoperability/) lets agents communicate and coordinate actions across enterprise applications. [ServiceTitan already says](https://www.servicetitan.com/blog/webinar-recap-ai-virtual-agents-call-booking) its AI virtual agents can book or reschedule field-service jobs.

Our buyer's agent could ask about coverage and the next assessment slot. I don't know how quickly leaders will delegate high-value purchases. Payment and liability remain hard, and each application still owns its authorization rules.

## A knowledge catalog sits underneath the answer

[Phil Risher uses "knowledge catalog"](https://youtu.be/xAIeA3ewRbo) for a verified dataset about a business that an agent can use.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>A knowledge catalog gives machines maintained business facts with ownership, review dates and limits on use.</p></div>

For the EV supplier, it might contain service areas, capacity, certifications and booking rules. Operations supplies the coverage answer. Compliance owns the certification record. Commercial owners approve pricing claims.

I think those boundaries are as important as the facts. Expired records need a human check, and requests outside the agent's authority stop. This is an emerging practice; Google does not define it as a product category in the cited sources.

## Structured content lets the facts travel

Google tells site owners that structured data shares content information in a machine-readable way.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Structured content lets one approved answer serve search, agents and generated interfaces.</p></div>

[Google's guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/05/succeeding-in-ai-search) also asks for unique, useful content. Structure makes the supplier's coverage readable to a machine; specific company knowledge gives the answer value.

Google's [A2UI project](https://developers.googleblog.com/introducing-a2ui-an-open-project-for-agent-driven-interfaces/) lets agents send an interface suited to the conversation. Like *The Matrix*, the surface can be generated for the moment while data beneath determines what appears. One approved coverage record can feed search, an assistant and a booking card.

## Headless CMS gives you a head start

A headless CMS already separates content from presentation and delivers it through APIs.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Mature content models may already hold part of the catalog. Agent actions still need separate controls.</p></div>

If the supplier stores services, regions and certifications as fields, the extraction work is smaller. The same separation underpins [The Quiet Truth About Headless CMS](/posts/the-quiet-truth-about-headless-cms/).

The CMS can supply modeled content. Live capacity may come from operations, while authorization belongs in the agent layer. [Sanity's Agent Actions](https://www.sanity.io/blog/agent-actions-ai-building-blocks-for-structured-content) and [Contentful's Remote MCP](https://www.contentful.com/blog/remote-mcp-now-generally-available/) already connect agents to structured content workflows. I think disciplined content models provide a head start.

## The Digital Singularity is showing up in infrastructure

[The Digital Singularity Shift](/books/the-digital-singularity-shift/) argues that AI collapses functional differentiation toward zero and shifts competition toward meaning, story and belief.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Agents make functional comparison cheaper. Companies still need distinct meaning, while their factual claims must become easier for machines to verify.</p></div>

When an agent compares suppliers in seconds, every functional claim needs a trustworthy source.

I think brand remains important for a different reason. The catalog helps the facilities director establish whether the supplier can do the work. The website can show why that supplier deserves belief.

## Choose help by the shape of the problem

Our EV supplier's catalog reaches beyond the CMS as soon as capacity and booking enter the journey.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Your content-platform consultancy may be well placed to extend it. Independence helps when the catalog crosses products.</p></div>

A platform specialist can move quickly inside one CMS. A broader catalog reaches several systems. Options include [Valtech](https://www.valtech.com/) and independent specialists in the [Contentful](https://www.contentful.com/partners/), [Sanity](https://www.sanity.io/agency-partners) and [Optimizely](https://www.optimizely.com/partners/) partner networks.

My take is to test incentives before credentials. Ask who owns the schema, whether every record is exportable and how access is revoked. The consultancy should be able to succeed without increasing your license footprint.

## Start with one customer journey

Twenty recurring buyer questions are enough for a useful first test.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Choose one buying journey, model its recurring questions and test the answers against approved sources. Add AI visibility measures beside existing search metrics.</p></div>

Use the facilities request. Write down 20 buyer questions about coverage, credentials, capacity and booking. Give every answer an owner, source and review date. Keep the first release read-only and compare its replies with approved answers.

Keep rankings, then add mention rate and AI share of voice. Optimizely already offers a [Competitive AI Share of Voice Agent](https://www.optimizely.com/agents/competitive-ai-share-of-voice-agent).

When an agent asks whether your company can serve 20 sites, it should receive an answer your business can still defend tomorrow. Build that before the next homepage redesign.
