Your website is becoming irrelevant to a growing share of customer decisions. It will still explain products, support customers and close business, but the answer that shapes a decision may now appear in Google, an AI assistant or an agent before the customer sees a page.

The deeper risk is loss of control. A company can update an approved fact while older, conflicting or out-of-scope answers continue to circulate elsewhere. I think leaders should call this the **answer-control gap**: the measurable distance between what the company has approved and what a customer receives.

Measure how long one changed fact takes to reach every interface you own, then test how often those interfaces return a stale, conflicting or unauthorized answer. If the current stack stays within the limits you set, buy nothing. If it cannot, the missing capability is a governed knowledge catalog.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The website is becoming one interface among many. The strategic issue is whether approved company knowledge remains accurate and within scope when the interface changes.</p></div>

## The answer is leaving the page

The shift is measurable, although the headline needs boundaries. [SparkToro, an audience-research company, and Similarweb, a digital-behavior measurement company](https://sparktoro.com/blog/in-2026-less-than-one-third-of-google-searches-still-send-a-click/), reported in June 2026 that 68.01% of Google searches in Similarweb's US desktop-and-mobile web panel ended without a click from January through April. A **zero-click search** ends before the user opens a result. The estimate excludes Google's mobile app, uses a weighted desktop-mobile split and is not directly comparable with an earlier panel from Datos, a browsing-data provider, which estimated 59.7% zero-click searches in the European Union in 2024.

Only 0.34% of searches in the early-2026 panel reached AI Mode, Google's conversational search interface, so zero-click behavior is not proof that agents have taken over. Yet [Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/), a nonpartisan American research organization, found the same direction in the Google-search browsing of 900 US adults in March 2025. People clicked a traditional result in 8% of visits with an AI summary and 15% without one, while cited sources received a click in only 1% of visits with a summary. Pew found summaries on 60% of queries beginning with “who,” “what,” “when” or “why.” Search shaped like a conversation is more likely to end as an answer rather than a visit.

The interface and the actor are changing together. A **generative user interface**, or GenUI, builds a screen for a specific request instead of selecting a finished page; Google demonstrated this with its Gemini 3 AI model in [November 2025](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/gemini/gemini-3-gemini-app/). An **AI agent** is software that works through several steps toward a goal. At Google Cloud Next, Google's enterprise technology conference, on [April 9, 2025](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/a2a-a-new-era-of-agent-interoperability/), the company announced the open Agent2Agent protocol, or **A2A**, with more than 50 technology partners. A protocol is an agreed communication method. A2A lets separately built agents exchange information and coordinate work, but the receiving company still decides what an agent may see or do.

I don't know how quickly customers will trust agents with expensive or regulated decisions. I think the direction is clear enough to act on because interfaces are changing while the company's responsibility for accuracy remains where it was.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Zero-click behavior is widespread, while direct use of Google's AI Mode was still small in early 2026. Agent adoption remains uncertain, but search answers, generated interfaces and A2A are already separating customer journeys from fixed pages.</p></div>

## A knowledge catalog governs the answer

[Phil Risher of Phlash Consulting](https://youtu.be/xAIeA3ewRbo), a digital-marketing firm serving local-service businesses, described a **knowledge catalog** in June 2026 as “a verified data set of information about your business.” [Google Cloud also announced a product named Knowledge Catalog](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/data-analytics/introducing-the-google-cloud-knowledge-catalog) in April 2026 as the successor to its Dataplex data-governance service, aimed at organizing enterprise data for people and AI agents. Risher's broader business definition is not Google's product definition.

I use the term here as a vendor-neutral operating model for approved company knowledge: product claims, pricing rules, policies, credentials and answers to real customer questions, each connected to a source, owner, market, effective date and permitted use. A product information management system can remain the source for product attributes, a content management system for published explanations, a data catalog for locating datasets and a knowledge graph for recording relationships. The knowledge catalog adds value only when those systems cannot preserve one claim's source and limits across interfaces.

The responsibility should stay divided:

| Layer | Responsibility | Accountable owner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Source systems | Hold the authoritative product, policy or content record | Business owner |
| Knowledge catalog | Index the approved claim, source, scope, version and status | Knowledge owner |
| Identity and policy controls | Decide who may retrieve information or request an action | Security and risk owner |
| Serving interface | Retrieve and present the record | Technology owner |
| Audit record | Preserve the version, access decision and output | Control owner |

The catalog does not authenticate a user, grant permission or compose the final answer. Imagine a manufacturer with evidence that one current product meets a rule in Sweden. The catalog preserves the model, market, evidence, dates and approved wording; policy controls prevent an assistant from extending the claim to Norway or to an older model; and the audit record shows which version produced the answer.

A true fact can still become an unsafe answer when software loses its boundaries. Measure **update lag**, the time from an approved source change to a consistent answer across owned interfaces, and **exception rate**, the share of a fixed test set that returns a stale, conflicting, wrong-market or unauthorized answer. These are proposed management measures, not industry standards. They make the answer-control gap concrete enough to improve or disprove.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>A vendor-neutral knowledge catalog connects an approved claim to its source, owner, scope and permitted use. It adds value only where existing systems fail to preserve that context, and it must remain separate from identity, policy, delivery and audit responsibilities.</p></div>

## Structured content is the head start

**Structured content** stores meaning in named, reusable fields instead of burying it inside finished page copy. This is broader than **web structured data**, also called schema markup, which adds machine-readable labels to information visible on a webpage. In [May 2025 guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/05/succeeding-in-ai-search), Google Search Central, the company's documentation for website owners, said its systems consider structured data and urged publishers to create unique, non-commodity content. Clear labels help machines read information, but proprietary knowledge and disciplined ownership make it worth reading.

A **headless content management system**, or headless CMS, stores content separately from website design and delivers it through software interfaces. I think organizations with mature content models, clear owners and reliable connections have a substantial head start because much of the catalog's raw material already exists. That is an architectural judgment, not a measured market result. It also explains the relevance of [*The Digital Singularity Shift*](/books/the-digital-singularity-shift/), my 2024 book about AI making functional differences easier to copy. Its thesis is that “meaning, story, and belief” become the remaining battleground. Generated interfaces make presentation easier to reproduce, while distinctive knowledge and the trust attached to it remain harder to copy.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Structured content preserves meaning beyond one page. A mature headless CMS supplies useful raw material, but only distinctive knowledge, clear ownership and reliable updates turn that material into a durable advantage.</p></div>

The mapping is the separation between interface and authority. The computer can change how it presents an answer, but it still needs current records and rules about who may retrieve or act on them. A knowledge catalog gives a company that governed connection without pretending it controls every outside AI system.

## Build proof before you buy a platform

The evidence assembled for this article contains no measured Swedish or Norwegian enterprise case with a published baseline and outcome. US search behavior and global technology releases show direction, not what your organization will achieve. My take is to create local evidence with a six-week pilot around one important answer, one market and one interface the company owns.

Name a permanent business owner, record the current update lag and exception rate, then test ordinary questions alongside questions designed to elicit stale, conflicting, unsupported or unauthorized answers. Review results weekly with content, technology and risk owners. Set the budget ceiling, success limits and scale-or-stop date before work begins, and retire a claim as soon as its source or approval expires.

External AI services should not decide whether the pilot passes because the company does not control them. Monitor them instead. **Mention rate** is the share of fixed questions whose answers name the company; **AI share of voice** is the company's share of mentions across a fixed competitor set. Keep rankings, but connect these new measures to corrections, escalations and qualified inquiries rather than treating visibility as revenue.

The consultancy that built your content platform may be the best place to start because it already knows the models and connections. A global digital consultancy such as Valtech, or an independent implementation partner experienced with Contentful, Sanity or Optimizely content platforms, may have the right skills. These are examples, not endorsements. Require bidders to disclose license-resale incentives, work with your current systems, grant full export rights and prove that another platform is necessary. Product expertise is useful; a sales target is not an architecture.

The stopping condition is simple. If existing systems move an approved fact across owned interfaces within the target time and preserve its limits, formalize that model and stop. If they cannot, start building the knowledge catalog now. The website can remain, but the verified knowledge behind it has to become the center.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Use a bounded six-week pilot to create local evidence, keep permanent ownership with the business and select help through a vendor-neutral test. Build no new layer when existing systems already close the answer-control gap. When they do not, invest in the governed knowledge behind every interface.</p></div>
