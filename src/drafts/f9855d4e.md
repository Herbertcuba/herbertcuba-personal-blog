Your website is becoming irrelevant to more business decisions.

It will still reassure people. Yet [Phil Risher's argument](https://youtu.be/xAIeA3ewRbo) points to a different hub. Consider a facilities director sourcing EV chargers for 20 offices. An AI assistant compares coverage and certifications, then requests a site assessment. The supplier's homepage may never appear.

I think visibility now begins inside the answer, before a visit and sometimes without one.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Websites still serve people. More discovery now happens inside AI systems, so companies need a second front door for software.</p></div>

## Search moved from keywords to conversations

[SparkToro and Datos](https://sparktoro.com/blog/2024-zero-click-search-study/) found that 58.5% of US Google searches ended without a click in 2024. The figure predates the agent push and includes sessions that ended or changed course.

[Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/) later found an 8% click rate for traditional results when an AI summary appeared, compared with 15% without one. Sources inside the summary received clicks in 1% of those visits. Our facilities director can get a shortlist before any supplier page appears. I think that behaviour is large enough to affect strategy.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Zero-click search came before the current agent wave. AI summaries now move more of the decision into the answer.</p></div>

## Agents turn answers into action

[Google launched Agent2Agent](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/a2a-a-new-era-of-agent-interoperability/) in April 2025 with support from more than 50 partners. The open protocol lets agents exchange information and coordinate work. Purchasing authority stays with the surrounding business systems and people.

In *Arrival*, two sides progress once they share a language. A2A gives software the mundane version. The facilities director's assistant could ask a supplier agent about coverage, while a person approves the request. I don't know whether 2027 becomes the year agents enter everyday purchasing. The protocol is a better signal than a slogan.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>A2A gives agents a common language for information and work. Enterprise authority and liability remain human choices.</p></div>

## A knowledge catalog in plain English

A knowledge catalog is a name for your company's approved facts in a form machines can query. Google has no verified product with that label, so I use it as a business concept.

For the EV supplier, one record could hold service coverage, required certifications, its source and owner. It would show the review date and define the action an agent may take. Stale capacity data sends the request to a person before any date is confirmed. I think verified knowledge beats a large pile of content.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>A knowledge catalog is a governed set of business facts for machines. Each claim needs provenance, ownership and a limit on agent action.</p></div>

## Structured content makes knowledge usable

Website copy traps several facts inside one paragraph. An agent must infer which service area is current and whether a certification applies. Structured content gives those facts separate fields. [Google's guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/05/succeeding-in-ai-search) calls this a machine-readable way to share information with its systems.

Risher describes content as "training data for the future internet." The phrase is a strategic metaphor. Pages may be retrieved or cited without becoming model training data. I think structured content turns corporate claims into reliable inputs.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Agents handle explicit fields better than facts buried in prose. Structure gives each claim a stable meaning and makes review possible.</p></div>

## Headless CMS gives you a head start

A headless CMS stores content as reusable fields and delivers it through APIs. Organisations using Sanity, Hygraph or Payload already have much of the modelling discipline a catalog requires.

In our EV example, the CMS owns service descriptions and policy. The operations system owns survey capacity. Both feed the answer. I think this foundation provides a genuine head start because the knowledge has already escaped the page.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>A headless CMS supplies reusable models and publishing governance. Current operational data turns that foundation into an agent-ready catalog.</p></div>

## Generative UI confirms the direction

[Google's A2UI work](https://developers.googleblog.com/a2ui-v0-9-generative-ui/) allows agents to generate interface widgets in real time. Our facilities director may see a comparison view made for the 20-office rollout inside the assistant already in use. The generated view makes the page temporary and raises the value of reliable facts.

*[The Digital Singularity Shift](/books/the-digital-singularity-shift/)* argues that AI collapses functional differentiation as creation becomes cheap, pushing value toward meaning and belief. Generative UI applies that pressure to the page. Any supplier can present a polished interface. Reliable knowledge and a reason to trust the company are harder to copy.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Generative interfaces make presentation easier to create for each moment. As function becomes abundant, trust and meaning carry more value.</p></div>

## Choose an independent builder

The consultancy that modernised your content platform is a place to start. It understands the schemas, workflows and ownership behind them.

One route is Eidra. Curamando can cover AI transformation, while Umain handles product and technology implementation. If your stack includes Hygraph or Payload, extend those assets without forcing a migration.

Require an exportable and auditable catalog. Make sure another team can operate it if either vendor changes. The commercial model should reward portability and business outcomes.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Use a consultancy that understands your platform and can work across the agent layer. Independence and portability should shape the contract.</p></div>

## Start with one controlled journey

Start with the EV site assessment. Give a small team four weeks to collect 20 likely questions. Assign an owner and source to each answer, then mark whether the agent may respond, request an assessment or escalate.

Release it in read-only mode. Review it monthly with content and operations, plus commercial and compliance owners. Name one executive who settles disputed facts.

Measure qualified assessments requested and time spent on manual verification. Add AI mention rate and share of voice to the visibility report, then connect both to a business outcome.

Your next buyer may arrive through software, and it will judge your company by the knowledge you made usable.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>One journey will expose gaps in ownership and freshness, plus limits on action. Build the catalog now, then expand it when the evidence earns trust.</p></div>
