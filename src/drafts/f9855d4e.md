Your website is becoming less important as the place where every digital customer journey begins.

It will still matter for complex offers, transactions and direct relationships. But customers no longer have to enter through the front door you designed. Search engines can answer their questions, while AI agents, software that can take approved steps on a customer’s behalf, can begin to act before a company page appears.

I think many leadership teams still treat the finished website as the asset and the information inside it as material for filling pages. That order is starting to reverse. As interfaces are generated for individual requests, the page becomes temporary while the company’s verified knowledge has to survive every interface.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The website is becoming one entrance to the business rather than the required entrance. This makes verified company knowledge more durable than any single design or channel.</p></div>

## Search is becoming an answer and an action

The movement away from website visits began before the current AI wave. In July 2024, [SparkToro, an audience-research company, and Datos, a browsing-data provider](https://sparktoro.com/blog/2024-zero-click-search-study/), estimated that 58.5% of US Google searches and 59.7% of EU searches ended without a click. These panel-based figures include sessions that ended and queries that were reformulated. They do not prove that AI caused the behavior, or that every searcher found a satisfactory answer, but they show that most measured searches did not produce a website visit.

AI-generated summaries make the next stage easier to see. [Pew Research Center, a nonpartisan US research organization, studied the Google browsing activity of 900 American adults in March 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/). People clicked a traditional result in 8% of visits when Google displayed an AI summary, compared with 15% when no summary appeared. They clicked a source cited inside the summary in only 1% of visits. Pew also found that about 60% of searches beginning with question words produced a summary. I think that is consistent with a broader movement from short keyword searches toward questions asked in ordinary language, although the study does not prove that historical shift by itself.

The next move is from answers to actions. At Google Cloud Next ’25, the company’s annual enterprise technology conference, Google launched [Agent2Agent, or A2A](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/a2a-a-new-era-of-agent-interoperability/), with support from more than 50 technology partners. A2A is an open set of rules that allows independently built AI agents to exchange information and coordinate work across business systems. A buying agent could ask a supplier’s agent about availability and terms, then prepare an order for a person to approve.

A common language between agents does not grant them authority. Each company still decides what an agent may see, what it may change and which actions require human approval. I don’t know how quickly people will delegate expensive or sensitive purchases because trust, liability and habit remain real barriers. An agent only needs to compare options, exclude a supplier or arrange the next step, however, to shape a decision before a website gets involved.

This changes what visibility means. Rankings remain useful, but I think leaders should add a controlled measure of mention rate, how often selected AI answers name the company, and AI share of voice, how those mentions compare with competitors across a fixed set of important questions. Vendors are already selling these measures, which gives them an incentive to promote the category. Establish a repeatable baseline and check the answers for accuracy; a large mention count with the wrong price or policy is not success.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Search already produces many journeys without a click, visits with AI summaries show fewer clicks, and A2A lets agents coordinate work across systems. Companies therefore need to be accurate inside answers and actions, not only prominent in a list of links.</p></div>

## A knowledge catalog gives each claim an owner

In a June 2026 video, [Phil Risher of the marketing consultancy Phlash Consulting](https://youtu.be/xAIeA3ewRbo) uses “knowledge catalog” to describe a verified set of business information that can be deployed through an agent. The Google materials reviewed for this article do not establish the phrase as an official Google product or standard. I think it is still a useful business concept if we define it without borrowing authority the term does not have.

A knowledge catalog is a company-owned body of approved information arranged so people and software can use a fact without guessing what it means, where it applies or who authorized it. It can cover products, services, pricing logic, policies, credentials and real customer questions. For every important claim, the company records an approved source, a business owner, the relevant product or market, a review date and whether the claim remains available for use.

Consider the promise “delivery within 24 hours.” On a conventional website, it may appear in campaign copy, a help article and several country pages with slightly different conditions. In a catalog, the promise is connected to the eligible products and markets, the approving policy, the responsible owner and the next review date. If the customer’s market is unknown, the safe response is to ask for it rather than quietly apply a US promise in Sweden.

This example reveals the difference between a catalog and a larger pile of content. When a commercial promise changes, the business can trace where it came from, withdraw it and test whether connected channels have stopped using it. The hypothesis is falsifiable: if this article’s argument is right, wrong AI answers and slow corrections will appear most often around claims that lack an owner, an approved source or clear conditions. Leadership can test that against its own customer-service cases rather than accept another prediction about the future.

Risher calls content “training data for the future internet.” The phrase captures the strategic importance of company knowledge, but it can mislead a business reader about the mechanism. AI systems can also retrieve information, meaning they look up external material while forming an answer rather than permanently teaching it to the underlying model. In both cases, the commercial challenge is similar: a fact needs to retain its meaning, scope and authority after it leaves the page where it was written.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>A knowledge catalog is a governed source of company facts rather than another collection of pages. It connects important claims to their conditions, approved sources, owners and current status so an answer can be used, challenged and corrected.</p></div>

## Structured content creates the practical head start

Structured content stores an idea in named parts instead of locking it inside the layout of a finished page. The delivery promise becomes connected information about time, market, eligible product, source policy and review date. Because each part keeps its meaning, one approved record can feed a website, a service tool or an agent without being copied into several conflicting versions.

AI systems can use unstructured documents too. Structure is valuable because it makes meaning, scope and ownership more explicit, which improves consistency and governance. It should also be distinguished from structured data: structured data is page-level markup that helps search systems identify what a page contains, while structured content is the reusable company-information model that exists beneath any one page.

Google’s own guidance supports the machine-readable direction. In May 2025, [Google Search Central, the company’s official guidance service for website owners](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/05/succeeding-in-ai-search/), said structured data is useful for sharing information in a machine-readable form its systems can consider. The same guidance emphasized original, useful content. Structure cannot make a generic claim distinctive or a stale claim true.

This is where a modern headless content management system can provide a practical head start. A headless CMS separates reusable content from the design of one website, then supplies it to sites, apps and internal tools through software connections. If a company has already modeled products, policies and approval flows this way, it holds useful raw material for a knowledge catalog. If the platform merely stores page-shaped copy, its architecture offers much less help.

The platform market is moving in the same direction. [Sanity](https://www.sanity.io/blog/agent-actions-ai-building-blocks-for-structured-content) and [Contentful](https://www.contentful.com/blog/remote-mcp-now-generally-available), two structured-content platform vendors, have announced ways for authorized AI tools to work with structured-content workflows. [Optimizely, a digital-experience platform provider](https://www.optimizely.com/agents/competitive-ai-share-of-voice-agent), offers a tool that reports visibility in AI answers. These are first-party product announcements from companies selling into the trend, not neutral measures of market adoption. They are useful because they show where the vendors themselves are investing.

My take is that the advantage belongs to organizations with reusable content, clear owners and working review processes, not simply to those that bought a headless system. The software can expose information, but the business still decides whether that information is approved, current and safe to use.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Structured content makes meaning, scope and ownership more explicit, while governance determines whether a fact can be trusted. A mature headless CMS supplies useful raw material for a knowledge catalog, but the platform alone does not create one.</p></div>

## Generated interfaces confirm the deeper shift

The 2024 book *[The Digital Singularity Shift](/books/the-digital-singularity-shift/)* argues that AI makes functional digital features easier to reproduce and moves value toward the meaning underneath them. The developments since then make that direction more concrete. AI answers weaken the compulsory click, agents begin to coordinate tasks, and generative user interfaces make the visible layer adaptable.

A generative user interface, often shortened to GenUI, is a screen assembled by AI for the request at hand rather than selected from a fixed path designed in advance. In November 2025, [Google’s Gemini 3 AI model introduced experiments that generated a custom interface from a user’s prompt](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/gemini/gemini-3-gemini-app/). In April 2026, Google released version 0.9 of [A2UI, an open proposal for agent-driven interfaces](https://developers.googleblog.com/a2ui-v0-9-generative-ui/). It lets an agent request suitable interface elements while the receiving application controls how those elements look and behave.

Think of the holodeck in *Star Trek: The Next Generation*, the science-fiction television series launched in 1987. The holodeck is a fictional room that renders a responsive simulated environment around its users. GenUI is a modest business version: one customer may receive a comparison and another a form, with each interface assembled around the immediate task. When the scenery can change on demand, the quality of the underlying world becomes decisive.

Design still matters because trust, accessibility and brand expression affect whether people use an interface. I think the strategic hierarchy changes, though. The front end becomes one expression of the business, while verified company knowledge has to work across many expressions. That is the part of *The Digital Singularity Shift* arriving now: as digital function becomes easier to generate, control over meaning becomes more valuable.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>GenUI makes the interface adaptable, which matches the direction described in <em>The Digital Singularity Shift</em>. Screens can change for every request, but the company facts beneath them still need stable meaning, ownership and authority.</p></div>

## Start with ten claims and an independent partner

The same consultancy that modeled and connected your current content may be well placed to extend it into a knowledge catalog. [Valtech, a global digital consultancy](https://www.valtech.com/), and implementation partners in the Contentful, Sanity or Optimizely ecosystems are examples of firms to evaluate, not endorsements. A Contentful, Sanity or Optimizely partner badge does not prove independence or delivery quality. The important question is whether the consultancy is rewarded for solving your information problem or for expanding one platform’s license footprint.

Group the supplier test around three decisions. First, ownership: your company should own the content model and be able to export the information in a usable form. Second, commercial independence: fees, platform incentives, data-residency choices, maintenance duties, handover and exit should be visible in the agreement. Third, access control: each employee and agent should receive only the information and authority needed for its task, and that access must be easy to withdraw. Agent-to-agent coordination never transfers responsibility away from the company.

A 30-day discovery exercise is enough to expose the real condition of your knowledge without pretending to prove the whole strategy. Choose one high-volume, high-risk set of facts, such as delivery promises, return policies or product eligibility. Select ten claims and record the approved wording, applicable market, source policy, business owner, review date, current status and withdrawal path. Then trace where they have been copied and find at least one stale, conflicting or unowned version.

Keep the first scorecard equally small. Count how many claims have an approved source and owner, how many are within their review date, and how often test questions return the correct answer for the correct market. Test whether a withdrawn fact disappears from the channels under your control. Rankings, AI mention rate and share of voice can sit beside those measures as visibility signals, but answer quality remains the standard.

This exercise is deliberately reversible. It reveals whether the existing content platform provides a real head start, where authority is missing and what a larger catalog would require before a major supplier commitment. If the evidence shows that current journeys still depend overwhelmingly on your website, or that unstructured retrieval answers just as reliably in your highest-risk use case, invest accordingly. But leadership should test those assumptions now rather than fund another redesign by habit.

The immediate board-level choice is whether to keep optimizing one interface or first establish control over the facts that every interface, employee and agent will reuse. I think the second investment lowers the risk of commercial misinformation, supplier lock-in and inconsistent customer service at the same time. The website is still part of the system, but governed knowledge is becoming the part the system cannot safely operate without.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Begin with a 30-day audit of ten valuable, risky claims and make their sources, owners, scope and withdrawal paths explicit. Choose a consultancy around ownership, independence, controlled access and tested exit, then expand only when the exercise proves that the company can keep its answers accurate.</p></div>
