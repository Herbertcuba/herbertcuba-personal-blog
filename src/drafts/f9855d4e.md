The most valuable part of your website may soon be the part no customer sees.

A website currently does two jobs. It presents an experience to people, and it holds the information that explains what a company offers, where it operates and under which conditions. The first job will remain useful, but it is no longer guaranteed to be the starting point. Search engines can answer on the results page, AI assistants can compare options, and software agents can pursue a customer’s goal without opening the company’s homepage.

The traffic data already weakens the old assumption. In 2024, [marketing research firm SparkToro and clickstream provider Datos found](https://sparktoro.com/blog/2024-zero-click-search-study/) that 58.5% of US Google searches and 59.7% of EU searches produced no click. That category included sessions that ended as well as searches that were reformulated, so it does not show that AI caused the behavior. The AI-specific evidence came later: [Pew Research Center tracked the browsing of 900 US adults](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/) in March 2025 and found that people clicked a traditional result on 8% of visits with a Google AI summary, compared with 15% without one. Links inside the summary received clicks on 1% of all visits to pages with such a summary.

I think the useful conclusion is more precise than “websites are dead.” A website is losing its privileged role as the digital hub. That makes the verified knowledge beneath it a strategic asset in its own right.

## AI is moving between the customer and the company

The important change is the unit of demand. A keyword asks a search engine for a list of possible destinations. A conversational request states a goal, adds constraints and expects a useful answer. An AI agent goes one step further: it is software that can pursue that goal and take only the steps for which it has been given authority.

Google supplied part of the coordination layer in April 2025 at Google Cloud Next ’25, its annual business-technology conference. [Agent2Agent, usually shortened to A2A](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/a2a-a-new-era-of-agent-interoperability/), is an open protocol that lets independently built AI agents communicate, securely exchange information and coordinate actions across business applications. Google announced it with contributions and support from more than 50 technology partners.

That protocol does not establish whether a supplier’s information is correct, nor does it give an agent permission to make a commercial commitment. It removes one obstacle to different systems working together. In a future purchasing journey, one agent could collect compatible options while another checks current availability, but an approved person or policy would still determine whether an order may be placed.

I don’t know how quickly customers will delegate expensive or sensitive decisions. I expect questions about commercial trust and liability, along with established habits, to slow some uses. I think the strategic signal is still clear: when software can interpret intent and coordinate the next step, being visible to a person on a results page is no longer enough.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Conversational requests express goals, and AI agents can coordinate authorized steps toward them. A2A makes that coordination easier, but companies still need trustworthy information and separate rules for what an agent may do.</p></div>

## A knowledge catalog gives the business a usable source

In a June 2026 video, [Phil Risher of Phlash Consulting](https://youtu.be/xAIeA3ewRbo) used “knowledge catalog” for a verified set of information about a business that AI systems can consume. It is a useful working term, not an official Google product or standard. I use it to mean a governed, machine-readable inventory of the facts a company is prepared to stand behind.

The inventory might cover products and services, market availability, pricing rules, policies, credentials, customer questions and approved evidence. Each important record also needs a source, an accountable owner and a review date. A warranty record, for example, should identify the relevant product, country, valid period and exceptions instead of leaving those conditions buried in several pages of prose.

“Verified” does not mean that every claim is permanently true. It means the claim can be traced to an approved source and reviewed when the business changes. The catalog can tell an AI system which warranty applies; a separate authorization policy decides whether an agent may quote that answer, open a case or approve a replacement. I think this distinction between knowledge and authority is essential for any serious implementation.

Risher’s phrase, “training data for the future internet,” works as a strategic metaphor. It should not be read as a claim that every page becomes part of an AI model’s permanent training. I think the practical requirement is simpler: publish proprietary knowledge so machines can find it, interpret it and connect it to a reliable source.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>A knowledge catalog is a governed inventory of company facts, not another website section. Its records need sources, owners and review dates, while separate controls define which actions an AI agent may take.</p></div>

## Structured content survives a changing interface

Most web pages mix meaning with presentation. Structured content separates that meaning into named fields and reusable records, such as a product name, market, price condition and effective date. Structured data adds labels another system can read when those facts leave the content platform. In May 2025, [Google Search Central advised site owners](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/05/succeeding-in-ai-search) that structured data helps provide information in a machine-readable form, while also stressing that the underlying content must be useful and original.

This is why a modern headless content management system can provide a practical head start. A headless CMS stores and manages content separately from the website’s visual presentation, then supplies it to different channels through controlled software connections. If its records are well structured and its ownership and review process are sound, much of the catalog’s raw material already exists. If the system contains stale, duplicated or generic copy, its architecture alone solves very little.

The interface is changing at the same time. A generative user interface, or GenUI, is a screen assembled by AI for the user’s current request rather than a page designed in advance for everyone. Google introduced prompt-generated interface experiments in the [Gemini 3 version of its AI assistant in November 2025](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/gemini/gemini-3-gemini-app/) and published [A2UI v0.9 in April 2026](https://developers.googleblog.com/a2ui-v0-9-generative-ui/), an open specification that lets agents describe an interface which the receiving application renders with its own components.

In my book [*The Digital Singularity Shift*](/books/the-digital-singularity-shift/), I argue that AI makes it harder to stand apart through what a product does and pushes business value toward meaning, story and belief. The current market direction fits that thesis. A polished interface will become easier to assemble for each moment, while complete proprietary knowledge and credible governance will remain difficult to reproduce.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Structured content preserves meaning outside a page, and a sound headless CMS can supply the starting architecture. As AI generates more of the interface, proprietary knowledge and governance carry more of the durable value.</p></div>

The mapping is specific: GenUI makes the visible screen more fluid, while the knowledge catalog becomes part of the underlying system that determines what can be shown, trusted and acted upon. A company that governs only the surface gives up influence when someone else generates the interface.

## Fund the knowledge layer before the next redesign

My take is to begin with one decision that matters, rather than attempting an enterprise-wide catalog. Choose a question for which a wrong answer has a clear cost, such as whether a product warranty applies in a particular market. Gather the approved facts, name the business owner, set a review cycle and define exactly what an agent may answer or initiate. Then expose that narrow domain through the existing content platform and test it against real customer questions.

Keep conventional search measures, but add measures for the new route to discovery. For one fixed set of questions, a company can define mention rate as the share of AI answers that name it, and AI share of voice as its mentions compared with a fixed competitor group. These are operating definitions, not universal standards, so the AI systems, languages, markets and time period must remain consistent. Productization is already visible: [digital-experience software company Optimizely lists an agent called Competitive AI Share of Voice](https://www.optimizely.com/agents/competitive-ai-share-of-voice-agent). That product’s existence does not prove that a mention creates qualified demand.

The consultancy that built the content platform may be the best place to start because it already knows how the records are structured and published. Possible firms to evaluate include [Valtech, a global digital consultancy](https://www.valtech.com/), and specialists in the partner networks of content-platform companies Contentful, [Sanity](https://www.sanity.io/agency-partners) and Optimizely. Those names are a search pool, not endorsements.

I think independence matters more than a partner badge. Select a consultancy that can work across platforms and show how it will handle knowledge ownership, source verification, access controls, data location and export rights. Its fees should not reward a particular license choice, and the engagement should end with a tested handover your own team can operate.

The immediate investment is not a new corporate website. Start with one governed body of knowledge that can support future search answers, agent actions and generated interfaces, then expand only when the evidence is reliable.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Start with one high-value decision, govern its facts and permissions, and test how AI systems represent it. The company that controls verified knowledge beneath the interface retains leverage even when the interface belongs to someone else.</p></div>
