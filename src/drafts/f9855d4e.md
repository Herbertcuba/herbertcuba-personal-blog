Websites will remain important for trust and direct customer experience, but they are becoming less dependable as the first place customers discover a company or get a routine answer.

When an AI system answers before a click, the page moves further down the journey. The bigger risk is a verification gap: an outside interface may speak about your business faster than you can prove, update or withdraw what it says. I think the strategic asset moving upstream is your ability to make and defend a claim wherever the customer asks.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The website still matters, but controlled company knowledge is becoming the foundation for discovery and service across AI systems.</p></div>

## Search is moving from answers to action

A [2024 analysis of browsing behaviour by marketing research firm SparkToro and data provider Datos](https://sparktoro.com/blog/2024-zero-click-search-study/) found that 58.5% of US Google searches and 59.7% of EU searches ended without a click. The measure includes ended and revised searches, so it does not show that AI caused the shift.

AI-specific evidence points the same way. [Pew Research Center, a nonpartisan US research organization](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/) tracked browsing in March 2025. Traditional links were clicked in 8% of visits with a Google AI summary and 15% without one; cited sources received clicks in only 1%. About 60% of question-form searches produced a summary. Search is moving from keyword matching toward conversation.

The likely next step is action. An AI agent is software that pursues a goal through several steps. At Google Cloud Next ’25, the company’s enterprise technology conference, Google announced [Agent2Agent, or A2A](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/a2a-a-new-era-of-agent-interoperability/), a shared protocol for exchanging information and coordinating work across applications.

A buyer’s agent could ask whether an industrial valve meets a temperature requirement and is available. A2A can carry the request, but it cannot verify the claim, decide who may see it or grant authority to make a commitment. Those controls stay with the supplier. I don’t know whether this becomes normal in two years or six, but I think leaders already need to decide which answers and actions their companies will authorize.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>AI summaries are associated with fewer visits to search results, while agent protocols create a possible path from a customer’s question into company systems. Permission and proof remain the company’s responsibility.</p></div>

## A knowledge catalog closes the verification gap

In a June 2026 video, [Phil Risher of Phlash Consulting uses “knowledge catalog”](https://youtu.be/xAIeA3ewRbo) for verified, structured information about a business. I use the term for a controlled set of approved company claims. Each claim has evidence, an owner, a review date, access rules and a record of changes.

The catalog does not replace the product database, policy repository or content platform. Those remain the sources. The catalog receives or points to approved fields, adds the controls needed for reuse, and presents a reviewed version to AI systems. When a source fact changes, the claim is reviewed, updated or withdrawn before reuse.

For the valve, the catalog could hold its operating temperature, certificate, owner and expiry date. If the certificate has expired, the system should refuse to confirm the claim and send the question to a person. I think a supported answer and a clear refusal will both matter more than fluent wording.

This prediction is testable. Given the same buyer questions, companies with owned, reviewed claims should produce more evidence-backed answers and fewer unsupported ones than companies that merely publish better pages. A pilot that cannot show the difference should not expand.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>A knowledge catalog makes approved claims usable outside the website without severing them from their sources, owners or evidence.</p></div>

## Structured content creates the head start

Structured content stores information in named fields instead of burying it in a page design. An “operating temperature” field lets a website and an agent retrieve the same value. Machine-readable means software can reliably recognize what that value represents.

In its May 2025 [Search Central guidance for site owners](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/05/succeeding-in-ai-search/), Google said structured data helps companies share machine-readable information its systems can consider. Google also stressed unique, useful content. Structure creates access, but it does not guarantee visibility or make generic information valuable.

A headless content management system, or headless CMS, keeps reusable content separate from website design and supplies it through standard software connections. I think existing fields, approval steps and change history provide a practical head start, although missing evidence and unclear ownership still have to be fixed.

Risher calls content “training data for the future internet.” That is a metaphor, not an operating model. The more controllable investment is an authoritative record an AI system can retrieve while answering. Model training is a separate process with different controls.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Structured content and a headless CMS provide reusable fields and workflows, but only proprietary knowledge with clear ownership and current evidence becomes dependable.</p></div>

## Generated interfaces move value beneath the page

Google’s [A2UI v0.9 project, released in April 2026](https://developers.googleblog.com/a2ui-v0-9-generative-ui/), illustrates generative UI: software assembles an interface for a specific request in real time. Think of the holodeck in *Star Trek: First Contact*, the 1996 science-fiction film in which a computer creates a setting on demand. The environment changes with the mission, while knowledge and rules determine what it can safely do.

*[The Digital Singularity Shift](/books/the-digital-singularity-shift/)*, my book about business in an age of abundant AI-generated output, argues that features become easier to copy and value moves toward what a company can credibly stand behind. I think generated interfaces make this concrete. The important difference is whether an agent can correctly answer an eligibility, availability or compliance question, not merely display a product page.

The website remains a direct surface the company can shape. Investment now also has to reach beneath that surface, into the facts and rules that any permitted interface may need.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>As interfaces become generated and distributed, the durable investment is checked knowledge and clear rules for what each interface may do.</p></div>

## Build the first catalog as a controlled investment

The consultancy that built your content platform may be a strong candidate because it knows the field structure and workflows. My take is to compare it with independent headless-CMS consultancies and specialist teams from vendor partner networks, then let evidence decide. Ask each team to show a prior content migration, explain how it assigns a source and owner to exposed claims, and demonstrate export and restoration.

Require full export rights, a named data location, access controls and a handover in which your team operates, exports and restores a sample. Ask for written disclosure of reseller commissions, referral fees, investments and platform commitments. Fees should not depend on resale or referrals, and the recommendation and acceptance criteria should stand without those incentives.

Turn the checklist into a four-week pilot around ten buyer questions and one source system. Start with product facts or service eligibility; exclude live prices, contracts and regulated decisions until the controls work. Name an executive owner for the outcome, a content owner for claim quality and a platform owner for access and rollback.

Set the pass rule first. Choose eight questions that should receive current, evidence-backed answers and two that should be refused and sent to a person. Expand only if all ten behave as expected, no unsupported claim appears and one business measure, such as handling time or qualified enquiries, clears a threshold agreed from the baseline.

Use the same questions for visibility. Mention rate is the percentage of answers that name the company. AI share of voice is the company’s portion of all company mentions across the same fixed market, AI services and competitor set. Repeat the test over time, but let business results and safety decide whether to expand.

I think leaders should start now without handing consequential decisions to an agent. The first investment is the controlled ability to answer ten important questions with evidence, ownership and the power to say, “we cannot confirm that.”

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Choose a vendor-neutral builder through proof, test a low-risk domain with named owners and fixed acceptance criteria, and expand only when checked answers improve a business result without creating unsupported commitments.</p></div>
