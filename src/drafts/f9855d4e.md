A company can own an excellent website and still be absent from the customer's decision. Search engines increasingly answer questions on the results page, while AI agents, software that can research and carry out parts of a task for a person, can compare options before anyone opens a company homepage.

The website will remain useful. What it is losing is its privileged role as the hub of discovery and action. For business leaders, that changes the investment question: the durable asset is becoming the verified knowledge beneath every interface, not the interface alone.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>The website is becoming one route into the business rather than the route. Investment therefore needs to move toward knowledge that other interfaces can understand and use.</p>
</div>

## Discovery is moving before the click

A [2024 analysis of browsing behavior](https://sparktoro.com/blog/2024-zero-click-search-study/) by SparkToro, an audience-research company, and Datos, a browsing-data provider, estimated that 58.5% of US Google searches and 59.7% of EU searches ended without a click on a result. The figures include abandoned and reformulated searches, so AI did not cause the whole number. They still show that a search no longer guarantees a website visit.

The AI-specific evidence points in the same direction. [Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/), a nonpartisan US research organization, examined the March 2025 browsing activity of 900 American adults. People clicked a traditional result in 8% of visits containing a Google AI summary, compared with 15% when no summary appeared. A source cited inside the summary received a click in only 1% of visits containing one.

The next shift is from answers to delegated tasks. At Google Cloud Next, Google's annual enterprise technology conference, the company [launched Agent2Agent, or A2A, in April 2025](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/a2a-a-new-era-of-agent-interoperability/) with more than 50 technology partners. A2A gives participating agents common rules for exchanging information and coordinating work. It does not make company facts accurate or give an agent business authority. Those remain the company's responsibility.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Discovery already produces fewer visits, and agent protocols extend the change from answers to tasks. Companies must still provide trusted facts and authority.</p>
</div>

## The asset behind the answer

Phil Risher, owner of the digital marketing firm Phlash Consulting, used *knowledge catalog* in a [June 2026 video](https://youtu.be/xAIeA3ewRbo) for verified business information made available to an agent. It is not a verified Google product category. I use it here for a governed publication layer containing approved facts about services, prices, policies, locations, credentials, and common customer questions.

Consider a delivery promise. On a conventional page, it may be buried in a paragraph. In a knowledge catalog, it can be stored with the relevant service, market, effective date, source system, and approver. If the source has expired or two systems disagree, the catalog should withhold the claim until its owner resolves the conflict. Structure makes uncertainty visible; it does not create truth.

This is why structured content matters: it stores information in reusable fields instead of binding it to a finished page. A headless content management system, or headless CMS, already separates content from presentation and delivers it through an application programming interface, a standard way for software to request data. That creates a head start, but the catalog must still draw each price, location, and policy from the system responsible for it.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>A knowledge catalog is a governed layer of approved business facts. Structured content provides the foundation, while clear ownership keeps those facts reliable.</p>
</div>

## Move investment upstream

The evidence proves a change in discovery and technical capability. It does not yet prove a return for a comparable Nordic enterprise. A proposal should therefore be treated as a testable investment tied to one business flow, with a named owner, a baseline, a spending limit, and a clear threshold to scale, change, or stop.

Search ranking remains useful, but it no longer describes the full market. Mention rate counts how often a company appears in a defined set of AI answers; AI share of voice compares its portion of tracked visibility with competitors. Both are directional measures, so they matter only when connected to a business outcome such as qualified demand, completed customer tasks, or service cost.

Implementation support should remain vendor-neutral. A capable partner needs headless-CMS implementation skills and a credible model for knowledge governance. The contract should settle export rights, data location, access controls, and a tested handover, while fees should not reward more platform licenses. A firm that already understands the content platform may move faster, but familiarity cannot replace independence or accountability.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Treat the knowledge layer as a bounded business investment. Judge it by outcomes, explicit ownership, reversible commitments, and support independent of platform incentives.</p>
</div>

## The interface can now be temporary

Generative UI, short for generative user interface, makes this investment shift easier to see. Instead of choosing from screens designed in advance, a person receives an interface assembled by AI for the request at hand. When [Google introduced Gemini 3, its AI model, in November 2025](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/gemini/gemini-3-gemini-app/), it described experiments in which the model generated a custom visual layout in real time.

A website still carries brand, legal information, and complex customer journeys. But it is becoming one possible presentation alongside AI search, assistants, and interfaces generated in the moment. This is the practical direction behind [The Digital Singularity](/posts/mckinsey-digital-singularity/), my framework for the convergence of AI and digital systems: value moves toward the governed knowledge that changing interfaces can draw upon.

Frontends will keep changing as generative UI makes it possible to assemble and personalize them for the moment. The most important digital investment now is the knowledge catalog beneath them: verified enough to trust, structured enough to reuse, and governed well enough to represent the business when the website is no longer in the room.
