Your website is losing its place at the center of your digital business. It will still explain products and close business, but the answer shaping a decision may appear in Google or an AI assistant before the customer sees a page.

That creates a problem I think most companies are not measuring. A business can approve one fact while an older, conflicting or out-of-scope answer reaches the customer. I call the distance between the two the **answer-control gap**. If existing systems keep it within clear limits, buy nothing. If they cannot, build a knowledge catalog.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The website is becoming one interface among many. Approved company knowledge must remain accurate as interfaces change.</p></div>

## The answer is leaving the page

The change is measurable, but the headline needs boundaries. [SparkToro, an audience-research company, and Similarweb, a digital-behavior measurement company](https://sparktoro.com/blog/in-2026-less-than-one-third-of-google-searches-still-send-a-click/) reported in June 2026 that 68.01% of searches in Similarweb's US desktop-and-mobile web panel ended without a click from January through April. A **zero-click search** ends before the user opens a result. The panel excludes Google's mobile app and is not directly comparable with a 59.7% European Union estimate published in 2024 by Datos, a browsing-data provider.

This does not prove that AI agents have taken over. Only 0.34% of searches in the early-2026 panel reached AI Mode, Google's conversational search interface. Yet [Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/), a nonpartisan US research group, found a related pattern in the March 2025 browsing of 900 American adults. People clicked a traditional result in 8% of Google visits with an AI summary and 15% without one. A cited source received a click in only 1% of visits with a summary.

The interface is changing too. A **generative user interface**, or GenUI, creates a screen for a specific request instead of serving a finished page; Google demonstrated it with the Gemini 3 AI model in [November 2025](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/gemini/gemini-3-gemini-app/). An **AI agent** is software that works through several steps toward a goal. At Google Cloud Next, Google's enterprise technology conference, the company announced the open Agent2Agent protocol, or **A2A**, on [April 9, 2025](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/a2a-a-new-era-of-agent-interoperability/) with more than 50 technology partners. This agreed communication method lets separately built agents coordinate work, although each organization still decides what an agent may see and do.

I don't know how quickly customers will trust agents with expensive or regulated decisions. I think the direction is clear enough to act on: discovery is moving from page to answer, while software is beginning to move from answer to action.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Zero-click behavior is widespread, while direct use of Google's AI Mode was still small in early 2026. Adoption remains uncertain, but answers and actions are already moving away from fixed pages.</p></div>

## A knowledge catalog carries the answer

[Phil Risher of Phlash Consulting](https://youtu.be/xAIeA3ewRbo), a digital-marketing firm for local-service businesses, described a **knowledge catalog** in June 2026 as “a verified data set of information about your business.” [Google Cloud, Google's enterprise cloud division](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/data-analytics/introducing-the-google-cloud-knowledge-catalog), introduced a narrower product with the same name in April 2026 to organize enterprise data for people and AI agents. The operating model here uses Risher's broader meaning and does not require that product.

A company knowledge catalog is a governed body of product facts, pricing rules, policies, credentials and answers to real customer questions. Each claim carries its source, owner, market, effective date and permitted use across interfaces instead of trapping that context in a page.

It does not replace every existing system. A product information system can remain the source for product facts, while a content management system holds approved explanations. The catalog indexes a claim and its boundaries. Separate identity and policy controls decide who may retrieve it or request an action, delivery software presents it, and an audit record preserves what happened. The catalog should not quietly become responsible for all those jobs.

Consider a manufacturer with evidence that one current model meets a Swedish product-safety rule. The catalog connects the claim to that model, Sweden, the supporting evidence and its expiry date. The policy layer can reject an attempt to apply it to Norway or an older model, while the audit record shows which version was used. A fact can be correct and still become unsafe when software loses its boundaries.

The idea is falsifiable. Measure **update lag**, the time from an approved change to a consistent answer across interfaces you own, and **exception rate**, the share of fixed tests that return a stale, conflicting, wrong-market or unauthorized answer. These are proposed measures, not industry standards. If existing systems stay within your limits, no new catalog layer is necessary.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>A knowledge catalog carries an approved claim and its limits across interfaces. It adds value only where existing systems lose that context.</p></div>

## Structured content creates the head start

**Structured content** stores meaning in named, reusable fields instead of burying it inside finished page copy. It is broader than **web structured data**, often called schema markup, which adds machine-readable labels to information on a webpage. In [May 2025 guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/05/succeeding-in-ai-search), Google Search Central, the company's documentation for website owners, said its systems consider structured data and urged publishers to create unique, non-commodity content. Machines need clear fields, but the knowledge inside them must still be worth using.

A **headless content management system**, or headless CMS, stores content separately from website design and sends it to different interfaces through software connections. I think organizations with mature content models, named owners and reliable updates have a real head start because much of the catalog's raw material already exists. This is an architectural judgment, not a measured market result. A headless CMS full of generic or poorly governed content offers little advantage.

This is also where the present shift meets [*The Digital Singularity Shift*](/books/the-digital-singularity-shift/), my 2024 book about AI making functional differences easier to copy. Its thesis is that “meaning, story, and belief” become the remaining battleground. I think GenUI now applies that pressure to the interface itself: polished presentation becomes cheaper to create for each request, while distinctive knowledge and the trust attached to it remain harder to reproduce.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Structured content gives mature headless-CMS organizations a head start, but the advantage comes from distinctive knowledge and disciplined ownership.</p></div>

The mapping is the separation between interface and authority. Presentation can change, but the computer still needs current records and rules for who can get information or request an action. A knowledge catalog creates that governed connection for a company's own answers without pretending to control outside AI services.

## Build capability before you buy a platform

My take is to start now with a six-week pilot around one important answer, one market and one interface you own. This creates local evidence because the research assembled here contains no measured Swedish or Norwegian enterprise case with a published baseline and outcome.

Make the charter short enough to forward. Name a permanent business owner, record the two measures and test ordinary questions alongside cases designed to trigger stale, conflicting, wrong-market and unauthorized answers. Content, technology and risk owners should review results weekly. Set the time limit, fee ceiling, success thresholds and scale-or-stop date before work begins. This small sample cannot predict every agent failure, but it can test whether today's systems preserve an approved answer and its limits.

The consultancy that built your content platform may be the right place to begin because it knows your content models and connections. Valtech, a global digital consultancy, or independent partners experienced with Contentful, Sanity or Optimizely content platforms are examples, not endorsements. Require bidders to disclose license-resale incentives, work with existing systems, grant full export rights and prove why another platform is needed.

Keep rankings, then add two bounded visibility measures. **Mention rate** is the share of fixed questions whose answers name the company. **AI share of voice** is its share of mentions across a fixed competitor set. Neither proves revenue or gives you control over an outside model, so connect both to corrections, escalations and qualified inquiries.

I think the immediate call to action is simple: begin building the knowledge-catalog capability, even if the first result is proof that your current systems are enough. The website can remain. The verified knowledge behind every interface now has to become the center.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Create local evidence with a bounded pilot and independent advice. Build no new layer if existing systems close the answer-control gap; when they do not, start the knowledge catalog now.</p></div>
