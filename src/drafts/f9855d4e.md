A website business case usually rests on one assumption: sooner or later, the customer will arrive at a page. That assumption is weakening. Search engines now answer many questions inside their results, while AI agents, software that can research and perform parts of a task for a person, can compare options or begin a purchase without visiting the company homepage first.

The website will not disappear, but it is losing its position as the digital hub. For business leaders, the investment implication is more important than the prediction: the durable asset is becoming the verified knowledge beneath every interface, not any single presentation of it.

<p>The website remains a valuable interface, but company knowledge must now work even when the customer never reaches it.</p>

## Search is turning into delegated work

The shift began before consumer agents arrived. A [July 2024 clickstream analysis](https://sparktoro.com/blog/2024-zero-click-search-study/) published by audience-research company SparkToro using panel data from Datos, a clickstream-data provider, estimated that 58.5% of US Google searches and 59.7% of EU searches ended without a click on a result. That measure includes abandoned and reformulated searches, so AI did not cause the whole number.

AI summaries strengthen the direction. The nonpartisan [Pew Research Center studied the March 2025 browsing activity of 900 US adults](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/). People clicked a traditional result in 8% of visits containing a Google AI summary, compared with 15% when no summary appeared. A cited source inside the summary received a click in only 1% of visits containing one.

The next change is from answers to delegated work. At Google Cloud Next, its annual enterprise technology conference, Google launched the open [Agent2Agent protocol](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/a2a-a-new-era-of-agent-interoperability/) in April 2025 with more than 50 technology partners. Usually shortened to A2A, it gives participating agents common rules for exchanging information and coordinating tasks. [Google's July 2026 Gemini Spark update](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-app/gemini-spark-updates-july-2026/) then showed its personal agent researching flights and starting a browser booking with permission, while the person still approves or completes payment.

A2A moves messages; it does not make a company's facts true or give an agent business authority. That boundary reveals the missing asset. Agents need reliable information before they can act reliably.

<p>Discovery already ends before the website in many journeys, and agent protocols extend that change from receiving an answer to beginning a task.</p>

## What a knowledge catalog actually is

Phil Risher, owner of the digital marketing firm Phlash Consulting, used *knowledge catalog* in a [June 2026 video](https://youtu.be/xAIeA3ewRbo) to describe verified business information made available to an agent. It is not an established Google product or shared technical standard. I use the term for a governed publishing layer that gives approved systems current facts about products, services, prices, policies, credentials, and real customer questions.

The catalog sits above product, customer, pricing, and policy systems. It selects facts from those authoritative sources and adds the context an agent needs: owner, market, language, version, validity period, approval status, and permitted use. A record might say that a service is available in Sweden until a stated date, identify the operating unit responsible, and mark the fact as public. If two source systems disagree or the record expires, the catalog should withhold it until the named owner resolves the conflict.

This is why structured content matters. Structured content stores information in reusable fields instead of burying it inside a finished page. Structured data, the related term in [Google's 2025 Search guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/05/succeeding-in-ai-search), is machine-readable markup associated with a page. Both help machines interpret information, but structure alone cannot create truth; the underlying knowledge must be distinctive, current, and approved.

A headless content management system, which separates managed content from the website that displays it and delivers that content through software connections, provides a real head start. Much of the modeling and many reusable fields already exist. The advantage is architectural rather than proven by a benchmark, and it only counts if the content contains knowledge competitors cannot reproduce from generic public information.

<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>A knowledge catalog is a controlled publishing layer above existing source systems; structured content and a modern headless CMS provide useful raw material, but governance makes it dependable.</p>
</div>

## Where the next digital budget should move

The investment case keeps the website while changing its weight in the portfolio. Human experience, accessibility, brand, and trust still matter. The mistake is to treat each redesign as the main digital asset while underfunding reusable content models, source connections, access controls, ownership, and maintenance.

Rankings still describe visibility in search results, while AI share of voice and mention rate describe how often answer systems surface a company. Those newer measures are useful leading signals. Business value still has to show up in a customer task, conversion, or service cost, with a baseline and a threshold for continuing the investment.

I cannot point to a comparable public Swedish or Norwegian case that proves the return yet. That uncertainty argues for a bounded decision: one customer journey, one market, a limited fact domain, a business owner, a data owner, a review rhythm, a spending ceiling, and an agreed scale, change, or stop rule. This keeps the investment reversible without reducing it to an experiment with no operational home.

The consultancy that built the existing content platform may be well placed to extend it because it understands the content model and integrations. Choose based on headless-CMS implementation capability, knowledge governance, export rights, data location, fees independent of platform incentives, access controls, and a tested handover. A software license cannot assign accountability inside the business.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Shift part of the digital budget from repeated page production to governed, reusable knowledge, and fund it against a bounded business outcome rather than promised AI visibility.</p>
</div>

## Generative UI makes the catalog more valuable

Generative UI, short for generative user interface, turns that interface shift into a product capability. Instead of showing everyone a prebuilt screen, software assembles a suitable view for the request at hand. In November 2025, [Google described Gemini 3, its AI model, generating an interface when a user prompts it](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/gemini/gemini-3-gemini-app/). Its open [A2UI project for agent-driven interfaces](https://developers.googleblog.com/introducing-a2ui-an-open-project-for-agent-driven-interfaces/) followed in December 2025 and reached version 0.9 in April 2026, giving agents a common way to describe interfaces across applications.

The roles are separate. A2UI describes presentation, A2A supports communication between agents, and a knowledge catalog publishes approved facts. Policy, identity, and authorization still decide what an agent may do. Confusing these layers turns an impressive demonstration into an unmanaged business risk.

This is the market direction behind [The Digital Singularity](/posts/mckinsey-digital-singularity/), my framework for understanding what happens when digital production and adaptation accelerate beyond the logic of fixed artifacts. Generative UI makes frontends faster to create and replace. The knowledge underneath must remain accurate across all of them.

That is why the near-term investment priority is the knowledge catalog. Interfaces are becoming more fluid; verified company knowledge is becoming the part that has to endure.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Generative UI shortens the useful life of any single frontend, increasing the strategic value of verified, structured knowledge that can safely support whatever interface comes next.</p>
</div>
