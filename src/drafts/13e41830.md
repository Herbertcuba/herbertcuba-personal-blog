In 1976, Alaska voters changed the state constitution so that at least a quarter of certain mineral revenue would enter the [Alaska Permanent Fund](https://apfc.org/about/history/). The fund invests part of the state's oil wealth instead of spending it at once. Residents received the first dividend in 1982; today's [annual payments](https://pfd.alaska.gov/Division-Info/About-Us) come from investment earnings tied to mineral royalties.

The important choice came before anyone knew the final size of the windfall. Alaska created a public claim on a valuable resource, then let future generations share in it. Half a century later, both *AI 2040: Plan A* and my book, *The Three Crucibles*, use that precedent to think about machine intelligence.

Their agreement proves nothing by itself. It does reveal the same concern from two directions: production may separate from wages before society decides who has a claim on the output.

## What AI 2040 asks us to imagine

[AI 2040: Plan A](https://ai-2040.com/AI-2040.pdf) is a 90-page scenario and policy proposal published on July 9, 2026, by the [AI Futures Project](https://ai-2040.com/about), a research nonprofit studying advanced AI. Four of its six authors also wrote scenario content for [AI 2027](https://ai-2027.com/), their 2025 account of a fast AI race. Plan A calls itself “primarily a recommendation, not a prediction.”

Its preferred path begins with a US-China agreement in 2029. By 2030, AI systems can fully automate the research that improves AI; without the agreement, the authors say superintelligence could follow by year-end. Their deal instead allows controlled growth within the human range until 2035, pauses at roughly top-human-expert performance, and moves toward superintelligence in 2040 after further safety work. Superintelligence means systems far more capable than humans across most mental tasks.

In the scenario, AI performs a third of cognitive labor, meaning information and decision work, by mid-2031. Real GDP, or economic output adjusted for inflation, grows by about 50 percent in 2032. As machines replace paid work, wage taxes shrink relative to output. Plan A therefore sells tradable permits to build robots or compute capacity, the processing power used to train and run AI. Its eventual US version directs a majority of permit fees to adults as a Citizen's Dividend and assigns a fixed fraction to a Compute Dividend Corporation with one share per citizen.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Plan A tries to slow the race, verify powerful AI, and convert part of an automation windfall into income that no longer depends on employment.</p>
</div>

## The three crucibles in plain language

My speculative book, [*The Three Crucibles*](https://www.cubagarcia.com/books/the-three-crucibles/), has been public since March 2026, before Plan A appeared. It describes three social thresholds rather than one technical takeoff.

- **The Correction** begins when AI pushes down the value of some human labor faster than wages, taxes, and institutions can adjust.
- **The Void**, the transformation period, follows when work and income start separating but no trusted replacement exists. The book explores a citizen dividend, taxes on AI labor, and distributed ownership, where ordinary people hold a claim on AI infrastructure or output.
- **The Alignment** is the political and moral fork: abundance can support broad stewardship, or harden into concentrated power.

The demand problem sits underneath all three. As the book puts it, “Robots do not buy iPhones.” More production cannot sustain an economy if households lose the income needed to buy what machines make.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>The crucibles describe a correction in labor value, a difficult transition to new distribution, and a final choice over whether abundance is shared or concentrated.</p>
</div>

## The overlap has a fault line

Both documents expect pressure on wages and the tax base before superintelligence, both reach for a Citizen's Dividend and Alaska, and both end at a choice between broad benefit and concentrated control. You do not need to accept Plan A's dates to see that structure.

The funding differs. My book considers taxes on AI labor and output; Plan A sells permits to build robots or compute capacity. More deeply, Plan A treats verification, making claims about chips, datacenters, and capabilities checkable, as the main route to safety. My map stresses ownership because technical control alone does not spread the gains.

Neither answer is complete. Plan A's covert-project appendix gives less than a 10 percent chance that a hidden project reaches Top-Expert-Dominating AI undetected before 2043. That means AI able to outperform the best human specialists. Ownership does not solve loss of control, while infrastructure that crosses borders can weaken national tax and dividend systems.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>The two maps share an economic diagnosis, but Plan A relies on verifiable control while The Three Crucibles asks who retains a durable claim on the gains.</p>
</div>

*Elysium* maps to the shared warning in these documents: capability can become extraordinary while ownership makes the surrounding society brittle. Plan A concentrates on controlling the capability; the crucibles keep asking who can claim its benefits.

## A preparation document for a rough passage

No one knows whether AI research will accelerate on this schedule, governments will coordinate, or labor markets will adapt in ways neither document expects. Agreement between two speculative works is a signal to examine, not confirmation that the future has been found.

Still, the order matters. If output rises while wage income and public revenue fall behind, the distribution problem begins before any machine deserves the name superintelligence. Preparation means working on verification and ownership together: who can inspect the systems, who can stop them, and who keeps a claim on what they produce.

My book is a preparation document for that reason. We cannot know what waits on the road to 2040, but if Plan A and *The Three Crucibles* are even partly right, it will be a bumpy road.
