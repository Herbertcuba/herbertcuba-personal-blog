In 1969, Alaska collected $900 million from leasing oil rights on its North Slope, home to the huge Prudhoe Bay oil field. The money went into infrastructure and social programs. In 1976, voters chose a more durable approach for future revenue: at least 25 percent of specified mineral income would enter the Alaska Permanent Fund, a state-owned investment fund. The first resident dividend followed in 1982, and eligible Alaskans have received annual payments since.

That old decision now sits inside two very different accounts of the AI transition. [*The Three Crucibles*](https://www.cubagarcia.com/books/the-three-crucibles/), my speculative book, was public on my site by March 2026. [*AI 2040: Plan A*](https://ai-2040.com/AI-2040.pdf) appeared in July. Both return to Alaska and to a Citizen's Dividend, meaning a direct public payment backed by returns from productive assets. Their agreement is not proof, but it is too specific to dismiss as a vague resemblance.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Alaska turned recurring resource revenue into a lasting public claim. AI 2040 and The Three Crucibles both ask whether societies can do something similar with AI-created wealth.</p></div>

## What AI 2040 is asking us to imagine

AI 2040 is a 2026 scenario from the AI Futures Project, a US research nonprofit also behind *AI 2027*, its 2025 account of a dangerous race toward advanced AI. The authors call Plan A primarily a recommendation, not a prediction. It describes the future they think governments should try to build.

The path runs through a US-China agreement in 2029, almost automated AI research in 2030, a pause around systems that can dominate top human experts in 2035, and a controlled move toward superintelligence in 2040. Superintelligence here means AI that broadly exceeds human cognitive ability. Governments track advanced chips, require labs to report their compute use, meaning the processing capacity used to train and run AI, inspect datacenters, and open research to outside scrutiny.

Its economy changes just as sharply. In the scenario, AI performs a third of cognitive labor by mid-2031, while inflation-adjusted economic output grows by roughly 50 percent in 2032. As wage income and its tax base weaken, the government caps and sells tradable permits for compute and robot-building capacity. The proceeds eventually support one share per citizen in a public dividend corporation.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Plan A combines a rapid AI transition with chip tracking, international inspection, controlled capacity, and a public dividend meant to keep citizens connected to the wealth machines produce.</p></div>

## The three crucibles, then the overlap

My book describes three pressures in sequence. **The Correction**, beginning in 2026, is the shock that arrives when scalable machine intelligence makes much human cognitive labor less economically scarce. Wages, business models, and tax systems built around paid work come under strain.

**The Void**, covering 2027 to 2032, is the uncomfortable period when output can recover without jobs returning at the same rate. Production may be abundant while households still need income to buy what the economy produces. **The Alignment**, from 2035 onward, asks whether that abundance will be broadly stewarded or controlled by a narrow owner class.

Set beside Plan A, the common structure is clear. Both expect automation to weaken labor income and the taxes tied to it. Both then use the same term, Citizen's Dividend, and the same Alaska precedent to reconnect people with productive wealth. They differ on funding: Plan A relies on permits for scarce capacity, while my book emphasizes AI labor taxation and distributed ownership. Both finally arrive at a political fork between shared abundance and concentrated power.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The Correction breaks the old labor bargain, The Void separates economic recovery from job recovery, and The Alignment decides who controls abundance. Plan A follows much the same sequence through different policy machinery.</p></div>

## The disagreement underneath the agreement

AI 2040 puts the control problem at the center: can states keep increasingly capable systems under human authority while rivals still have reasons to hide capacity? Its verification system uses records and inspections to test whether promises match reality. Even its covert-project appendix estimates a below 10 percent chance that a hidden project could reach “Top-Expert-Dominating AI,” a system exceeding the best human experts across fields, without detection before 2043. That is an estimate inside the scenario, not an observed probability.

The Three Crucibles puts ownership first. Verification may constrain who can build powerful AI, but it does not automatically give citizens a claim on the output. Ownership can spread that claim, but it cannot by itself keep a rogue system under control. Each map therefore exposes a missing layer in the other.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>AI 2040 is strongest on technical control and international verification; The Three Crucibles is strongest on ownership and distribution. A survivable transition may require both.</p></div>

## Preparation matters more than prediction

Agreement between two speculative works can still reflect a shared blind spot. No one knows how fast AI capabilities will move, whether governments will coordinate, or whether labor markets will bend rather than break. The dates may be wrong even if the pressures are real.

I offer *The Three Crucibles* as a preparation document whose value does not depend on an exact calendar. If the AI Futures Project and I are even directionally right, the road ahead is likely to be bumpy. The useful work now is to decide how control, ownership, and public claims should fit together before a crisis makes those choices for us.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Nobody knows what waits ahead. The convergence matters because it identifies choices worth preparing for before rapid change turns them into emergency decisions.</p></div>
