In 1976, Alaska voters changed the state constitution so part of the state's mineral revenue would enter the Alaska Permanent Fund. The fund invests that resource wealth for the long term. Since 1982, its earnings have helped finance an annual cash dividend for eligible Alaska residents.

That modest institution now appears in a much larger argument. [AI 2040: Plan A](https://ai-2040.com/), published in July 2026, uses Alaska as a model for sharing wealth created by artificial intelligence. My 2026 speculative book, [The Three Crucibles](https://www.cubagarcia.com/books/the-three-crucibles/), was public on my site by March and proposes the same idea under the same name: a Citizen's Dividend.

The chronology does not prove that either map is right, or that the works developed independently. It does show that the book's framework was public months before Plan A appeared with a more technical treatment of many of the same pressures.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Alaska shows how public ownership can turn resource wealth into citizen income. Both future maps apply that question to AI.</p></div>

## What AI 2040 actually proposes

AI 2040 is a 90-page scenario and policy proposal from the AI Futures Project, the nonprofit research group that also produced [AI 2027](https://ai-2027.com/), its April 2025 scenario about rapid progress toward superhuman AI. The authors call Plan A “primarily a recommendation, not a prediction.” That distinction matters because its dates are a designed path, not established facts.

In the scenario, the United States and China reach an agreement in 2029. By 2030, AI systems automate the research needed to build better AI systems, after which governments slow the process and demand transparency. Development pauses again around top-expert AI in 2035, meaning systems that can outperform leading human specialists, before a managed transition in 2040 to superintelligence, or AI that greatly exceeds the best human cognitive abilities.

The plan depends on verification: tracking advanced chips and inspecting data centers so countries can check whether others follow the limits. Its appendices also model a covert project operating outside that system, because agreed limits are only useful when evasion can be detected. Plan A anticipates an economic break too. In its scenario, AI performs a third of cognitive labor by mid-2031, weakening wage income and the taxes built on it. The response is permits on computing capacity, the processing power supplied by advanced chips, and on robots, with part of the proceeds going to citizens through mechanisms including a corporation in which each U.S. citizen owns one share.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Plan A combines a managed technical slowdown with chip tracking, inspections, and a new way to return AI-created wealth to U.S. citizens.</p></div>

## The three crucibles, in plain language

My book describes three periods of pressure. They concern how society and the economy fit together, rather than three generations of AI models.

**The Correction (2026)** is the shock that begins when AI makes some cognitive work much cheaper. Productivity moves toward the owners of capital, while labor loses bargaining power.

**The Transformation (2027–2032)** is the unstable passage that follows. If fewer people earn enough through work, income and consumer demand need another route; the book therefore treats basic income, regular cash support not tied to a job, and a Citizen's Dividend as economic infrastructure.

**The Alignment (2035+)** is the political settlement around abundance. Ownership and control decide whether powerful systems are governed as shared productive capacity or become engines of lasting concentration.

These stages are a preparation map rather than a promise that history will respect the dates printed beside them.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The three crucibles describe a labor shock, a new system for distributing income, and a final struggle over ownership and control.</p></div>

## Where the maps converge and diverge

The Correction matches Plan A's economic diagnosis. When machines can do more paid cognitive work, output may rise while wages and labor-tax revenue fall. The central problem becomes the weakening link between production, household income, and demand.

The Transformation has the closest match. Both works call for a Citizen's Dividend and point to Alaska, although they fund it differently. My book emphasizes claims on AI labor and infrastructure; Plan A uses permits on computing capacity and robots. The mechanisms differ, but both treat distribution as part of the productive system rather than a charitable repair added later.

The Alignment reveals the main disagreement. Plan A places more weight on verifiable control between states: declared chips, inspected data centers, transparent research, and coordinated limits. My book places more weight on ownership: who holds a durable claim on the value these systems create. Verification cannot by itself distribute wealth, while broad ownership cannot by itself make a superintelligent system safe. We may need both layers.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The works share an economic diagnosis and a dividend, but Plan A centers verification while The Three Crucibles centers ownership.</p></div>

## A preparation document, not a prophecy

Nobody knows whether AI progress will follow Plan A's timetable, and I do not know whether my crucibles will unfold in their stated years. The point of comparing them is narrower: two maps now describe a similar passage from labor-based income toward machine-created abundance, followed by a struggle over control and ownership.

The Three Crucibles is my preparation document for that passage. If AI 2040 and I are even directionally right, the road ahead will be bumpy because institutions will have to change while the technology is still moving. Alaska offers one useful lesson: establish the public claim before the new wealth has already found its permanent owners.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>No one knows the timetable. The shared warning is enough to justify preparing institutions for disruption, distribution, and ownership now.</p></div>
