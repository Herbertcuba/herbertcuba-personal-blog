In 1976, Alaska voters faced a practical question: what should remain after the oil is gone? They amended the state constitution so that at least a quarter of certain mineral revenue entered the Alaska Permanent Fund. The state invests that principal, and eligible residents have received dividends from its earnings since 1982.

The fund matters because it gives ordinary people a lasting claim on a valuable resource. Fifty years later, both [AI 2040: Plan A](https://ai-2040.com/) and my speculative book, [The Three Crucibles](https://www.cubagarcia.com/books/the-three-crucibles/), return to that idea. Their overlap does not prove that either future will happen. It does suggest that the ownership question is arriving before most institutions are ready to answer it.

  <span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
  <p>Alaska turned temporary resource income into a durable public claim. Two very different AI maps now argue that we may need a similar move for machine productivity.</p>
</div>

## AI 2040 offers a path, not a prophecy

AI 2040 is a July 2026 scenario and policy proposal from the AI Futures Project, a nonprofit that studies advanced AI and recommends responses. Four of the five authors of its earlier 2025 scenario, AI 2027, also worked on Plan A. The new document describes itself as “primarily a recommendation, not a prediction.”

Its preferred path begins with a 2029 agreement between the United States and China to slow and verify development of the most powerful systems. After scaling within roughly human capability, the parties pause again in 2035 at the level of top human experts. In 2040, they make a controlled move toward superintelligence, meaning AI that is better, faster, and cheaper than the best humans across every task.

The economic passage is just as dramatic. In the [full scenario](https://ai-2040.com/AI-2040.pdf), AI performs a third of cognitive labor, meaning knowledge work, by mid-2031, and real economic output grows by about 50 percent in 2032. Plan A proposes tradable permits for new robots and compute, the chips and data-center capacity used to run AI. It pairs them with a Compute Dividend Corporation in which every US citizen owns one share and receives an equal dividend. These are scenario projections, not measured outcomes.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
  <span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
  <p>Plan A imagines negotiated limits on advanced AI, followed by explosive growth and a citizen dividend funded through rights to build scarce machine capacity.</p>
</div>

## The three crucibles describe the same passage

My book was public by March 2026, before Plan A appeared, and organizes the transition into three crucibles. In plain language, they are:

- **The Correction.** AI makes cognitive output much cheaper, so the old relationship between jobs, wages, and economic value starts to break.
- **The Void.** Between 2027 and 2032, the old bargain weakens before a credible replacement is ready. The current public book page also describes this wider passage as The Transformation; the crucible in the edition used here is The Void.
- **The Alignment.** From 2035 onward, society faces a choice between shared stewardship of abundance and concentrated ownership by a small group.

The resemblance is strongest in the middle. Plan A expects work-based tax revenue to shrink relative to the economy; The Correction begins from the same pressure on labor. Both texts call the response a “Citizen’s Dividend,” and both use Alaska as the precedent. They differ on funding: my book considers AI labor taxation and distributed ownership, while Plan A sells permits to build robots and compute. Their final concern is also shared. Productivity can become abundant while power remains scarce.

That specificity is striking, but it is not independent confirmation. Both maps grew from the same broad technological moment, and Alaska is an older public model. The overlap gives us a hypothesis worth testing, not permission to call the future settled.

  <span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
  <p>The Correction, The Void, and The Alignment match Plan A’s sequence of labor disruption, income redesign, and a final choice over concentrated or shared power.</p>
</div>

## Control and ownership can pull apart

Plan A devotes most of its machinery to verification: tracking chips, inspecting data centers, sharing information, and enforcing pauses. My book puts more weight on ownership: who holds a legal and economic claim on AI output when wages carry less of the load. These are separate problems.

Plan A even stress-tests its own answer. Within its scenario assumptions, a hidden project has a below 10 percent chance of reaching AI that outperforms top human experts without detection before 2043. Low is not zero. Verification can miss secret capacity, while broad dividends cannot make an uncontrollable system safe.

The reverse failure is easier to overlook. Tradable permits could create an audit point and public revenue. Yet if the best-funded incumbents accumulate the scarce rights, technical control may improve while ownership narrows. A well-managed system could still produce orderly concentration.

The first honest checkpoint is 2029, Plan A’s opening policy fork. If broad AI adoption leaves labor’s share of income and real median wages intact while infrastructure ownership spreads, the fast version of The Correction weakens. If work-based public revenue erodes, ownership concentrates, and enforceable stop rights remain absent, the shared warning gets stronger.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
  <span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
  <p>A safe transition requires both enforceable control and broad economic claims. Solving one side can still make the other side worse.</p>
</div>

## A preparation document for an uncertain road

I do not know whether Plan A’s dates will hold, whether its treaty could survive strategic pressure, or whether labor will adapt faster than either map expects. The AI Futures Project does not claim certainty either. That is precisely why my book is a preparation document rather than a promise about the future.

If AI 2040 and The Three Crucibles are directionally right, the road ahead is likely to be bumpy. Preparation means deciding what can be stopped and who should share the output while those choices are still open. Alaska made its ownership decision while the resource windfall was still being shaped. We should notice the timing.
