Alaska made its ownership decision before the windfall became routine. In 1976, as the Trans-Alaska Pipeline was being built to carry North Slope oil, voters created the [Alaska Permanent Fund](https://apfc.org/about/history/) and dedicated part of the state's mineral income to it. The first resident dividend followed in 1982. The public claim came first.

Fifty years later, [*AI 2040: Plan A*](https://ai-2040.com/) returns to that precedent. Plan A is a policy recommendation told through a conditional future scenario by the AI Futures Project, the research nonprofit behind [*AI 2027*](https://ai-2027.com/), its earlier scenario about rapidly advancing AI. Four of AI 2027's five authors worked on Plan A, which says plainly that it is primarily a recommendation rather than their best-guess forecast.

[*The Three Crucibles*](https://www.cubagarcia.com/books/the-three-crucibles/), my speculative map of AI's economic transition, was public on my site by March 23, 2026; the PDF edition used here is dated June 24. A [public link to Plan A appeared on July 9](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48848425). The sequence establishes timing, not influence or predictive accuracy. I think the useful claim is narrower: the works converge on one economic mechanism, then reveal different tests of AI control.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>AI 2040: Plan A is an AI Futures Project policy recommendation expressed through a conditional scenario, while The Three Crucibles is Herbert Cuba Garcia's 2026 speculative framework. Their shared Citizen's Dividend, a public payment tied to AI-driven gains, is thematic convergence rather than forecast validation.</p></div>

## The closest match sits in the second passage

The edition matters because the book has two first-party labels for its middle passage. The [June 24 PDF maps the three crucibles as Correction, Void, and Alignment](https://www.cubagarcia.com/downloads/three-crucibles.pdf#page=6). Its current landing page uses Transformation as the short label for the second crucible, while the PDF places a Transformation chapter inside that second passage. Calling Transformation the unqualified canonical label obscures the source map.

The [Correction](https://www.cubagarcia.com/downloads/three-crucibles.pdf#page=19) predicts a market reckoning in which inflated AI expectations meet uneven returns and surviving companies automate under pressure. Plan A has no matching correction. It begins with geopolitical rivalry and takeoff, the period in which AI may automate enough AI research to accelerate its own improvement.

The close match begins in the Void. The book imagines output recovering while many jobs do not, then its Transformation chapter follows that break into household demand: “Robots do not buy iPhones.” Plan A reaches the same loop through different assumptions. In its scenario, AI performs [one third of all cognitive labor by mid-2031](https://ai-2040.com/AI-2040.pdf#page=13), meaning work based mainly on information, analysis, communication, and judgment. By 2032, fewer paid roles shrink income and payroll-tax receipts while companies deduct or depreciate heavy investment in data centers, factories, and robots. Output can rise even as demand and the tax base weaken.

Both works answer with a Citizen's Dividend, a public claim on AI-related productivity. [The book proposes an AI Labor Equivalence Tax](https://www.cubagarcia.com/downloads/three-crucibles.pdf#page=37) based on what a person in the equivalent role would have contributed through taxes and social payments. [Plan A instead auctions tradable permits](https://ai-2040.com/AI-2040.pdf#page=17) for new robot and computing capacity, then assigns a share of the fees to a dividend corporation owned equally by US citizens. In the scenario, the payment rises from $45,000 per adult in 2032 to about $1 million in 2035, in real 2025 dollars.

I think this is unusually specific: the same broken income loop, Alaska precedent, and policy name. “Citizen's Dividend” predates both works, so the phrase proves little by itself. The real convergence is structural, while one design taxes human-equivalent AI labor and the other charges for permission to expand physical capacity.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The June 24, 2026 PDF edition of Herbert Cuba Garcia's The Three Crucibles maps Correction, Void, and Alignment; Transformation is a chapter within the second passage. The AI Futures Project's conditional Plan A scenario does not share the market Correction, but it echoes the Void-to-Transformation mechanism: automated knowledge work weakens wages, demand, and labor-tax revenue. Both propose a Citizen's Dividend through different funding systems.</p></div>

## The maps disagree about control

Plan A's main lever is international verification. [In its preferred scenario](https://ai-2040.com/AI-2040.pdf#page=4), the United States and China reach an agreement in 2029, avoid a race to superintelligence by the end of 2030, pause at top-expert ability in 2035, and resume toward superintelligence in 2040. Superintelligence here means AI that outperforms the best humans across fields while working faster and more cheaply. Every date belongs to the scenario.

The agreement depends on evidence that countries respect the limits: major chip and data-center transactions are declared, rivals inspect infrastructure, and AI research becomes almost entirely transparent. Plan A tests this design with [an alternate covert-project branch](https://ai-2040.com/AI-2040.pdf#page=45), where roughly 500,000 H100-equivalents are hidden in tunnels beside China's Medog Hydropower Station and about 200 people know. An H100-equivalent is a rough unit of AI computing performance based on Nvidia's H100 processor, not a literal chip count.

This is where *The Matrix*, the 1999 science-fiction film about intelligent machines concealing physical domination behind a simulated world, becomes useful. Its code looks weightless, but the machine world still needs hardware and energy. Advanced AI has the same physical shadow: chips, power, cooling, and buildings can leave evidence even when software and intent remain hidden.

The book's [Alignment passage](https://www.cubagarcia.com/downloads/three-crucibles.pdf#page=56) asks who owns AI infrastructure and receives its output once it becomes ordinary economic machinery. Plan A gives citizens shares in the dividend corporation, not direct ownership of the chips, models, or companies producing the value. The book instead argues for infrastructure spread among individuals, cooperatives, and municipalities, including models that run locally or on an owner's premises.

I don't know which balance can survive geopolitical pressure. Plan A is stronger on technical loss of control and secret capacity; the book offers no containment plan for superintelligence. The book is stronger on distributed ownership; inspection can verify compliance while economic power remains concentrated. Each map supplies a layer the other largely leaves out.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The AI Futures Project's conditional Plan A scenario uses declared computing capacity, inspections, and transparent research to limit a race toward superintelligence. Alignment, the third passage in Herbert Cuba Garcia's 2026 book The Three Crucibles, asks who owns AI infrastructure and can claim its output. Verification can expose a breach; it does not distribute economic power.</p></div>

## Control becomes real at renewal

A third boundary appears when these civilizational maps reach an ordinary institution. [The book uses a thought experiment](https://www.cubagarcia.com/downloads/three-crucibles.pdf#page=21) about two insurers. One keeps 5,000 claims adjusters; the other deploys coordinated AI agents, software systems that divide and execute the claims process, cuts headcount by 40 percent, lowers premiums by 20 percent, and wins within six months. These are illustrative numbers, showing how competitive pressure can outrun a buyer's ability to leave.

My take is that every critical AI contract should require exit proof before renewal. Exit proof is a supplier-funded handover test showing that a replacement team can run one agreed business process. A first test can cover one claims workflow, one portable export of the relevant records, one replacement team, and 30 days. The chief information officer owns the renewal decision, the relevant business executive accepts the result, and the buyer owns the historical cases and expected outcomes used for evaluation.

The handover package should include versioned workflow settings, model instructions, tool connections, permissions, source records, restoration guidance, and data lineage, which shows where each input came from and how it changed. A replacement may use a different model, instructions, retrieval method, or workflow. What must remain stable is the agreed business result on the buyer's cases, within fixed limits for error, time, and cost.

Missing records, blocked access, results outside those limits, or decisions that cannot be traced to their sources should fail the test. One failure would not prove migration impossible forever. It would show that no workable exit has been demonstrated on the agreed terms, so automatic renewal stops, funded transition assistance begins, and a failed retest allows departure without a termination fee. I think this leverage exists before signing.

The [European Union's AI Act](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj/eng), the bloc's 2024 risk-based AI law, addresses a related boundary. Rules for general-purpose AI models, models able to perform many tasks and support other systems, have applied since August 2, 2025; most of the Act began applying on August 2, 2026. Its transparency duties can require disclosure in specified AI interactions and for certain AI-generated or manipulated content. Disclosure still does not prove that a buyer can transfer a working process.

That is where institutional power can fail next. A state may inspect hidden capacity, and citizens may own shares in a dividend fund, while a hospital or insurer remains unable to move a vital workflow. Alaska secured the public claim before oil income became routine. With AI, I think we must inspect capacity, assign the economic claim, and test the exit before dependence becomes infrastructure.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Exit proof is a proposed supplier-funded handover test before renewal: a replacement team must run one agreed process within limits for error, time, and cost using buyer-owned evaluation cases. Neither the AI Futures Project's conditional Plan A scenario nor Herbert Cuba Garcia's 2026 book The Three Crucibles develops this institutional control. Transparency and a shared dividend still leave power concentrated when a buyer cannot move the work.</p></div>
