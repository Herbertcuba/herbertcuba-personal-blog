Alaska learned an expensive lesson before creating one of the world's most unusual public institutions. The state received $900 million from its 1969 oil-lease sale on the North Slope and spent the money on infrastructure and public programs. In 1976, voters chose a different rule for later mineral income: at least 25 percent of certain revenues would go into the Alaska Permanent Fund, a state-owned investment fund. Its first resident dividend followed in 1982, and eligible Alaskans have received an annual payment ever since.

The fund turned part of a temporary resource boom into an enduring public claim. Half a century later, both [AI 2040: Plan A](https://ai-2040.com/), a 2026 governance scenario, and my 2026 speculative book, [The Three Crucibles](https://www.cubagarcia.com/books/the-three-crucibles/), return to that idea. My book has been public on my site since March; AI 2040 appeared in July.

That timing proves no forecast right. What matters is that two different maps reach the same social problem: if machine intelligence becomes a major source of wealth, wages may no longer be enough to distribute it or fund the state.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Alaska shows how citizens can retain a lasting claim on shared resource wealth. Two recent AI scenarios use that precedent to ask what happens when intelligence itself becomes productive capital.</p></div>

## What the two maps actually say

AI 2040 comes from the AI Futures Project, a US research nonprofit that also produced AI 2027, its 2025 account of a dangerous race toward advanced AI. Four of AI 2027's five authors also wrote the new document. They describe Plan A as primarily a recommendation rather than a prediction.

The scenario starts with a US-China agreement in 2029, followed by almost automated AI research and a transparency-backed pause in 2030. Systems then scale within the range of human ability until a 2035 pause around AI that can outperform top human experts. A planned move toward superintelligence, meaning AI that exceeds humans across almost all cognitive work, follows in 2040. These dates are assumptions, not observed facts.

My book divides the transition into **The Correction**, **The Void**, and **The Alignment**. The current public book page still calls the same 2027–2032 middle phase **The Transformation**, so I am making that naming difference explicit.

- **The Correction (2026)** is the economic shock as scalable machine intelligence reduces the value of some human labor.
- **The Void (2027–2032)** is the unstable period when output may recover faster than jobs and wages, forcing society to rebuild how purchasing power reaches people.
- **The Alignment (2035+)** is the political choice between broadly shared abundance and a system controlled by a narrow owner class.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>AI 2040 offers a governed route to superintelligence. The Three Crucibles describes the social passage through labor shock, a broken link between work and income, and a final struggle over who controls abundance.</p></div>

## The overlap is economic before it is technological

In AI 2040's scenario, AI performs a third of cognitive labor by mid-2031. The mechanism matters more than the number: when paid human work shrinks, taxes on wages shrink with it, even if machine output grows quickly. That is the same break described by The Correction.

Both maps then reach a **Citizen's Dividend**, a direct public payment tied to productive AI assets. AI 2040 imagines permits for advanced chips and robots feeding a Compute Dividend Corporation. Every citizen holds one share, which entitles them to part of its profits. My book puts more weight on taxing AI labor and distributing ownership, but it reaches the same Alaska-inspired principle: people need a claim on production that does not depend entirely on having a job.

The third convergence is political. AI 2040 worries about irreversible concentration of power; The Alignment asks whether we become stewards of abundance or allow a new owner class to dominate it. In both accounts, technical success leaves the hardest social question unanswered.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The specific convergence is a sequence: automated labor weakens wages and taxes, a citizen dividend reconnects output with demand, and ownership determines whether abundance is shared or concentrated.</p></div>

## Control and ownership solve different failures

AI 2040 places its main bet on verification. Compute, the processing capacity used to train and run AI, would be declared and limited; advanced chips would be tracked, datacenters inspected, and major research made transparent. Its appendix tests the weak point, estimating below a 10 percent chance that an undetected covert project reaches what it calls Top-Expert-Dominating AI, a system better than the strongest human specialists, before 2043.

My book places more weight on ownership. A well-inspected AI system can still concentrate its financial returns, while broadly distributed ownership does not make a technically dangerous system safe. Verification asks what can be built and whether anyone can hide it. Ownership asks who benefits from what gets built.

I do not know which safeguards will matter most, and AI 2040 may be wrong about the speed of progress. Yet its governance layer and my economic layer look less like competing answers than two parts of the same preparation.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>AI 2040 tries to keep advanced AI visible and controllable; my book tries to keep its economic gains broadly owned. A durable settlement would need to address both failure modes.</p></div>

## Preparation without prophecy

No one knows what is waiting for us. The authors of AI 2040 do not claim to know, and neither do I. Their document is a proposed route through uncertainty; my book is a preparation document for the same reason.

The dates may slip and the technologies may stall. But if these two maps are even directionally right, we have a bumpy road ahead as work, income, control, and ownership come apart. The point of preparation is to see those choices before events make them for us.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Convergence is not proof. It is a reason to prepare for a difficult transition in which controlling AI and sharing its returns become inseparable public questions.</p></div>
