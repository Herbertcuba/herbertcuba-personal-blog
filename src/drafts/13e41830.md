In 1976, Alaska made an unusual decision about wealth that had barely begun to arrive. Voters created the Alaska Permanent Fund, a state-owned investment fund that saves part of the income from non-renewable resources such as oil and invests it for long-term public benefit. North Slope oil started flowing through the pipeline in 1977, and the fund paid its first resident dividend in 1982.

The important choice came early: some value from a scarce public resource should outlast the boom and remain connected to citizens.

Fifty years later, [AI 2040: Plan A](https://ai-2040.com/AI-2040.pdf), a 2026 policy scenario about governing advanced AI, reaches for the same precedent. So does [my 2026 speculative book, *The Three Crucibles*](https://www.cubagarcia.com/books/the-three-crucibles/). A downloadable PDF and homepage link for my book appeared on cubagarcia.com in March, before AI 2040 was publicly available in July. That sequence proves neither map right, but it makes their specific overlap worth examining.

<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>The Alaska Permanent Fund turns income from a scarce resource into lasting public ownership. Both AI 2040 and The Three Crucibles use it to ask who should benefit when AI becomes productive infrastructure.</p>

## A scenario is an argument in the future tense

AI 2040 is a 90-page scenario from the AI Futures Project, the US research nonprofit that also produced *AI 2027*, its April 2025 study of rapid AI development. The authors call Plan A “primarily a recommendation, not a prediction.” Its dates make a proposed path concrete rather than promise a timetable.

The scenario imagines a US-China agreement in 2029 that prevents the race from reaching fully automated AI research in 2030. Systems then expand within the range of human ability, pause in 2035 at roughly the level of leading human experts, and move toward superintelligence in 2040. Here, superintelligence means AI that is significantly better, faster, and cheaper than the best humans across essentially every task.

Plan A would limit additions to compute, the processing capacity used to train and run AI, as well as robot capacity. Tradable permits would govern new capacity, and the proceeds would fund a Compute Dividend Corporation in which every US citizen receives a share. Inspections, chip tracking, and research transparency would make the limits verifiable. In its stress test of a hidden Chinese project, the report puts the chance of reaching AI beyond top human experts without detection before 2043 at below 10 percent.

The economic warning is more immediate. In the scenario, AI performs a third of cognitive labor by mid-2031. As paid work shrinks relative to machine output, taxes tied to wages weaken, so citizens need another claim on the returns.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Plan A proposes a verified slowdown in advanced AI, limits on new compute and robots, and citizen ownership funded by permits. It aims to preserve control and mass income as machine work expands.</p>
</div>

## The three crucibles name the pressure

*The Three Crucibles* is preparation rather than a dated prophecy. Each crucible names a pressure in the passage from a labor economy to an AI economy.

**The Correction** begins when productivity moves from human labor toward machine-owning capital. Output may rise while wages, jobs, and the tax systems built around them lose their old role.

**The Void** is the unstable interval after that bargain weakens but before a dependable replacement exists. The book proposes a citizen’s dividend, a direct public claim on returns from AI infrastructure, alongside a tax on AI-produced labor to help bridge the gap.

**The Alignment** is the political settlement that follows. It asks whether productive intelligence will be broadly owned and governed for shared abundance, or concentrated among a small group with lasting control.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>The Correction breaks the old link between work and income, The Void is the gap before a replacement is secure, and The Alignment decides whether AI abundance is shared or concentrated.</p>
</div>

## The overlap is in the shape of the problem

The maps first meet on economics. If machines take more useful work, output can rise while wage income and labor-tax revenue fall. Both documents therefore move from automation to distribution rather than stopping at productivity.

They meet again with unusual specificity: both use “citizen’s dividend” and cite Alaska. The term is older than either work, so this is no claim of invention. What matters is the shared chain from a scarce productive resource to a public claim on it, then to income that does not depend entirely on wages.

Both also end at a political fork. Plan A asks whether advanced AI can avoid extinction or irreversible concentration of power. The Three Crucibles asks whether we become stewards of abundance or accept technological feudalism. Their common question is whether AI spreads power or narrows it.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Both works move from machine labor to weaker wage-based income, then to a citizen dividend and a choice between shared benefit and concentrated power. This is structural convergence, not proof of a timeline.</p>
</div>

## Control is where the maps separate

Plan A puts its heavier bet on verification. Governments declare capacity, inspectors check data centers, chips are tracked, and major research is opened to scrutiny. Its covert-project appendix exposes the weakness: an agreement only works if cheating can be detected.

My book puts more weight on ownership. Verification can show whether actors obey a limit, but it does not decide who receives the value created inside it. Broad ownership has the opposite weakness: it cannot make a dangerous system safe or stop a state from hiding capacity. The approaches cover different parts of the problem, and neither feels complete alone.

No one knows what waits beyond the next few years. If Plan A and my book are even directionally right, the road will be bumpy because technical capacity may move faster than the institutions that distribute income and enforce restraint. I regard *The Three Crucibles* as a preparation document for that gap. Its value depends less on exact dates than on helping us recognize the choices while they are still choices.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>AI 2040 relies on verification to restrain dangerous capacity, while The Three Crucibles emphasizes ownership of the resulting value. We cannot know the path, but preparing for both control problems is more honest than assuming a smooth transition.</p>
</div>
