In 1976, Alaska voters amended their state constitution and created the Alaska Permanent Fund. At least a quarter of certain mineral revenues would go into a public investment fund rather than disappear into the current budget. The principal would be invested, and dividend payments to residents began in 1982.

It was a practical answer to a large philosophical question: who should benefit from a resource that belongs to a place, but can be extracted only once? Fifty years later, [AI 2040](https://ai-2040.com/) and my book, [*The Three Crucibles*](https://www.cubagarcia.com/books/the-three-crucibles/), both return to that precedent when asking who should benefit from machine-created wealth. Their overlap is not proof that either future will arrive. It does suggest that the distribution problem is becoming harder to ignore.

<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>The Alaska Permanent Fund turned part of a one-time resource windfall into shared, lasting ownership. Both AI 2040 and The Three Crucibles see that model as relevant to AI-created wealth.</p>
</div>

## AI 2040 is a proposal in the form of a future

Published in July 2026, [*AI 2040: Plan A*](https://ai-2040.com/AI-2040.pdf) is a scenario and policy proposal from the AI Futures Project, the forecasting group behind *AI 2027*, its 2025 scenario about rapid progress toward superhuman AI. Plan A openly calls itself “primarily a recommendation, not a prediction.” Its imagined future is there to show what coordinated action might look like.

In the scenario, a 2029 agreement between the United States and China averts a rush toward automated AI research and superintelligence in 2030. Development continues within the range of human ability, pauses around top-expert AI in 2035, and resumes toward superintelligence in 2040. Top-expert AI means systems able to rival the best human specialists; superintelligence means capability beyond humans across most cognitive work.

The economic shift follows a clear mechanism. As AI replaces paid cognitive tasks, wage-based tax revenue weakens. Plan A therefore proposes tradable permits to build compute or robots; compute is the processing capacity used to train and run AI. A fixed, rising share of the permit fees belongs to a Compute Dividend Corporation, with one share owned by each US citizen and profits paid as dividends. In this scenario, AI performs a third of cognitive labor by mid-2031 and real economic output grows by roughly 50 percent during 2032.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>AI 2040 imagines a US-China agreement, staged AI development, explosive growth, and a US citizen dividend financed through a rising share of permit fees for new compute and robots.</p>
</div>

## The three crucibles, stated plainly

My book was public on my site by March 2026, months before Plan A appeared. Its downloadable edition names the three crucibles **The Correction**, **The Void**, and **The Alignment**. The public book page calls the middle period **The Transformation**; both names refer to the same difficult passage in the book’s sequence.

**The Correction** begins when AI makes repeatable cognitive work far cheaper. The claim is directional rather than literal: as productivity shifts toward capital, wages lose their old role as the main way society distributes income.

**The Void**, or Transformation, is the unstable period after that shift begins but before new institutions catch up. A citizen’s dividend becomes “systemic lubrication” because production still needs human demand, even when it needs much less human labor.

**The Alignment** is the political settlement that follows. The central question is whether AI abundance produces broad stewardship or a new class of owners with disproportionate economic and political power.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>The three crucibles describe a sequence: labor loses economic leverage, society struggles to rebuild distribution, and ownership determines whether abundance is shared or concentrated.</p>
</div>

## Agreement on the problem, disagreement on the remedy

The strongest convergence is causal. Both works expect AI productivity to weaken wages and the tax systems built around them. Both then arrive at a “Citizen’s Dividend” and cite Alaska as a model. They differ on funding: my book proposes taxing the value produced by AI labor, while Plan A assigns a rising share of permit fees to a citizen-owned corporation.

They also see the same political danger. Extraordinary production can coexist with extraordinary concentration. Yet AI 2040 spends most of its attention on verification: declared compute, tracked chips, inspected data centers, research transparency, and safety cases, which are structured arguments backed by evidence that a system can be deployed safely. Its covert-project appendix estimates a below-10-percent chance that a hidden program reaches what it calls Top-Expert-Dominating AI, a system able to outperform the best human experts, without detection by 2043.

My book puts more weight on ownership. Verification can reduce the risk of a secret race, but it does not decide who receives the gains. Distributed ownership can spread those gains, but it does not prove that one of the most capable AI systems will remain under control. The documents therefore cover different layers of the same transition, and each leaves a problem that the other makes visible.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>The documents converge on disruption, dividends, and the danger of concentrated power. AI 2040 focuses on controlling development; The Three Crucibles focuses on ownership after the value shifts.</p>
</div>

<p>The television series portrays an Earth where Basic Assistance supplies necessities while meaningful work and opportunity remain scarce. The parallel matters because a dividend can protect consumption, but the Alignment question remains: whether people also retain ownership, agency, and a real claim on the future being built.</p>

## Preparation is the honest response

No one knows whether AI progress will follow Plan A’s dates, my sequence, or a slower and stranger path. Energy, chip production, politics, and human resistance may all change the pace. Independent agreement on a mechanism is still not the same as evidence that a timetable is correct.

That uncertainty is exactly why my book is a preparation document. If AI 2040 and I are even directionally right, the road ahead will be bumpy: first through economic correction, then through institutional strain, and finally through a contest over control. We still have time to prepare verification and ownership together, before events force us to improvise both.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>No forecast can remove the uncertainty. Preparation means building credible controls and broader ownership before a rough transition turns both into emergency measures.</p>
</div>
