In 1976, Alaska voters created the Alaska Permanent Fund after the Prudhoe Bay oil field had been discovered and before North Slope oil began flowing in 1977. The state-owned investment fund saves part of Alaska's non-renewable oil wealth so it can create lasting public value. Its first resident dividend was paid in 1982.

Fifty years later, that choice sits near the center of two very different attempts to think about artificial intelligence. [AI 2040: Plan A](https://ai-2040.com/) imagines a governed route through advanced AI. [My book, *The Three Crucibles*](https://www.cubagarcia.com/books/the-three-crucibles/), maps the economic and political transition around it. The book page was public in March 2026; AI 2040 became publicly available in July. That chronology cannot prove either map correct, but their overlap tells us which problems keep returning.

<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Alaska turned shared resource wealth into a lasting public claim. Both AI 2040 and The Three Crucibles ask whether societies can do something similar with AI wealth.</p>
</div>

## What Plan A proposes

AI 2040 is a 90-page policy scenario from the AI Futures Project, a US research nonprofit that also produced [AI 2027](https://ai-2027.com/), its April 2025 scenario about rapid AI development. Four of AI 2027's five front-page authors appear in Plan A's top byline; the fifth, Scott Alexander, is credited separately for rewriting sections and giving extensive feedback. The authors describe Plan A as “primarily a recommendation, not a prediction.”

Its scenario imagines a 2029 US-China agreement that averts fully automated AI research and development in 2030. AI capability then grows within the human range through 2035, pauses at the level of leading human experts, and moves toward superintelligence, meaning AI far beyond those experts, in 2040.

The economic mechanism matters more than the dramatic dates. Compute means the advanced-chip and data-center capacity used to train and run AI. Plan A limits additions to compute and robot capacity, sells tradable permits for that capacity, and directs the proceeds toward a Compute Dividend Corporation in which each US citizen receives a share. In the scenario, AI performs a third of cognitive labor, the mental work now done by people, by mid-2031, while the old labor-based tax system loses ground.

That system depends on verification. Data centers must be inspected, chips tracked, and hidden projects found before they escape the limits. AI 2040 even tests this problem through a detailed covert-project scenario about a secret effort to evade those controls. Its desired future rests on making enormous technical capacity visible enough to govern.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Plan A combines staged AI development with verifiable limits on compute and robots, then turns permit revenue into a dividend for US citizens.</p>
</div>

## The three crucibles

My book uses three stages to describe the same broad transition from the human side.

**The Correction** begins when AI makes capital far more productive and reduces the market value of some human labor. The immediate problem is not a lack of output. It is that wages still carry most people's income while machines take a growing share of the work.

**The Void** is the unstable interval after that old link weakens but before a replacement earns public trust. A citizen dividend can bridge part of the gap by keeping people able to participate in the economy. As the book puts it, robots do not buy iPhones.

**The Alignment** is the political choice that follows: will AI-generated abundance be stewarded broadly, or controlled by a small class of owners? Technical safety belongs here, but so do power and property.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>The Correction breaks the old labor bargain, The Void exposes the missing distribution system, and The Alignment decides who controls the abundance that follows.</p>
</div>

## Where the maps meet, and where they do not

The first match is economic. Plan A describes a shrinking labor-tax base; The Correction describes the underlying shift from labor toward machine-owning capital. Both then arrive at the same bridge, using the exact phrase “Citizen's Dividend” and citing Alaska as a precedent. The overlap is specific, but it establishes neither influence nor proof.

Their funding routes differ. Plan A sells permits to expand scarce compute and robot capacity. My book connects the dividend to returns from AI infrastructure, distributed ownership that spreads claims beyond a few firms, and AI labor taxation: a levy intended to replace part of the tax base lost when AI takes over paid work.

The deeper split concerns control. Plan A asks whether states can measure, inspect, and constrain advanced AI capacity. My book asks who holds an enforceable claim on the output when labor no longer provides one. Neither answer can carry the transition alone. Verification without broad ownership may preserve concentration, while ownership promises mean little if hidden capacity can overturn the rules.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>The maps share a diagnosis, an Alaska-inspired dividend, and a political fork. They diverge on emphasis: AI 2040 prioritizes verification, while The Three Crucibles prioritizes ownership.</p>
</div>

The mapping is about how we use uncertain foresight. A scenario should expose choices and failure points before they arrive; it should not be mistaken for evidence that one future has already been decided.

## Preparing before certainty

I do not know whether either timeline will hold. AI 2040 calls itself a recommendation, and that is also the purpose of my book: a preparation document for a transition whose timing and shape remain uncertain.

If both maps are even roughly right, the road ahead is bumpy. We should ask early what replaces income tied to labor, how AI capacity can be checked, and who owns a claim on its gains. Alaska made its ownership decision before North Slope oil began to flow. We cannot copy that answer at AI scale, but we can learn from its timing.
