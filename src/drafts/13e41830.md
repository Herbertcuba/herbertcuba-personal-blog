A European insurer could secure audit rights for its claims model and still have no practical exit from its provider.

That tension sits inside [AI 2040: Plan A](https://ai-2040.com/AI-2040.pdf), a scenario from the AI Futures Project. Four of its six authors also wrote [AI 2027](https://ai-2027.com/). Plan A coordinates frontier AI through an international deal and public research, followed by a pause.

A public edition of my book [The Three Crucibles](/books/the-three-crucibles/) was [available by 23 March 2026](https://github.com/Herbertcuba/herbertcuba-personal-blog/commit/ce380abb4f4f13f5fc660a2c2e3a6a75011184a8). [AI 2040 was publicly linked on 9 July](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48848425). Both documents choose the term "Citizen's Dividend" and use Alaska's oil dividend as the precedent. I think their divergence is even more useful. It exposes the dividend-control split: people can receive AI income while holding little authority over the systems generating it.

## Plan A ends with a handoff

Under Plan A's handoff, the provider's role could expand from contractor to governor.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The scenario uses a 2029 US-China agreement and a 2035 pause to preserve human control. In 2040, development resumes and institutions begin passing authority to AIs judged aligned.</p></div>

The authors frame Plan A as a recommendation. Their timeline sets a 2029 US-China agreement, a 2035 pause at top-human-expert level, then a 2040 return toward superintelligence ([summary, p. 4](https://ai-2040.com/AI-2040.pdf#page=4)).

The final chapter passes institutions and equipment to AIs until humans can no longer shut everything down (["Passing the Torch to AIs," p. 33](https://ai-2040.com/AI-2040.pdf#page=33)). I think this handoff is the load-bearing assumption. Verification buys time. Alignment supplies the reason to yield control.

In the report's uncertain model, AI and robots perform about 20 percent of labour by economic value in 2032 and about 85 percent in 2035. A permit-funded dividend rises from $45,000 per person to roughly $1 million, in real 2025 dollars ([pp. 17-18](https://ai-2040.com/AI-2040.pdf#page=17)). The structure can be tested without accepting those dates.

## The cross-map has four parts

The insurer exposes where the two documents make different promises.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The documents converge on economic disruption and a citizen dividend. They differ on timing, funding, and the rights that must accompany distribution.</p></div>

| Question | AI 2040 | The Three Crucibles | Test inside the insurer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Capability | Pause at top-human-expert AI, then a 2040 handoff | Correction: the labour-value link breaks | Claims work moves into AI faster than payroll and demand adjust |
| Verification | Declarations, inspections, public research | Open infrastructure and models | The insurer can audit the provider but struggles to leave |
| Economics | Compute and robot permits fund the dividend | Transformation: the dividend bridges the Void | Costs and premiums fall while wage-linked revenue weakens |
| Control | AIs judged aligned receive more authority | Alignment: ownership is broadly distributed | A contract change reveals whether portability is real |

On page 35, The Three Crucibles says, "Robots do not buy iPhones." Both works use "Citizen's Dividend" and Alaska's oil dividend. I think the shared mechanism and sequence are meaningful. I make no claim that either work coined the phrase.

Funding reveals a genuine difference. Plan A prices scarce capacity through permits. The book proposes taxes on machine labour and computational output. Those mechanisms create different enforcement problems.

## The dividend-control split

At the insurer, audit rights and operational control could sit in different hands.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Plan A distributes cash claims and makes frontier AI legible. The Three Crucibles pushes further toward rights over infrastructure, because visible and partially distributed systems can still concentrate rule-setting.</p></div>

Plan A uses declarations, inspections, compute accounting, and public research. Appendix D imagines China hiding about 500,000 H100-equivalents beside the Medog Hydropower Station. In that branch, the chance of reaching Top-Expert-Dominating AI undetected stays below 10 percent through 2043 ([Appendix D, pp. 44-49](https://ai-2040.com/AI-2040.pdf#page=44)). The test measures discovery. Lawful concentration remains outside it.

Plan A gives each US citizen one share in its Compute Dividend Corporation and seeks many competing AI companies. The Three Crucibles goes further, calling for open infrastructure and legal frameworks for distributed ownership. I think the remaining gap is control: cash rights can spread while rule-setting remains concentrated.

Picture *Snowpiercer*. Inspecting every carriage would reveal how the train runs. Wilford would still control the engine. A dividend shares some output. Control rights decide who may change direction.

I don't know which ownership arrangement can hold at frontier scale. I think verification is essential. The concern weakens if transparency also produces enforceable public claims and practical exit. It strengthens if citizens receive cash while a few operators retain rule-setting power. That is a falsifiable political forecast.

## Europe meets the fork in procurement

For a European insurer, the first version of this political problem arrives in the contract.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Audit rights reveal model behaviour. Strategic control depends on portability and exit, which leaders must negotiate before the provider becomes operational infrastructure.</p></div>

Return to the claims model. It may satisfy audit and data-residency requirements. Strategic control appears when the provider changes its terms, price, or model access.

Three questions belong in the decision:

- Can the insurer retrieve its decision history in a usable form?
- Can it move the workflow and evaluations to another model within an acceptable window?
- Which rights survive if the provider withdraws the model?

This is where [reversibility becomes a strategic property](/posts/reversibility-is-the-new-correctness/). A dividend can soften the distributional shock. Enterprise exit and citizen authority require separate rights.

The leverage exists before the contract is signed. AI 2040 maps how to slow the train. The Three Crucibles asks who owns the engine. The future becomes governable when visibility and ownership travel together.
