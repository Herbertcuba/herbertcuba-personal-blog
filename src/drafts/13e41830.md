[*AI 2040: Plan A*](https://ai-2040.com/AI-2040.pdf) is a 2026 conditional policy scenario from the AI Futures Project for slowing and governing a path to superintelligence, meaning AI far beyond the best human experts across most cognitive work. Its authors call it a recommendation, not their best-guess forecast. Four of its six authors also wrote [*AI 2027*](https://ai-2027.com/), the group's 2025 scenario about rapidly advancing AI.

[*The Three Crucibles*](https://www.cubagarcia.com/books/the-three-crucibles/) is my 2026 book about the economic and political passage through advanced automation. The June 24 edition's opening map names the Correction, the Void, and the Alignment; Transformation is the relevant chapter within the Void, not the second crucible's canonical name.

Both works trace the same economic chain: automated work can raise output while weakening labor income, tax revenue, and consumer demand. Plan A emphasizes verification, evidence that declared chips, data centers, and frontier AI research match reality. The book asks who owns productive AI and receives its returns. I think institutions also need exit proof: a funded, timed handover of one working AI service to a replacement team.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>An Alaska-style dividend answers part of the distribution problem, but it cannot reveal a secret data center or prove that an organization can replace its AI supplier.</p></div>

## The Void is the economic match

The [book's opening map](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Herbertcuba/herbertcuba-personal-blog/3bd6772f634c1c5dbeef60a71d012532302aae6c/src/static/downloads/three-crucibles.pdf#page=6) describes the Correction as the break between AI expectations and economic reality. In the Void, production recovers through automation without a matching recovery in employment. Its Transformation chapter connects lost labor income to weaker purchasing power and proposes a Citizen's Dividend, a universal cash payment funded by AI infrastructure. The Alignment asks whether that infrastructure creates shared abundance or concentrated power.

Plan A quantifies that chain. By mid-2031, AI performs one-third of cognitive labor, mainly analysis, communication, and professional judgment. As jobs are automated, income and payroll taxes shrink, so the United States sells permits for new computing and robot capacity. Most fees fund a Citizen's Dividend projected at $45,000 per adult in 2032 and about $1 million in 2035 within the scenario ([PDF pages 17–18](https://ai-2040.com/AI-2040.pdf#page=17)).

The funding methods differ. The book proposes a charge for each human-equivalent unit of deployed AI labor, the work one person would otherwise perform; Plan A charges for new capacity. Both still answer the same demand problem. As the book puts it, “Robots do not buy iPhones”: cheaper production cannot sustain an economy if households cannot buy its output.

I think this is meaningful convergence, not one-to-one validation. The book PDF entered its author-controlled public repository on March 23, 2026; a public Plan A link followed on July 9. Those dates establish the public sequence, not who privately developed the idea first.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The Void and Plan A independently connect automated work to weaker purchasing power and a Citizen's Dividend, although one taxes deployed AI labor and the other sells permits for new capacity.</p></div>

## Verification cannot settle ownership

Plan A answers covert state projects with declarations, chip tracking, infrastructure inspections, and largely transparent frontier research. Its appendix imagines 500,000 H100-equivalents hidden beside China's proposed Medog Hydropower Station, a huge hydro project whose construction and power could camouflage a data center. An H100-equivalent measures computing power against Nvidia's high-end H100 AI chip. In the scenario, the authors put the chance of reaching AI that beats the best human specialists without detection before 2043 below 10 percent.

The Death Star program in *Rogue One*, the 2016 *Star Wars* film, hides a planet-destroying weapon behind compartmentalized work. Its purpose can remain secret, but its construction, energy, cooling, and equipment still leave a footprint. Plan A makes that physical constraint the basis of verification.

The Alignment tackles visible, legal capacity. It can still concentrate returns and decision rights, so the book argues for distributed ownership and technical foundations that no private provider controls alone. My take is that inspection and ownership complement rather than validate each other. I don't know which control will face the harder test, but I think either alone lets power reconcentrate.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Inspection can make an undeclared AI project harder to conceal, but it cannot decide who receives the returns from capacity that is legal and visible.</p></div>

## Exit proof belongs in the renewal decision

Even verified and broadly owned infrastructure can leave an organization unable to change suppliers. The provider may still control the working setup, data connections, permissions, and tools that keep a service alive. Exit proof tests that dependence before another renewal deepens it.

Start with one workflow, one data export, one replacement team, and 30 days. The chief information officer owns renewal; the supplier funds the handover and transition help. The buyer owns the evaluation set, a sample of past cases with accepted outcomes, and receives the model version, system instructions, data-source history, permissions, tool details, and restoration guide.

A replacement may change the model, instructions, data-retrieval method, or workflow if it preserves the agreed business result. Missing the deadline, error limit, or cost ceiling means failure. Renewal remains conditional on passing; failure triggers continued access, supplier-funded repair, and enforceable transition support.

Since August 2, 2026, Article 50 of the [EU AI Act](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj/eng#art_50), the European Union's AI regulation, has required disclosure for certain AI interactions and AI-generated or manipulated content. Disclosure identifies AI involvement; it cannot prove that a working service will survive a supplier change. I think a failed handover predicts dependence that makes leaving costly or impractical even when every required disclosure is present.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Plan A makes secret capacity harder to hide, and The Three Crucibles keeps distribution in view. A timed handover closes the institutional gap by testing whether a buyer can still leave before dependence becomes too costly.</p></div>
