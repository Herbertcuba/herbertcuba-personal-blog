In 1976, Alaska chose to treat part of its mineral wealth as an inheritance rather than ordinary revenue. Voters amended the state constitution so that at least 25 percent of certain mineral revenues would enter the [Alaska Permanent Fund](https://apfc.org/the-fund/), a state-owned pool whose principal is invested instead of spent. The first Permanent Fund Dividend, a cash payment funded from investment earnings, followed in 1982.

That decision matters far beyond oil. It asks who should benefit when a productive asset becomes exceptionally valuable, even though its returns are not created by each resident's labor. Advanced AI raises the same question at a much larger scale.

[AI 2040: Plan A](https://ai-2040.com/) appeared in July 2026. The core framework of my speculative book, [The Three Crucibles](https://www.cubagarcia.com/books/the-three-crucibles/), was [in writing by February 3](https://github.com/Herbertcuba/herbertcuba-personal-blog/commit/f20db364742f9965d41e7d84ea2ccf1b0b41b97c), more than six months before this article, and the book was [publicly downloadable by March 23](https://github.com/Herbertcuba/herbertcuba-personal-blog/commit/ce380abb4f4f13f5fc660a2c2e3a6a75011184a8). The later overlap does not prove my map right. It makes the shared structure worth taking seriously.

  <span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
  <p>Alaska shows that distribution is an ownership decision made before resource income reaches citizens. AI 2040 and The Three Crucibles apply that old question to machine intelligence.</p>

## Plan A is a conditional future

The AI Futures Project, a research nonprofit that forecasts advanced AI and recommends policy responses, presents [Plan A](https://ai-2040.com/AI-2040.pdf) as “primarily a recommendation, not a prediction.” Four of its six authors also wrote [AI 2027](https://ai-2027.com/), their 2025 scenario about AI capabilities rising very quickly. Plan A imagines a United States-China agreement in 2029, a pause around top-human-expert AI in 2035, and superintelligence in 2040. Superintelligence means systems that outperform the best humans across essentially every task while working faster and more cheaply.

Its economic mechanism is just as important as its timeline. In the scenario, AI performs a third of cognitive labor, meaning work based mainly on reasoning and information, by mid-2031. Real economic output then grows by roughly 50 percent in 2032. As wage-tax revenue loses ground, the plan sells permits to build compute or robots and uses the proceeds for a Citizen's Dividend modeled on Alaska. Here, compute means the chips and data-center capacity used to run AI.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
  <span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
  <p>Plan A is a proposed route through rapid AI progress: international restraint, verifiable limits, extraordinary growth, and a public dividend when wages can no longer carry the tax system.</p>
</div>

## The three crucibles in plain language

My book organizes the transition into three periods. These are lenses for preparation, not promises that history will respect a calendar.

- **The Correction (2026):** AI-driven productivity begins separating economic output from human labor, exposing how much income and public revenue still depend on wages.
- **The Void (2027–2032):** the old wage-based settlement weakens before a replacement is secure. The book explores a Citizen's Dividend, distributed AI ownership, and taxes on AI labor as ways to bridge that period.
- **The Alignment (2035+):** society decides whether abundance is stewarded broadly or controlled by a small group. Here, alignment concerns institutions and ownership as well as the technical behavior of AI.

The middle period is transformative, but The Void is its current author-canonical name. Its danger comes from timing: productive capacity can change faster than the rules that give people income, standing, and influence.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
  <span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
  <p>The Correction breaks the work-income link, The Void is the unstable passage to a new settlement, and The Alignment determines who governs and benefits from abundance.</p>
</div>

## The agreement reveals a two-part problem

Both documents follow the same causal chain. AI takes on more cognitive work, labor income and the wage-tax base weaken, and rising output no longer guarantees broad purchasing power. Both arrive at a Citizen's Dividend and cite Alaska. They differ on funding: Plan A sells scarce permits for compute and robots, while The Three Crucibles emphasizes AI labor taxation and distributed ownership. They also differ on pace: my first period begins in 2026, while Plan A places its major labor and tax shock in 2031 and 2032.

Their deeper difference is between **control** and **claim**. Control asks whether powerful AI systems can be observed, limited, and stopped. Claim asks who owns the productive assets and receives their returns. The possible combinations are easy to miss:

- Strong control with broad claims can make abundance both governable and widely shared.
- Strong control with narrow claims can produce orderly concentration.
- Weak control with broad claims can distribute value while leaving dangerous systems unchecked.
- Weak control with narrow claims combines technical risk with concentrated power.

Plan A leans toward control through chip records, data-center inspections, and research transparency. A permit system could improve control while deepening concentration if only the largest firms can buy scarce capacity; paying out the fees would distribute income without distributing ownership. Plan A's own covert-project appendix estimates, within the scenario, a below 10 percent chance that a hidden project reaches AI better than top human experts undetected before 2043. My book leans toward claims through ownership and distribution. Each emphasis covers a weakness in the other: verifiable AI can still concentrate wealth, while broad ownership cannot make an uncontrollable system safe.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
  <span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
  <p>The shared warning is economic, but the combined framework is larger: a viable future needs both enforceable control over advanced AI and a broad claim on its productive returns.</p>
</div>

## Preparation without prophecy

No one knows which timeline, if any, will survive contact with reality. Over 2027–2029, I would weaken this diagnosis if labor share, the portion of national income paid to workers, does not fall; median wages after inflation rise each year; and ownership of leading AI infrastructure becomes less concentrated. If output races ahead while those measures move the other way, the institutional gap is opening even if every date in both maps is wrong.

That uncertainty is why The Three Crucibles exists as a preparation document. If Plan A and my book are even directionally right, capability will move before distribution and governance are ready. The useful question is therefore not whether 2040 arrives on schedule. It is whether we have decided, before the pressure peaks, who can stop the systems and who can claim what they produce.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
  <span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
  <p>The future is unknown, but the warning is testable. Watch labor income, real wages, ownership, and enforceable control, then prepare before technological capacity outruns the institutions meant to contain and share it.</p>
</div>
