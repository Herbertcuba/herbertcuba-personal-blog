In 1976, Alaskans voted to put at least 25 percent of certain mineral revenues in the [Alaska Permanent Fund](https://apfc.org/about/history/), a state-owned investment fund. Development at Prudhoe Bay, the huge oil field on Alaska's North Slope, had created an oil windfall. Instead of spending it all, Alaska invested a share. The first resident dividend followed in 1982 from surplus oil revenue, and eligible residents have received annual payments since.

Fifty years later, [AI 2040: Plan A](https://ai-2040.com/) and [The Three Crucibles](https://www.cubagarcia.com/books/the-three-crucibles/), my 2026 speculative book, both return to Alaska. Each imagines a Citizen's Dividend, meaning a direct public payment backed by society's claim on AI-related wealth. My book was public on my site in March 2026; AI 2040 appeared in July. That sequence is worth noting, but it proves neither account correct.

My book describes three Crucibles. The Correction is the shock as scalable machine intelligence weakens the economic value of human labor. The edition used here calls the second Crucible The Void, while the current book page calls the same 2027–2032 phase The Transformation; it is the passage in which wages weaken and new ways to distribute income must take over. The Alignment asks whether machine abundance will be broadly stewarded or controlled by a narrow owner class.

## AI 2040 is a plan presented as history

AI 2040 is a 2026 scenario from the AI Futures Project, a US research nonprofit also behind [AI 2027](https://ai-2027.com/), its 2025 account of a dangerous race toward advanced AI. The authors call Plan A primarily a recommendation, not a prediction, and tell it as future history so its moving parts can be seen together.

The scenario has the United States and China reaching an agreement in 2029 and avoiding near-total automation of AI research in 2030. Capability then grows within the human range, pauses at top-expert level in 2035, and proceeds to superintelligence in 2040: systems better than the best humans across essentially every task, while also faster and cheaper.

The plan relies on verification. It tracks advanced chips, declares compute, the processing capacity used to train and run AI, inspects datacenters, and opens research to scrutiny. It also projects that AI performs a third of cognitive work, meaning work based on reasoning and information, by mid-2031, followed by roughly 50 percent real economic growth in 2032. Those figures belong to the scenario; they are not observed evidence.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Plan A imagines a negotiated, verified path to superintelligence alongside economic change far outside normal policy cycles.</p>
</div>

## What happens when work stops carrying the system?

The Correction and AI 2040 start with the same mechanism. When firms replace paid work with machines, wage income and labor-tax revenue can shrink even as production rises. AI 2040 says its imagined tax base collapses as a share of the economy without reform. The Correction widens the question: what happens to demand when the old link between work and income can no longer support it?

The dates may be wrong, but the issue begins before superintelligence wherever machine output grows faster than institutions built around human wages.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Rising machine output can weaken the wage and tax systems that still support demand.</p>
</div>

## What does a citizen own?

Both accounts answer with a Citizen's Dividend and point back to Alaska. Plan A proposes permits for large AI and robot deployments, then a public Compute Dividend Corporation with one share per citizen and profits paid as dividends. The Three Crucibles emphasizes AI labor taxation, a levy on work performed by AI systems, and distributed ownership, which spreads claims on AI assets beyond a few firms.

The philosophical point is larger than basic income. Alaska created a public claim before the resource wealth was spent. If AI becomes essential infrastructure, waiting until ownership is settled may leave governments offering relief from wealth in which citizens hold no durable stake.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>A durable dividend needs a claim on the source of wealth, not only a promise of relief.</p>
</div>

## Can control be made trustworthy?

Here the accounts separate. AI 2040 puts much of its weight on transparency and verification. Its covert-project appendix estimates a below 10 percent chance that a hidden project reaches what it calls Top-Expert-Dominating AI without detection by 2043. The term means AI able to dominate top human experts. The scenario treats the risk as low, but not gone.

The Alignment puts ownership closer to the center. Verification can reveal whether a lab or country crossed an agreed capability threshold, but it cannot decide who deserves the resulting wealth. Broad ownership can address that claim, yet it cannot prove a powerful system safe. Each account exposes a gap in the other.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Verification governs dangerous capability; ownership governs the claim on its value.</p>
</div>

<p>Isaac Asimov's 1951 science-fiction novel follows mathematician Hari Seldon, who cannot stop an imperial collapse but creates an institution to shorten the disorder that follows. The mapping is limited: neither AI 2040 nor my book has Seldon's fictional certainty. The useful connection is preparation before events settle the argument.</p>

## Preparation without prophecy

No one knows what waits for us. AI progress may be slower, politics may reject the proposed bargains, and different problems may dominate. Two speculative works reaching similar conclusions are still two speculative works.

But if AI 2040 and The Three Crucibles are broadly right, we have a fairly bumpy road ahead. Institutions tied to wages will have to adjust while AI capabilities keep improving, and claims on new wealth will be contested after ownership has begun to concentrate. The Three Crucibles exists for that reason: as a preparation document, not a certificate that the future has been predicted.
