[AI 2040: Plan A](https://ai-2040.com/AI-2040.pdf) imagines humanity reaching superintelligence without losing control. Even its successful route passes through a labor shock and an economy rebuilt under geopolitical suspicion.

The authors call Plan A a recommendation written as future history. Four also wrote [AI 2027](https://ai-2027.com/), about a dangerous race to the same destination.

Written in 2025, [The Three Crucibles](/books/the-three-crucibles/) predates AI 2040 by more than six months. A public edition was on my site by March 23, 2026. I think the overlap tells us more than priority. Both begin with collapsed wage income and end with control.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>AI 2040's successful route still contains an economic and political rupture. The Three Crucibles described a similar passage months earlier.</p></div>

## The plan behind the date

The title sounds like a forecast, but the authors built Plan A as a policy scenario.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>A US-China agreement delays superintelligence until 2040. Declarations, inspections, a training pause, and visible frontier research are meant to keep the race under human control.</p></div>

In the scenario, the United States and China strike a deal in 2029. Without it, fully automated AI research would lead to superintelligence by the end of 2030. The deal slows that feedback loop. AI stays within the human range until 2035, pauses at top-expert capability, then resumes toward superintelligence in 2040.

The political bet feels closer to *Arrival* than *The Terminator*. Rivals must share enough information to act together. Major chip purchases are declared, datacenters inspected, and frontier research made visible to outsiders.

I think Plan A succeeds only through extraordinary coordination.

## The first Crucible is the labor shock

The book's fictional Company B makes the first convergence small enough to see.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Both works begin with automation beating the labor-heavy alternative. Output rises while wage income and the employment-based tax system weaken.</p></div>

Company A keeps 5,000 claims adjusters. Company B deploys an agentic system, cuts headcount by 40 percent, drops premiums by 20 percent, and wins within six months. This is the Correction: customers reward the lower price, so the harsh choice spreads.

AI 2040 expands that pressure across the economy. In its scenario, AI performs one third of cognitive labor by mid-2031. Real GDP growth reaches about 50 percent in 2032, while the tax base collapses as a share of GDP without reform.

I think both documents see the same paradox. Company B can become more productive while fewer wages circulate and governments lose revenue tied to human work.

## The same bridge over the income gap

Both maps use the same name for their answer to the demand problem: Citizen's Dividend.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Both works propose a Citizen's Dividend and cite Alaska's oil fund. Their funding mechanisms differ, while the economic purpose is the same.</p></div>

The book calls the second Crucible the Void: production recovers while purchasing power lags. "Robots do not buy iPhones." Company B can process claims cheaply, but it still needs paying policyholders. The book returns the proceeds from taxes on AI labor and computational output to citizens.

Plan A uses permits. Its Consortium caps new compute and robot production, then auctions the right to expand. Most American permit revenue funds a Citizen's Dividend. The report compares its Compute Dividend Corporation with the Alaska Permanent Fund. In the scenario, payments rise from $45,000 per person in 2032 to about $1 million in 2035.

Those numbers may be wrong by a wide margin. I think the shared arithmetic carries more weight: once labor stops distributing purchasing power, another channel has to do the job.

## Verification meets ownership

AI 2040 and The Three Crucibles use different safeguards against concentrated power.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>AI 2040 relies on verification to restrain concentrated power. The Third Crucible adds the question of who owns the intelligence infrastructure.</p></div>

Plan A makes compute visible and spreads frontier development among regulated actors. Its covert-project appendix has China hide the equivalent of 500,000 H100 GPUs in tunnels near the Medog Hydropower Station. US intelligence estimates that the chance of reaching AI above top human experts undetected stays below 10 percent until 2043.

Even below 10 percent, Plan A still depends on detection working across rival states for years.

The Alignment asks whether AI owners become stewards or feudal lords. Plan A gives each US citizen one share in a dividend corporation. The book pushes further toward distributed ownership of the intelligence layer.

For Company B, a dividend may help its former adjusters pay their bills. Ownership decides who can set the terms of the system that replaced them. I think a stable settlement needs verification and a public claim on the productive asset.

## A preparation document for the in-between

These maps remain useful even when their dates drift.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The timelines can be wrong while the structural warning holds. Preparation means writing new rules for income and ownership before the old bargain becomes impossible to restore.</p></div>

I don't know whether the 2029 treaty appears, takeoff arrives in 2030, or Company B wins that fast. The AI 2040 authors claim no certainty either. [Long-range plans become fragile](/posts/i-stopped-planning-five-years-out/) when capabilities move this quickly.

The Three Crucibles was written as a preparation document for that uncertainty. It identifies the decisions about income and ownership that arrive before abundance.

If AI 2040 and The Three Crucibles are even roughly right, the road ahead will be bumpy. Company B's six-month decision becomes society's long passage. The machines may produce abundance. People will still need an income, and we will still have to decide who owns the machines.
