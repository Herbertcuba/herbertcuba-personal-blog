In 1976, Alaska’s voters amended their constitution to send at least 25 percent of certain mineral revenue to the [Alaska Permanent Fund](https://apfc.org/about/history/), a state-owned investment fund. It turns finite oil wealth into a lasting public asset. The first dividend was paid in 1982, and eligible residents now receive an annual payment funded by the fund’s earnings. The decision established a public claim before the resource wealth was gone.

Half a century later, [AI 2040: Plan A](https://ai-2040.com/AI-2040.pdf) uses that precedent for artificial intelligence. The 2026 scenario comes from the AI Futures Project, a research nonprofit focused on advanced AI. Its proposed Compute Dividend Corporation receives a share of compute and robot permit-fee revenue, rising from 25 percent in 2032 to 75 percent in 2035, and pays its profits as a citizen dividend. Compute is the processing capacity used to train and run AI.

This resembles the sequence in my 2026 speculative book, [*The Three Crucibles*](https://www.cubagarcia.com/books/the-three-crucibles/), which was public before Plan A appeared. One scenario does not prove another, but their shared question deserves attention.

## What Plan A is trying to do

Plan A calls itself “primarily a recommendation, not a prediction.” Four of its six authors also worked on [AI 2027](https://ai-2027.com/), their 2025 scenario about rapid AI development and loss of control.

Its preferred route begins with a US-China agreement in 2029. Major chip sales and data centers are declared, facilities can be inspected, and AI research is shared. This is verification: countries receive evidence that agreed limits are being kept. Development pauses near top-human-expert level in 2035, then advances in 2040 to superintelligence, meaning systems better than the best humans at essentially every task while also faster and cheaper.

The economic shock comes first. Plan A projects that AI performs a third of cognitive labor, work based mainly on knowledge, by mid-2031. It also projects roughly 50 percent growth in real GDP, or inflation-adjusted economic output, in 2032. As wage income and its tax base weaken, scarce compute and robot permits fund the dividend. This depends on international coordination, visible computing capacity and enforceable limits.

## The three crucibles in plain language

*The Three Crucibles* frames the transition as three social problems.

**The Correction** is the economic break. As AI makes cognitive work cheaper, more of the gain flows to capital owners while wages and labor-based taxes lose part of their role.

**The Transformation** is the bridge. The book proposes universal basic income, recurring cash without a work requirement, together with a Citizen’s Dividend tied to AI infrastructure and a tax on AI labor.

**The Alignment** is the political choice. It asks whether society becomes a steward of abundance or lets ownership and power settle among a small group of new feudal lords.

<p>The crucibles describe an economic correction, a distribution bridge and a choice over who benefits from abundance.</p>

## The shared sequence hides a real disagreement

Both works say the decisive break may arrive before superintelligence. Production can separate from wages, the tax system can weaken with them, and a citizen dividend can become economic infrastructure. The dividend idea is older than either work, but their shared Alaska mechanism is unusually specific.

They differ over what carries the system across that break. Plan A details verification and collection: coordinated limits make permits scarce, and their fees create the dividend pool. My book emphasizes distributed AI ownership, meaning ownership and control of AI systems and infrastructure spread beyond a few firms. I treat that as a direction, not a finished cross-border legal vehicle. Verification constrains what gets built; ownership determines who receives a claim on permitted output.

Plan A tests its weak point in an appendix. It puts the chance below 10 percent that a covert project reaches AI capable of dominating top human experts before 2043 while remaining undetected. The permit model weakens if capacity escapes the agreement, while broader ownership cannot stop a dangerous capability race by itself.

The sequence is testable: if US employee compensation and federal payroll-tax receipts both stay within one percentage point of their 2026 shares of GDP through 2030 while output grows, the near-term Correction is late or wrong.

<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>The maps share an economic sequence but rely on different safeguards. Plan A needs enforceable visibility; distributed ownership still needs a durable legal form and capability controls.</p>
</div>

## Preparation without prophecy

I do not know whether a 2029 treaty, a 2035 pause or Plan A’s permit economy will happen. Its authors do not claim to know either. The convergence matters because economic disruption can outrun the institutions meant to distribute its gains.

That is why *The Three Crucibles* is a preparation document. It is meant to make the stages recognizable before emergency policy becomes a permanent settlement.

No one knows what awaits. If Plan A’s authors and I are broadly right about the sequence, the road to abundance will be bumpy long before anyone can declare that superintelligence has arrived.
