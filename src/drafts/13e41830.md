[*AI 2040*](https://ai-2040.com/) is a story about civilization. Its economic logic fits inside two insurance companies.

One keeps its 5,000 claims adjusters. The other deploys an AI claims system, cuts headcount by 40 percent, lowers premiums by 20 percent, and wins within six months. That thought experiment comes from [*The Three Crucibles*](/books/the-three-crucibles/).

Plan A scales the choice from a claims desk to nations. It is a [90-page scenario and policy proposal](https://ai-2040.com/AI-2040.pdf) from the AI Futures Project. Four of its six authors also wrote [*AI 2027*](https://ai-2027.com/). They ask whether world governments can reach superintelligence and govern the transition.

The manuscript for *The Three Crucibles* was written in 2025. [A public edition was on my site by March 23, 2026](https://github.com/Herbertcuba/herbertcuba-personal-blog/commit/ce380abb4f4f13f5fc660a2c2e3a6a75011184a8). [*AI 2040* was publicly linked on July 9](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48848425). I think the sequence is interesting. AI 2040 starts with frontier labs and geopolitics; the book starts with companies and households. The overlap is structural evidence. Proof will have to wait.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>AI 2040 turns the race to superintelligence into a policy scenario. The Three Crucibles approached the same transition from work and demand months earlier, and the overlap is unusually specific.</p></div>

## A plan for the dangerous middle

The authors present Plan A as a recommendation told through a scenario. In their path, the US and China make a deal in 2029. It interrupts a reckless branch where fully automated AI research arrives in 2030 and drives a sprint toward superintelligence. Development stays inside the human range, pauses at top-human-expert capability in 2035, then resumes toward superintelligence in 2040.

The governance is physical. Countries declare major compute holdings, and inspectors verify datacenters. Nearly all AI research becomes public while inference stays private. Additional compute requires permits. Several companies remain near the frontier, limiting any one institution's grip on the stack.

I think every future scenario is also a moral document. Plan A chooses human control, then gives that value inspectors and brakes. Our insurer's provider trains inside declared infrastructure, exposes most research, and needs permits to scale.

The scenario tests its weakest seam too. A covert Chinese datacenter is built in tunnels beside the Medog Hydropower Station, using 500,000 smuggled H100-equivalent chips. About 200 people know. I think that appendix earns its place. Verification narrows uncertainty, yet secrecy keeps the race alive.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Plan A uses a 2029 deal, public research, and verified compute to buy time before a 2040 move to superintelligence. Its covert-project branch shows how a hidden datacenter could reopen the race.</p></div>

## The maps meet at the broken paycheck

The book's interior describes three stages. The Correction forces companies to automate as labor loses value. During The Void, output recovers faster than jobs. Alignment is the political settlement over who owns the machines and how abundance is shared.

*AI 2040* reaches the same pressure through its scenario economics. AI and robots perform about 20 percent of labor by economic value in 2032 and about 85 percent in 2035. Labor-tax revenue shrinks. Most permit income eventually flows to adults through the "Citizen's Dividend." Plan A puts that annual figure at $45,000 per person in 2032 and about $1 million in 2035.

The phrase is identical to the one in *The Three Crucibles*. Both documents point to the Alaska Permanent Fund as a precedent for sharing income from a common resource.

Return to the insurer. Cutting 40 percent of its claims staff improves its prices, but those former employees were also consumers and taxpayers. Its win spreads a problem through the wider economy. The book puts it bluntly: "Robots do not buy iPhones." Output can rise while demand loses the wages that supported it.

The documents choose different funding mechanisms. Plan A taxes access to scarce compute and robotics. *The Three Crucibles* argues for AI labor taxation, distributed AI ownership, and basic income. I think the clearest convergence is that automation must create a claim for the people whose wages it reduces.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Both documents expect output to climb while household income loses its old anchor in work. The same Alaska precedent leads them toward a Citizen's Dividend, financed through different mechanisms.</p></div>

## Verification and ownership answer different questions

Plan A makes research visible and keeps inference private. *The Three Crucibles* places more weight on who owns the intelligence layer. Its Alignment stage argues for open infrastructure, open models, and local hardware. I think both approaches need each other.

Picture *Snowpiercer* with perfect sensors in every carriage. The sensors can show an overheating engine or hidden fuel. They cannot choose the destination. The insurer faces the same split. An audit shows how its provider behaves. Ownership and a practical exit determine whether it can choose another one.

> Visibility can reveal the machine. Ownership decides who may redirect it.

Plan A carries ownership further than a cash transfer. Each US citizen receives one share in a Compute Dividend Corporation. Yet its 2040 endpoint gradually hands institutions and equipment to AIs judged aligned, until humans can no longer shut everything down. This is the sharpest divergence. *The Three Crucibles* treats Alignment as a human settlement over ownership; Plan A eventually passes the torch.

I don't know which path geopolitics will allow. If AI productivity rises while ordinary wages keep demand alive and ownership remains broad, the book's core argument weakens. That remains possible.

If *AI 2040* and *The Three Crucibles* are even roughly right, the road ahead will be bumpy. Plan A is a vehicle for stress-testing recommendations. *The Three Crucibles* exists as a preparation document for that road, while human choices can still change the destination.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Treaties and inspections may buy time, while ownership keeps agency distributed. Preparation preserves human choice before today's defaults harden into tomorrow's institutions.</p></div>
