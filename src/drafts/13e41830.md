[AI 2040: Plan A](https://ai-2040.com/AI-2040.pdf) is a 90-page attempt to make an almost absurd question operational. How could the United States, China and the rest of the world approach superintelligence without a race ending in catastrophe or extreme concentration of power?

The report comes from the [AI Futures Project](https://ai-2040.com/). Four of its six authors also wrote [AI 2027](https://ai-2027.com/). They describe Plan A as a recommendation expressed through a scenario. Its dates put that proposal under pressure.

I think its value comes from forcing institutions into the picture. Chip inventories, datacenter inspections, production permits and public dividends all appear on the same map.

In *The Three Crucibles*, one insurer keeps 5,000 claims adjusters. A rival deploys an agentic AI system, cuts headcount by 40 percent, lowers premiums by 20 percent and wins the thought experiment within six months. At national scale, that same efficiency reaches the tax base and the customer.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>AI 2040 turns superintelligence into a concrete governance scenario. The insurer thought experiment shows the same pressure at company level: automation lowers costs while wages, tax revenue and consumer demand come under strain.</p></div>

## The deal that buys time

In Plan A's scenario, the United States and China make a deal in 2029 to avoid a reckless race. Fully automated AI R&D in 2030 would otherwise lead to superintelligence by year-end. The deal stretches the transition: capabilities grow within the human range until 2035, pause at top-human-expert level, then move toward superintelligence in 2040.

Verification carries much of the weight. Major datacenter owners declare chip purchases and sales. Rival powers inspect infrastructure. AI research becomes almost entirely transparent while inference remains private.

In a hypothetical branch, China hides 500,000 H100-equivalents of compute in tunnels adjacent to the Medog Hydropower Station. Roughly 200 people know about the project. The branch exposes the verification problem inside the authors' own proposal.

Our insurer could still automate fast through providers operating inside inspected datacenters and declared capacity. I think the timeline is more useful as an institutional rehearsal than a calendar. Plan A treats compute like the spice in *Dune*. It counts the stock, controls the routes and searches for hidden reserves. That visibility is valuable even when authority gathers in very few hands.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Plan A slows takeoff through a 2029 deal, verified datacenters and transparent research, then pauses at top-human-expert AI in 2035. Its covert-project branch shows how much trust, inspection and state capacity the bargain would require.</p></div>

## One dividend in two maps

A public edition of [*The Three Crucibles*](https://www.cubagarcia.com/books/the-three-crucibles/) was available on cubagarcia.com by March 23, 2026. [AI 2040 was publicly linked](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48848425) on July 9. This sequence says nothing about influence or independent discovery.

I think the convergence deserves attention because it is unusually specific. *The Three Crucibles* moves through the Correction, the Void and the Alignment. Firms automate for survival. The economy recovers without the old job base. The final conflict concerns ownership and distribution.

Plan A runs through the same economic rupture. In its scenario, AI and robots rise from about 20 percent of labor by economic value in 2032 to about 85 percent in 2035. Payroll revenue shrinks. The state caps and auctions permits to build compute and robots, then directs most permit fees into a Citizen's Dividend. For American adults, the annual payment rises from $45,000 in 2032 to roughly $1 million in 2035, in real 2025 dollars.

Both documents use the term "Citizen's Dividend." Both point to Alaska's oil dividend as precedent. The book states the demand problem in one blunt line: "Robots do not buy iPhones."

If every insurer removes payroll faster than it creates new buyers, low premiums cannot repair the demand side. A dividend keeps customers in the economy. The book proposes AI labor taxation. Plan A auctions scarce production capacity. Both routes assume productivity can detach from wages and a new demand circuit must follow.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The two maps converge on forced automation, a shrinking labor-tax base and a Citizen's Dividend tied to shared productive wealth. Their funding mechanisms differ, but both treat distribution as economic infrastructure.</p></div>

## The ownership question remains

Plan A gives its strongest answer through broad visibility, dozens of frontier firms and one share in a Compute Dividend Corporation for every US citizen. That is a serious institutional design extending well beyond cash transfers.

*The Three Crucibles* pushes ownership further upstream through distributed AI ownership, open infrastructure and open models. For the insurer, distributed ownership has to survive a supplier change to mean anything. It needs the right to replace the provider while keeping the decision history and evaluations required to operate.

My concern weakens if transparency creates enforceable governance rights, practical portability and durable access to that history. It gets stronger when dividends spread while rule-setting remains concentrated.

AI 2040 examines technical loss of control in much more depth. *The Three Crucibles* spends more time on the political economy of abundance. I think those gaps belong in the same conversation. Plan A watches the hidden project. *The Three Crucibles* watches legal concentration that happens in plain sight. A model that escapes human control can defeat both.

I don't know which path geopolitics will allow. The uncertainty survives every scenario. Both maps suggest a bumpy road through forced automation, weakened labor income and a struggle over who controls the productive system. *The Three Crucibles* exists for that reason, as a preparation document for choices that arrive before certainty does.

Waiting for certainty would leave the ownership decision to whoever builds first.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Plan A spreads money and research visibility, while The Three Crucibles pushes harder on ownership and exit. They leave us with different meanings of human control at the far side of the transition. Their shared warning gives us enough reason to prepare now.</p></div>
