In 1976, Alaska made a decision before its oil wealth had fully arrived. Voters created the Alaska Permanent Fund, a state-owned investment fund that saves part of the revenue from a non-renewable resource. Eligible residents have received annual cash dividends from its earnings since the first payment in 1982.

That policy choice sits near the center of two new maps of the AI future. One is [AI 2040: Plan A](https://ai-2040.com/), a policy scenario from the AI Futures Project. The other is my speculative book, [*The Three Crucibles*](https://www.cubagarcia.com/books/the-three-crucibles/). A downloadable book PDF and a homepage link were added in March 2026; AI 2040 was publicly available by July 9.

Both works ask what happens when machine intelligence becomes a productive resource, human labor loses economic weight, and citizens need a claim on the resulting wealth. Their agreement cannot prove that either map is right, but it makes the warning harder to dismiss.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Alaska turned resource income into a public asset and a resident dividend. Both AI maps see that model as relevant when wealth shifts away from labor.</p></div>

## What Plan A asks us to imagine

[AI 2040](https://ai-2040.com/AI-2040.pdf) is a 90-page scenario from the AI Futures Project, a US research nonprofit. Four of the five front-page authors of [*AI 2027*](https://ai-2027.com/), its April 2025 rapid-development scenario, also appear in Plan A's main byline. They call the new work “primarily a recommendation, not a prediction.”

The scenario imagines a US–China agreement in 2029 that prevents fully automated AI research and development in 2030. Development continues within the range of human ability, pauses in 2035 at the level of leading human experts, then moves toward superintelligence in 2040. Here, superintelligence means AI broadly better than the best humans while also faster and cheaper.

Plan A tries to control that path through compute, the processing capacity used to train and run AI. By mid-2031, AI performs a third of cognitive labor in the scenario, weakening wage-tax revenue. Governments limit new compute and robot capacity, then sell buyable permits granting the right to add more. Proceeds go to a Compute Dividend Corporation in which every US citizen receives a share. Inspections, chip tracking, and research transparency are meant to make the limits verifiable rather than merely promised.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Plan A slows the race, monitors computing capacity, and turns permits for new AI infrastructure into citizen-owned income.</p></div>

## The three crucibles in plain language

My book describes the transition through three stages.

**The Correction** is the repricing of human cognitive labor as AI makes more of it cheap and abundant. Business models, wages, and public finances built around scarce human expertise begin to break.

**The Void** is the unstable interval after paid work loses part of its role as society's main distributor of income, status, and purpose. The book proposes a Citizen's Dividend to preserve purchasing power. By AI labor taxation, I mean a levy paid by firms when productive work moves from taxed human wages to AI systems, helping replace part of the shrinking wage-tax base. The book sets a direction, not a finished tax code.

**The Alignment** is the political choice that follows. AI can broaden ownership and make more people stewards of abundance, or it can concentrate wealth and decisions among the owners of the infrastructure.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The Correction breaks the old price of labor, the Void exposes the gap left by wage-based income and purpose, and the Alignment decides how AI wealth and power are shared.</p></div>

## Why the maps converge

AI 2040 begins with safety and geopolitics, while The Three Crucibles begins with economics. They still trace the same chain. Automation weakens wage income and its tax base. Production can keep rising, but demand falters when people lose purchasing power. As the book puts it, “Robots do not buy iPhones.”

Both works then use the name Citizen's Dividend and the Alaska precedent. Both also end at a question of power: whether AI's gains spread widely or harden into lasting concentration. That is a specific convergence, although it remains a shared hypothesis rather than evidence that their dates will come true.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Both maps connect automated labor, weaker wage-based income, a citizen dividend, and a struggle over concentrated power.</p></div>

## Where the bets differ

Plan A places its largest bet on verification: states declare capacity, inspectors check it, and tracked chips make hidden development harder. Its appendix tests this idea with a covert Chinese project. The report estimates less than a 10% chance that such a project reaches what it calls top-expert-dominating AI before 2043 without being detected. The term means AI that outperforms leading human specialists.

The Three Crucibles puts more weight on ownership. Verification may show that a system follows agreed limits, yet it cannot decide who receives the gains. Broad ownership can improve distribution, but it cannot prevent loss of control by itself. A durable path needs answers to both problems.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>AI 2040 asks how advanced AI can remain visible and controlled. The Three Crucibles asks who owns its output.</p></div>

## Preparation without prophecy

No one knows whether the 2029 agreement, the 2035 pause, or the economic sequence will happen. Physical limits, politics, and human resistance may bend the path beyond recognition. I do not know which parts of either map will survive contact with reality.

But if AI 2040 and The Three Crucibles are even directionally right, the road ahead is likely to be bumpy. Institutions will have to renegotiate income, ownership, and control while the technology keeps moving. That is the purpose of my book: a preparation document for choices that become much harder once the Correction is under way.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Neither work can predict the future. Together they offer a reason to prepare for a difficult transition in labor, distribution, ownership, and control.</p></div>
