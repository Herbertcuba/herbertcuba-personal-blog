In 1976, the US state of Alaska created a permanent fund from part of its public oil wealth. Payments began in 1982, giving eligible residents a direct financial claim on a shared resource rather than leaving all the proceeds inside government.

[*AI 2040: Plan A*](https://ai-2040.com/) is a 2026 governance scenario from the AI Futures Project, a forecasting group focused on advanced AI. Four of its six authors also wrote [*AI 2027*](https://ai-2027.com/), their 2025 scenario about a race toward systems beyond human control. Plan A is a recommendation rather than the authors' best-guess forecast. It uses Alaska to model a Citizen's Dividend: public payments funded by AI-era production.

[*The Three Crucibles*](https://www.cubagarcia.com/books/the-three-crucibles/), my book about AI, work, and ownership, reaches the same mechanism. An [author-controlled repository](https://github.com/Herbertcuba/herbertcuba-personal-blog/commit/ce380abb4f4f13f5fc660a2c2e3a6a75011184a8) added a PDF on March 23, 2026. The immutable edition cited here is a later artifact dated June 24; a public submission linked to *AI 2040* on July 9. The records show a public sequence, but not whether every relevant passage was in the March file, when either idea began, or who influenced whom.

I think the value lies in the thematic parallel. Both works connect automated work to weaker labor income and a public claim on machine output. Their difference reveals three layers: verification locates AI capacity, ownership allocates its gains, and exit proof shows whether an institution can act when a supplier relationship fails.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The public dates provide context without proving independence or influence. Both works reach a Citizen's Dividend, then offer different answers to the control problem.</p></div>

## The Correction and the Void meet Plan A

The [June 24 edition of *The Three Crucibles*](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Herbertcuba/herbertcuba-personal-blog/3bd6772f634c1c5dbeef60a71d012532302aae6c/src/static/downloads/three-crucibles.pdf#page=6) names the Correction, the Void, and the Alignment. The Correction begins as AI raises output while reducing paid work. In the Void, the economy can recover without restoring those jobs. Its Transformation chapter proposes a [Citizen's Dividend](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Herbertcuba/herbertcuba-personal-blog/3bd6772f634c1c5dbeef60a71d012532302aae6c/src/static/downloads/three-crucibles.pdf#page=36) to preserve demand because “Robots do not buy iPhones,” while the Alignment asks who owns the productive systems.

Plan A reaches the same chain through scenario arithmetic. By mid-2031, AI performs one-third of cognitive labor, meaning work based mainly on analysis, communication, research, or professional judgment. As paid work contracts, wage and payroll taxes weaken. Plan A sells permits to build robots and AI computing capacity, the chips and data centers used for AI work, then directs most revenue to a Citizen's Dividend projected at $45,000 per American adult in 2032 ([PDF pages 13 and 17–18](https://ai-2040.com/AI-2040.pdf#page=13)).

The funding methods differ: my book taxes deployed AI work based on its human equivalent, while Plan A charges for permission to build robots or computing capacity. I think the overlap is the cause-and-effect chain and Alaska precedent, not the phrase, formula, or date. You do not need to accept Plan A's timeline to test that structure.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The Correction and the Void describe production separating from wages and demand. Plan A models the same chain, but the book taxes deployed AI work while Plan A charges for new robots and computing capacity.</p></div>

## Verification can expose capacity

Plan A centers on takeoff, a feedback loop in which AI automates research that improves AI itself. In its scenario, the United States and China agree in 2029 to slow the loop, avoiding a route to superintelligence by the end of 2030. That means systems better than humans across almost all intellectual work. Progress pauses in 2035 at top-expert capability, roughly the level of leading specialists on defined tasks, and resumes in 2040.

The agreement depends on verification: records and inspections used to reveal undeclared chips and data centers. Plan A stress-tests this with an imagined Chinese facility beside the planned Medog Hydropower Station in Tibet. Like *The Matrix*, the 1999 science-fiction film in which a virtual world depends on physical machines, secret software still needs chips, power, cooling, and people. I think those physical needs make verification credible.

The Alignment starts where inspection ends. The book calls for [distributed AI ownership](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Herbertcuba/herbertcuba-personal-blog/3bd6772f634c1c5dbeef60a71d012532302aae6c/src/static/downloads/three-crucibles.pdf#page=57), including open infrastructure, open models, and laws against a private intelligence monopoly. Visibility gives citizens no claim on output, so even perfect verification could certify concentrated ownership. I don't know whether Plan A's international system could withstand sustained US-China rivalry, and its authors do not claim certainty. Verification governs hidden capacity; ownership allocates the gains.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Plan A uses records, inspections, and physical infrastructure to expose secret AI capacity. The Alignment addresses a separate risk: visible capacity can still produce concentrated ownership and power.</p></div>

## Exit proof makes bargaining power testable

At company scale, control can move to an AI supplier even when the buyer owns the final documents. The supplier may still hold the usable data, permissions, settings, tool connections, and instructions needed to keep a recurring process alive. A company can receive transparent reports and remain unable to leave.

The book's [fictional insurer example](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Herbertcuba/herbertcuba-personal-blog/3bd6772f634c1c5dbeef60a71d012532302aae6c/src/static/downloads/three-crucibles.pdf#page=21) makes the pressure concrete. One insurer keeps 5,000 claims adjusters, who investigate claims and decide payments. A rival uses AI, cuts staff by 40 percent, lowers premiums by 20 percent, and wins within six months. The numbers are illustrative, not a reported case.

I call the practical test exit proof: a funded, independent handover showing that another team can run one bounded workflow. The contract should make renewal conditional on a supplier-funded 30-day test using a complete data extract, transferable permissions, versioned settings, working tool connections, and operating and recovery instructions. The buyer owns the evaluation set, a fixed sample of past cases used to judge the result. The replacement may change the AI model or internal setup, but must preserve the agreed outcome within limits for errors, time, and cost.

The chief information officer, or CIO, should own acceptance and renewal. A missing or unusable artifact, permission, connection, or instruction fails the test, as does a result outside the agreed limits. No pass means no ordinary renewal; the supplier funds correction and transition support. If another team cannot run the workflow under controlled conditions, supplier dependence has been demonstrated.

Since August 2, 2026, [Article 50 of the EU AI Act](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj/eng#art_50), the European Union's main AI law, has required disclosure in certain AI interactions and machine-readable marking for some AI-generated or manipulated content. That helps reveal AI use, but it does not deliver usable data, permissions, settings, tool connections, or instructions to a replacement team.

I think this is where the civilizational argument becomes a contract question, because leverage exists before dependence. If exit cannot be demonstrated before renewal, control already belongs to the supplier.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Exit proof makes bargaining power falsifiable: a supplier-funded replacement team must run a bounded workflow from transferred data, permissions, settings, tool connections, and instructions. Missing inputs or failed outcomes block ordinary renewal and trigger funded correction and transition support.</p></div>
