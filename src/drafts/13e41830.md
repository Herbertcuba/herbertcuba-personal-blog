The Alaska Permanent Fund began with a decision about an oil windfall. After Prudhoe Bay transformed the finances of the US state, Alaskans voted in 1976 to set aside part of its mineral revenue for long-term investment. The first resident dividend came in 1982 from surplus oil revenue, and eligible Alaskans have received annual payments since then.

The fund did not make every Alaskan equally wealthy. It did give citizens a direct claim on value from a shared resource, which is why this old institution has become relevant to artificial intelligence.

Alaska appears in both [*AI 2040: Plan A*](https://ai-2040.com/AI-2040.pdf), a 2026 scenario from the US nonprofit AI Futures Project, and [my speculative book, *The Three Crucibles*](https://www.cubagarcia.com/books/the-three-crucibles/). Both call their proposed public share a Citizen's Dividend. My book was publicly downloadable on March 23, 2026; *AI 2040* was publicly available on July 9, 2026. Those dates show sequence, not influence or truth.

The three Crucibles are easy to state. **The Correction** is the break between work and income as AI makes more labor economically optional. **The Transformation** is the rebuilding of demand and distribution after that break. **The Alignment** asks whether productive AI capacity will be broadly owned and governed or concentrated among a few institutions.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Alaska supplies both works with a real precedent: shared resource wealth can create a direct citizen claim. The comparison then follows labor, distribution, and power.</p></div>

## The Correction changes what work pays for

*AI 2040* comes from the organization behind *AI 2027*, its earlier account of a dangerous race toward advanced AI. Plan A is a recommendation rather than a prediction. It imagines a US-China agreement in 2029, a slowdown in 2030, several years near human expert ability, and a guarded move to superintelligence in 2040. Here, superintelligence means AI that exceeds human ability across almost all important intellectual work.

By mid-2031, AI performs a third of cognitive labor in the scenario. That means less income arriving through human knowledge work and less tax collected from wages. The Correction describes the same mechanism without depending on that exact timetable: when machines do more valuable work at lower cost, employment can no longer support the economic system in the way it once did.

The two accounts may be wrong about speed. Their common point is harder to dismiss: productivity can keep rising even while labor loses bargaining power and purchasing power.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Plan A gives the labor break a rapid timeline; The Correction gives it a structural meaning. More output does not guarantee that wages remain the main route to income.</p></div>

## The Transformation makes distribution part of the infrastructure

Plan A responds by governing compute, the processing capacity used to train and run AI systems. It would cap advanced compute, track powerful chips, inspect data centers, and let firms buy and sell permits within the limit. The permit fees would become public revenue.

A Compute Dividend Corporation would give each citizen one share and pay out profits. My book also proposes a Citizen's Dividend tied to AI infrastructure and cites Alaska. Plan A charges for rights to scarce compute; *The Three Crucibles* emphasizes taxes on work performed by AI and distributed ownership of AI capacity.

The philosophy is shared. Production alone does not create public abundance; people also need a claim on the income and assets behind it. Once wages weaken, distribution becomes part of the system's basic design.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Both works call for a Citizen's Dividend and cite Alaska, but they fund it differently. Their deeper agreement is that distribution cannot remain an afterthought.</p></div>

## The Alignment asks what control is for

Plan A answers dangerous capability with verification, meaning evidence that actors stay within agreed limits. It combines chip tracking, data-center inspections, research transparency, and international coordination. The system depends on finding hidden capacity and enforcing the rules.

Appendix D tests that weakness. Its authors put the chance below 10 percent that a covert project reaches Top-Expert-Dominating AI without detection before 2043. Their term means a system that outperforms leading human experts. The estimate is low, but the failure would be enormous.

The Alignment starts from another concern. Even perfect compliance leaves a political question open: who receives the income and decision-making power attached to AI infrastructure? Plan A partly answers through its public dividend corporation. My book goes further toward distributed ownership, while giving less detail to keeping highly capable systems under human control.

Those are complementary gaps. Restraint without a durable public claim can preserve concentrated power, while broad ownership without technical control can distribute the benefits of a system that nobody can safely govern.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Plan A concentrates on verifiable restraint; The Alignment concentrates on ownership and political legitimacy. A stable future may need both.</p></div>

## Useful agreement without false certainty

Two speculative works reaching similar conclusions does not validate either one. AI may advance more slowly, physical bottlenecks may dominate, and political coordination may fail in forms neither document anticipates. No one knows what comes next, including me and the authors of *AI 2040*.

That uncertainty is why *The Three Crucibles* exists as a preparation document rather than a prophecy. If both accounts are directionally right, we face a bumpy transition from wages to new forms of distribution, and from private technical capacity to public questions of control. Preparation cannot remove uncertainty, but it can stop us from meeting those questions empty-handed.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The overlap is a warning, not proof. If labor, distribution, ownership, and technical control shift together, preparing before the timetable is certain is the more responsible choice.</p></div>
