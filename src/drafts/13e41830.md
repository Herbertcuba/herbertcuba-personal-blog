[AI 2040: Plan A](https://ai-2040.com/AI-2040.pdf) is a 90-page scenario and policy proposal published in 2026 by the AI Futures Project, the US research nonprofit behind [AI 2027](https://ai-2027.com/), its 2025 scenario about rapid AI progress. The authors call Plan A “primarily a recommendation, not a prediction.” That distinction matters because its dates are conditional, while its political questions are already real.

One of its clearest answers comes from the Alaska Permanent Fund. Alaska voters created the public investment fund in 1976 to preserve part of the state’s oil wealth, rather than spend it all at once. Since 1982, investment earnings have supported annual payments to eligible residents. The principle is simple: citizens retain a financial claim on extraordinary wealth drawn from a shared resource.

My 2026 speculative book, [The Three Crucibles](https://www.cubagarcia.com/books/the-three-crucibles/), condenses the coming pressure into three stages:

- **The Correction (2026)** is the economic shock as AI turns more cognitive work into capital, weakening wages and labor-based taxes.
- **The Void (2027–2032)** is the unstable interval after the old bargain around work begins to fail but before a new system of income and ownership is secure.
- **The Alignment (2035+)** is the political choice between broad stewardship of AI-created wealth and concentrated control.

The current book landing page uses **The Transformation** for that same second phase; the taxonomy I use here names it The Void. A public repository commit dated March 23, 2026, added the book PDF and its download link. The AI 2040 site was publicly archived by July 9, 2026. That chronology places a version of the book in the public record before the documented July archive of Plan A; it proves neither independent origin nor accuracy.

## Work stops carrying the system

In Plan A’s scenario, a US–China agreement in 2029 prevents the near-total automation of AI research that would otherwise arrive in 2030. Capability then grows within the human range, pauses around top-human-expert level in 2035, and resumes in 2040 toward superintelligence, meaning systems that outperform the best humans across almost every task while working faster and more cheaply.

The economic break arrives earlier. As machines replace paid labor, income and payroll taxes shrink relative to production. Plan A calls this a tax-base collapse. The Correction describes the same mechanism from the other side: productivity increasingly belongs to capital, so employment can no longer carry income distribution, consumer demand, and public revenue as reliably as before.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Both accounts expect machine production to weaken the link between work, household income, and the taxes that support the state.</p>
</div>

## Distribution becomes infrastructure

Once wages stop distributing enough purchasing power, abundance can coexist with weak demand. My book compresses the problem into one line: “Robots do not buy iPhones.” The Void is difficult because production may accelerate while the social machinery for sharing its value still lags.

Plan A proposes tradable permits that authorize a limited amount of robot or compute capacity. Compute means the processing power used to train and run AI. Permit revenue helps finance the transition, while a Compute Dividend Corporation would give every citizen a share and distribute profits. It calls the result the Citizen’s Dividend and explicitly compares it with Alaska. The Three Crucibles reaches the same idea, but puts more weight on AI labor taxation and distributed ownership.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>The two works converge on a citizen dividend, then diverge over whether permits, taxation, or direct ownership should carry the load.</p>
</div>

## Control and ownership solve different problems

Plan A concentrates on making advanced AI visible and stoppable. It proposes declared chip transactions, inspected data centers, transparent AI research, and international agreements. Its hypothetical covert-project appendix estimates below a 10 percent chance that a hidden Chinese program could reach AI that dominates top human experts before 2043 without detection. Verification is the foundation of its safety case.

The Alignment asks a different question: who holds the claim on AI output and the authority that comes with it? Verification can constrain secret capacity while leaving ownership concentrated. Distributed ownership can spread economic power while doing little to stop an unsafe system. One problem concerns control over capability; the other concerns control over the value it creates.

This is also where the comparison must remain modest. Shared structure does not validate Plan A’s timetable or my book’s dates. It tells us that two very different exercises ran into the same unresolved dependency: societies need credible rules for both restraint and ownership.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Plan A is strongest on verified restraint, while The Three Crucibles makes ownership central. A durable settlement needs an answer to both.</p>
</div>

<p>Pixar’s 2008 animated film imagines machines providing material comfort while one corporation absorbs civic authority and people lose agency. It maps to this argument because productive abundance does not decide who governs it: Plan A supplies controls for the machines, while the Alignment asks whether people remain owners and political actors once production needs far less human labor.</p>
</div>

## Preparation matters more than prediction

No one knows whether 2029, 2035, or 2040 will look anything like Plan A. The crucibles may arrive later, overlap, or take another form entirely. Yet slower progress would not remove the basic questions. It would only give us more time to answer them.

I see The Three Crucibles as a preparation document, not a calendar. Its purpose is to make three decisions visible before events force them on us: what replaces wage-based income, who owns machine output, and who can restrain dangerous capability. If AI 2040 and the crucibles are directionally right, we have a bumpy passage ahead. Preparation cannot smooth it completely, but it can keep uncertainty from becoming an excuse to wait.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Nobody knows the route or timing. The agreement between these two accounts is a reason to prepare institutions for a difficult transition, not to pretend the future is settled.</p>
</div>
