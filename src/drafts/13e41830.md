In 1976, after oil was discovered at Prudhoe Bay, Alaskans approved a constitutional amendment creating the Alaska Permanent Fund. The fund invests part of the state's mineral wealth so a temporary resource boom can leave a lasting public asset. Its first resident dividend was paid in 1982.

That arrangement answers a deep political question in a practical way. When exceptional wealth comes from a resource held in common, citizens should keep a direct claim on some of it. Fifty years later, both [AI 2040: Plan A](https://ai-2040.com/) and [my speculative book, *The Three Crucibles*](https://www.cubagarcia.com/books/the-three-crucibles/), use Alaska to think about wealth created by artificial intelligence.

The manuscript is more than six months old and its framework has been public since March 2026; AI 2040 appeared in July. That timing proves neither correctness nor novelty, but it makes the convergence worth examining.

  <span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
  <p>Alaska turned shared oil wealth into a lasting public claim. AI 2040 and The Three Crucibles ask whether AI-created wealth will require a similar settlement.</p>

## What AI 2040 actually proposes

AI 2040 is a 2026 scenario and policy proposal from the AI Futures Project, which includes several researchers behind *AI 2027*, their 2025 rapid-AI scenario. Its authors describe Plan A as “primarily a recommendation, not a prediction.”

The scenario imagines a US-China agreement in 2029 and nearly automated AI research by 2030. It then proposes transparency, compute limits, and pauses before top-expert AI in 2035 and superintelligence in 2040. Compute is the processing capacity used to train and run AI. A technical takeoff is a rapid, self-reinforcing capability increase as AI accelerates AI research; superintelligence means AI far beyond human ability across cognitive tasks.

In the [Plan A scenario](https://ai-2040.com/AI-2040.pdf), AI performs a third of cognitive labor by mid-2031. Cognitive labor means information, analysis, and decision work. For 2032, the model projects roughly 50 percent real GDP growth, meaning growth in inflation-adjusted output, while wage-linked tax revenue shrinks relative to the economy. Governments sell permits for chips and robots, then return the proceeds through a citizen-owned company. The projected dividend rises from $45,000 per person in 2032 to about $1 million in 2035. These are scenario outputs, not observed facts.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
  <span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
  <p>Plan A proposes a managed path to superintelligence, using permits on scarce AI capacity to replace a weakening labor-tax base with citizen dividends.</p>
</div>

## The three crucibles in plain language

My book describes three social passages rather than a detailed technical timeline.

**The first crucible, The Correction,** begins when AI productivity reduces the market value of human labor. Output can keep rising while wages, bargaining power, and the tax system built around employment weaken.

**The second crucible, The Void,** is the unstable interval after that old bargain starts failing and before a credible replacement exists. A citizen dividend becomes more than welfare here because it keeps purchasing power connected to an economy that needs fewer workers.

**The third crucible, The Alignment,** is the political choice that follows. Society can steward abundant productive capacity, or allow control to settle among a small group of owners.

The overlap is clear. The Correction matches AI 2040's automation and tax-base problem. The Void matches its chapter called “The Citizen’s Dividend,” including the Alaska precedent. The Alignment matches its effort to spread the gains and prevent irreversible concentration of power. Together, those moves describe labor weakening, distribution being rebuilt, and control becoming the central political question.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
  <span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
  <p>The Correction weakens labor's economic role, The Void forces a new distribution model, and The Alignment decides whether abundance is broadly held or narrowly controlled.</p>
</div>

## The deeper disagreement is about safeguards

Plan A puts enormous weight on verification: making claims about chips, datacenters, computing capacity, and AI capabilities checkable through disclosure and inspection. *The Three Crucibles* puts more weight on ownership: who holds a durable claim on the systems and the value they create.

Plan A tests its own approach. Its covert-project appendix imagines 500,000 H100-equivalents of computing capacity, a unit based on Nvidia's H100 AI processor, hidden in tunnels in China with about 200 people aware. The authors put the chance below 10 percent that such a project reaches Top-Expert-Dominating AI undetected before 2043. That term means AI able to outperform the best human specialists.

The low probability still reveals a dependency. Permit revenue works only if limits bind and hidden computing capacity can be found. Ownership rules also fail if nobody can verify what exists or where value is created. The documents supply different layers of the same answer: verification makes governance enforceable, while ownership determines whom it serves.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
  <span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
  <p>Plan A emphasizes verifiable limits and my book emphasizes public claims on output. A workable settlement needs both ownership and enforcement.</p>
</div>

## Preparation is the useful part

I do not know whether Plan A's dates will survive engineering limits, geopolitics, or public resistance. Its authors claim no certainty, and agreement between two speculative works does not confirm my book. Shared assumptions can lead both accounts astray.

Yet the common pressure is worth taking seriously. AI-created output may grow faster than wages, tax systems, and public institutions can adapt. Conflict over livelihoods, revenue, and control could then begin well before anyone can prove that superintelligence is near.

*The Three Crucibles* is a preparation document for that interval. Its purpose is to help us face a rough transition before the hardest choices are made under pressure. Nobody knows what waits for us, but if AI 2040 and my book are directionally right, the old bargain will be coming apart before the new one is ready.
