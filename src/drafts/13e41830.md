In 1976, Alaska voters amended the state constitution to direct at least a quarter of certain mineral revenues into the [Alaska Permanent Fund](https://apfc.org/about/history/), a public investment fund that protects and invests its principal. The Permanent Fund Dividend is an annual cash payment to eligible residents, first paid in 1982 and financed from the fund's returns.

The sequence matters. Alaska created a public claim before spending the full proceeds. Fifty years later, [*AI 2040: Plan A*](https://ai-2040.com/AI-2040.pdf), a conditional scenario and policy proposal published on July 9, 2026, by the AI Futures Project, a research nonprofit focused on advanced AI and policy, uses the same precedent for AI-created wealth.

The core framework of [my book, *The Three Crucibles*](https://www.cubagarcia.com/books/the-three-crucibles/), was in writing by February 3, 2026, more than six months before this article, and publicly downloadable by March 23. The overlap is striking, although it is not independent confirmation. Both works ask what happens when human work is no longer the main route to income and economic power.

<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Alaska turned resource income into a lasting public claim. Both works ask whether society must do something similar with AI-created wealth.</p>

## The future Plan A recommends

Four of Plan A's six authors also wrote [*AI 2027*](https://ai-2027.com/), the group's 2025 scenario about a rapid race toward advanced AI. Plan A calls itself “primarily a recommendation, not a prediction.”

Its conditional timeline includes a United States-China agreement in 2029 and a pause in 2035 when AI reaches roughly the capability of the best human experts. That is distinct from superintelligence, which Plan A places in 2040 and defines as AI that is significantly better, faster, and cheaper than the best humans across essentially all tasks.

In the scenario, AI performs a third of cognitive labor, meaning work based mainly on reasoning and information, by mid-2031. Plan A then projects about 50 percent real, inflation-adjusted economic growth in 2032. As wage income and its tax base weaken, permits for robots and compute, the processing capacity supplied by chips and data centers, fund a Citizen's Dividend: a broad cash payment to adults.

Control depends on verification, meaning evidence that agreed limits are being followed. Plan A proposes records tied to advanced chips, data-center inspections, and enough research transparency for other parties to check progress. Its covert-project appendix gives an undetected secret effort less than a 10 percent chance of reaching “top-expert-dominating AI,” the scenario's threshold for broadly outperforming leading human experts, before 2043.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Plan A combines a staged route to superintelligence with international verification and a dividend intended to replace income lost as AI takes over cognitive work.</p>
</div>

## The three crucibles in plain language

My book describes three pressures that force institutions to change, rather than dates that history has promised to keep.

**The Correction** breaks the old labor equation. As AI makes productive capacity cheaper and more scalable, labor can lose economic value even while output rises. Jobs need not disappear at once for wages to become a weaker way of distributing the gains.

**The Void** is my current name for the second crucible, the 2027–2032 transition presented as the Transformation on the public book page. Old arrangements no longer provide enough demand or security, while their replacements remain unfinished. A Citizen's Dividend connects machine production to human purchasing power.

**The Alignment** is the political fork from 2035 onward. Abundance can support broad ownership and shared returns, or a narrow group can own and control the systems. Here, alignment concerns institutions and incentives as well as model behavior.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>The Correction weakens labor's value, the Void is the unstable transition to new income, and the Alignment decides whether AI abundance is shared or concentrated.</p>
</div>

## Agreement does not settle the hard question

The closest match is economic. Both works foresee AI output rising faster than labor income, weakening household demand and the wage-tax base. Both use the phrase Citizen's Dividend and cite Alaska. My book emphasizes taxes on AI labor and output, while Plan A relies on permits that can be bought and sold for compute and robots.

The larger difference is what each tries to secure. Plan A asks whether powerful development can be observed, limited, and stopped. My book asks who retains a claim on the output. Good monitoring can coexist with concentrated gains, while broad ownership does not make an unsafe system controllable.

Permits reveal the tension. They can make scarce compute visible and taxable, yet favor established firms able to pay for it. Their revenue broadens ownership only if citizens receive a durable share and the market does not harden existing concentration. Plan A supplies a detailed control layer; *The Three Crucibles* keeps the distribution layer in view.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>The works share an economic diagnosis but stress different safeguards: Plan A focuses on verified control, while my book asks who owns the systems and receives their returns.</p>
</div>

## Preparation without prophecy

Nobody knows whether these dates, growth rates, or political agreements will survive contact with reality. The works may share assumptions and therefore share errors. If AI complements labor, wages rise broadly, and ownership spreads, the transition could be much smoother than either account suggests.

But if the two accounts are directionally right, the road ahead is likely to be bumpy. Preparation means working on verification, public claims on AI-created wealth, and ownership before power makes those choices harder. That is why *The Three Crucibles* is a preparation document rather than a prophecy: uncertainty is not a reason to wait until institutions start breaking.
