In 1976, Alaskans faced a practical question: what should a public do with an oil windfall that will not last? Voters changed the state constitution so part of its mineral revenue would enter the Alaska Permanent Fund, a publicly owned investment fund. Since 1982, the fund's earnings have helped finance annual cash dividends for eligible residents.

Residents did not need to work in oil to share in the return from a public resource. Fifty years later, [AI 2040: Plan A](https://ai-2040.com/) explicitly borrows that idea for an economy driven by artificial intelligence. My book, [*The Three Crucibles*](https://www.cubagarcia.com/books/the-three-crucibles/), reaches a similar arrangement by another route.

<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Alaska turned a temporary resource windfall into a lasting public claim. AI 2040 applies that precedent to wealth created by advanced AI.</p>

## What Plan A is planning for

AI 2040 is a 2026 scenario and policy proposal from the AI Futures Project, the nonprofit research group behind *AI 2027*, its April 2025 study of rapid AI development. The authors call Plan A “primarily a recommendation, not a prediction.” Its dates describe a preferred path, not a settled future.

In that path, the United States and China reach an agreement in 2029, track advanced chips and data centers, inspect declared facilities, and share research information. Development pauses at top-human-expert AI in 2035, meaning systems that can match the best people across important cognitive tasks. In 2040, the scenario moves toward superintelligence: systems that outperform the best humans across tasks while working faster and more cheaply.

Plan A also proposes tradable permits, limited permissions that can be bought and sold, to build robots or computing capacity. Computing capacity, often shortened to *compute*, is the processing power used to train and run AI. Most permit fees would support a citizen dividend, while a Compute Dividend Corporation would give each citizen a share.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Plan A pairs international monitoring and staged pauses with a plan that turns permits for AI capacity into income for citizens.</p>
</div>

## The three crucibles in plain language

**The Correction** begins when useful output depends less on paid human labor. Productivity shifts toward the owners of AI systems, putting wages, labor taxes, and consumer demand under pressure.

**The Void** is the unstable middle period. The old bargain between work and income is weakening, but no trusted replacement has taken its place. A citizen dividend becomes one possible bridge because people still need income and the economy still needs buyers.

**The Alignment** is the political settlement that follows. In my book, alignment means deciding who owns productive intelligence, who receives its gains, and whether abundance is broadly stewarded or tightly concentrated. This is wider than the AI-safety use of the word, which usually means making a system behave according to human intentions.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>The crucibles describe an economic break, an unstable transition, and the eventual choice over ownership and power.</p>
</div>

## Where the arguments meet and separate

The first overlap is the weakening link between labor and economic security. Plan A expects labor-tax revenue to collapse as machines replace paid work. The Correction describes the same pressure as a shift of productivity from workers to owners. As the book puts it, “Robots do not buy iPhones.”

The second overlap is distribution. Both works use the phrase “Citizen's Dividend.” Plan A would fund it mainly through permits to build robots or computing capacity. My book lists AI labor taxation as one funding pillar: some value created by deployed AI would return to society, although the exact tax base remains open.

Then the arguments separate. Plan A relies on chip tracking, declared compute, data-center inspections, and research transparency. Its own appendix tests that approach: the authors put the chance of a covert project reaching top-expert AI undetected before 2043 below ten percent. Verification looks difficult, even in their preferred future.

My book puts more weight on distributed ownership. That answers a question verification cannot: even when every rule is followed, who receives the value? Yet ownership alone cannot solve Plan A's concern that superintelligent systems could escape human control. Each work supplies a layer the other treats lightly.

When AI 2040 appeared in July 2026, my framework was already public. That sequence makes the convergence interesting, but it does not prove either account. It shows that different methods reveal similar fault lines without telling us when they will open.

<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Both works foresee labor disruption, a citizen dividend, and concentrated power. Plan A emphasizes verification; The Three Crucibles emphasizes ownership.</p>
</div>

<p>Neill Blomkamp's film imagines powerful technology concentrated in an orbital enclave while most people remain outside its benefits. The mapping to this argument is about access and ownership: technical abundance can coexist with social scarcity when a small group controls the productive system and the terms for sharing it.</p>

## Preparation without prophecy

No one knows whether AI will follow Plan A's timetable, whether physical limits will slow it, or whether international cooperation will hold. I do not know either. But if AI 2040 and *The Three Crucibles* are directionally right, the rough part comes from the order of events: economic correction may arrive before new systems of distribution and control are ready.

That is the job of my book. It is a preparation document, not a calendar. Alaska offers a modest lesson: a society can decide who shares in a windfall before the money grows and the interests around it harden. The equivalent choice for AI is still open, although it may not remain open forever.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>The future is unknowable, but both works point toward a bumpy transition. Preparation means settling questions of distribution, ownership, and control while those choices remain open.</p>
</div>
