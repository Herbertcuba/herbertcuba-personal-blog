Alaska voters created the mineral-revenue-backed Alaska Permanent Fund in 1976; dividends began in 1982. Fifty years later, two AI futures use the same precedent and label: the Citizen’s Dividend.

[AI 2040: Plan A](https://ai-2040.com/AI-2040.pdf) is a 2026 conditional policy scenario from the nonprofit AI Futures Project. Its authors call it a recommendation, neither a prediction nor their best guess. Four authors return from [AI 2027](https://ai-2027.com/), their April 3, 2025 rapid-race scenario.

My book, [The Three Crucibles](https://www.cubagarcia.com/books/the-three-crucibles/), was on my site by March 23, 2026; the current PDF followed on June 24. AI 2040 added its PDF link on July 9. That establishes order, not influence or predictive success. I think the comparison is more useful: the overlap identifies an economic break, while disagreement exposes three controls on concentrated AI power.

## The maps meet at the economic break

The Three Crucibles opens with The Correction, The Void, and The Alignment. The Correction marks labor’s loss of economic weight. The middle begins as The Void after work-income links weaken, then develops through The Transformation, the current landing-page label. The Alignment frames the ownership fork.

In Plan A’s scenario, AI performs one third of cognitive labor, work based on reasoning and judgment, by mid-2031. For 2032, it projects about 50 percent growth in real gross domestic product, or inflation-adjusted output, as job losses weaken income- and payroll-tax revenue. Machine-investment deductions reduce taxable corporate income. The report warns that this output cannot be translated directly into 2025 purchasing power, so it does not establish weaker household demand.

Plan A responds with capacity permits, tradable permissions to build robot or computing infrastructure. In its scenario, United States permit fees reach $50 trillion in fiscal 2032. A fictional Compute Dividend Corporation legally owns a fixed share of those fees, gives each citizen one share, and pays $45,000 per American adult in real 2025 dollars.

[The Three Crucibles PDF](https://www.cubagarcia.com/downloads/three-crucibles.pdf) also proposes a Citizen’s Dividend and an AI Labor Equivalence Tax, a levy tied to public revenue from equivalent human work. Its demand argument is blunt: “Robots do not buy iPhones.” I think the convergence lies in the diagnosis and need for distribution. The funding differs: one charges for scarce capacity; the other taxes human-equivalent AI work.

This claim should be able to lose. [Statistics Norway](https://www.ssb.no/en), Norway’s official statistics agency, publishes industry data on [AI use](https://www.ssb.no/en/statbank/table/13265), [employee compensation](https://www.ssb.no/en/statbank/table/09170), and [productivity](https://www.ssb.no/en/statbank/table/09174). Freeze 2023–2025, then compare industries with the largest and smallest rises in AI use, matching them on earlier productivity and labor-share trends. Labor share is employee compensation divided by value added: output after subtracting the goods and services used in production.

If fast adopters gain ten percentage points more productivity growth while relative labor share stays within two points of baseline, that counts against my thesis. A fall above five points supports it; the middle is inconclusive. Norway’s [tax and social-contribution data](https://www.ssb.no/en/statbank/table/10799) is sector-level, so it cannot prove an industry effect. Tax reform, oil shocks, reclassification, or a weak adoption gap would cloud it. I think a Nordic series that can defeat the framework is better than a decorative example.

## Verification and ownership solve different failures

Takeoff is AI automating AI research and accelerating its improvement. Plan A’s path reaches a United States–China agreement in 2029, pauses in 2035 at top-human-expert capability, and resumes in 2040 toward superintelligence: AI better than the best humans at all tasks, faster and cheaper.

Its restraint system depends on compute, the capacity used to train and run AI. Major chip transactions would be declared, datacenters inspected, and AI research made almost entirely transparent while private use stayed private. Developers would submit safety cases, evidence-based arguments against irreversible catastrophe, for public and expert criticism.

Plan A stress-tests that design through a fictional covert Chinese project. Smuggled chips feed a datacenter in tunnels beside the unfinished Medog Hydropower Station, where construction provides cover and the tunnel carries away heat. The operation still needs equipment, electricity, cooling, and roughly 200 informed people. I think such capacity leaves traces, but inspectors may find them too late.

Ownership addresses another dependency. Plan A’s corporation legally owns a fixed fraction of permit fees. The Three Crucibles proposes AI infrastructure ownership by individuals, cooperatives, and municipalities. One assigns a payment stream; the other spreads legal claims on productive infrastructure.

The distinction sharpens at each map’s end. Plan A’s 2040 path hands institutions and equipment to AI until humans can no longer shut down the institutions and equipment under AI control. The Alignment asks whether those controlling abundance become “stewards of abundance” or “the new feudal lords.” I don’t know which governance path is right; Plan A does not claim to know either. My take is that verification and economic rights belong together: evidence about machinery cannot decide who benefits, while ownership cannot establish safety.

<p>Plan A makes dangerous computing capacity inspectable; The Three Crucibles spreads productive ownership. Visibility reveals machinery; legal rights determine who receives gains.</p>

## Exit applies the same logic inside an organization

The scale changes at a supplier contract, but the problem remains. A citizen’s economic right limits an asset owner’s claim on automated income; a tested exit right limits a supplier’s power over operations. I think both preserve bargaining power before dependence hardens.

My book illustrates that speed through two fictional insurers. One keeps 5,000 claims adjusters; the other uses coordinated AI agents, cuts headcount by 40 percent, reduces premiums by 20 percent, and wins within six months. Adoption can move faster than reversibility.

Reversibility capture occurs when a supplier retains what a workflow needs to run elsewhere: data, licences, interfaces, permissions, certification, or computing access. Exit proof is a live handover test, agreed at signing and run before renewal with buyer-controlled cases. Require it for critical or potentially harmful workflows, or when any two risks are high: data sensitivity, supplier concentration, and replacement difficulty.

Disaster recovery tests restoration; portability tests whether data and settings move. Exit proof adds a buyer-owned workflow replay, an acceptance record, and a renewal consequence. The leverage only exists before you sign.

| Decision field | Contract rule |
| --- | --- |
| Scope | Test critical workflows and those with at least two high dependency risks. |
| Test pack | Freeze system versions, allowed tools and data, buyer-owned cases, expected results, error, time, and cost limits, plus the grader, the approved scoring method. |
| Pass rule | Run three times. Every run meets safety, permission, audit, time, and cost limits; the median quality score meets the error threshold. Allow one retry for a recorded external outage. A changed version, unavailable grader, or partial run is indeterminate and requires a full rerun. |
| Record and cost | Store versions, case ID, scores, logs, cost, result, and approver. The business owner holds the budget and accepts residual risk, meaning risk left after testing. The buyer funds staff, review, and downtime; supplier participation has a fixed fee; the supplier funds repairs caused by missing access or documentation. “Case 118: output correct; audit log missing” fails. |
| Renewal | Failure triggers repair and one rerun, followed by portability support, repricing, or non-renewal unless the group responsible for risk approves a time-limited exception. |

A claims-triage test, for the workflow that routes insurance cases, could allow 30 days and 15 person-days for 200 cases. Median error stays within two points of the current system; each run finishes within an hour and under budget. These are proposals, not a standard.

The [European Union’s 2024 AI Act](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai), Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, generally applies from August 2, 2026. Rules for general-purpose models, which support many tasks, have applied since August 2, 2025; some high-risk rules apply later. Documents and logs support exit proof, but cannot demonstrate a handover.

My take is that visibility, citizen rights, and exit rights limit different concentrations of power. Alaska established the public claim before the revenue became ordinary. AI systems may be built under many plans, but who can inspect them, claim their output, or walk away is being decided now.
