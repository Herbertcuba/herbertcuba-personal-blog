In 1976, Alaska voters decided that an oil boom should leave more than a spent budget. A constitutional amendment put at least a quarter of certain mineral revenue into the Alaska Permanent Fund. The state invests that principal, and eligible residents began receiving dividends in 1982.

Alaska settled an ownership question before the windfall became enormous: part of a scarce public resource would remain invested for the public. Fifty years later, both [AI 2040: Plan A](https://ai-2040.com/AI-2040.pdf) and my 2026 speculative book, [The Three Crucibles](https://www.cubagarcia.com/books/the-three-crucibles/), return to that precedent while asking what happens when intelligence becomes the productive resource.

## What AI 2040 actually proposes

The [AI Futures Project](https://ai-2040.com/), a nonprofit that forecasts advanced AI and recommends policy responses, published Plan A in July 2026. Four of the five authors of its 2025 scenario [*AI 2027*](https://ai-2027.com/) also worked on it. Plan A calls itself “primarily a recommendation, not a prediction,” so its dates describe a preferred path rather than promises.

In that path, the United States and China make an agreement in 2029 to avoid a 2030 race toward fully automated AI research. Capability stays within the human range, pauses around the level of the best human experts in 2035, then resumes in 2040 toward superintelligence, meaning systems that broadly exceed those experts.

Plan A’s economic mechanism is simple: as AI takes over more paid work, wage income and labor taxes lose ground even while output rises. The scenario puts a third of cognitive labor, work based mainly on information and judgment, in AI hands by mid-2031 and projects roughly 50% growth in inflation-adjusted output in 2032. It responds with limited, transferable permits required before adding robots or compute, the processing capacity supplied by advanced chips and data centers. Plan A also proposes a public Compute Dividend Corporation with one share per citizen and profits paid as dividends.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Plan A combines negotiated limits on advanced AI with rapid automation and a public dividend that reconnects machine production with human demand.</p></div>

## The three crucibles in plain language

The framework in my book was public by March 2026, months before Plan A appeared. It describes a transition rather than setting a timetable:

- **The Correction** begins when AI performs enough valuable work that higher productivity no longer guarantees broad wage growth. Companies have a strong reason to automate while households and tax systems still depend on paid labor.
- **The Void** is the gap after that old bargain weakens and before a replacement works at scale. Output can rise while income and demand lag behind.
- **The Alignment** is the eventual choice over stewardship. AI infrastructure can support broadly shared abundance, or its control and returns can collect around a small group of owners.

The Citizen’s Dividend is one proposed answer inside The Void because people still need purchasing power when less income arrives through jobs. As the book puts the demand problem: “Robots do not buy iPhones.”

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The three crucibles describe a correction in the value of labor, a dangerous distribution gap, and a final choice over ownership and stewardship.</p></div>

## Agreement stops at control and ownership

The Correction resembles Plan A’s shrinking labor-tax base, while The Void resembles its Citizen’s Dividend, down to the same name and Alaska precedent. The Alignment then matches Plan A’s choice between widely shared gains and concentrated power. The published chronology places my framework first, although timing alone proves nothing about whether either map is right.

Their funding ideas also differ. My book emphasizes distributed ownership and taxes tied to work performed by AI systems. Plan A would constrain capacity through robot and compute permits while creating a citizen-owned corporation.

The deeper difference is emphasis. Verification asks whether dangerous capability can be seen and limited, so Plan A would compare declared computing capacity with tracked-chip records and data-center inspections. Its covert-project appendix tests that system and estimates less than a 10% chance that an imagined secret project reaches AI beyond the best human experts before 2043 without detection.

Ownership asks who keeps the gain when the system works. A controllable system can still concentrate wealth, while broad ownership cannot make an uncontrollable system safe. Control and claim are separate questions.

This convergence is not independent confirmation. Automation anxiety, public dividends, and Alaska’s example predate both documents, so shared starting ideas are a serious explanation. What the overlap gives us is a sharper problem: production and purchasing power cannot drift apart forever.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Both maps connect automation to a citizen dividend, but technical verification and broad ownership address different failures and neither can replace the other.</p></div>

## Preparation is more useful than belief

There is a credible alternative to this rough transition. AI may complement workers, lower costs, create new demand, and keep wages connected to productivity. The shared map weakens if labor share, the portion of national income paid to workers, stays stable, real wages rise with output, and ownership of productive AI becomes less concentrated.

That gives leaders a simple control-and-claim test for any serious AI decision:

- Can the capability be observed, bounded, and reversed?
- Who owns the productive asset, and who receives the gain?

Europe already has a partial answer to the first question. The bulk of the European Union’s risk-based AI Act began applying on August 2, 2026, while obligations for general-purpose AI models, models usable across many tasks, have applied since August 2, 2025. Better oversight still does not decide how the economic return is shared.

No one knows what waits beyond the current acceleration. If Plan A and my book are directionally right, the road will be bumpy even if every date is wrong. The purpose of *The Three Crucibles* is preparation: to make ownership and distribution choices visible while they are still choices.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Watch whether AI remains controllable and whether its gains stay connected to human income; uncertainty is a reason to prepare, not to pretend we know the dates.</p></div>
