[*AI 2040: Plan A*](https://ai-2040.com/AI-2040.pdf) reaches an economic structure I recognize. The 2026 policy scenario comes from the AI Futures Project, a six-person group sharing four authors with [*AI 2027*](https://ai-2027.com/), its 2025 forecast of AI surpassing the best humans across fields. [*The Three Crucibles*](https://www.cubagarcia.com/downloads/three-crucibles.pdf), my book about AI and economic power, approaches the structure through companies and ownership.

Both connect automated work to weaker labor income, use the name Citizen's Dividend, and cite the Alaska Permanent Fund, an oil-wealth fund established in 1976 and paying residents since 1982. The book PDF entered its [public repository on March 23, 2026](https://github.com/Herbertcuba/herbertcuba-personal-blog/commit/ce380abb4f4f13f5fc660a2c2e3a6a75011184a8); a [public link to AI 2040 followed on July 9](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48848425). Private origins remain unknown.

I think the public sequence supports a precise claim: the book named the shift before this scenario supplied its machinery. Convergence strengthens the diagnosis while both timelines remain unproven. Divergence separates verification of hidden capacity, ownership of the gains, and an institution's power to leave a supplier.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The shared term and Alaska mechanism make the convergence substantive; unknown private origins and unproven timelines limit the claim.</p></div>

## The Void is the direct match

The canonical crucibles are the Correction, the Void, and the Alignment ([printed p. 5](https://www.cubagarcia.com/downloads/three-crucibles.pdf#page=6)). The Correction marks the break; the Void covers output recovery without equal job recovery; the Alignment asks who owns the machines. Transformation is a chapter inside the Void, where “Robots do not buy iPhones” explains how rising production can accompany collapsing household demand ([printed p. 35](https://www.cubagarcia.com/downloads/three-crucibles.pdf#page=36)).

In the book's fictional insurer example, one company keeps 5,000 claims adjusters while another uses an AI claims system, cuts staff by 40 percent and premiums by 20 percent, then wins within six months ([printed p. 20](https://www.cubagarcia.com/downloads/three-crucibles.pdf#page=21)). The company gains as wage-supported demand shrinks.

Plan A scales that pressure nationally. In the scenario, a 2029 US-China agreement stops a race that would otherwise reach superintelligence by the end of 2030. AI stays within the human range through 2035, pauses at the level of the best specialists, then resumes toward superintelligence in 2040 ([p. 4](https://ai-2040.com/AI-2040.pdf#page=4)). By mid-2031, AI performs a third of cognitive labor, work centered on knowledge and reasoning.

As payroll and income-tax receipts shrink, the US government caps new robot and computing capacity, sells tradable permits to build it, and sends a growing share of the fees to a dividend corporation with one share per citizen. The scenario's payment rises from $45,000 per adult in 2032 to about $1 million in 2035 ([pp. 17–18](https://ai-2040.com/AI-2040.pdf#page=17)). The book proposes an AI Labor Equivalence Tax, a charge on each human-equivalent unit of deployed AI labor ([printed p. 36](https://www.cubagarcia.com/downloads/three-crucibles.pdf#page=37)).

I think the dividend is the one-to-one convergence. The funding method and speed diverge, while the shared mechanism remains testable: output rises, labor income and tax receipts fall, and consumer demand weakens.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The canonical map is Correction, Void, and Alignment. Plan A meets the Void at the dividend, using permits where the book proposes an AI labor tax.</p></div>

## Verification leaves ownership open

Plan A centers verification, meaning evidence that declared chips, datacenters, and research on the most capable models match reality. Its hypothetical covert-project test hides 500,000 H100-equivalents, a measure of high-end AI computing power, in tunnels beside China's planned Medog Hydropower Station. Roughly 200 people know about the project ([pp. 45–50](https://ai-2040.com/AI-2040.pdf#page=45)).

Like *Rogue One*, the 2016 *Star Wars* film, knowledge is compartmentalized, but the machine leaves traces in power, cooling, and construction. In the scenario, US intelligence puts the chance of a covert project reaching Top-Expert-Dominating AI, a level where systems outperform the best human specialists, undetected below 10 percent until 2043.

The book's Alignment examines who receives returns from visible, legal capacity. It favors distributed AI ownership through open models that people can run on local or organizational hardware ([printed p. 55](https://www.cubagarcia.com/downloads/three-crucibles.pdf#page=56)). Inspection can succeed while gains remain concentrated. Broad ownership can spread gains while a state conceals a dangerous project.

I don't know which map will prove closer to events, and Plan A's authors explicitly separate their recommendation from their best guess. My take is that the controls must work together. I think treating either as sufficient leaves one route to concentrated power untouched.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Verification constrains hidden capacity; ownership determines who benefits from visible capacity. Each leaves the other's risk exposed.</p></div>

## Exit proof belongs in the renewal decision

Even if verification and broad distribution succeed, an institution remains exposed when a critical AI service cannot move. I would add an exit proof: a funded, timed handover of one working service. Test one claims workflow, one data extract, and one replacement team over 30 days. The chief information officer (CIO) owns renewal, which depends on the result.

The buyer owns the test set: a sample of past cases, the accepted result for each, and the scoring rule. To reproduce decisions, the supplier provides decision records plus saved model and prompt settings. To move the service, it provides usable data, connection details, permissions, and a history of the data's origin and changes. A restore guide supports rebuilding. The replacement team may change the model, prompts, method for finding records, or workflow while preserving the agreed business outcome.

The handover passes only within the error limit, deadline, and cost ceiling. Missing access, late delivery, or excess errors fail it. Failure triggers transition help, continued access, and supplier-funded repair. I think this gives a board evidence of bargaining power before a critical service is at risk.

Most of the [EU AI Act](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj/eng#art_50), the European Union's AI regulation, applies from August 2, 2026. Article 50 can require transparency about certain AI interactions and generated content. Disclosure identifies AI use; a handover tests whether a live service can be reproduced and moved. Without that proof, the supplier controls the buyer's ability to leave at renewal.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>An exit proof makes portability a CIO-owned renewal condition. Without practical exit, AI power remains concentrated.</p></div>
