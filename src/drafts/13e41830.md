In 1976, Alaska voters amended the state constitution so that at least a quarter of certain mineral revenue would enter the [Alaska Permanent Fund](https://apfc.org/about/history/). This public investment fund turns finite oil and mineral wealth into lasting financial assets. Residents received the first dividend in 1982; today's annual payments come from investment earnings tied to mineral royalties.

The decision settled a difficult question early: when exceptional wealth comes from a shared resource, citizens should retain a claim on it. Fifty years later, [AI 2040: Plan A](https://ai-2040.com/) and my speculative book, [*The Three Crucibles*](https://www.cubagarcia.com/books/the-three-crucibles/), return to that question from different directions. Their overlap proves neither future, but it shows where pressure is likely to build.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Alaska created a public claim on concentrated resource wealth before the stakes became overwhelming. Two accounts of advanced AI suggest we may need to make the same choice again.</p></div>

## AI 2040 is a proposal, not a timetable

Plan A is a 90-page scenario and policy proposal published on July 9, 2026, by the AI Futures Project, a US nonprofit researching advanced AI. Four of its six authors also wrote scenario content for *AI 2027*, the group's 2025 account of a rapid AI race. Plan A calls itself “primarily a recommendation, not a prediction,” which is how its dates should be read.

Its preferred path has the United States and China reach an agreement in 2029, followed by near-automated AI research and a pause in 2030. Systems improve within the human range until another pause at roughly top-human-expert level in 2035. In 2040, after safeguards and institutions are in place, the scenario allows a move toward superintelligence, meaning systems far more capable than humans across most cognitive work.

The economic mechanism starts earlier. As AI replaces paid labor, wage-based tax revenue can weaken even while output grows. In the scenario, AI performs a third of cognitive labor by mid-2031, and real GDP, or inflation-adjusted economic output, grows by roughly 50 percent in 2032. Governments then sell tradable permits, or rights to build or use controlled chips and robots, and put the proceeds into a corporation owned equally by citizens. Governance rests on chip tracking, research transparency, and datacenter inspections; an appendix about a covert Chinese project shows how hidden computing capacity could undermine the deal.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Plan A imagines a managed climb to superintelligence, using international verification to contain danger and permit revenue to support citizens as labor taxes weaken.</p></div>

## The three crucibles name the human passage

The framework for my book was written by February 3, 2026, months before Plan A appeared. I use *crucible* to mean a severe test that changes the society passing through it. The [downloadable edition](https://www.cubagarcia.com/downloads/three-crucibles.pdf) names The Correction, The Void, and The Alignment. The public book page calls the middle phase The Transformation, so the two versions use different names; here I follow the book's numbered map.

**The Correction** begins when AI productivity weakens labor as the main route to income, tax revenue, and bargaining power. Production may grow while the bargain built around human work starts to fail.

**The Void** is the unsettled interval before a replacement bargain becomes legitimate. A Citizen's Dividend, a direct public share of gains from AI infrastructure, acts as a bridge rather than a complete answer.

**The Alignment** is the political choice that follows. AI abundance can be held in trust for many people, or control can harden around the few who own the productive systems.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The Correction breaks the labor bargain, The Void exposes the gap before a new one exists, and The Alignment decides who holds power over the resulting abundance.</p></div>

## The agreement is about sequence

The clearest convergence is economic. Plan A models automation eroding the labor-tax base; The Correction describes the same break in social terms. The trouble begins once output, wages, and public revenue stop moving together, even if many people remain employed.

That break creates a distribution problem. Both works use the name Citizen's Dividend and point to Alaska as precedent. Plan A funds it through permits for scarce chips and robots, while my book emphasizes taxes on work performed by AI and ownership spread among citizens. Permit revenue, however, depends on countries enforcing scarcity across borders.

Distribution then becomes a question of power. Plan A puts verification first: can governments see dangerous capabilities and enforce a pause? The Alignment puts ownership first: do citizens have a durable claim on what the systems produce? A system can be technically controlled and still leave its gains highly concentrated.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Both accounts move from a broken labor bargain to public distribution and then a struggle over power, while placing different weight on verification and ownership.</p></div>

## Preparation is the honest use of both

No one knows how fast AI capability will improve, which policy deal might hold, or how society will react. Plan A presents a recommendation; my book is a preparatory document for a transition whose exact shape cannot be known.

If both accounts are even directionally right, the road will be bumpy because institutions built around human labor cannot change at the speed of AI capability. Alaska offers a modest lesson: decide who has a claim before concentrated wealth grows large enough to decide the politics for us.

We cannot choose 2040 in advance. We can choose whether ownership, distribution, and accountability remain afterthoughts until a crisis makes the choice on our behalf.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The uncertainty does not remove the need to prepare. Institutions can be readied for a rough transition without treating either account as prophecy.</p></div>
