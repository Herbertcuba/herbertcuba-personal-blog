In 1976, Alaska's voters created the Alaska Permanent Fund. This state-owned investment fund saves and invests part of the revenue from Alaska's finite oil reserves. Eligible residents have received an annual cash dividend from its earnings since the first payment in 1982. It made an abstract question practical: when a shared resource creates exceptional wealth, what claim should citizens have on it?

That question sits inside both [AI 2040: Plan A](https://ai-2040.com/AI-2040.pdf) and [my 2026 book, *The Three Crucibles*](https://www.cubagarcia.com/books/the-three-crucibles/). Both use Alaska as a precedent, and both call their proposed response a Citizen's Dividend.

The book names three pressures. **The Correction** begins when cheap AI labor weakens wages, employment, and labor-based taxes. **The Void** follows as production grows while purchasing power remains tied to jobs that are disappearing. **The Alignment** asks who owns the new productive capacity and whether its gains widen freedom or concentrate power.

A downloadable PDF of the book and a homepage link were public on March 23, 2026. AI 2040 was publicly available by July 9. That establishes the public order, but it proves neither influence nor accuracy. The useful fact is that a later, much more detailed scenario reaches many of the same structural questions.

<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Alaska turned shared resource wealth into a citizen claim. AI 2040 and The Three Crucibles apply that question to AI through labor disruption, new distribution, and an ownership choice.</p>

## What Plan A is proposing

AI 2040 is a 90-page scenario from the AI Futures Project, a US research nonprofit. Four of the five main authors of its April 2025 scenario, *AI 2027*, also worked on this report. They call Plan A “primarily a recommendation, not a prediction.”

The scenario starts with a US–China agreement in 2029. It prevents fully automated AI research and development in 2030, meaning AI systems doing most of the work required to improve AI itself. Development continues within the range of human ability, pauses near the level of leading experts in 2035, then moves toward superintelligence in 2040. The report defines that as AI broadly better than the best humans while also faster and cheaper.

By mid-2031, AI performs a third of cognitive labor in the scenario, weakening revenue from wages and payrolls. Governments cap new compute, the chips and datacenters used to train and run AI, as well as robot capacity. Tradable permits allow expansion, and a growing share of the fees funds a corporation in which every US citizen owns one share and receives equal dividends.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Plan A links limits on advanced AI with a new economic bargain: controlled growth in machine capacity helps finance citizen dividends when labor can no longer support the old tax base.</p>
</div>

## How the crucibles reappear

The Correction appears in Plan A as the decline of wage-based public revenue. The documents differ on speed, but share a mechanism: replacing taxed human work with machines can raise productivity while shrinking the income stream built around employment.

The Void appears in the response. My book proposes a dividend funded partly by taxing AI systems when they take over work that previously produced taxed wages. Plan A instead sells permits for new chip and robot capacity and directs part of the fees to citizens. Different funding methods address the same gap between machine production and human purchasing power.

The Alignment appears in the final choice. AI 2040 seeks to avoid the extinction or irreversible concentration of power described in *AI 2027*. The Three Crucibles frames the political fork as stewardship of abundance or a new feudal concentration. This overlap proves neither scenario, but it shows where safety and distribution meet: power.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>The maps converge on broken labor economics, a citizen dividend, and the danger of concentrated power. Their strongest disagreement concerns how a better outcome should be secured.</p>
</div>

## Verification is only half the problem

Plan A relies on inspections, chip tracking, and research transparency so limits can be checked rather than merely promised. Its appendix estimates below a 10 percent chance that a covert Chinese project could reach AI that outperforms leading human experts across relevant fields before 2043 without detection. This is a scenario estimate, not evidence that hidden projects will be found.

My framework puts more weight on ownership. Verification asks whether an actor stays within an agreed capacity limit; ownership asks who has a lasting claim on the value created within it. One addresses an uncontrolled race, while the other addresses a controlled system that still concentrates power. I do not know which problem will prove harder, so I assume both matter.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Plan A makes capacity limits visible and enforceable. The Three Crucibles adds the separate requirement that citizens hold a real claim on the wealth produced by permitted AI systems.</p>
</div>

## Preparation without prophecy

Nobody knows whether advanced AI will follow Plan A's dates, my stages, or a path neither document contains. The exact timeline can be wrong while the shared mechanism still matters: machine productivity may grow faster than wage-based taxes, distribution rules, and international trust can adapt.

I see *The Three Crucibles* as a preparation document for that gap. We need to decide how citizens share AI-produced wealth, who can verify dangerous concentrations of capacity, and who can act when rules fail. If AI 2040 and my book are directionally right, those institutions will have to change while the technology is still moving. That will be a turbulent transition.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Neither map removes uncertainty. Their value is to expose the distribution, ownership, and verification decisions that become much harder once labor and revenue systems are already under strain.</p>
