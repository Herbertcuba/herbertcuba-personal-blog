In 1976, Alaska voters created the Alaska Permanent Fund through a constitutional amendment. It invests a required share of the US state's mineral revenue, turning part of a temporary oil windfall into a lasting public asset. Dividend payments to residents began in 1982. Its central idea is that citizens should hold a direct claim on wealth produced by a scarce resource they collectively own.

Half a century later, that institution appears in two accounts of an AI-shaped future. [AI 2040: Plan A](https://ai-2040.com/) is a July 2026 scenario and policy proposal from the AI Futures Project, the forecasting group behind *AI 2027*, its 2025 scenario about rapid progress toward superhuman AI. My 2026 speculative book, [*The Three Crucibles*](https://www.cubagarcia.com/books/the-three-crucibles/), was already public when Plan A appeared. Both return to Alaska when asking how ordinary people might share machine-produced wealth.

This overlap does not prove either account. AI 2040 is another map, not data confirming mine. The resemblance still deserves attention because both maps describe much the same social problem before offering different ways through it.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>The Alaska fund turns shared resource wealth into a public asset and resident payments. Both works apply that logic to a citizen dividend: a payment linked to wealth created by AI chips, datacenters and robots.</p>
</div>

## What Plan A proposes

Plan A calls itself “primarily a recommendation, not a prediction.” It imagines the United States and China reaching an agreement in 2029, then slowing down after AI research becomes nearly automated in 2030. The plan pauses around top-expert AI in 2035 and moves toward superintelligence in 2040. Top-expert AI would outperform the best human specialists across important fields; superintelligence would exceed human ability across almost all cognitive work.

Its economic story starts with a tax problem. As wages shrink relative to machine-produced output, governments lose much of the labor income they used to tax. Permit revenue from AI chips and robots flows into a proposed public company that assigns one share to every citizen. The projected scale is dramatic: in the scenario, real GDP, the economy's output after adjusting for inflation, grows by roughly 50 percent in 2032, while the dividend rises from $45,000 per person that year to about $1 million in 2035.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Plan A combines a negotiated slowdown and monitored AI development with explosive growth and a public dividend funded by permits on AI chips and robots.</p>
</div>

## The three crucibles in plain language

My book describes the transition as three social stages rather than a detailed policy timetable.

**The Correction** is the economic break. AI lets firms produce more with fewer people, so labor income can fall before taxes and institutions find another way to support households.

**The Void** is the unstable interval after that break. Output may recover while jobs and household income do not, weakening the old connection between work, purchasing power and social belonging.

**The Alignment** is the political choice created by that gap. It asks who owns the productive machines, who receives their output, and whether abundance produces broad stewardship or concentrated power.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>The Correction breaks the labor economy, The Void exposes the gap between output and livelihoods, and The Alignment decides who owns and shares machine-produced wealth.</p>
</div>

## Where the maps meet and part

The first meeting point is the separation of productivity from wages. Plan A describes the resulting collapse of the labor-tax base. The Three Crucibles carries the same break into household demand: machines may produce goods, but “robots do not buy iPhones.” Greater productive capacity cannot sustain a consumer economy if households lose their income.

Both works then propose a Citizen’s Dividend and cite Alaska. Plan A would fund it through permits and public shares. My book gives more weight to an AI labor tax, meaning a levy on work performed by AI systems, and to spreading ownership of those systems. The mechanisms differ, but both try to reconnect people to output when wages no longer do the job. Both also end at a choice between broad participation and concentrated power.

The main separation is control. Plan A emphasizes verification: governments track advanced chips, inspect datacenters and check whether countries and laboratories follow agreed limits. Its appendix about a hidden AI project matters because any agreement must survive concealment and cheating. My book emphasizes ownership, since a technically controlled system can still concentrate wealth and political power.

Neither layer can replace the other. Broad ownership does not make an uncontrollable AI system safe, while successful safety controls do not decide who benefits. The maps are strongest when read as separate layers of the same problem.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>The maps share an economic diagnosis, a dividend and a warning about concentrated power. Plan A leads with verification; The Three Crucibles leads with ownership.</p>
</div>

## Meanwhile in sci-fi

<p>This science-fiction film places advanced technology inside a wealthy orbital enclave while most people remain on a deprived Earth. In this article’s terms, it shows The Alignment going wrong: abundance exists, but access follows ownership and political power. That is why controlling powerful machines and sharing their benefits are related but different tasks.</p>

## Preparation is the point

No one knows which parts of these accounts, if any, will happen on schedule. AI progress may slow, political agreements may fail, and economic adaptation may take forms neither document expects. Plan A is explicit about being a recommendation. The purpose of my book is also preparation rather than prophecy.

If both maps are even directionally right, the transition will be rough because capability could move faster than tax systems, ownership rules and public trust. Naming The Correction, The Void and The Alignment lets us discuss that possibility before the choices become unavoidable. That modest purpose is enough reason to take the overlap seriously.

<div class="chapter-tldr">
<span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span>
<p>Nobody knows the route or timing. The shared warning is that AI may outrun the institutions connecting productivity to income, ownership and trust, which makes preparation useful now.</p>
</div>
