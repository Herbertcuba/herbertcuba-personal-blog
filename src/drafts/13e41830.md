[*AI 2040: Plan A*](https://ai-2040.com/) is a 90-page scenario about superintelligence. A page in [*The Three Crucibles*](/books/the-three-crucibles/) about two insurers arrives at the same uncomfortable place.

One insurer keeps 5,000 claims adjusters. Another deploys an agentic system and cuts headcount by 40 percent. It lowers premiums by 20 percent and wins within six months. It is a thought experiment about forced automation. Once one company crosses that line, its rival loses the luxury of waiting.

The AI Futures Project widens that room until it contains governments and the global economy. Four of Plan A's six authors also wrote [*AI 2027*](https://ai-2027.com/). They describe Plan A as a policy recommendation carried by a scenario. The consequences that follow are conditional predictions.

I think the overlap with *The Three Crucibles* is unusually precise. Both maps move from labor displacement through a Citizen's Dividend and into the risk of concentrated power. They begin in different rooms, and they disagree on the guardrail.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>AI 2040 scales the problem shown by the insurer: automation becomes compulsory once a rival captures the cost advantage. Both documents begin with labor and end with power.</p></div>

## What AI 2040 predicts

[The report's scenario](https://ai-2040.com/AI-2040.pdf) starts with a deal between the United States and China in 2029. The deal avoids a 2030 path in which fully automated AI research produces superintelligence before year-end. Development continues within the human range, pauses at top-human-expert AI in 2035, and resumes toward superintelligence in 2040.

Most of its governance happens in hardware. Major datacenter owners and suppliers declare large purchases and sales. Rival states inspect infrastructure. AI research becomes almost entirely transparent while inference stays private.

Robot and compute capacity are rationed through cap-and-trade permits sold to the highest bidder. Most of the fees eventually fund a Citizen's Dividend. In the scenario, it rises from $45,000 per American adult in 2032 to roughly $1 million in 2035. Over the same period, AI and robots go from doing about 20 percent to about 85 percent of labor weighted by economic value.

I think the dates are less useful than the causal chain. The insurer automates. Payroll and tax receipts shrink, while customers still need income to buy the cheaper policy. The structure survives even if the timeline slips.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Plan A slows takeoff by making compute visible and AI research open. It then uses the scarcity value of robot and compute permits to keep purchasing power in human hands.</p></div>

## The three crucibles inside Plan A

The book's interior names its sequence the Correction, the Void and the Alignment. Its public page uses Transformation for the middle passage. Read against Plan A, the sequence becomes concrete.

### The Correction

The insurer is pushed into automation by competition. Plan A projects the same pressure across an economy and says the current tax system fails as labor income disappears. Both documents place the first break in an economy built around paid human work.

### The Void

The book puts the demand problem bluntly: "Robots do not buy iPhones." It proposes a "Citizen's Dividend" and points to Alaska's oil dividend as precedent. Plan A uses the identical term, cites the Alaska Permanent Fund, and gives every US citizen one share in a Compute Dividend Corporation.

This framework was in my site's repository by March 23. AI 2040 appeared in July. The dates establish sequence, while influence would need evidence I do not have.

### The Alignment

The book ends with a choice between stewardship and a new feudal order. Plan A begins from the danger of extinction or irreversible concentration of power. I think the shared phrase is almost a distraction. An enterprise thought experiment and a superintelligence scenario still arrive at who receives the abundance and who controls its source.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The Correction breaks the labor economy, the Void exposes the demand problem, and the Alignment turns abundance into a question of power. Plan A reaches the same arc through a geopolitical scenario.</p></div>

## Where the maps part

Plan A places its main bet on verification. Governments count compute and inspect datacenters while research is shared. *The Three Crucibles* places more weight on distributed ownership and taxes tied to AI labor or output.

Plan A's own covert-project appendix shows the strain. In that fictional branch, China diverts 500,000 H100-equivalents to a secret stockpile in 2028. It later builds a datacenter in tunnels adjacent to the Medog Hydropower Station, with only around 200 people aware of the project.

Plan A treats compute like spice in *Dune*. Whoever can count and constrain the scarce input can shape the system. *The Three Crucibles* follows the claim on the abundance that system creates.

I think the two safeguards cover different failures. Verification can expose capacity while leaving the gains concentrated. Broad ownership can spread the gains while a hidden project outruns the rules.

At the insurer's contract desk, control has a plain meaning: auditability and exit. Its board should ask whether automated decisions can be inspected and stopped. It should also ask whether claims data and the records used to evaluate decisions can move to another supplier. For a European insurer, an American dividend leaves open who governs European customer data and production systems. The bargaining power exists before the contract is signed.

I don't know which timeline will survive contact with reality. AI 2040 declines to call Plan A its best guess. *The Three Crucibles* was written as a preparation document for this uncertainty. If both maps are directionally right, the bumpy road begins well before superintelligence arrives.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Verification and ownership protect against different failures, so preparation needs both. The road becomes safer when audit rights and ownership terms are decided before automation removes the bargaining power.</p></div>
