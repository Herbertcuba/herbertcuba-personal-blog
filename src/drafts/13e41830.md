In 1976, Alaska voters amended the state constitution to protect part of the oil income from the giant Prudhoe Bay field. The Alaska Permanent Fund is the public investment fund created for that purpose. Since 1982, its earnings have helped finance annual cash payments to eligible residents.

Fifty years later, this practical arrangement appears in [*AI 2040: Plan A*](https://ai-2040.com/AI-2040.pdf), a 2026 policy scenario, and [my 2026 speculative book, *The Three Crucibles*](https://www.cubagarcia.com/books/the-three-crucibles/). Both cite Alaska because it answers a question that AI may make urgent again: how can citizens retain a claim on wealth produced by scarce assets they do not personally own?

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Alaska shows how income from a finite productive asset can become a lasting public benefit.</p></div>

## What AI 2040 actually proposes

AI 2040 is a scenario and policy proposal from the [AI Futures Project](https://ai-2040.com/), the nonprofit group behind [*AI 2027*](https://ai-2027.com/), its April 2025 scenario about rapid AI development. Plan A calls itself “primarily a recommendation, not a prediction.” Its dates describe one designed path, not settled facts.

In that path, the United States and China reach an agreement in 2029, track advanced chips, inspect data centers, and share more research on their most capable systems. Compute means the processing capacity supplied by chips and data centers. The scenario pauses in 2035 at AI matching top human experts, then moves in 2040 toward superintelligence: systems better than the best humans across almost all tasks, while faster and cheaper. An appendix puts below 10% odds on a covert Chinese project reaching AI that dominates top experts without detection before 2043.

Plan A also creates tradable permits, or authorizations that can be bought and sold, to build robots or computing capacity. Most fees go to the proposed Compute Dividend Corporation, with one share per U.S. citizen. Its citizen dividend is a recurring cash payment tied to that common asset rather than to employment.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Plan A combines international verification with a public claim on income from automated production.</p></div>

## The three crucibles in plain language

The public chronology is narrow but clear: a version of *The Three Crucibles* was available on my site by March 23, 2026; AI 2040 was publicly available by July 9, 2026. The timing makes comparison worthwhile, but it proves neither influence nor accuracy.

**The Correction (2026)** breaks the link between work and production. As AI makes productivity increasingly a property of capital, employment becomes a weaker source of income, bargaining power, and tax revenue.

**The Transformation (2027–2032)** is the adjustment that follows. The book proposes a Citizen’s Dividend, a direct transfer from AI infrastructure to people, while society rebuilds meaning beyond paid work.

**The Alignment (2035+)** is the ownership choice. Abundance can be held broadly and governed by stewards, or concentrated among what the book calls new feudal lords.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Work loses its economic role, society rebuilds income and meaning, then ownership decides who benefits.</p></div>

## Where the two accounts converge

The first agreement is economic. The Correction says production can grow while labor loses value. Plan A reaches the same problem through tax arithmetic: when wages shrink relative to machine output, a state that relies on labor taxes loses revenue as demand for support rises.

The second is distribution. Both use the name Citizen’s Dividend and point to Alaska, although their funding differs. Plan A charges for permission to build robots or compute; my book proposes taxing AI labor and spreading ownership across society. Both reconnect purchasing power to production when wages no longer do that job.

The third is the choice between shared abundance and concentrated power. This convergence matters, but AI 2040 is still a scenario, not data from the future.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The works find the same economic sequence independently, which is a useful signal rather than proof.</p></div>

## What their disagreement reveals

AI 2040 puts the heavier burden on verification: declared computing capacity, tracked chips, inspected facilities, shared research, and evidence before powerful systems are released. Its central fear is a race that states cannot monitor and AI that humans cannot control.

*The Three Crucibles* puts more weight on ownership and power: who owns the systems, who receives their value, and who sets their purpose. It says less about technical control. If Plan A secures control but leaves ownership concentrated, a stable system can still produce an unacceptable society. If my book gets distribution right but control wrong, fair ownership will not contain a system beyond human direction.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Verification limits dangerous capability; ownership limits dangerous concentration. We need to ask both questions.</p></div>

## Preparation is the honest use

Nobody knows whether a 2029 agreement, a 2035 pause, or my sequence will happen. I think the honest use of both works is preparation for choices that may arrive before certainty does.

That is why *The Three Crucibles* is a preparation document rather than a calendar. If AI 2040 and my book are even directionally right, the road will be bumpy. Income may separate from work before institutions are ready, while technical control may improve without ownership becoming fair. We do not know the route, but we can decide which forms of control, distribution, and ownership are worth defending.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The future is unknowable, but both works prepare us for decisions that become harder once disruption begins.</p></div>
