Alaska established its Permanent Fund in 1976. Annual dividends began in 1982, giving residents a share of public resource wealth.

Fifty years later, [AI 2040: Plan A](https://ai-2040.com/AI-2040.pdf) uses Alaska to explain a dividend funded by permits on compute and robots. [The Three Crucibles](/books/the-three-crucibles/) uses the same precedent to ask how purchasing power might survive as machines take over more work.

A public edition of the book was available on cubagarcia.com by 23 March 2026, before AI 2040 appeared publicly in July. I think the timing is less interesting than the overlap. Each work sees labour income weakening before machine wealth has a public channel.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>AI 2040 and The Three Crucibles use Alaska's dividend to think about machine wealth. Their methods differ, yet each warns that abundance could begin with a difficult social transition.</p></div>

## What AI 2040 is preparing for

Plan A asks how humanity could slow a superintelligence race while continuing to develop AI.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>AI 2040 proposes a 2029 US-China agreement and broad visibility into frontier research. Its scenario pauses at top-human-expert AI in 2035, then resumes in 2040 after deciding that aligned systems can receive control.</p></div>

Four members of its six-author team also wrote [AI 2027](https://ai-2027.com/): Thomas Larsen, Romeo Dean, Eli Lifland, and Daniel Kokotajlo. They describe Plan A as primarily a recommendation and use the scenario to test what might follow if it worked.

The United States and China make a deal in 2029.

AI then scales within the human range until 2035, pauses at top-human-expert level, and resumes toward superintelligence in 2040. Major datacenter transactions are declared, sites are inspected, and frontier research becomes visible across the international consortium.

In the scenario, institutions and equipment are handed to AIs until humans can no longer shut everything down by the end of 2040. The report calls this "Passing the Torch to AIs."

Plan A has the political logic of *Dune*: whoever controls the scarce input shapes the world built on top of it. Compute becomes the spice. The Alaska question then moves to planetary scale, because the dividend and the power to decide still travel through different hands. I think the authors are right to leave friction inside success.

## The economy beneath the forecast

Alaska enters the argument before superintelligence does.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Automation weakens wage income and attached taxes in both accounts. Their shared response is a Citizen's Dividend based on the Alaska Permanent Fund.</p></div>

The book's interior names three passages: the Correction, the Void, and the Alignment. Its public page calls the middle period the Transformation.

The Correction removes human labour from more production. The middle period confronts an economy that can make goods while households lose purchasing power. The Alignment asks who owns the machines.

AI 2040 reaches that pressure through its economic model. In the scenario, AI and robots perform about 20 percent of labour by economic value in 2032 and about 85 percent in 2035. The tax base collapses as a share of GDP without reform.

Permits for new compute and robot capacity fund a Citizen's Dividend, projected at $45,000 per person in 2032 and about $1 million in 2035.

The Three Crucibles uses the identical term and the same Alaska precedent. It puts the demand problem in one blunt sentence: "Robots do not buy iPhones." I claim no novelty for the term. I think its independent use in the same role shows why distribution belongs inside the economic model.

## The right beyond the dividend

A dividend can distribute income while compute remains concentrated.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Plan A leans on international verification and distributed frontier development. The Three Crucibles gives more weight to distributed ownership, since income rights and control rights can separate inside a well-governed system.</p></div>

AI 2040 builds around declarations, inspections, tracked chips, and visible research. Its hypothetical covert-project appendix imagines China hiding around 500,000 H100-equivalents of compute in tunnels near the Medog Hydropower Station. Within that branch, the chance of reaching Top-Expert-Dominating AI (TED-AI) undetected remains below 10 percent until 2043. The treaty has to survive cheating.

The Third Crucible asks whether AI infrastructure owners become stewards of abundance or feudal lords. Distributed AI ownership, open infrastructure, and open models form its answer. [Open-source AI infrastructure](/posts/genai-open-source-renaissance/) offers one practical route, although frontier compute can still concentrate in very few hands.

Plan A gives each US citizen one share in its Compute Dividend Corporation. That is meaningful economic ownership.

The Alaska analogy also exposes its limit: residents receive proceeds while operational decisions sit elsewhere. I think verification catches hidden activity, while ownership determines who retains a claim when every rule is followed.

## Preparation without certainty

Neither work can tell us when its sequence will arrive.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The dates may fail while the institutional problems still arrive. If both accounts are directionally right, the years before abundance will be bumpy enough to justify preparing now.</p></div>

AI 2040 admits uncertainty about how much time remains. The Three Crucibles starts from another premise: machine productivity can outrun wage distribution before anyone settles the superintelligence debate.

I don't know which dates will survive contact with reality. I think the exact year matters less than what breaks first. Work changes before income systems adjust, and ownership turns political because the infrastructure already carries the economy.

Alaska created a claim on resource wealth before anyone could know the size of every future dividend. If AI 2040 and The Three Crucibles are even directionally right, we have a bumpy period ahead of us.

The Three Crucibles exists for that reason: a preparation document for the years when machine capability outruns institutional readiness.
