[AI 2040: Plan A](https://ai-2040.com/) is a 90-page attempt to describe how the United States, China, and the rest of the world could delay superintelligence until 2040 and manage the path toward it. Six people wrote it. Four of them also wrote [AI 2027](https://ai-2027.com/). Its authors present Plan A as a policy recommendation carried through a detailed scenario.

I think its scale becomes easier to grasp inside one claims department. In a thought experiment from *The Three Crucibles*, one insurer keeps 5,000 claims adjusters. A rival deploys agentic AI, cuts headcount by 40 percent, lowers premiums by 20 percent, and wins within six months. AI 2040 asks what happens to tax receipts and consumer demand when that result repeats across every large employer.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>AI 2040 turns superintelligence policy into a detailed scenario. One insurer makes its civilizational argument concrete: automation changes price and payroll together, then pushes the problem into public finance.</p></div>

## What AI 2040 asks governments to do

In Plan A's scenario, the US and China reach an agreement in 2029. That deal prevents fully automated AI research in 2030 from driving superintelligence by year-end. AI capability grows within the human range until 2035, pauses at top-human-expert level, and resumes scaling to superintelligence in 2040.

The bargain depends on visibility. Major datacenter owners and chip suppliers declare large purchases and sales. Rival powers inspect infrastructure. AI research becomes almost entirely transparent, while inference remains private. Our insurer could therefore operate inside a visible research system while keeping individual claims decisions away from public view.

Plan A also imagines permits to build robots and compute being auctioned. In the American version, most permit revenue funds a Citizen's Dividend. The scenario puts that payment at $45,000 per adult in 2032 and about $1 million in 2035, measured in real 2025 dollars. I think the numbers are deliberately uncomfortable. They force us to consider an economy where machines perform most valuable work and wages lose their old role as the main distribution system.

By the end, institutions and equipment have gradually been handed to AIs until humanity can no longer shut the whole system down. Plan A treats this as a deliberate handoff after years spent building control and trust.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Plan A slows the race through a US-China agreement and a verification regime for research and compute. Its economic answer is a dividend funded by permits to build the productive machinery.</p></div>

## The same rupture appears in The Three Crucibles

The [public framing of *The Three Crucibles*](/books/the-three-crucibles/) moves through the Correction, the Transformation, and the Alignment. The insurer enters the Correction when automation becomes the price of survival. The Transformation begins when fewer salaries can support demand. The Alignment asks who receives the output and who controls its source.

I think the repeated name is almost the least interesting part. Once the insurer automates, payroll contracts and tax receipts shrink. Demand still needs money from somewhere. A [public repository record](https://github.com/Herbertcuba/herbertcuba-personal-blog/commit/ce380abb4f4f13f5fc660a2c2e3a6a75011184a8) shows *The Three Crucibles* available on my site by March 23. [AI 2040 was publicly linked on Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48848425) on July 9. Those dates establish sequence only. Two projects reached similar machinery from different starting points.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>Both works begin with shrinking labor income and arrive at a Citizen's Dividend. Their funding mechanisms separate them.</p></div>

## The dividend-control split

Plan A may already contain enough counterweight to concentration. It imagines dozens of frontier companies, public research, mutual inspections, and one Compute Dividend Corporation share for each US citizen. That combination could spread knowledge alongside economic gains.

Its own Appendix D then tests the inspection regime with a hypothetical covert Chinese project. In 2028, 500,000 H100-equivalents are diverted to a secret PLA stockpile. In 2030, a datacenter is built in tunnels adjacent to the Medog Hydropower Station. The report estimates that roughly 200 people would need to know. It feels like the control room in a good sci-fi film: every console glows green while a second engine runs behind the wall.

I think the useful name for the disagreement is the **dividend-control split**. Our insurer can receive a healthy economy and still depend on one model supplier. Control becomes measurable when changing supplier preserves the insurer's data, decision history, and evaluations. A European insurer still has to protect its data and remain accountable for decisions, whatever America does with compute revenue.

Evidence of durable exit rights under Plan A would weaken my claim. Until then, transparency can reveal capacity while ownership remains concentrated in plain sight.

I don't know which dates will hold. I think preparation begins before certainty because the insurer negotiates its exit rights before automation becomes unavoidable. *The Three Crucibles* exists as that kind of preparation document. If both projects are even directionally right, the bumpy road starts in a contract that looked harmless when everyone signed it.

<div class="chapter-tldr"><span class="chapter-tldr__label">In short</span><p>The dividend solves the consumer's income problem while leaving the insurer's exit problem on the contract page. Preparation starts while those rights are still negotiable.</p></div>
