If you felt Claude Opus 5.5 was different, I think you are right. Less than a year after Opus 4.5, the previous game changer, we are in the next 4.5 moment. I see Anthropic's [Opus 5.5](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-opus-5-5), released on 22 September 2026, as a historic inflection point and a game changer.

In my view it is more reliable, efficient and well rounded for most tasks, which moves what I call the delegation line, the boundary of what we dare hand to agents. Less correction, fewer retries and less supervision matter as much as raw intelligence.

## Confidence is the upgrade that compounds

When agents finish work consistently, the hidden bill of reruns and review shrinks, and checking moves from every step to the end.

Reliability lets us hand over a task; judgement lets us hand over a process, and I think 5.5 is starting to show it. Anthropic reports it is much less likely than recent models to take hard-to-reverse actions or step outside its boundaries.

## The evidence points at finished work

Anthropic's launch results cover coding, knowledge work, business workflows and computer use, with typical workloads 40% cheaper than Opus 5 at default settings.

After moving its own code-remediation agent from Opus 5 to 5.5, [Sonar reports](https://www.sonarsource.com/blog/the-sonarqube-remediation-agent-using-claude-opus-5-5/) 11.9% more issues resolved end to end at about 58% lower cost per fix. Independent coding tests are clearer on savings than on correctness, so security-sensitive code still deserves a human reviewer.

## Why it belongs beside Opus 4.5

Anthropic launched [Opus 4.5](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-opus-4-5) on 24 November 2025 as "the best model in the world for coding, agents, and computer use" that "requires less intervention." Its documentation lists 4.5 as deprecated, with 5.5 as the recommended replacement. I rank 5.5 beside it because it makes broad, dependable work cheap enough to hand over at scale. No matched evaluation backs that ranking. It is my judgement, and I stand behind it.

## What dependable judgement unlocks

I expect three kinds of innovation. First, neglected work gets done: security backlogs, migrations and the maintenance that always lost to new features. Second, companies will design operations around agents, with people setting goals and handling exceptions. Third, services will sell finished outcomes instead of software seats.

## 2027 is when enterprises dare

I think enterprises have held back because supervision ate the savings, and large organisations buy certainty before speed. So yes, I believe they will finally dare to deploy fleets of agents inside their core systems, and I expect it to show in the first half of 2027.

To be early, widen assignments wherever first-pass acceptance rises and review time falls.

Opus 4.5 showed us how far agents could go. I think Opus 5.5 and beyond is when we finally let them run.
