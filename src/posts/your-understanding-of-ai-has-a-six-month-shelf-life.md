[Stanford's 2026 AI Index](https://hai.stanford.edu/ai-index/2026-ai-index-report) reports that performance on SWE-bench Verified, a software engineering benchmark, rose from about 60% to nearly 100% during 2025.

I think that pace changes what it means to understand AI. If you settle for understanding today, you are already falling behind. The only way to understand and keep up is to project what today will look like six months from now, and that is not an easy task. It is probably also the one thing we still have over AI: true imagination and conviction.

## Today's picture expires quickly

A benchmark is not a job, but the trend matters more than any single score. In March 2025, the AI evaluator [METR reported](https://metr.org/blog/2025-03-19-measuring-ai-ability-to-complete-long-tasks/) that the length of software tasks agents could complete with 50% success, measured by human completion time, had been doubling roughly every seven months.

METR is careful about extrapolating that trend, but if anything like it continues, the AI you studied six months ago is a poor guide for today's decisions.

## Two forecasts I put in writing

A year ago, I wrote [The Digital Singularity Shift](https://www.cubagarcia.com/books/the-digital-singularity-shift/) to understand how this disruption would reshape business. It argues that AI commoditizes functional differentiation, so companies compete on narrative and meaning.

Nine months ago, I wrote [The Three Crucibles](https://www.cubagarcia.com/books/the-three-crucibles/) to prophesy where AI is taking us over the next ten years, through economic correction, transformation and alignment.

I think both are, more or less, becoming true. Writing them kept me from being caught off guard, helped me stay focused and feel less anxious about the speed of change, and showed me where to place my bets and my clients'.

## Imagination is the edge we still hold

In an [online experiment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00506), Anil Doshi and Oliver Hauser found that AI-generated ideas improved ratings of short stories, especially for less creative writers, but made the stories more alike. I read that as a pull toward the likely. AI can lift the average; picturing an unlikely future and committing to it is where I think we still lead.

Conviction does not mean refusing to update. In a [two-year geopolitical forecasting tournament](https://escholarship.org/content/qt4rg4n9vr/qt4rg4n9vr.pdf), Barbara Mellers and colleagues found that probability training, teamwork and tracking top forecasters improved calibration. Write your predictions down and check them.

## Make your three bets

If you want to do yourself a favour, try to estimate where we will be in six months, one year and five years. Make the six-month estimate specific enough to check, and let the five-year view hold a few scenarios rather than one prediction.

Then revisit it. It will help you navigate the noise and focus your efforts.

AI will keep getting better at explaining today. Imagining tomorrow, and betting on it, is still our job.

Godspeed, everyone!
