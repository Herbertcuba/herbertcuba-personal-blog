In my book The Three Crucibles, I argued that the people who build AI will not voluntarily share the abundance it creates. My reason was structural rather than moral: the logic of capital accumulation has no "share with everyone" function.

On 18 October, Anthropic announced a commitment of 5% of its shares to a public-benefit fund, subject to stockholder approval. I think it is a significant concrete step on the question my book ends with, though not a solution. AI abundance will not become shared prosperity by itself, because ownership and distribution structures decide who benefits.

## The fork in the Third Crucible

My book describes three tests of the AI transition: The Correction, The Void and The Alignment. The third is a fork between abundance for all and techno-feudalism, where a few own the engines and everyone else rents intelligence.

My route to abundance combines distributed AI ownership, universal basic income and a tax on AI labor. The income is a Citizen's Dividend, which I frame as a royalty on humanity's accumulated knowledge rather than charity.

## Shares instead of a donations budget

Most corporate generosity sits in a budget that gets cut in hard times. Anthropic's fund would hold equity that gains value as the company grows, and the board may add 2% for each 50% rise in value above a baseline. I want every AI company to copy that link to the upside.

## Where my warning still holds

The fund is a wholly owned Anthropic LLC, not a charity. Direct payments to specific groups are one possible use, and governance details are still to come.

So this remains a voluntary, company-controlled commitment, the arrangement my book warns against relying on. Who chooses the beneficiaries, who checks the results, and when does share value reach people whose work AI changes?

## Why so soon?

I think Anthropic expects the demand for a share of AI's gains to arrive sooner rather than later. Its announcement already says "bolder approaches may be needed" for workers facing instability. Whoever has a structure ready when it arrives starts in pole position, with trust, goodwill and a structural head start.

The cynic in me notes the calendar. Anthropic confidentially submitted a draft IPO filing in June, then filed publicly eight days after announcing the fund. Goodwill is worth a lot before an IPO.

The optimist in me hopes Anthropic genuinely wants to be a force for good for all humanity, steering away from techno-feudalism and pressuring competitors to follow.

## What would change my mind

If competitors follow with equity commitments, independent oversight and payments that reach people, I will gladly soften my warning. Even then, a fork this large cannot rest on corporate goodwill, whatever the motive. Anthropic has shown that public benefit can be written into ownership. Next, I want it in public policy, beyond any single board. I'm watching closely.
